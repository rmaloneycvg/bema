### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Caesar's comet / the ascension of Julius Caesar** — Octavian (later **Caesar Augustus**) claimed the comet was his adoptive father Julius ascending to become a god, making himself "son of God / son of the Most High." Twelve senators "witnessed" it and were commissioned to take Rome "to the ends of the earth." The Gospel/Acts language (Son of God, Lord of Lords, Prince of Peace, ascension, witnesses to the ends of the earth) deliberately *subverts* this imperial propaganda. Jesus ascends from the Mount of Olives with *eleven* (one short of twelve — hence the next story replaces Judas).
- **"are you going to restore the kingdom to Israel?"** — can be read as the disciples still *not* getting it, or (after 40 days of Kingdom teaching) as the proper Jewish response: "we get it, it's our turn to partner with you." A rabbi who leaves means his students got it.
- **Pentecost = Shavuot** — the Festival of Weeks, 50 days after Passover; the spring harvest thanksgiving and the celebration of the giving of Torah at Sinai.
- **Sinai–Pentecost parallels**: the mountain (Sinai / Mount Zion), **glossa** (Greek for both "thunder" and "tongues"), the inauguration of God's temple (the tabernacle / the people as living stones), fire from God, and 3,000 — Moses sanctions 3,000 slain at the golden calf; Peter sees 3,000 *saved*. Pentecost redeems the golden-calf story.
- **t'shuvah** — repentance/return. The 3,000 are Jews *returning* (repenting), not converting; Gentiles don't formally enter until Acts 10.
- **barley vs. wheat harvest** — First Fruits (barley, the poor man's grain) is the Sunday after Passover: Jesus is raised on First Fruits (the poor man's sacrifice/first fruits). Shavuot (wheat, the finer grain) 50 days later.
- **uncut corners** — at Shavuot you leave the field corners for the alien, orphan, and widow (cf. Ruth). Ray Vander Laan: "If there are no uncut corners in your celebration of Jesus, you are not Pentecostal, no matter how many are speaking in tongues." The early church sells possessions and gives to those in need — true Shavuot.
- **mamzer / mumzer** — the outsider the community is to welcome.
