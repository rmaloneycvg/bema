### Hebrew Words Explained

- **halakhah** (Hebrew, “the way one walks”) — the body of *oral* interpretation and tradition surrounding the written Torah: how you actually walk out the commandments. The restriction against a Jew eating with Gentiles was *halakhah*, not written Torah — which is why Peter's withdrawal in Antioch (and his earlier Cornelius vision, “do not call unclean what I have called clean”) is about tradition, not Scripture. “Jewish customs” in Gal 2:14 = *halakhah*.
- **miqsat ma'ase haTorah** (“some of the works of the law”; Greek **erga nomou**, ἔργα νόμου) — the identity-marker works that make a person distinctly Jewish (circumcision, kosher, tassels, Sabbath). Paul's claim: no one is justified by these; justification is by faith.
- **justification vs. salvation** — a crucial Jewish distinction the modern evangelical world collapsed (c. 1,500 years later, via a Reformation steeped in legal/penal categories). *Salvation* is entirely God's business and always by grace — no Jew ever taught salvation-by-law. *Justification* (why God **declares** someone righteous, as he did Abraham) is the actual debate: **Shammai** said God declares us righteous because we do the works of the law; **Hillel** said we are justified by faith, like Abraham. Jesus's community (and Paul) sided with Hillel.
- **pillars** / Jewish midrash of three-then-four — calling Peter, James, and John “pillars” (Gal 2:9) echoes the midrashic “pillars” of the Jewish people: **Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob** (three patriarchs — then a fourth, **Joseph**). Marty hears a “wink”: three pillars … but who is the fourth?
- **circumcision** = sign of the whole covenant — “must Titus be circumcised?” is shorthand for “must he become fully Jewish — kosher, tassels, the whole covenant?” not just the single command.
- **right hand of fellowship** — the Roman forearm-clasp handshake symbolizing kinship (“you are me and I am you”); the pillars' acceptance of Paul as a full covenant-community member.
- **Cephas** (Aramaic *kefa*, “rock”) = Peter.
- **euangelion** — the gospel as the announcement of God's kingdom and its radical inclusion.
