### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Mount Arbel** — the mountain due west of the Sea of Galilee from which ~80% of Jesus's recorded ministry is visible.
- **Magdala** — at the foot of Mount Arbel; home of Mary Magdalene.
- **Tiberias** — Herod Antipas's Herodian capital on the sea's edge.
- **the triangle** — Chorazin, Capernaum, Bethsaida (with Gennesaret and the harbor of Magadan), the heartland of the religious (Pharisaic) Jews.
- **Gamla** — the Zealot compound east of the sea (their second-to-last stand).
- **Masada** (מְצָדָה) — "fortress"; the Zealots' final stand (cf. "the LORD is my fortress").
- **Hippos / Susita** — a northern city of the Decapolis across the sea (pagan territory).
- **Decapolis** (δεκάπολις) — the "ten cities"; so unclean to a Pharisee that even naming it rendered one impure. Beit Shean / Scythopolis is the one Decapolis city west of the Jordan.
- **eremos topos** (ἔρημος τόπος, Greek) — "a solitary/certain place"; the twin hills (site of the Church of the Beatitudes) — a strong candidate for the Sermon on the Mount and the feeding of the 5,000.

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- Herod Antipas ("that fox," Luke 13:32) and Jesus setting up in Capernaum right under his nose rather than fleeing
- The site of the Gerasene/Gadarene swine (Mark 5) — the only steep bank running to the shoreline, across the sea below Susita
- Jesus's "tell no one" (in the Jewish triangle) vs. "go and tell everyone" (in the pagan Decapolis) — explained by geography
- A metaphorical mapping of the four Gospels onto the region: Matthew → the religious triangle, Mark → the Decapolis (Rome), Luke → the triangle/Gamla (service), John → Tiberias (the Hellenistic world)
