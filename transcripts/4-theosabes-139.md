### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Saul changes his own name to Paul** — unusual (names are normally changed by one in authority, e.g., Naomi→Mara). It matches **Sergius Paulus**, the Roman proconsul (and first convert); signals a shift in Paul's self-understanding and mission toward Rome.
- **"Barnabas and Saul" → "Paul and Barnabas"** — after Pisidian Antioch, the Greek word order flips (with one exception in Acts), signaling Paul becoming the lead teacher — Barnabas humbly yielding.
- **Pisidian Antioch** — a "mini-Rome" on seven hills in the tribal region near **Galatia**; its city gate was built by Sergius Paulus. The out-of-the-way route there suggests Paul chasing his high-profile convert's hometown (and ultimately aiming at Rome).
- **Hillel vs. Shammai on God-fearers** — before Jesus, the Jewish world of Asia Minor debated what to do with Gentiles who worshiped the God of Israel. Shammai: they must convert (circumcision + the whole Law). Hillel: welcome — justified by faith like Abraham before circumcision — but still not "brothers," more like distant cousins. Asia Minor leaned Hillel (inclusion); **Galatia** was rugged Shammai country.
- **three groups** Paul addresses: **brothers** (Jews), **children of Abraham / b'nei Avraham** (proselytes/converts), and **theosabes** ("God-fearers" — God-fearing Gentiles). The whole debate was *only* about the theosabes.
- **the Jews did NOT reject the gospel** — the synagogue begs Paul to return; they only grow jealous when the *whole city* (unclean Gentiles) shows up. The scandal is never Jesus/resurrection/crucifixion; it's *who gets in*. "We now turn to the Gentiles" is because that's always been the commission (Isaiah 49:6), not merely a reaction.
- **ioudaioi / Judeans** — Paul blames the *Judean* leadership (not "the Jews") for Jesus's death.
- **mamzer / mumzer** — Matthew's agenda; the same "outsiders brought in" story.
- **PaRDeS** — Paul's sermon is itself a chain of remez quotations (a hidden second sermon).
