### Hebrew Words Explained

- **hagah** (הָגָה) — to meditate / growl (the lion over its prey); the spirit of the "hagah projects" — biblical brain-teasers whose answers lie in the text, not in logic.
- **nakah** (נָכָה) — to strike, to smite in order to kill. The same word used when Moses killed the Egyptian, when God struck the gods of Egypt, and when God told Moses to strike the rock at Horeb; here Moses *nakah*-s the rock twice.
- **paniym** (פָּנִים) — "faces," the word behind "before/in front of." In Exodus 17 God stands *paniym* the rock (between Moses and the rock); in Numbers 20 the *assembly* is gathered *paniym* the rock — the same phrase, a deliberate parallel.
- **marah** (מָרָה) — bitter / rebellious; Moses calls the people "you rebels" (*marah*), tying this scene to the bitter well of Marah (the test of the heart).
- **Kadesh / Meribah / Massah** — the place is named Kadesh, but called Meribah ("quarreling"), linking it by name to Massah and Meribah in Exodus 17 (the first striking of the rock).
- **kadosh** (קָדוֹשׁ) — holy, set apart, different. God's charge: "because you did not treat me as *kadosh* before the people" — Moses failed to show that God is different (a God of shalom, mercy, voice — not Pharaoh's stick).
