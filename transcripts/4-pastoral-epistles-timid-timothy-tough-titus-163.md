### Hebrew Words Explained

- **mamzer / mumzer** (Hebrew, often rendered “bastard”) — a child of an illegitimate (non-Torah-approved) union. Not a moral slur so much as a legal status: a *mamzer* could not be circumcised or enter the assembly of God's people. **Timothy** (Jewish mother, Greek father) was a literal, legal *mamzer* from Lystra — which is why Paul circumcised him later (Acts) and why “don't let anyone look down on you” (1 Tim 4:12) is about his *outsider status*, not merely his youth. “There are no mamzers in Christ Jesus.”
- **lech acharai** (Hebrew, “come, follow me”) — the rabbi's call to a *talmid*. Paul, a student of Gamaliel now following Yeshua, calls the rejected *mamzer* Timothy with this “follow me” — choosing, like Jesus, the ones the world rejected.
- **Yeshua haMashiach** (Hebrew, “Jesus the Messiah”) — Paul's rabbi, whose pattern (recruiting unlikely disciples) Paul imitates.
- **Shammai vs. Hillel country** — Lystra sits in the strict, obedience-driven **Shammai** region (as in Galatians), intensifying the stigma against a *mamzer* like Timothy.
- **ecclesiology / polity** — the pastoral epistles concern church order (qualifications of elders/overseers). Marty treats them at a macro level (like Leviticus) and deliberately sidesteps the hot-button polity debates, serving as a “conduit,” not an authority.
- **right-handed vs. left-handed power** (via Capon) — the Kingdom is “upside down”: *losing is the way of the Kingdom*, not a sneaky new way of winning. Paul stations **timid Timothy** (not tough Titus) at the huge, high-risk church of **Ephesus** and **tough Titus** on the small island of **Crete** — dying to worldly wisdom about who's “qualified.”
- **“Cretans are always liars, evil brutes, lazy gluttons”** (Titus 1:12) — Paul quoting one of Crete's own prophets (Epimenides); Titus's assignment is a “pagan, pagan” crowd.
- **theopneustos / “God-breathed”** — “all Scripture is God-breathed” (2 Tim 3:16).
