### Hebrew Words Explained

- **remez** (רֶמֶז) — a "hint": a rabbi/author quotes a snippet of text knowing the audience has the whole passage memorized, so the surrounding context carries the meaning. Matthew's quote of Isaiah 9 is a remez — "a new day is dawning."
- **mamzer / mumzer** (מַמְזֵר) — the outsider/illegitimate one. The hosts (via Bruce Chilton) suggest Jesus was culturally seen as a mamzer, which is why no rabbi would train him — making John the Baptist his informal rabbi, and Jesus a "rogue rabbi" calling dropout students.
- **lech acharai** (לֵךְ אַחֲרַי) — "come, follow me"; a rabbi's invitation to a disciple he believes can be like him.
- **eremos topos** (ἔρημος τόπος, Greek) — "a certain/solitary place"; the hillside of the Sermon on the Mount.
- **tzedakah** (צְדָקָה) — righteousness, but also benevolence/generosity/care for the poor. The Beatitudes read as a chiasm: hungering/thirsting for *righteousness* is answered at the center by *mercy* — true righteousness leads to mercy.
- **the Beatitudes** — not a list of states to aspire to but pronouncements of God's favor on the very people the world counts out (the poor in spirit, mourning, meek) and on those who engage the brokenness (merciful, pure in heart, peacemakers, persecuted).
