### Hebrew Words Explained

- **tavilat t'shuvah** — the baptism of repentance John the Baptist practiced at the Jordan.
- **olam hazeh** — "this age"; the present, broken age.
- **olam haba** — "the age to come"; the Kingdom of God, everything set right.
- **two-part vs. three-part eschatology** — Two-part (Essene / John the Baptist): this age ends abruptly in a great judgment that ushers in the age to come (axe at the root, winnowing fork). Three-part (Pharisaic / Jesus): the age to come overlaps this age and grows gradually (mustard seed, yeast, wheat-and-weeds).
- **diyn** — retributive justice (what John expected).
- **mishpat** — restorative justice (what Jesus brings).
- **biazo** (Greek) — "violence/force"; Marty argues it renders the Hebrew **pratz** ("to break out / burst through," as Perez burst from the womb in Genesis 38). So "the Kingdom suffers violence" is better read as the Kingdom *explosively advancing*.
- **pratz / Perez** — "breaking out"; the one who "breaks open the way" (Micah 2:13), i.e., the forerunner who kicks open the gate.
- **bechor** — firstborn (why the scarlet thread marked the twin).
- **chutzpah** — bold audacity (John's prophetic fire).
