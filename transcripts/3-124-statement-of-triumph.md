### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the triumphal entry as political theater** — Pilate enters Jerusalem from the *west* on a war stallion with soldiers (empire/chaos); Jesus enters from the *east* on a donkey with his talmidim (shalom/order). Two kingdoms colliding.
- **hoshana / hosanna** — "Lord, save us" (Psalm 118), the Sukkot cry.
- **palm fronds (lulav)** — the symbol of **Sukkot**, and the emblem of the **Zealots** (later a crucifiable offense to wave publicly). Waving them at Passover signals a readiness for revolution.
- **Zechariah 9 and 14** — Jesus on the donkey evokes Zechariah 9:9; the crowd knows Zechariah ends (ch. 14) with the Lord standing on the Mount of Olives, fighting the nations, and all coming to keep Sukkot. But 9:10 says he takes away the warhorses and proclaims *peace* — which is why Jesus weeps (Luke 19:41): they want violence.
- **two donkeys / two of everything** — Matthew's device of inserting an extra character (two blind men, two demoniacs, two donkeys) to invite the hearer into a moment of decision: which kingdom do you want? There's a riderless donkey for you.
- **the Booths of Ananus (Annas)** — the temple market where the priests changed money and sold/approved/priced/raised the sacrifices — corruption in the court reserved for Gentiles. "A house of prayer... a den of robbers."
- **the whip of cords** — possibly a rabbi shaking his **tzitzit/tallit** to call for obedience (John 2). Two cleansings (John at the start, the Synoptics at the end) echo Leviticus 14's two-stage house inspection for mold: cleared once, then — the "mold" having spread — the house must be torn down (the temple destroyed).
- **the cursed fig tree** — a fig tree represents God's *leaders* (not Israel; cf. Proverbs 27:18 — guarding a fig tree / serving one's **rav** yields sweet fruit/teaching), so Jesus curses the corrupt leadership. "This mountain" = Herodium, a mountain Herod literally built; faith like a mustard seed can move it.
- **s'micha** — authority. The priests challenge Jesus's; he deflects to John's baptism (his rabbi's authority).
- **mamzer** — the outsider; possibly Jesus (why he may not have gone through bet sefer).
- **gat shemanim** — Gethsemane, an olive press in the Kidron Valley.
