## Book of Bible

2 Peter 1:1-4; 2 Peter 2:1-22

### 2 Peter 1:1-4 (NET)

**1** From Simeon Peter, a slave and apostle of Jesus Christ, to those who through the righteousness of our God and Savior, Jesus Christ, have been granted a faith just as precious as ours. **2** May grace and peace be lavished on you as you grow in the rich knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord! **3** I can pray this because his divine power has bestowed on us everything necessary for life and godliness through the rich knowledge of the one who called us by his own glory and excellence. **4** Through these things he has bestowed on us his precious and most magnificent promises, so that by means of what was promised you may become partakers of the divine nature, after escaping the worldly corruption that is produced by evil desire.

### 2 Peter 2:1-22 (NET)

**1** But false prophets arose among the people, just as there will be false teachers among you. These false teachers will infiltrate your midst with destructive heresies, even to the point of denying the Master who bought them. As a result, they will bring swift destruction on themselves. **2** And many will follow their debauched lifestyles. Because of these false teachers, the way of truth will be slandered. **3** And in their greed they will exploit you with deceptive words. Their condemnation pronounced long ago is not sitting idly by; their destruction is not asleep. **4** For if God did not spare the angels who sinned, but threw them into hell and locked them up in chains in utter darkness, to be kept until the judgment, **5** and if he did not spare the ancient world, but did protect Noah, a herald of righteousness, along with seven others, when God brought a flood on an ungodly world, **6** and if he turned to ashes the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah when he condemned them to destruction, having appointed them to serve as an example to future generations of the ungodly, **7** and if he rescued Lot, a righteous man in anguish over the debauched lifestyle of lawless men, **8** (for while he lived among them day after day, that righteous man was tormented in his righteous soul by the lawless deeds he saw and heard) **9** —if so, then the Lord knows how to rescue the godly from their trials, and to reserve the unrighteous for punishment at the day of judgment, **10** especially those who indulge their fleshly desires and who despise authority. Brazen and insolent, they are not afraid to insult the glorious ones, **11** yet even angels, who are much more powerful, do not bring a slanderous judgment against them in the presence of the Lord. **12** But these men, like irrational animals—creatures of instinct, born to be caught and destroyed—do not understand whom they are insulting, and consequently in their destruction they will be destroyed, **13** suffering harm as the wages for their harmful ways. By considering it a pleasure to carouse in broad daylight, they are stains and blemishes, indulging in their deceitful pleasures when they feast together with you. **14** Their eyes, full of adultery, never stop sinning; they entice unstable people. They have trained their hearts for greed, these cursed children! **15** By forsaking the right path they have gone astray, because they followed the way of Balaam son of Bosor, who loved the wages of unrighteousness, **16** yet was rebuked for his own transgression (a dumb donkey, speaking with a human voice, restrained the prophet’s madness). **17** These men are waterless springs and mists driven by a storm, for whom the utter depths of darkness have been reserved. **18** For by speaking high-sounding but empty words they are able to entice, with fleshly desires and with debauchery, people who have just escaped from those who reside in error. **19** Although these false teachers promise such people freedom, they themselves are enslaved to immorality. For whatever a person succumbs to, to that he is enslaved. **20** For if after they have escaped the filthy things of the world through the rich knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, they again get entangled in them and succumb to them, their last state has become worse for them than their first. **21** For it would have been better for them never to have known the way of righteousness than, having known it, to turn back from the holy commandment that had been delivered to them. **22** They are illustrations of this true proverb: “A dog returns to its own vomit,” and “A sow, after washing herself, wallows in the mire.”

### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the “other side” of the conversation** — if 1 Peter says “endure persecution, keep your distinctiveness,” 2 Peter addresses the opposite temptation: *don't dissolve into the culture*. Aimed especially at **Gentile** converts: “to those who … have received a faith as precious as *ours*” (1:1) — “ours” = the Jews, “those” = the Gentiles.
- **righteousness = generosity/kindness** — for a Jewish teacher, Jesus's “righteousness” means his generosity; because of it, Gentiles received a faith as precious as the Jews'.
- **orthodoxy vs. orthopraxy** — the “false teachers” of 2 Peter are NOT teaching wrong *belief* (orthodoxy) but leading people into wrong *practice* (orthopraxy). “Denying the Lord” is by *conduct*, not doctrine. The three things they refuse to acknowledge: (1) the ungodliness/idolatry of the culture, (2) the lure of power/wealth/prestige/influence, (3) the sexual immorality incongruent with Jesus's way.
- **guild feasts** — the cultural engine behind the temptation: monthly vocational-guild parties (labor union + fraternity) centered on a patron god, featuring *raw meat* eaten to “ingest” the god (e.g., Zeus) and ending in drunken sexual debauchery with prostitutes. (John's “eat my flesh / drink my blood” likely plays on this — Flusser thought it too un-Jewish to be authentic, but Marty disagrees.)
- **Midrash references** (fuller treatment deferred to the Jude episode): fallen **angels in chains of darkness** (Book of Enoch / the Watchers / Nephilim), **Noah and “seven others”** (8 total), **Sodom and Gomorrah** (whose sin — per Ezekiel — was failing the alien/orphan/widow and pursuing power/idolatry), and **Balaam son of Beor** (per Midrash, advised Balak to send Moabite women to seduce Israel — idolatry + lust + greed; affirmed by the NT).
- **Tartarus** (2 Pet 2:4, “hell”) — a *different* Greek word than Jesus's **gehenna**; worth noting.
- **sarx** (“flesh / animal appetites”) — “like unreasoning animals, creatures of instinct” (2:12); the false teachers follow the *sarx*.
- **shephelah** (Hebrew, the “lowland” between the coastal plain and the Judean hills) — the Session 2 image of living “in the tension” at the crossroads; the same mission of God (a kingdom of priests showing the world what God is like) from Joshua/Judges to Greco-Asia. “Don't have any pig bones” (an earlier lesson) = don't compromise your distinctiveness.
