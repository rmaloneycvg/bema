### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Pharisees / P'rushim** (פְּרוּשִׁים) — the "separated ones"; the second Hasidim group (with the Zealots), who channeled their zeal not into violence but into absolute devotion and obedience to the commands and text of God. Their answer to "what do we do about Rome?" — let God deal with Rome once we are finally obedient enough.
- **Hasidim** (חֲסִידִים) — "pious ones"; the devoted remnant in the Galilee from whom came both Zealots and Pharisees.
- **insula** (Latin) — the multi-family dwelling the Pharisees shared, reflecting communal commitment over Hellenistic private ownership.
- **bema** (בִּימָה) — the reading platform at the center of the synagogue; text is central to the Pharisaic world.
- **the Galilean "triangle"** — Chorazin, Capernaum, and Bethsaida (sometimes also Gennesaret and Magadan), the heartland of the Orthodox Pharisaic world where Jesus centered his ministry.

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- Matthew 11:20-24 ("Woe to you, Chorazin... Bethsaida... Capernaum") — Jesus's rebuke is for failing to *repent* (not for failing to believe); the message didn't change how they lived
- Tyre and Sidon (prophetic image of arrogance, cf. Isaiah) and Sodom (lack of hospitality) rising to judge them
- Capernaum as the "Harvard/Yale" of the Pharisaic world, where Jesus set up shop
- Bethsaida as the likely home of Peter, Andrew, James, John, and Philip
- Ezekiel 34 (shepherds who lost God's sheep) behind "the lost sheep of Israel"
- The hosts' indictment: the modern church as "cultural Herodians and religious Pharisees" — the bad of both, lacking the Pharisees' devotion and missing the one piece that matters: compassion
