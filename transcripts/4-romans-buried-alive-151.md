### Hebrew Words Explained

- **pistis Christou** (Greek πίστις Χριστοῦ) — a hotly debated phrase usually rendered “faith **in** Christ” but, modern scholarship argues, better rendered “faith **of** Christ.” “Faith in Christ” (as a creedal affirmation) fails Paul's own argument — Abraham could not have creedal faith in Christ, yet he had *the faith of Christ* (trust in God's promises). This reframes faith as *trust*, not creedal assent.
- **faith = trust (emunah)** — “trusting in the promises of God” / “trust the story”: trusting the Genesis 1 truth that God loves, values, and calls his creation good. Our *image-of-God-ness* is *more true* than our depravity.
- **kal va'chomer** (Hebrew, “light and heavy” / lesser-to-greater) — the rabbinic argument form Jesus used (e.g., the persistent widow, Luke 18; Luke 11). The tell is the phrase **“how much more.”** Romans 5 is, Marty says, “the greatest *kal va'chomer* of all time”: if sin/death came through one man (Adam), *how much more* did grace/life come through one man (Jesus). Sin is a big deal — but it “ain't got nothing” on grace.
- **Adam as pattern/type** — “Adam, who is a pattern of the one to come” (Rom 5:14). Read through a Jewish lens (not Augustine), Paul uses Adam as a *representative/type* for a lesser-to-greater comparison, not primarily a doctrine of inherited guilt.
- **eternal life = qualitative, not just quantitative** — “eternal life” is a *quality* of life to be lived *now* (“life before death, not just life after death”), echoing Session 2.
- **tzedakah** (Hebrew, “righteousness / generosity”) — the gospel sets us free *to* tzedakah: righteousness, generosity, and self-sacrifice (vs. a life enslaved to fear and self-preservation).
- **baptism / forsaking** — “buried with him through baptism into death”: a watery grave picturing death-and-resurrection; the early church (per the *Didache*) paired baptism with a *forsaking* of the old pagan/Greco-Roman idolatrous way.
- **Session 1 root of sin** — we sin out of *fear*, *insecurity*, and “not enough” (scarcity); grasping God's grace means dying to that worldview.
