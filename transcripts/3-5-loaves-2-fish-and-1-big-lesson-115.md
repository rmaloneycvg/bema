### Hebrew Words Explained

- **parashah** — the weekly Torah portion (the five books of Moses read through in a 52-week cycle).
- **haftarah** — the paired reading from the Prophets/Writings (roughly a 3-year cycle). Jesus reads the haftarah in Nazareth — but the Isaiah passage he reads matches no known lectionary, suggesting he chose it pointedly.
- **dereshah** (same root as **drash**) — the short synagogue sermon (30–90 seconds) after a long reading; Jesus's was "Today this scripture is fulfilled in your hearing."
- **netzer** — "shoot/branch" (Isaiah's shoot from Jesse's stump); the likely root of **Nazareth** ("Shootville").
- **s'micha** — informal authority; why rulers feared touching John the Baptist.
- **mamzer** (also **mumzer**) — the outsider; Jesus citing Elijah's widow of Zarephath (Phoenicia) and Naaman the Syrian signals a mission to Gentiles.
- **Number symbolism**: 1 = God; 2 = the tablets of Moses; 3 = community; 4 = the Gentiles (four corners); 5 = the books of Moses; 6 = sinful man; 7 = seven pagan nations / creation-completion; 10 (3+7) = complete community; 12 = the tribes of Israel. In the feeding: 5 loaves + 2 fish = the complete Law, given to the disciples to feed the 5,000 (all of Torah's people), with 12 baskets left over — Jesus as the second Moses.
- **eremos topos** — the solitary place; traditional site of the Sermon on the Mount and the feeding.
