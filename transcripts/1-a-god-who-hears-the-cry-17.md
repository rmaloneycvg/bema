### Hebrew Words Explained

- **hagah** (הגה) — the low rumbling growl of a lion; the posture of meditating over the Text (sitting over it like a lion over prey) — the basis for BEMA's "Hagah projects."
- **hatan** (חתן) — "bridegroom / son-in-law"; the "bridegroom of blood" in the Zipporah story (Exodus 4). It appears only 4 times in Torah — twice here and twice in the Genesis 18-19 (Sodom/Lot) story, linking the two.
- **"uncircumcised lips"** — Moses' excuse in Exodus 6:12/30 ("faltering lips"); literally "uncircumcised of lips." The Zipporah circumcision story sits structurally between Moses' two speech excuses, pointing at his refusal to embrace his covenant identity.
- **rasha** (רשע) — "wicked / wickedness"; its first four Torah uses are in the Sodom and Exodus stories.
- **tsa'aqah** (צעקה) — "the cry of distress" of the oppressed; this form's uses cluster in Sodom/Gomorrah and the Exodus ("I have heard their cry").
- **shaphat** (שפט) — "to judge"; its first four Torah uses are again in these two stories.
- **mishpat** (משפט) — "justice" (restorative — putting the world right), built on *shaphat*.
- **diyn** (דין) — "justice" in the retributive sense; sometimes part of *mishpat*, but not the driving impetus of God's arrival.
