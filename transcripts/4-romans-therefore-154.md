### Hebrew Words Explained

- **“therefore”** — the hinge of Romans 12: “when you see a *therefore*, ask what it's there for.” After eleven chapters (three groups / one problem → all justified by one faith → God shows mercy to everyone), the only logical response is **love**.
- **living sacrifice (singular)** — newer translations (ESV “present your bodies as a living *sacrifice*”) make it singular: not many individual bodies as many sacrifices, but our *many bodies offered as one united sacrifice* — one body of Christ, a single pleasing aroma to God.
- **burning coals** (Rom 12:20, quoting **Proverbs 25:21-22**) — NOT passive-aggressive torture-by-kindness. In Hebrew, *burning coals* are consistently connected to the *presence of God*; to respond to evil with good is to bring God's presence into an enemy's life, and “God will show up.”
- **sarx** (Greek σάρξ, “flesh” = animal appetites) — recurs: “do not think about how to gratify the desires of the *sarx*” (Rom 13:14). The ongoing call to *master* the animal-appetite nature so we can lay our lives down for others.
- **Hillel-style summary of Torah** — “love your neighbor as yourself … love is the fulfillment of the law” (Rom 13:8-10); Brent notes Paul “might have been discipled by Jesus” — a Jesus/Hillel stance.
- **governing authorities (Rom 13:1-7)** — read in context: these authorities are the very Romans who persecuted and executed believers, not just a rival political party. The call is to *reverence/respect/honor*, not blind obedience — the early church defiantly obeyed God over Rome when the two conflicted. Marty flags how *both* political sides quote Romans 13 only when it suits them.
