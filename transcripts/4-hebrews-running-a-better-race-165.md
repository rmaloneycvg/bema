### Hebrew Words Explained

- **homily / “word of encouragement”** — Marty's favored theory: Hebrews is a *synagogue sermon* (Heb 13:22 uses a Greek phrase, “word of encouragement,” used only of a synagogue homily), written *after* the AD 70 destruction of the temple to help Jews reimagine worship without the temple — from a Jesus perspective. (Possibly read annually on the day commemorating the temple's destruction.)
- **inclusio** (Greek literary bracketing) — not the same as a chiasm (no inverted parallelism needed). Hebrews 1–10 is one giant inclusio bracketed by the identical phrase **“he sat down at the right hand of God”** (1:3 and 10:12), signaling: understand *this argument* before Hebrews 11–13.
- **“better” / supremacy of Jesus** — the theme: Jesus is a *better* version of everything Jewish readers already cherish — better messenger, better humanity, better Moses, better Sabbath/rest, better high priest, better sacrifice, better temple. Not something *new* — the same story from Genesis, made clearer in Christ. (Must be handled carefully to avoid **supersessionism**, since the author is a Jew writing to Jews.)
- **high priest in the order of Melchizedek** — the “red ribbon” theme (2:17; 3:1; 4:14-15; 5; 6:20; 7; 8; 9). Jesus can't be a Levitical priest, so the author appeals to **Melchizedek** (Abraham's priest, Gen 14) — a priesthood that *predates* and (some argue) supersedes the Levitical one. Jesus is a **merciful and faithful** high priest, “made perfect through suffering.”
- **perseverance** — the dominant exhortation: Jesus suffered and persevered, so we persevere (10:19-39), culminating in the **“Hall of Faith”** (ch. 11) and the “great cloud of witnesses … run the race with perseverance” (ch. 12).
- **“outside the city gates”** (Heb 13) — the faithfulness that matters is not kosher/tassels/Torah-to-the-letter but going *out beyond the gates* to the outsiders and becoming a priest/sacrifice for them — faithfulness to the heart of God, which is loving others.
- **the “lullaby effect”** (Fohrman) — “you have become dull of hearing” (5:11): we grow so used to the familiar system that we stop really hearing; “let us move beyond elementary teachings … to maturity” (6:1).
- **justification ≠ atonement** — setup for the next episode: Christian theology wrongly fused faith → atonement → justification → salvation; Romans (which the show did verse-by-verse) is about *justification* and never discusses *atonement* — the two were not linked in the first-century Jewish world.
