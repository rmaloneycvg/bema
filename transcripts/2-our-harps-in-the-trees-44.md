### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Tehillim** (Hebrew תהלים, “Songs of Praise”) — the Hebrew title of Psalms. Striking, given how *diverse* (and often un-praise-like) the 150 psalms are. Reed's closing thesis: to bring *anything* honestly to God — even rage or lament — is itself an act of *praise*, a commitment to hang on to God as God in every season.
- **Psalm vs. Psalms** — grammar note: it's “Psalm 22” (singular) for a single one; “the Psalms” for the book. Also, the 150 are not “chapters.”
- **Brueggemann's three categories** — (1) **Orientation**: things make sense, an ordered/reliable world; grounded in Creation and Law (Ps 104, Ps 19, Ps 119, Ps 136 “give thanks … his love endures forever”). (2) **Disorientation**: alienation, anger, lament, suffering — “How long?” (Ps 13), “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?” (Ps 22), Ps 88 (ends in darkness, no resolution), Ps 137 (“by the rivers of Babylon … happy is he who dashes your infants on the rocks”). (3) **New Orientation**: joy/trust *beyond* the pit, hard-won (Ps 30, Ps 40 “lifted me out of the slimy pit”). Not fixed/linear — Ps 22 moves from disorientation to new orientation within one psalm.
- **imprecatory psalms** — the vengeance psalms (Ps 137; Ps 139 “I hate those who hate you”). Brueggemann: you voice the desire for vengeance *to God in a psalm* so you *don't* act it out in the world — “vengeance is yours, God; here it is.” The Bible includes these; God invites *all* of you, not just decorum.
- **the Psalms are theological but not systematically so** — both-and, not either-or. The same David says “conceived in sin” (Ps 51) *and* “you knit me together in my mother's womb” (Ps 139); “you hide your face” *and* “you hem me in behind and before.” You can't build a doctrine on a psalm alone — check it against Torah / the rest of Scripture. God is not a *subject of study* but the *object/hearer* the psalmist addresses; even God's *absence* produces argument, not atheism.
- **Songs of Ascent** (Psalms 120–134) — recited/sung on the pilgrimage road up to Jerusalem/the Temple — the communal/liturgical function (alongside the deeply personal function, e.g., Ps 51 after Nathan confronts David over Bathsheba).
- **the five books of Psalms** — the Psalter has editorial “seams” dividing it into five books; **Psalms 1 and 2** (anonymous) serve as an *introduction* before the Davidic psalms begin at Psalm 3. Roughly half are ascribed to David.
- **yada / honest relationship** — “God is the appropriate dialogue partner for every season of life” (Derek, CCF): keep the conversation going; don't hang up when it's hard *or* when it's great.
