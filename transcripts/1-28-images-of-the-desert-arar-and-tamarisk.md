### Hebrew Words Explained

- **kohen** (כֹּהֵן) — priest. From cognate languages: "one who undertakes another's cause" (a deputy/delegate) plus a sense of opulence/abundance — an "opulent delegate" who represents God's realm to the people and the people's cause to God.
- **ar'ar** (עַרְעָר) — the desert bush that looks vibrant and bright green but is toxic/poisonous inside; its fruit pops open to a hollow, cobwebby emptiness with a milky, toxic coating.
- **arur** (אָרוּר) — "cursed"; the strongest word for curse, to bind someone to a curse (execration), with an image of razing/stripping down. An audible pun with *ar'ar* in Jeremiah 17.
- **arava** (עֲרָבָה) — the desert/wilderness (another word for it, distinct from *midbar*); a third pun in Jeremiah 17: *arur / ar'ar / arava*.
- **acacia** (שִׁטָּה, *shittah*) — looks dead but is "the gift of the desert"; the wood of the Ark, the poles, the boards of the Mishkan, and the altars — overlaid with gold (an "opulent delegate" image).
- **tamarisk** (אֵשֶׁל, *eshel*) — a cultivated "luxury" tree (also called salt cedar) whose shade is cooler because it draws moisture from the air. It takes ~80 years to mature, so you plant it for your grandchildren — "How many tamarisk trees did you plant today?"
- **olam** (עוֹלָם) — eternal / forever / in perpetuity; the vanishing line of the horizon. Abraham calls on *El Olam*, the Eternal God, at the tamarisk — a witness to what God will keep doing for future generations.
