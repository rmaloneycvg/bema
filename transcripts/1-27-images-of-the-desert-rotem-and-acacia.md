### Hebrew Words Explained

- **rotem** (רֹתֶם) — the broom tree / broom bush, the desert image of *shade*. It is a small, solitary bush offering only "just enough" shade for one person — not abundant comfort. Elijah collapses under a *rotem* (1 Kings 19); rabbinic tradition places Ishmael under one (Genesis 21).
- **acacia** (שִׁטָּה, *shittah*) — "the gift of the desert." Grows at the end of wadis; its wood builds and burns well, its sap/pitch heals, its pods feed livestock. It can look dead for a decade of drought, then spring to life when the rains come. The biblical botanist Noga Hareuveni identified it as the tree of Psalm 1 and Jeremiah 17.
- **hagah** (הָגָה) — to meditate; but onomatopoeically the low, rumbling *growl* of a lion hunched over its prey (Isaiah 31:4). Psalm 1's "meditate on his law day and night" is not a calm morning devotion — it is to growl over the word, hungry to devour it.
- **wadi** — a desert canyon/ravine; dry most of the year but subject to flash floods, the number-one desert killer. The acacia grows where the floodwater spreads and soaks in.
