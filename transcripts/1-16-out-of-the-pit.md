### Hebrew Words Explained

- **akhu / akh** (אחו / אח) — "reeds" vs. "brother"; Rabbi Akiva's wordplay — the cows come up "out of the reeds," which can be heard as "out of the brothers," tying Pharaoh's dream to Joseph's family story.
- **Reeds (Field of Reeds)** — in Egyptian mythology, the name of the paradise/afterlife — possibly evoking Joseph's lost "paradise" of childhood with his father.
- **"beautiful of form / sleek"** — the cows in Pharaoh's retelling (v. 18) are described with the same Hebrew used of Rachel when we first meet her — another cue linking the dream to Joseph's mothers.
- **bechor** (בכור) — "chosen / firstborn"; Elle suggests Joseph's long garment marks him as trained in administration (running the *beit av*), skills he now applies to all Egypt.
- **Zaphenath-Paneah** (צפנת פענח) — the Egyptian name Pharaoh gives Joseph.
- **"you meant it for evil, but God meant it for good"** (Genesis 50:20) — Elle's key verse on forgiveness: grounded not in excusing the brothers but in the all-sufficiency of God.
