### Hebrew Words Explained

- **prophets as guerrilla theater** — not *gorilla* (the animal) but *guerrilla*: subversive, camouflaged, with teeth. Prophetic literature is beautiful, poignant poetry delivered to provoke — not a source of doctrinal proof-texts (you won't find a church “what we believe” page citing Lamentations). It's about *how* the message is delivered, not just the data (cf. Ezekiel baking over dung).
- **the first of the pre-Assyrian prophets** — Amos speaks to the *northern kingdom of Israel* (Uzziah king of Judah, Jeroboam king of Israel) when all seems well; a word of *warning* that the army is coming because the “empire” they built is not the kingdom of Shalom.
- **“for three sins … even for four”** — a Hebrew figure of speech (like “six, even seven”) that rounds off/completes an idea. Amos marches through Israel's *Gentile neighbors* (Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, Ammon, Moab) — lulling Israel into cheering — then turns to **Judah**, then finally to **Israel itself**. Notably, the Gentile nations are indicted for *justice* crimes (atrocities, slave-trading, ripping open pregnant women), not idolatry.
- **Source A vs. Source B in Amos** — the test case: Amos is overwhelmingly *Source B* (justice), stated right on the surface — selling the innocent for silver / the needy for sandals, trampling the poor, sleeping on garments taken in pledge, levying a straw-tax on the poor, dishonest scales. Only Judah gets a *Source A* (idolatry/forsaking the law) note. Brent and Marty flag that A and B are *related* (idolatry *produces* injustice) — the “both-and” to be developed later.
- **cows of Bashan** (Amos 4:1) — not merely “calling women cows”: the Bashan (Golan Heights) cattle were famously plump/well-fed — a prophetic image of over-fed *luxury* bought at the expense of the poor.
- **“let justice roll on like a river”** (5:24) — God despises their religious festivals, assemblies, and harp-music *while* they exploit the poor: “no matter how pure your worship, if you trample the needy, it's a stench to me.” Marty applies it to the modern West (cheap T-shirts from exploited labor, the iPhone supply chain): “do we *care*?” — and stresses the *corporate/systemic* (not merely individual) reading.
- **the image: the plumb line** (Amos 7:7-8) — the memory-image for Amos. A *plumb line* (a weighted string using gravity, the pre-modern “level”) tests whether a wall is “true to plumb”/straight. God sets a plumb line among Israel — the verdict is crooked. (A second image: a basket of **ripe fruit**, Amos 8 — “the time is *ripe* for my people.”) God relents from locusts and fire, but the plumb-line verdict stands.
- **Sakkuth / Kaiwan vs. Molek / Rephan** (Amos 5:26) — Brent's note: the Hebrew names star-gods (Sakkuth, Kaiwan), which the Septuagint renders Molek/Rephan — updated for a later audience's relevance (“what would we put in if rewriting Amos today?”) — a dash of *Source A* idol-worship under the *Source B* indictment.
