### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Emmanuel** (עִמָּנוּ אֵל) — "God with us" (Matthew 1:23, quoting Isaiah 7:14). In context Isaiah's sign was about his own day (the prophetess's child, likely tied to Hezekiah); Matthew invokes it to say those same days of oppression are upon us now — and here is God with us.
- **kataluma** (κατάλυμα, Greek) — "guest room," the word Luke uses in the birth story (not *pandocheion*, the word for a commercial "inn" he uses in the Good Samaritan). "No room in the *kataluma*" points to a crowded household/shame, not a hotel.
- **tekton** (τέκτων, Greek) — "builder"; Joseph more likely a stonemason (Herod's quarry near Nazareth) than a woodworker.
- **mumzer / mamzer** (מַמְזֵר) — the outsider; Matthew's agenda runs through the messy, shame-filled birth narrative.
- **Magi** — eastern (Babylonian/Persian) astrologer-priests who "saw his star when it rose" — echoing Balaam's oracle, "a star will come out of Jacob" (Numbers 24:17).
- **Mithra / MenEskenu / the zodiac** — the fast-growing Greco-Roman mystery religion (Mithraism) built on the precession of the equinoxes (the sun shifting from the house of Taurus to Aries to Pisces, ~5 BC); its savior was born in a cave, attended by shepherds, with seven sacraments — the very story God enters and outdoes at just the right moment.
