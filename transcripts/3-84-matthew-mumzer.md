### Hebrew Words Explained

- **mumzer / mamzer** (מַמְזֵר) — a child of an illegitimate, Torah-disapproved birth (often rendered "bastard"); the hosts use it poetically for the *outsider / outcast*. Matthew's whole agenda: in Jesus's Kingdom there are no mumzers — the outsiders are brought in.
- **euangelion** (εὐαγγέλιον, Greek) — "gospel": the proclamation of a new King and a new Kingdom; for Matthew that Kingdom has no outsiders.
- **Q source** — the hypothesized shared material behind the overlapping sayings in Matthew and Mark.
- **Didache** (Διδαχή) — "teaching"; an early-church text that may reflect related source material ("the teaching of the apostles").
- **Kingdom of Heaven vs. Kingdom of God** — Matthew, a Jew writing to Jews, avoids saying "God" and substitutes "heaven" (as Jews still say *Hashem*, "the Name," or *Adonai*). The terms are interchangeable (cf. the prodigal: "I have sinned against heaven").
- **oikos** (οἶκος, Greek) / "the House" — the reverent substitute for "temple," which was too holy to name directly.
