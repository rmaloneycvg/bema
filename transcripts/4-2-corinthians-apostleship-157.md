### Hebrew Words Explained

- **“the second letter” is really at least the third** — 1 Cor 5:9 (“I wrote to you in my letter…”) refers to a *lost* earlier letter, and 2 Cor 7:8 refers to a painful letter, so what we call 1 and 2 Corinthians are really later letters in a longer correspondence.
- **“thorn in the flesh”** (2 Cor 12:7) — several theories: *blindness* (cf. Gal 4:15, “you would have torn out your eyes and given them to me”), a *deserted wife*, or (Marty's text-based lean) the *Gentiles/pagan nations* — since “thorn in the side” is a recurring biblical image for the nations (Numbers 33:55; Joshua 23:13). “My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.”
- **Paul almost certainly married** — from a Jewish perspective a student of Gamaliel would have had to be married, because the first commandment of Torah is “be fruitful and multiply”; one could not be a rabbi while ignoring the first command. (Jesus's singleness is the odd case Jewish readers puzzle over.)
- **apostleship / spiritual authority** — the big theme of 2 Corinthians: the Corinthians disrespect Paul's authority (“his letters are weighty, but in person unimpressive”). Paul refuses to boast in pedigree, only in suffering and self-sacrifice — “imitate me as I imitate Christ.” The “super-apostles” are his rivals.
- **“examine yourselves”** (2 Cor 13:5) — the closing call, paralleling 1 Corinthians: test whether you are in the faith; authority is “for building you up, not tearing you down.”
- **spiritual gifts — unique to Corinth** — Paul discusses tongues/miraculous gifts only with Corinth, nowhere else (not Rome, Philippi, Colossae, Ephesus); Marty takes this to mean something special was happening *there*, declining to rule for or against modern charismatic practice.
