### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Shabbat** — the Sabbath; "we rest, we play, no work, God loves us." Torah's direct Sabbath commands are few; the complexity comes from interpretation.
- **halakhah** (from **halakh**, "to walk") — the rabbinic rulings on how to *walk out* / fulfill Torah; the oral tradition. Jesus breaks halakhah (tradition), never Torah itself.
- **Mishnah / Talmud** — the written "fences" built around Torah's 613 commands (c. 100 and 200+ years after Jesus).
- **mitzvot** — commandments.
- **yoke** — a rabbi's interpretive lens for Torah, defined by his answer to "what is the second greatest commandment?"
- **Hillel vs. Shammai** — the two rabbinic schools preceding Jesus. Shammai's second commandment ≈ "obey the Sabbath" (a yoke of obedience); Hillel's ≈ "love your neighbor" (a yoke of love). Jesus almost always sides with Hillel.
- **the weightiness of the law** — determining which commandment is weightier when two conflict (e.g., the donkey/sheep in a pit on the Sabbath).
- **kal va'chomer** — a "light and heavy" argument (how much more...): if you'd rescue a sheep, how much more a person.
- **Son of Man** — here "a human being is lord of the Sabbath" (Sabbath was made for people).
- **mishpat** — justice (Isaiah 42: "he will proclaim justice to the nations").
