### Hebrew Words Explained

- **two sources, two agendas** — **Source A (Samuel/Kings)** = “agenda-driven headlines” from Israel's perspective, nearer real-time; its agenda is **immorality / idolatry** (David's turning point is *Bathsheba*; Solomon's is his *700 wives* leading to idolatry). **Source B (Chronicles)** = the “documentary” written ~400–500 years later in exile, from Judah's perspective; its agenda is **Empire / injustice**. Chronicles *omits Bathsheba entirely* and *omits Solomon's wives/idolatry*, and *adds* a huge amount about the **temple**.
- **kiddush HaShem** (Hebrew, “hallow the Name”) — early David acts void of self-indulgence, continually hallowing the Name (bringing the Ark to Jerusalem, prayer/praise). After the hinge, that fades.
- **666 as the turning point** — in Samuel/Kings, Sheba's gift/treaty-tax weighs **666 talents** (1 Kgs 10 // the chariot “600 shekels” in 2 Chron 1:17): the author's signal that the story has turned toward the beastly. In Chronicles the temple *request* (1 Chron 17 // 2 Sam 7, “why should I live in a palace of cedar while God lives in a tent?” — God: “when did I ask for a house?”) functions as David's hinge, *replacing* Bathsheba.
- **the king's checklist (Deuteronomy 17:16-17)** — a king must NOT (1) accumulate many *horses* / return to Egypt for them, (2) take many *wives*, (3) amass *silver and gold*. 2 Chronicles 1:14-17 shows Solomon doing *all three* — “Solomon made the checklist and forgot the word ‘not.’” He becomes an *arms dealer* (chariots = tanks/Humvees), building Empire at the people's expense (hence Rehoboam's “loosen the yoke”).
- **“the Lord” vs. “Satan” incited David** — the census story: **2 Samuel 24:1** says “the anger of the Lord … incited David” (Samuel/Kings: David's *personal* immoral/bad-leadership failure); **1 Chronicles 21:1** says “**Satan** rose up … and incited David,” and adds “why should he bring **guilt on Israel**?” — Chronicles reframes it as the serpent's lie / lust for Empire bringing guilt on the whole *nation* (fitting its exile vantage). A census implies a *tax* — resources for a building project.
- **the anti-story** — the kings mostly “lose the plot”: instead of blessing all nations at the crossroads, Israel becomes self-preserving oppressors building empires — “becoming Pharaoh.” When God's people become the anti-story and oppress, God hears the cry of the oppressed. **Solomon did use forced labor/slaves** (1 Kings 5:13; 9:15,21; 2 Chron 8:8).
- **buildings are amoral** — cedar palace or gold temple, tent or temple: God doesn't care about the opulence; the danger is the *heart* that forgets the field, the rotem bush, and slavery in Egypt and drifts toward Empire.
- **not like the Gospels** — Brent's caution: Samuel/Kings vs. Chronicles is *not* like the four Gospels (near-contemporaneous, mutually aware). These are *centuries apart*, different peoples, different vantage points — both inspired, neither “more right.”
