### Hebrew Words Explained

- **TaNaKh** (Hebrew acronym — **T**orah, **N**evi'im, **K**etuvim) — the Hebrew Scriptures contain the *same books* as the Christian Old Testament but in a *different order/categorization*. **Torah** = the five books of Moses (same order). **Nevi'im** = the Prophets — which, surprisingly, *includes* **Joshua, Judges, Samuel, and Kings**. **Ketuvim** = the Writings (wisdom books, the five scrolls, Ruth, Esther — and **Chronicles**, which is literally the *last* book of the Hebrew Bible).
- **history is prophetic** — because Samuel/Kings sit among the *Nevi'im*, Hebrew history-telling is *prophetic*: not primarily future-telling, but delivering God's message/pathos to wake people up in their *present*. So the David-and-Goliath account isn't neutral reportage — it provokes you to be a certain kind of person. (This is also why Joshua reads as a prophetic *conquest narrative*, not flat literal history.)
- **two sources, one story** — the kingdom era (Saul, David, Solomon, the divided kingdom of Israel/Judah) is told *twice*: **Source A = Samuel/Kings** and **Source B = Chronicles**. This is why a “Bible in a year” reader hits Saul/David/Solomon again in 1 Chronicles and gets disoriented.
- **Source A (Samuel/Kings)** — told from “Israel's perspective” (poetically/historically, not geographically): nearer-to-real-time, “agenda-driven headlines.” Ancient history is deliberately *biased/agenda-driven* (unlike the Western ideal of neutral history) — if it's expensive to write down, you write to *teach*.
- **Source B (Chronicles)** — told from “Judah's perspective,” written ~400 years later *during the exile*, with hindsight — like a *documentary* vs. a newspaper headline. Same events, shaped differently to make different points (explored next episode).
- **the 9/11 analogy** — reading the headlines in 2001 (real-time, limited, in-the-moment) vs. watching a good documentary 20 years later (hindsight, motivations, backstory): both “right,” just different perspectives with different limits. That's Samuel/Kings vs. Chronicles.
- **Source A / Source B as training wheels** — helpful at first, but “lose your grip” as you go; the technical (documentary-hypothesis-adjacent) language is used loosely/poetically here, with historical/dating debates deferred.
- Full meta-narrative review recapped: Preface (Gen 1–11) → Introduction (Gen 12–50) → “Tale of Two Kingdoms” (Exodus → Sinai wedding → Leviticus/priesthood & partying → Numbers honeymoon → Deuteronomy “remember”: 14 “remember” + 5 “do not forget,” the **AOW** alien/orphan/widow) → Joshua (crossroads) → Judges (redemption cycle) → Ruth → the kingdom era.
