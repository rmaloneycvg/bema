### Hebrew Words Explained

- **millstone** — Capernaum's main export (dark basalt grain mills). Jesus, in Capernaum on the shore, points to a real "large millstone" when warning against causing a "little one" to stumble.
- **little ones** — the children of the prior teaching, possibly also a term for one's talmidim (spiritual children).
- **aionios** ("eternal") — a *qualitative* term (a *kind* of fire/reality that is of God's enduring "stuff"), not primarily *quantitative* (unending duration). Paired with 1 Corinthians 3's fire that tests the *quality* of what you build.
- **gehenna** — "the fire of hell"; a present, poetic/qualitative image (cf. the lake of fire in Revelation), not a systematic doctrine here.
- **the lost sheep** — in Matthew the shepherd rejoices because he *found* it (a God who loves finding lost things / the mamzer); subtly different from Luke's "sinner who repents."
- **77 / 70-times-7** — Jesus's answer on forgiveness is a remez to Lamech (Genesis 4:24), the seventh generation of Cain, who vowed vengeance "seventy-seven fold." You must *out-forgive* the full lineage of evil.
- **three times** — the Mishnah's limit on required forgiveness (3 = community/restoration); Peter upped it to seven (completion).
- **binding and loosing** — authority given in the context of resolving conflict.
- **the key to your own prison** — the Greek pronoun in Matthew 18:34 ("until *he* should repay") may mean the first servant is jailed until he *forgives* the second — he holds the key to his own release.
- **mamzer / mumzer** — the outsider; Matthew's agenda.
