### Hebrew Words Explained

- **euangelion** (Greek, “gospel/good news”) and its Hebrew counterpart **tov basar** (טוב בשר, “good news / good tidings”) — “how beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news” (Isaiah 52:7). *Tov* = good; *basar* = news/tidings/announcement.
- **the pronoun shift (Ephesians 1–2)** — the key observation: Paul deliberately moves from “**we/us**” (the Jews — “who were the first to put our hope in Christ,” 1:12) to “**you also**” (the Gentiles — “you also were included when you heard … the gospel of your salvation,” 1:13; “as for *you*, *you* were dead in your transgressions,” 2:1). The whole opening is about Jew-and-Gentile family membership.
- **mystery / revelation** — as in Galatians, Paul says he received his gospel “by revelation”; the *mystery* is “that through the gospel the Gentiles are heirs together with Israel, members together of one body” (3:6).
- **“one new humanity”** (Eph 2:15) — Marty's favorite phrase: Christ makes the two groups one, demolishing “the dividing wall of hostility,” creating a *new humanity* that displays reconciliation.
- **sarx** (Greek σάρξ, “flesh / animal appetites”) — “gratifying the cravings of our *sarx*” (2:3); the leveling point (as in Romans) that removes all boasting — salvation is a gift of grace (2:8-9).
- **neokoros** (Greek, “temple-warden / chief temple”) — Ephesus was the *neokoros* (capital) of **Artemis** worship (goddess of sexual fertility), home to the Temple of Artemis (a wonder of the ancient world); cf. the Acts 19 riot, “Great is Artemis of the Ephesians.”
- **the Artemisian festival** — the annual procession washing the Artemis statue in the harbor to “restore her virginity,” followed by public sexual debauchery. Paul's “husbands, love your wives … cleansing her by the washing with water” (5:25-27) deliberately subverts this imagery — so the “wives submit” instruction is addressed to the *Ephesian context*, not a timeless abstract gender role.
- **the armor of God** (Eph 6) — the struggle is “not against flesh and blood” but against spiritual powers; truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, and the word.
