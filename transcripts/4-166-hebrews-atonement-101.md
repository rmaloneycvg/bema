### Hebrew Words Explained

- **atonement ≠ justification ≠ salvation** — Christian systematic theology fused faith → atonement → justification → salvation into one mechanical chain. In the first-century Jewish world these are *separate* conversations: **Romans** is about *justification* (and never discusses atonement); **Hebrews** is the NT book that actually treats *atonement*.
- **the six theories of atonement** — each explains a facet of the mystery (Scot McKnight: “clubs in a golf bag”): (1) **ransom captive** (poetic; Christ as ransom to free captives — cf. Aslan in Narnia; born in the age of Roman persecution); (2) **Christus Victor** (Christ conquers sin/death; the plague/Crusades era; revived by Greg Boyd); (3) **penal substitutionary atonement / PSA** (Reformation-era legal/penal framing — Calvin the lawyer; Jesus dies as your legal substitute); (4) **moral exemplar** (Abelard vs. Anselm; Jesus's death as the ultimate self-sacrificial example); (5) **solidarity** (incarnation — God joins and shares our humanity, suffering, death; Tony Jones); (6) **scapegoat theory** (René Girard; Jesus the once-for-all scapegoat cleansing the conscience).
- **the central Hebrews phrase: cleansing the conscience** — repeated ~5x: the old sacrifices “could not clear the conscience of the worshiper” (9:9; 9:14; 10:2, 22). The sacrifice is not something *God needs* (Psalms/prophets: God doesn't desire sacrifice) — it's for *us*, to cleanse the *guilty conscience*. God could simply forgive; the problem is our conscience.
- **Jesus as temple, priest, and sacrifice** — in Hebrews Jesus becomes all three at once; a high priest “in the order of Melchizedek” (non-Levitical, heavenly tabernacle).
- **“obsolete” = simply “old” (Heb 8:13)** — Marty argues “obsolete” is a bad translation; the Greek just means *old*. The new covenant doesn't make the old *irrelevant* (replacement theology) but *older* — like an iPhone 5 vs. 11: the newer is built on the older; you wouldn't go back, but the old wasn't worthless.
- **Yom Kippur / Day of Atonement** — the one day a year the high priest enters the Most Holy Place with blood for the mercy seat and the scapegoat; the “endless, year-after-year” system Hebrews contrasts with Christ's “once for all.”
- **“made perfect” (Heb 10:14)** — the rare biblical “perfection” language is *Hellenistic* (Platonic); “made perfect those who are being made holy (set apart)” — perfect tense + ongoing sanctification.
- **two-part vs. three-part eschatology** — “the culmination of the ages” (9:26) = the three-part view's inbreaking Kingdom; “the Day approaching” (10:25) = the day of restoration/judgment.
- **the Trinity problem with PSA** — PSA risks splitting the Trinity into an angry Father, a nice-guy Jesus deflecting the Father's wrath, and a helper Spirit — “Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God,” which Marty calls poor orthodoxy.
