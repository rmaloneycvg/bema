### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Hamesh Megillot** (Hebrew, “the Five Scrolls”) — the Jewish category (within the *Ketuvim*) where Song of Songs sits; Judaism doesn't really use a “wisdom literature” label even though it has a rich wisdom tradition. The wisdom books function as a *toolbox* (songs, wise sayings, relationships/intimacy) for living well amid the chaotic narrative.
- **three Hebrew words for love** (via Rob Bell's NOOMA) — **rayah** (רעיה): “neighbor/friend” love; in romance, the electric *infatuation* (“no, you hang up first”) — feeling. **ahava** (אהבה): *committed* covenant love — “I'll be here regardless of how I feel.” **dowd** (דוד, also “uncle”): the *erotic* love of Song of Songs (“your *dowd* is sweeter than wine,” 1:2). A healthy romance needs *all three* working together (Rob Bell's bonfire: three small flames → one blaze).
- **ordering of the loves across cultures** — ancient arranged marriage: *ahava* → *dowd* → *rayah*. Modern Western: *rayah* → *ahava* → (meant to wait for) *dowd*. Hookup culture: *dowd* + *rayah* with no *ahava* — empty/destructive. Western weddings are almost all *rayah* (romance/aesthetics) when the wedding is really about *ahava* (commitment).
- **Song of Songs as allegory *and* erotic poetry** — Marty corrects his earlier over-insistence on “it's literally about sex”: it *is* erotic poetry (marital sexuality in its proper context), AND the rabbinic tradition rightly reads the *allegory* of intimacy/**communion** between God and his people (the Sinai “marriage,” the tabernacle “honeymoon suite”). We are *sexual beings* — denying it sets us up for dysfunction.
- **the “untouched oasis of misunderstanding”** — the church mostly discusses sexuality only as a moral code / purity culture (the “wilted rose”), rarely teaching healthy marital intimacy or self-sacrifice in that context — leaving youth to assemble scraps from media/peers, producing shame.
- **two lovers reading of Song of Songs** — a literary theory (which Marty finds compelling): the woman is torn between **Solomon/the king** (imperial right-handed power — wine, palace, perfume) and a poor **shepherd** she actually loves — i.e., Solomon riffing on the *limits of imperial power to win a heart*. (NET footnote even questions whether it's the same woman throughout.)
- **affirming singleness/celibacy** (1 Cor 7:8-9) — Paul affirms celibacy as a beautiful calling *and* that the loves belong together (“better to marry than to burn”). **1 Timothy 4:1-5** calls forbidding marriage / forbidding foods “teachings of demons” — “everything God created is good.” Song of Songs' wisdom is for the single as well as the married.
- **growing (not changing) our ethic** — as culture pushed marriage later (Industrial/educational/economic shifts), the church failed to be *honest* about the resulting struggle; our commitment to human dignity/flourishing must *grow* with the world, discipling the “why” of the struggle rather than only “don't.”
