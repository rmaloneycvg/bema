### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Judas Iscariot** — **ish** (man) + **Keriot** = "man from Keriot," likely a Zealot compound. As a Zealot, his "concern for the poor" and his skimming from the money bag fit a Robin Hood mentality (possibly funneling Herod's own money — via Chuza/Joanna, Luke 8:3 — back to oppressed Jews).
- **Judas misreads Jesus as a Zealot** and "betrays" him to force the revolution/confrontation with the chief priests; when it leads to the cross instead, he is wrecked with guilt.
- **the 30 pieces of silver thrown into the temple** — a remez to Zechariah 11 (the shepherd's wages "thrown to the potter at the house of the Lord"); even in his end, Judas acts out of the Text.
- **Peter Rollins, *The Fidelity of Betrayal*** — the (not-wholly-endorsed) theory of a prearranged agreement between Jesus and Judas; Marty suggests a middle ground (Jesus knows where Judas is at).
- **two betrayals** — Judas is named "the betrayer" by the Gospel writers *insistently*, because a Jewish reader would more naturally name **Peter**: a talmid publicly disassociating from his rabbi is the ultimate offense (Peter goes back to fishing — he assumes he's no longer a disciple).
- **"go tell his disciples and Peter"** (Mark 16:7) — the angel names Peter specifically, or the women would have left him out; the God of second chances reinstates him.
- The real difference: Judas lets his story *end* (no coming back); Peter trusts the God of new tomorrows. The only true betrayal of Jesus is believing there is no tomorrow — echoing Cain/Abel, Lamech, and the 70-times-7 forgiveness.
- **talmid / havurah** — disciple / the fellowship Peter led.
