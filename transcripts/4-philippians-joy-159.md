### Hebrew Words Explained

- **imitation / discipleship (“imitate me as I imitate Christ”)** — the rabbinic *talmid* model running through Philippians (ch. 2 and 4:9, “whatever you learned or received or heard from me or saw in me, put it into practice”). The apostle offers himself as a flawed, flesh-and-blood model who is following hard after Jesus.
- **Jewish vs. Greek discipleship** — both the rabbinic system (teacher → *talmid*, focused on *doing/behavior*) and the Greek philosophical system (Socrates/Plato/Aristotle, teacher → student, focused more on *ideas/data*) had master-follower structures. Different emphases, but similar enough that Paul's call to “imitate me” would land with a Greek audience — “apples to oranges, not apples to cats.”
- **“grasped / held on to” (Phil 2, the Christ hymn)** — Jesus “did not consider divinity something to be *grasped / held on to*” but became a servant; the Greek verb reinforces the self-emptying humility Paul urges them to imitate. (Discussed, not read verbatim here.)
- **“I can do all this through him who gives me strength” (Phil 4:13)** — flagged by Brent as “perhaps the most quoted out-of-context verse in all Scripture”: in context it is about *contentment* in plenty or want, not achievement.
- **“only together with us … made perfect”** — Marty connects Philippi to the coming Hebrews theme (Heb 11:40): the faithful who ran before us have their work “made perfect” only together with us; he longs to “stand on their shoulders well.”

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- *Kingdom, Grace, Judgment* (a one-volume trilogy) by **Robert Farrar Capon** (former Episcopal priest, d. 2013) — recommended for its wrestling with the humanity of Christ (chs. 5–6); Marty loves his writing even where he disagrees with the conclusions.
- **Timothy** — co-sender of Philippians (“Paul and Timothy, servants of Christ Jesus”); Marty assumes Paul wrote all 13 traditionally attributed letters, including the Pastoral Epistles, and wonders when Timothy was placed as pastor in Ephesus.
- **Epaphroditus** — the one who carried the Philippians' gift to Paul.
- The contrast with **Corinth** — a wealthier church that failed to support Paul (he had to work), vs. poor-but-generous **Philippi**, who “shared with me in the matter of giving and receiving” even from **Thessalonica/Macedonia**.
- Forward reference to **Hebrews 11:40** (“only together with us would their work be made perfect”).
- Greek philosophers **Socrates, Plato, Aristotle** — the Greek analogue to rabbinic discipleship (cf. the episode-0 slideshow contrasting Eastern/Western thought).
