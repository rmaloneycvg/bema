### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Docetism** (a Gnostic heresy) — the belief that Jesus never truly came in the flesh but was always of a purely spiritual/divine nature. 1 John's repeated insistence on the *physical* incarnation (and the earlier Colossians Gnosticism) is an argument against Docetism.
- **sons of light / sons of darkness** — Essene language (found in the Dead Sea Scrolls): walking in God's way = a *son of light*; opposing it = a *son of darkness*. 1 John 1:5-7 (“God is light … walk in the light”) reads almost like an Essene writing with the Jesus piece added — fitting if the author is John, closely tied to Essene/John-the-Baptist streams.
- **“walk as Jesus walked”** — the rabbinic *talmid* principle (Ray Vander Laan's mantra): a disciple imitates the rabbi in every way. “Whoever claims to live in him must live as Jesus did” (2:6). So the defining question is: *how* did Jesus walk? Answer: he **loved**.
- **love is what makes truth true** — Marty's central claim, read *one direction*: the Bible never says “if it's not true it's not loving,” but it *does* say “if you do not love, you do not walk in the light” / “whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love” / “whoever claims to love God yet hates a brother is a liar.” If it lacks love, it is **not truth** — no matter how factually “correct.” We keep qualifying love with truth; the Bible qualifies truth with love.
- **orthodoxy vs. orthopraxy** — as with Jesus (“a tree is known by its fruit”) and Paul (“all knowledge but no love = a clanging gong,” 1 Cor 13), John ties right *belief* to right *practice*: wrong orthopraxy (failure to love) means your orthodoxy is false.
- **Johannine authorship** — Marty holds the same John wrote the Gospel, 1–3 John, and Revelation (a minority scholarly view); others posit a “Johannine school.” *Yohanan* is John in Hebrew.
- **perfect love drives out fear** (4:18) — echoing the Session 1 root of sin (fear/insecurity).
