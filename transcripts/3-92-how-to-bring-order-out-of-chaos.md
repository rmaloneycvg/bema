### Hebrew Words Explained

- **tohu va-vohu** (תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ) — "formless and empty / wild and waste"; the chaos at the start of creation (Genesis 1:2).
- **merahefet** (מְרַחֶפֶת) — "hovering"; the Spirit hovered over the waters like a *dove*. The dove descending on Jesus at his baptism echoes this — a hint of a *new creation*.
- **ruach** (רוּחַ) / **pneuma** (πνεῦμα, Greek) — spirit / breath / wind; rare in that the one word carries all three meanings in both Hebrew and Greek.
- **the six-step "water" template** — chaos → water → spirit over water → God speaks → order (the expectation) → testing. It recurs in Creation, the Flood, the Red Sea, and the Jordan — each ending in *tragedy*. At Jesus's baptism-and-temptation the same template finally ends in *triumph*.
- **Shema** — Jesus's three temptations correspond (in Matthew's order) to the three wilderness tests of Israel — heart ("man does not live by bread alone," Deut 8:3), soul/"do not put the LORD to the test" (Deut 6:16), might/"worship the LORD only" (Deut 6:13) — Jesus taking on the role of Israel and showing how to pass them.
