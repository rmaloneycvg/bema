### Hebrew Words Explained

- **euangelion** (εὐαγγέλιον, Greek) — "gospel": the imperial proclamation of a new king and kingdom, spread by heralds, coins, advents, *parousias*, and coronations. Mark writes his *euangelion* to a Roman audience.
- **coronation** — the nine-step Roman enthronement ceremony (best attested in Nero's coronation, documented by Thomas E. Schmidt). Mark tells the crucifixion step-by-step as Jesus's coronation: the Praetorian guard gathered, robe/crown/scepter, the procession, the sacrifice-bearer, Head Hill, wine mixed with myrrh refused/offered, prisoners on his right and left, the acclamation, and the sign in the heavens.
- **Praetorium / Praetorian guard** (Latin) — the governor's palace and the elite guard "the whole company" called together (Mark 15:16).
- **Golgotha** — Mark glosses it "the place of the skull," but the hosts note the word points to "head" (as in Rome's *Capitoline* = "head hill"), reinforcing the coronation imagery.
- **Pax Romana** (Latin) — "the peace of Rome"; the empire's claim, which Mark subverts by presenting Jesus's apparent defeat as his true triumph.
