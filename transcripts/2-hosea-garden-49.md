### Hebrew Words Explained

- **yasha** (ישע) — "to rescue, save, deliver." The root behind the name *Hosea* and the name *Jesus/Yeshua*. It denotes rescue that brings God's people out into an open, wide place — a liberation-focused deliverance. The scroll's name itself begs the question: what is the rescue, and what are the people being rescued from?
- **yada** (ידע) — experiential, relational knowledge (Greek *gnosis*). Hosea 4 says the people are destroyed from lack of *yada*: they may know things *about* God but lack relationship *with* God.
- **Jezreel / Yizreel** (יזרעאל) — a place name that is also a pun. It sounds like *Yisrael* (Israel) but carries the Z sound and means "God sows" or "the seed of God." God is planting something new; the valley of bloodshed (2 Kings 9, Jehu's slaughter) becomes the seedbed of God's new garden.
- **Ephraim / pri** (אפרים / פרי) — God repeatedly addresses *Ephraim*. The word *pri* ("fruit") sits inside the name; Ephraim means "I will be fruitful." God keeps calling the people fruitful even as he confronts their unfaithfulness.
- **Achor** (עכור) — "roiling trouble," trouble boiling over. The "Valley of Achor" (often left untranslated; NET renders it "Valley of Trouble") is transformed by God into a "door of hope" — God takes the most hopeless, chaotic places and breathes life into them.
- **Ba'al / baalim** (בעל) — not merely a Canaanite deity's name but the word for "master, lord" (still used for "husband" in modern Hebrew). God promises to remove *Ba'al* — the slavery/master mentality — from the people's mouths, replacing it with the intimacy of "my husband" (*ishi*).

**The prophet's image: Garden.** Elle and Brent weigh "transformation" against "garden" and settle on *garden* (a "garden of transformation") — grounded, visual, and Edenic: breaking up fallow ground (10:12), the trees and vines of chapter 14, and the renewed-Eden covenant with the beasts of the field in chapter 2.

**Structure (Elle's outline):** Part A = the problem (our willingness to pursue violence and force instead of mercy, illustrated by Jehu's bloodshed at Jezreel). Part A requires A1 (acknowledge your guilt) and A2 (seek my face) — both are necessary. Part B (Hosea 2) = God's *yasha*/solution, a sevenfold transformation: alluring/relationship, free gifts (vineyards), turning the Valley of Trouble into a door of hope, restoring first love, replacing the *Ba'al* master-relationship with intimacy, ending the suffering of creation and abolishing the bow/sword/war, and renewing identity ("Not My People" → "You are my people").

**Spiritual discipline:** Alongside the Shema (taught in Episode 21), the Hebrew of Hosea 2:19-20 — *Verastikh li l'olam, verastikh li b'tzedek u've'mishpat u've'chesed u've'rachamim, verastikh li b'emuna, v'yadat et Adonai* ("I will betroth you to me forever, in righteousness and justice, in steadfast love and mercy, in faithfulness; and you shall know the LORD").
