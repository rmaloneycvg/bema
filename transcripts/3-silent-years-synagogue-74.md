## Book of Bible

Historical context (the Silent Years / Second Temple Judaism)

This is a historical-context episode on the synagogue and the Jewish education system; it does not work through a specific scripture passage. The one text quoted in passing is Psalm 119:105 — "Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path" — tied to reading Torah by the sunlight let in through the synagogue's basilica windows ("you read God's light by God's light").

### Hebrew Words Explained

- **synagogue** — the new center of Jewish worship that arose in Babylonian exile after the temple's destruction; began as house gatherings around the text, not as a replacement for the temple. Pre-AD 70 synagogues faced their doors *away* from Jerusalem so as not to rival the temple.
- **mikvah** (מִקְוֶה) — the ritual immersion pool outside the synagogue; a weekly cleansing (Pharisee form) before entering to worship — not a one-time conversion baptism.
- **basilica** — the pillared section holding up the roof, with clerestory windows letting sunlight in to read Torah.
- **bema** (בִּימָה) — the raised platform in the *center* of the synagogue; the community gathered *around* the text (contrast the Greek stage/audience model). The podcast takes its name from this word.
- **Moses seat** — the seat where the assigned reader sat to read the Torah portion; there were no clergy — ordinary members of the community read.
- **chief seats** — benches around the walls reserved for the gray-haired, seasoned members; a place of honor (cf. Jesus's teaching on the chief seats).
- **Torah closet** — the ornately decorated cabinet holding the scrolls — usually the single most elaborate feature, showing what the community valued most.
- **Bet Sefer** (בֵּית סֵפֶר, "house of the book," ages ~5-9) — memorizing the Torah.
- **Bet Midrash** (בֵּית מִדְרָשׁ, "house of study," ages ~10-13) — memorizing the rest of the Tanakh (only a small percentage advanced this far).
- **talmid / talmidim** (תַּלְמִיד) — disciple(s); the best of the best who applied to follow a rabbi, aiming to become just like him.
- **hazan** (חַזָּן) — the synagogue leader/attendant.
- **parashah** (פָּרָשָׁה) — the appointed Torah portion read each Shabbat (paired with a prophets reading).
