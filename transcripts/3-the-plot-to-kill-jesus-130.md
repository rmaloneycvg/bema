### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Who killed Jesus?** Not "the Jews" (the Jewish people loved Jesus; the plot had to be kept *away* from the public), and not the Pharisees as a party (Jesus spent three years confronting them and lived; some corrupt individuals helped, and some Pharisees had earlier *warned* him — Luke). It was the **chief priests** — the corrupt priestly mafia (the house of Ananus/Annas) — who conspired and arranged it. Rome *authorized and carried out* the crucifixion.
- **crucifixion** — Rome's torture-execution reserved for insurgents/"terrorists" (zealots); the two "robbers" beside Jesus were zealots mocking him for not acting like one.
- **the two Sanhedrins** (per Josephus): the **formal** Sanhedrin (~70–72, half Pharisee/half Sadducee, an official ruling body) and the **informal** one — the chief priests, run by the high priest, meeting at his house, making backroom decisions the formal body simply ratified. Jesus's night trial at Caiaphas's house was the *informal*, corrupt one, breaking many Jewish rules for a lawful trial.
- **the chief priesthood's origin** — sold to the highest bidder under Herod the Great; hence their corruption and their political leverage with Rome.
- **Pilate** — Rome's "bulldog" governor over the hardest region to rule (the Jews would not worship Caesar). The trial is really the intersection of Pilate's need to keep the peace at Passover and the priests' threat: "If you let this man go, you are no friend of Caesar." Jesus's whole ministry is anti-Empire ("my kingdom is not of this world"), but Rome sees only a harmless peasant — a mustard seed.
- **Coptic tradition** — a legend that Pilate (prompted by his wife's dream) later repented, fled to Egypt, and helped found the Coptic Church. Tradition, not history.
