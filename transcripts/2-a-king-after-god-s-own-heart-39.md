### Hebrew Words Explained

- **ish habeinayim** (Hebrew, “man of the between”) — the *champion* of champion-warfare: rather than armies slaughtering thousands, each side's best fighter fights in the valley, winner-take-all. Hence **Goliath** (nine feet / six cubits) in the **Valley of Elah** (one of the four *shephelah* valleys snaking from Philistia toward Jerusalem).
- **the morning-and-evening defiance** — Goliath came out at the exact times of the daily temple sacrifice (the shofar at ~9 am / 3 pm), shouting insults at the God of Israel precisely during Israel's prayer/worship.
- **40 = a time of testing** — Goliath stands for 40 days; God tests Saul (“what's in your heart?”) and Saul does nothing. Then “David, you're up.”
- **shema** (Hebrew, “hear/heed”) — “David *heard* it” is not casual overhearing but a perceiving, heeding *shema*.
- **nasah** (Hebrew, “test/prove”) — David “had not *tested*” Saul's armor (17:39); its first biblical mention is Genesis 22 (“God *tested* Abraham”) — possibly tying David's refusal of armor to “the Lord will provide.”
- **666 + scales = the serpent** — Goliath is six cubits tall, his spearhead six hundred shekels, and (elsewhere) he has a six-fingered brother — 6-6-6 — wearing *scale* armor. He is painted as the Genesis serpent; David deals the **death blow to the head** (Gen 3:15, “he will crush your head”), while the serpent strikes the heel.
- **Benjamin's 700 left-handed slingers** (Judges 20:15-17) — an elite “special forces” sniper unit who could “sling a stone at a hair and not miss.” **Saul is from Benjamin** — so the one who *should* march down with a sling is Saul; instead it's the shepherd boy David.
- **600 vs. 700** — after Samuel's rebuke (1 Sam 13), Saul is left with ~**600** men — *incomplete* (six = beastly, like day six without the seventh/rest). Saul relies on *his own strength* (the **ar'ar bush** / “cursed is the one who trusts in his own strength,” Jeremiah) — lush-looking but with poisonous, empty fruit.
- **kiddush Hashem** (Hebrew, “hallow the Name”) — David's concern is not self-preservation or logic but *God's reputation/representation* — “the whole world will know there is a God in Israel.” (Not that God needs protecting, but that we steward how God is seen.)
- **shepherd vs. donkey-herder** — David the *shepherd* from **Judah** vs. Saul the donkey-herder from Benjamin (prior episode).
- **“throw your stone”** — God can do nothing with a stone that stays in your pocket; be *willing* (David) rather than well-equipped-but-idle (Saul).
- **every vocation is a holy, sacred calling** — “Israel without weapons” (1 Sam 13): there was no blacksmith in Israel because the *Philistines* monopolized metal technology to *oppress* (sharper swords). Had Israel held the leading craft, they could have *blessed* all nations at the crossroads — so be the best at your vocation (blacksmith, CPA, artist, stay-at-home parent) to bring *shalom* to chaos.
