### Hebrew Words Explained

- **orthodoxy vs. orthopraxy** — Paul structures both Colossians and Ephesians the same way: the first half is *orthodoxy* (“right belief/word”), the second half *orthopraxy* (“right practice/behavior” — household codes for husbands/wives, parents/children, masters/servants). Compare Ephesians 4–6 with Colossians 3–4.
- **Gnosticism / Platonic dualism** — built on Plato's *theory of the forms* (the “cave” allegory): the material world is mere flickering shadow; true reality is the immaterial realm of forms. Gnosticism adds that matter is *evil* (creation itself as “the fall”) and that a few *enlightened* people secretly “get it.” Marty warns this Platonic strain wrongly shapes the common Christian idea of “heaven” as a perfect place elsewhere — “smells more of Plato than the Bible.”
- **two forms of Gnostic dualism** — since only the *spiritual* matters: (1) libertine — the body is irrelevant, so indulge it however you like; or (2) ascetic — deny the body everything to attain enlightenment. Marty argues Colossae faced the *ascetic*, “hoity-toity” self-denial version (“Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch,” 2:21).
- **Paul's tongue-in-cheek subversion** — he co-opts Gnostic vocabulary: he prays they be filled with *knowledge and wisdom* (what Gnostics crave) — but “all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden in Christ” and available to *everyone* (no elitism, 2:3). “Absent in body, present in spirit” and “fine-sounding arguments / hollow philosophy” are jabs at Greek thought.
- **“fullness of the Deity in bodily form”** (Col 1:19; 2:9) — Paul's maximally *inclusive, anti-dualist* language: all things visible and invisible, heaven and earth, created and reconciled *in Christ* — denying any split between spiritual and physical.
- **sarx** (“flesh”) — Christ already dealt with and “triumphed over” the flesh at the cross (2:11-15), so one need not pursue Gnostic self-restraint to prove spirituality.
- **festivals / new moon / Sabbath (Col 2:16-17)** — Marty argues this is *not* “don't bother with Sabbath/festivals” but the opposite: *do* engage them even though the Gnostic world of Colossae judges such “fleshly” creation-affirming practice. “A shadow of things to come … the reality is in Christ” is a tongue-in-cheek play on Plato's shadows.
