### Hebrew Words Explained

- **kensos** (Greek) — "tribute" (not ordinary taxes); the particular tribute coin every resident bought as a receipt proving they'd paid tribute (worship) to Caesar. The debate is about *idolatry*, not money.
- **tribute temples** — Herod built three (e.g., Omrit, near Caesarea Philippi) where the coin bought incense to offer to Caesar as a god.
- **Hillel vs. Shammai** on the coin: Shammai (obedience) = don't buy it (idolatry); Hillel (love), citing Jeremiah (God gave the nations even to Nebuchadnezzar), = buy it, you're giving back what God already granted the ruler. Jesus takes Hillel's view and goes further: **render** (give back) to Caesar his coin, but never your *worship* ("whose image/inscription is on *you*?").
- **the Sadducees and resurrection** — they rejected the Pharisaic *bodily* resurrection in the age to come, not the afterlife entirely.
- **olam haba** — the age to come.
- **kavod** — "weight"; the "greatest commandment" question asks which laws carry the most weight (one's interpretive yoke). Shammai: love God + keep Sabbath (obedience); Hillel: love God + love neighbor (love). Jesus sides with Hillel (Deuteronomy 6:5 + Leviticus 19:18).
- **who is my neighbor** — in Leviticus the Hebrew means fellow Jew. Shammai: only fellow Jews. Hillel: even Romans (but not Samaritans). Jesus's Good Samaritan pushes *past* Hillel — and makes the hated Samaritan the one *obeying* love.
- **priest / Levite / Pharisee** — the standard parable template (priest corrupt, Levite stuck, Pharisee gets it right); Jesus swaps the expected Pharisee for a **Samaritan**.
- **orthodoxy vs. orthopraxy** — only the one who *does* something is a neighbor.
- The Good Samaritan's remez: **2 Chronicles 28** — Samaritans clothing, feeding, and donkey-carrying Judean captives back to Jericho (even the donkeys and oil are there).
- **Mashiach / son of David** — Jesus's counter-riddle from Psalm 110:1 ("The LORD said to my Lord"): the Messiah is more than merely David's descendant.
