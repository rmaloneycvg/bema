### Hebrew Words Explained

- **“you (plural) are God's temple”** (1 Cor 3:16-17) — the “temple” is *corporate/singular*, the pronoun *plural*: “don't you know that y'all together are **one** temple?” It's about the community's unity (disunity “destroys the temple”), not a proof-text against personal smoking/drinking.
- **kal va'chomer** (“light and heavy,” lesser-to-greater) — Brent flags it in 1 Cor 6:3: “we will judge angels — *how much more* the things of this life!”
- **Cephas** (Aramaic *kefa*, “rock”) = Peter; one of the party-slogans (“I follow Paul / Apollos / Cephas / Christ”).
- **“I, not the Lord” / inspiration** — in 1 Cor 7 Paul explicitly marks some counsel as his own concession (“I say this, I not the Lord,” v.12) vs. the Lord's command (v.10). Marty uses this to show the text is an *inspired conversation*: Paul offers an opinion the Corinthians must then *bind and loose* for their context, not a flat moral code.
- **binding and loosing** — the rabbinic authority to apply/permit; Paul hands the Corinthians his marriage counsel to wrestle with and apply.
- **“weak” vs. “strong” conscience** (1 Cor 8) — same narrower/wider framing as Romans 14; the “strong” must not let their freedom become a *stumbling block* to the “weak.”
- **permissible vs. beneficial** — “'I have the right to do anything' — but not everything is beneficial / constructive” (6:12; 10:23). The test is not “is it allowed?” but “is it good for others?”
- **Eucharist / Lord's Supper** — the Corinthians turned it into a guild-feast of division (some drunk, some hungry); Paul recalls “the night he was betrayed” to restore it as the sign of the one body.
- **chiasm of 1 Cor 12–14** — the “love chapter” (ch. 13) sits deliberately at the *center* of the spiritual-gifts/unity discussion: love is the cure for division.
