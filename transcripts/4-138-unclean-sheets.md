### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Saul's "conversion" (Acts 9)** — the letters are to *synagogues* (the Way is still a Jewish movement). Jesus says Saul is persecuting *him*, not just his followers. Trained under **Gamaliel the Elder** (grandson of Hillel — so already primed to receive Hillel-like/Jesus teaching). Marty's framing question: was this a **conversion** (switching religions) or a **repentance** (same God, same story, new understanding)? Romans 11: Gentiles are *grafted into* the Jewish tree — God didn't plant a new one.
- **the Cornelius story (Acts 10)** — the first Gentile not assumed to become a Jewish convert.
- **Peter's vision is NOT about food.** God does *not* declare unclean animals clean — that's why Peter keeps refusing three times ("do not call unclean what *I* have called clean" — and Peter agrees the lizard is unclean). The parenthetical "declaring all foods clean" reads like a later insertion. Peter himself interprets the vision: "God has shown me I should not call any *person* impure or unclean." It's about *people*.
- **halakhah** — the oral law/tradition on how to walk out Torah's 613 commands. The oral tradition (not written Torah) said a Jew shouldn't eat with a Gentile. Jesus upholds written Torah to the letter but critiques oral tradition when it misinterprets it.
- **nomos** — "law"; without the definite article (as in Acts 10:28), it's "our (little-l) law/custom," i.e., oral tradition, not Torah.
- **goyim** — Gentiles/nations. The Spirit falls on Gentiles (a second, Gentile Pentecost); "even to the Gentiles God has granted repentance."
- **ioudaioi** — better rendered **Judeans** (a regional term) than "the Jews"; the Judean leadership (Sadducees/Herodians), not Jews ethnically, pushed Jesus's death. Translating it "the Jews" fuels anti-Semitism.
- **diaspora** — the scattering; the church's center shifts from Jerusalem to **Antioch**, raising the (real) tension between the Jewish Jerusalem church (James) and the expanding church of Asia Minor (Paul/John).
- **shephelah** — the "in-between" borderland; Brent's reframing: the Kingdom now *invites the Gentiles in as family*, not merely blesses them from a distance.
