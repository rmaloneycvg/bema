### Hebrew Words Explained

- **“sin cycle” vs. “redemption cycle”** — the famous six-part Judges cycle (things go well → fall into sin → come under judgment → cry out → God raises a deliverer → rescue/rest). Marty's thesis: it's traditionally (and in Google image searches) called the *sin cycle*, framing it around *our badness* — but sin occupies only two-sixths of the cycle; redemption occupies four-sixths. It is better named **God's redemption cycle**, a testament to God's *endless patience* (“more bottomless than Red Robin's fries”).
- **shephelah** — the Session-2 “in-between”: trying and failing while engaging the crossroads (too much Judah-Mountain comfort or too much coastal-plain compromise) is exactly what God has *endless patience* for. If you're not making mistakes, you probably aren't engaging the *shephelah* enough.
- **Joshua vs. Judges** — some textual critics hold both describe the *same* period: Joshua as an embellished *conquest* framing, Judges as the more *historical* “moved in and assimilated” account. Marty neither endorses nor requires this but finds both framings could each be “exactly right, authoritative, inspired” (like telling your own story as triumph vs. the long honest version).
- **the anti-story** — the key distinction: *trying and failing* (God has endless patience) vs. *becoming the opposite* of the mission. The book ends with the Levite and his concubine — a retelling of **Sodom and Gomorrah** (abusing/violating visitors), showing Israel has *become* Sodom. That — becoming the anti-story, harming the vulnerable — is what *rouses God to action*, not lost patience but total commitment to his redemptive arc.
- **a thousand to three** — God's self-description to Moses (“Adonai, Adonai, gracious and compassionate, slow to anger, abounding in love and truth … a thousand generations … but three”, Exod 34). It's not a thousand to *zero*; God's patience doesn't “run out,” it's so committed to redemption that he must protect the abused.
- **chiasms in Judges** — the pattern (short, short, longer, long stories; 40 / 80 / 40 / 40 / 6-year rests) and the “master chiasm” of the whole book made of smaller chiasms. Note the missing expected 40-year rest after triumphant **Samson** — hinting Samson wasn't the deliverer God wanted; right after Samson, the Danites abandon their post and settle in Laish.
