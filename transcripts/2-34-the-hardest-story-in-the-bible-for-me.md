### Hebrew Words Explained

- **cherem / harem** (Hebrew חרם) — often translated “destroy” in the conquest commands, but it is not the ordinary word for “destruction/violence.” Its root sense is *to set apart, consecrate, devote* (to God). Elle: when you hit “destroy” in English, check a concordance — *cherem* carries the idea of consecration/devotion, which reframes what God actually commanded.
- **“exterminate” / to make desolate** — the Hebrew pictorial sense is *to make desolate*, not necessarily “exterminate.” In Exodus 23 God says he will *drive out* the peoples (which also leaves cities desolate); “exterminate” is likely an over-reading filtered through translators' cultural lens.
- **conquest-narrative genre** — ancient Near Eastern kings recorded conquests on **stelae** (plaster/carved stones telling an *embellished, hyperbolic* story — e.g., the **Sennacherib Stele**, “Hezekiah shut up like a bird in a cage”). Reading Joshua against this genre is a *gift*: Joshua is introduced not as a muscular warrior but as the one who lingered in the tent for love of God's presence — revealing what God prizes in his heroes.
- **matsevot** (Hebrew, “standing stones” / stones of witness) — unlike a *stele*, these bear **no writing**; they prompt “what happened here?” but require a *witness* to tell the story (e.g., the 12 Jordan stones, Josh 4; Jacob's anointed stone at Bethel, Gen 28). Application: what “standing stones” do we keep to tell our children the stories of God's faithfulness?
- **chazak ve'ematz (meod)** (Hebrew, “be strong and courageous (very)”) — the refrain of Joshua 1, which the rabbis note is spoken among all three parties: God → Joshua, Joshua → the people, the people → Joshua. Such *unity* among God's people can empower miracles — not violent conquest, but redeeming the world and restoring **shalom**. Elle: *ematz* connotes “quick feet” — feet quick to carry out God's word — more than mere emotional bravery.
- **a thousand to three** — the Session 1 principle that the handful of brutal “outlier” stories (Sodom, the flood, the Egyptian firstborn, the conquest) do not overturn the overwhelming narrative of a God of grace and endless pursuit.
- **Gibeonites & the suzerain-vassal covenant** — Israel honors its treaty even after the Gibeonites' deception (where an ancient suzerain would normally punish), because they are a covenant people with God — one of the “softer” notes amid conquest.
