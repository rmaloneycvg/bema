### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the pronoun shift** — the Beatitudes are "blessed are *those*" (pronouncements over the crowds), but at Matthew 5:11 Jesus turns to his disciples: "blessed are *you* when people persecute you." Living out this Kingdom brings opposition.
- **shephelah / tel / city on a hill** — God places his people at the crossroads (shephelah); a *tel* is a mound of stacked ruined cities, so a city sits "on a hill," visible like a lamp. Jesus's "town built on a hill cannot be hidden" draws on this.
- **casemate housing** — the apartments built into the city walls (middle class); the wealthy lived inside, the poor in unwalled "daughter/sister" villages or at the dump.
- **Gehenna / gei hinnom** (גֵּי הִנֹּם) — the Valley of (Ben) Hinnom, Jerusalem's smoldering trash dump (Jeremiah); the image behind "hell," "where the worm never dies" and "weeping and gnashing of teeth" — a place of poverty and misery outside the city, not a red-pitchfork underworld. "Hell" appears ~12 times in the Bible, 11 of them on Jesus's lips.
- **mishpat** (מִשְׁפָּט) — justice; what people should find when they look to God's city on a hill (with *tzedakah*, righteousness/benevolence).
