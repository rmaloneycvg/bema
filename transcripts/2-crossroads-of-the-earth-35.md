### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Via Maris** (Latin, “way of the sea”) — the single main highway of the ancient world, funneling all traffic between Egypt and Assyria/Persia/Babylon/the Hittites straight through the land of Israel. God placed his people at the “crossroads of the earth” (the “turnstile of the ancient world”) precisely so they could *bless all nations* — not hide in a holy huddle.
- **a kingdom of priests** — just as the tabernacle sat in the *middle* of the camp (so all could access priesthood/atonement), God sets his priestly people in the *middle* of the nations.
- **land of milk and honey** — NOT an image of Western abundance. *Milk* comes from flocks grazing the desert hills; *honey* = sweets (even “honey from the rock,” Deut). The promised land is less lush than Goshen — a land where you must *depend on God*. The blessing is the *Abrahamic* (external) blessing: blessed to bless others.
- **shephelah** (Hebrew, from a root “to bow/kneel”) — the lowland that “bows down” from the comfortable, secure **Judah Mountains** (where God's people want to settle; Jerusalem is here) toward the **coastal plain** (where the pagans live — **Philistines** south, **Phoenicians** north). God calls his people *into the shephelah*, the in-between tension where the two worlds interact — the crossroads lesson in a tighter package. (The land “BLT”: top bun = Galilee, bottom bun = the southern deserts; the Rift Valley/Jordan on the east, coastal plain on the west.)
- **tel** (Hebrew, a mound of stacked ruined cities) — cities were rebuilt on the same spot (17–21 layers at **Tel Gezer**) because of a **makor** (Hebrew, “source” — of water); you build where there is water, defensibility, commerce, and farmland.
- **sha'ar / sha'arim** (Hebrew, “gate(s)”) — the chambered city gate: a *defensive* bottleneck (one room per side in Abraham's day → four-chambered in David's → six-chambered Solomonic → eight-chambered by Hezekiah, after which armies switched to siege ramps). The other ~79 of every 80 years it was the *municipal center*: courthouse, elders/judges (Lot, Eli “at the gate”), and the *welfare system*.
- **casemate housing** — middle-class apartments built *into* the city wall; in war they're filled with stones so the wall thickens. Explains **Rahab** and **Paul** being lowered out a window (outer wall). The rich live nearest the center; the poor outside; the destitute at the burning city dump (“weeping and gnashing of teeth”).
- **“city on a hill / light of the world”** (Matthew 5:14-16) — now pictured concretely: the city = refuge, provision, and welfare for the needy outsider; God calls his people to *be* that city gate / light.
- **the tribe of Dan** — allotted the coastal plain to engage the **Philistines** (with big-brother **Judah** next door — Judah's 3,000 defend Samson). Dan abandons its post and migrates far north to **Laish/Dan** (lush springs) — but there it's the *first stop* for every invading empire and gets obliterated; Dan is notably *missing* from the tribes in Revelation. Rabbinic lesson: *do not forsake the mission God gives you to steward.*
