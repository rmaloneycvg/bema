### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Onesimus** (Greek, “useful”) — the runaway slave's name literally means *useful*; Marty speculates he may have been a *sublatus* foundling raised by slave-traders and named for the auction block. Paul puns on it: “formerly he was *useless* to you, but now he has become *useful*.”
- **law works vs. faith works** — the Jewish debate over *why* one obeys. *Law works*: obey to avoid consequences (don't speed so you won't get a ticket). *Faith works*: obey because it's genuinely the right thing and life goes better that way. Paul argues for *faith works* throughout the NT — and jokes with it here: “I could be bold and *order* you … yet I prefer to appeal to you on the basis of love” (law works vs. faith works in one sentence).
- **sarcasm / Greek over-the-top humor** — scholars widely read Philemon as drenched in tongue-in-cheek rhetoric: Paul “an old man … a prisoner,” “my very heart,” “you owe me your very self,” and — crucially — he CCs the *whole church* so Philemon must read it publicly and answer in front of everyone. Not coercion, but confident love.

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- **Roman citizenship and class** — three ways to become a citizen: *freeborn* (born to citizen parents), *purchase* (as the tribune in Acts bought his), or **manumission** (a slave freed by a master so honored that the freedman's *children* gained citizenship). Church tradition from **Jerome** (4th–5th c.) says **Paul's parents were manumitted slaves**, explaining how a Jew of Tarsus was “born a Roman citizen” and coloring Paul's servant/slave language (e.g., Philippians 2).
- Roman social classes above the citizen line: **Senator, Equestrian (“Epicurean” as said), Decurion, Freeborn**; below it: **Freedman** and **slave**.
- A runaway slave was legally a dead slave; Onesimus's three options — flee, appeal to the courts, or seek a citizen *patron* (patronage/“I scratch your back”). Onesimus runs to Rome to lean on **Paul's citizenship**.
- **sublatus** — the horrific Roman practice of “exposing” unwanted/defective infants in fields outside the city gates; **Soranus**, a Greek physician, wrote *How to Recognize a Child Worth Raising* instructing slave-traders which exposed babies to collect and raise (in cages) for the slave market.
- **Colossae** — Philemon lived there; the letter is addressed to the whole house church (Apphia, Archippus); fun to read atop the unexcavated Tel Colossae on the BEMA **Turkey trip**.
- Names in the greeting: **Timothy** (co-sender), **Epaphras, Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, Luke**.
