### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Christology through three lenses** — **incarnation** (birth/God becoming flesh), **crucifixion** (death), **resurrection** (new life): all equal, but the one you prioritize shapes your theology. Penal substitution centers the crucifixion; solidarity Christology (Tony Jones) centers the incarnation; Marty favors the preeminence of the **resurrection**.
- **the resurrection answers Ezekiel's "can these dry bones live?"** — death isn't as real as we feared; the order of life gets the last word. 1 Corinthians 15: without resurrection, faith is useless.
- **the Great Commission ≠ the Ascension** — they are different events in different places. The Ascension is at the Mount of Olives (Jerusalem); the commission is given on a *mountain in Galilee*. Marty suggests Mount Hermon ("holy mountain," the Transfiguration site), forcing the disciples to walk past Jesus's whole ministry — and to Caesarea Philippi, where Jesus said he'd "build my church."
- **"some doubted"** — Jesus commissions the Eleven *without* separating believers from doubters; doubt is acceptable for commissioned disciple-makers.
- **teach / baptize / make disciples**: teaching requires knowing the Text; baptism is an act of t'shuvah (repentance/return) marking entry into the new Kingdom.
- **mikveh** — the Jewish ritual immersion; **tavilat t'shuvah** was John's/Essene baptism of repentance. The commission's baptism "of all nations" is something new, tied to the Holy Spirit and Jesus's Kingdom (cf. Paul and Apollos).
- **make disciples** — Marty reads this as rabbinic "come, follow me" discipleship (become just like the rabbi): a calling for *someone*, not necessarily everyone (only ~1% were disciples in Jesus's day).
- **all authority** — unusually, the resurrected rabbi keeps *all* authority for himself rather than passing it to disciples.
- **olam** — age; "to the very end of the age (olam)."
- Underneath it all: **A Tale of Two Kingdoms** — Empire vs. Shalom; trust the story.
