### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the whole Olivet discourse is about the destruction of Jerusalem**, not the "end times." The disciples ask when the stones will fall; Jesus answers that question. "This generation will not pass away..." Reading it as end-times prophecy ignores his closing point that no one can know the future.
- **this age / the age to come** — Jewish eschatology the disciples keep trying to time.
- **Nevi'im** (Prophets) vs. **Ketuvim** (Writings) — Jesus's apocalyptic imagery (Daniel, Isaiah) is quoted from the Prophets; messages of *perseverance*.
- **"one taken, one left"** — contra "Left Behind": like Noah's flood and the wheat-and-weeds, it's the *wicked* who are taken (to slaughter/exile in the coming destruction); you *want* to be left.
- **Number symbolism in the talents**: 5 (books of Moses) / 2 (tablets of Moses) / 1 (**echad** — God is one): deep knowledge of the Text, basic knowledge, or only an awareness of God. God expects you to use whatever you have.
- **kal va'chomer** ("lesser and greater" / "how much more") — the rabbinic device behind the talents/minas parable; the harsh "king" is **Herod Antipas** (per Luke 19's fuller context), not God.
- **mishpat** — justice; the criterion separating sheep from goats.
- Three parables reinforce Matthew 24: bridesmaids (be ready), talents (give an account), sheep and goats (care for the oppressed — do what the priests won't).
