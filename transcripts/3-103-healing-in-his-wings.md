### Hebrew Words Explained

- **tallit** (pl. **tallitiot**) — the (outer) garment / prayer shawl Jews wore, per Numbers 15.
- **tzitzit** (pl. **tzitziot**) — the tassels commanded on the corners of the garment, a tactile reminder to obey God's commands.
- **kanaf** (pl. **kanafot**) — "corner," but also "wing"; the corner of the garment where tassels hang. Malachi 4:2 promises the "sun of righteousness" with "healing in its wings" (kanafot) — a messianic hope the bleeding woman reaches for by grabbing Jesus's tzitzit.
- **mamzer** — illegitimate child / outsider; here the bleeding woman, unfit for the assembly.
- **chutzpah** — bold audacity; the woman pushing through the crowd though she should not.
- **remez** — a "hint"; a deliberate quotation pointing back to a fuller text (e.g., "Son of David," the shepherd/harvest imagery).
- **the Messianic secret** — Jesus's instruction (in the religious triangle) not to spread word of a healing; Marty argues it's driven by *location* and the religious fallout, not the miracle itself.

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- Numbers 15 (the command for tassels) — cf. David cutting Saul's tzitzit (1 Samuel 24)
- Malachi 4:2 ("healing in its wings") — ESV preserves "wings" (kanaf)
- 2 Samuel 5:8 (the "blind and lame" who will not enter the palace) — Jesus subverting it by healing the blind
- Ezekiel 34 (woe to the shepherds of Israel who fail the flock)
- Jeremiah 24–25 (the shepherds/leaders of the flock)
- The Book of Ruth (workers and harvest; gleaning for the one who has nothing)
