### Hebrew Words Explained

- **euangelion** (Greek, “good news / gospel”) — in the biblical world this is the *pronouncement of a new king and a new kingdom*, not merely a package of theological truths about personal salvation. Paul's “gospel” is the announcement that in Christ everyone — Jew and Gentile alike — is welcomed at God's table and belongs to God's family (radical inclusion).
- **theosebes / theosebeis** (Greek, “God-fearer(s)”) — God-fearing Gentiles who worshiped the God of Israel without full conversion. The crisis of Galatians is what to do with them: convert or be excluded.
- **Hillel vs. Shammai** — the two rabbinic schools. The region of Galatia was dominated by the strict **Shammai** view: a Gentile who wants in must convert to Judaism. **Hillel** was a step softer (welcome at the table but not full children of Abraham). Paul argues further still — full-fledged sibling membership and adoption as *children of Abraham* by faith.
- **b'nei Avraham** (“children of Abraham”) — full covenant membership; Paul insists Gentiles attain this *through faith in Christ*, not through conversion/circumcision.
- **yoke** — a rabbi's particular interpretation and way of applying Torah. Leaving Gamaliel for Jesus means Paul must learn an entirely new *yoke* under a new rabbi.
- **bechor** (Hebrew, “firstborn”) — raised in the aside about which James is which; one would expect the *bechor* / a prominent brother to be elevated.
- **Cephas** (Aramaic *kefa*, “rock”) — Peter's Aramaic name, used by Paul in Galatians 1:18.
- **talmidim** — Peter, James, and John are the actual *talmidim* (disciples who followed Jesus in the flesh), whose endorsement validates Paul's gospel.

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- *The Holy Epistle to the Galatians* by **Thomas Lancaster** (published by **First Fruits of Zion**) — strongly recommended for its first ~80% (five-star, New-Perspective scholarship by a Messianic Jewish author); Marty disagrees with the final chapters' turn back toward Torah-observance.
- The **“New Perspective on Paul”** scholars whose work underlies Lancaster's bibliography: **Mark D. Nanos**, **James D. G. Dunn**, **N. T. Wright**, and **E. P. Sanders**.
- The **“Jewish exception”** — the historical arrangement (traced to **Herod the Great**, who offered a daily sacrifice *for* Caesar rather than *to* him) exempting Jews from imperial emperor-worship; being outside the Jewish community left a God-fearing Gentile exposed to this capital demand.
- Dating note: Marty holds **Galatians is the earliest letter in the New Testament**, written *before* the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15), since Paul never cites that council's ruling.
- The identity of **James, the Lord's brother** — argued to be James son of Alphaeus (a kinsman/cousin), one of the Twelve, not James son of Zebedee (John's brother).
