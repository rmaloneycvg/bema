### Hebrew Words Explained

- **parousia** (Greek παρουσία) — usually translated “second coming,” but loaded with Roman imperial context: the *return visit of an emperor*. After an earthquake the emperor would come, survey the damage, and leave a large sum (e.g., 30 million sesterces) to rebuild — then *return* (a second coming) to see what the city did with his deposit. Thessalonica had experienced at least **two** such parousias in the first century. Paul casts Jesus's return as a parousia: God left us a deposit (the Spirit) and will come to see what we did — so, work! (against the Thessalonians' disengagement).
- **eis apantēsin** (Greek εἰς ἀπάντησιν, “to meet”) — the technical term for the city going *out* to meet the arriving emperor, to take him (figuratively) by the hand and show him the rebuilt city. “Caught up … to meet the Lord in the air” (1 Thess 4:17) uses this word — and crucially, Jesus is coming *down*: we go out to greet and escort him back, not evacuate. This undercuts rapture-as-escapism readings.
- **the parousia sequence mirrored in 1 Thess 4** — (1) the *trumpet* (the city lookout's signal of the emperor's approach); (2) the *dead rise first* (the emperor pays respects at the **necropolis** / city-of-the-dead just inside the gate before entering); (3) the living go *eis apantēsin* to meet and escort him.
- **tikkun olam** (Hebrew, “repairing the world”) — the Kingdom work we're to be doing *now*, physically participating so that at the parousia we have something to show — “your will be done on earth as it is in heaven.”
- **shalom** — the ordered, whole, peace-filled state the Kingdom work moves creation toward.
