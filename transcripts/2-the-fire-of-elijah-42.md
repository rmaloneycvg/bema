### Hebrew Words Explained

- **binding God to his text** — Elijah declares the drought on his *own* word (1 Kgs 17:1); the narrator only *later* says “the word of the Lord came” (17:8), so Jewish tradition holds God did NOT tell him to say it. Elijah has the *chutzpah* to invoke God's own promise — Deuteronomy 11:16-17 (“if you worship other gods … he will shut up the heavens so it will not rain”) — and “bind God to his word.” Since Ahab/Jezebel are leading Baal worship, Deuteronomy applies.
- **Baal and Asherah** — the Phoenician fertility myth (brought into Israel via Solomon's → Omri's → Ahab's treaty with Phoenicia, sealed by marrying the Asherah high priestess **Jezebel** to **Ahab**): *Baal* = agricultural fertility (bull his animal, lightning his weapon, thunder his voice, rain his “seed”); *Asherah* = sexual fertility. The seasons are explained by Baal chasing Asherah into the underworld. Elijah's drought and the Carmel “ask for rain/fire” contest directly attack this myth — giving Baal “home-court advantage” (his mountain, altar, bull, specialty).
- **the sins of Jeroboam** — after Solomon's Empire, Rehoboam's cruelty (“my pinky is thicker than my father's waist … scorpions”) splits the kingdom; Jeroboam sets up golden calves at **Bethel** and **Dan** for political expediency so the north needn't worship in Jerusalem/Judah. Reed notes the complexity: the north's protest against injustice was legitimate even though pursued outside God's parameters (cf. Jesus's “the Gentiles lord it over them … not so with you”).
- **“waver between two opinions”** (1 Kgs 18:21) — the Hebrew connotes a *sexual gyration* / shrine-prostitution dance: “how long will you gyrate between Baal and Adonai?” The people say *nothing* (contrast Joshua's “we will serve the Lord!”).
- **the water / twelve stones** — Elijah's 12 stones (reuniting the tribes) and four jars poured three times (= 12) evoke the **Sukkot** water ceremony *asking God for rain* — not just making the miracle flashier.
- **right-handed vs. left-handed power** (Reed) — *right-handed* = force/coercion (a hammer): gets compliance, not change. *Left-handed* = invitation (fresh bread drawing people to the table): changes the heart. Elijah's drought, contest, taunting, and slaughter are all right-handed — and they “work” on *everyone except Ahab*, the one person it was for. The flashy “fire from heaven” isn't what God is ultimately after.
- **the still small voice** (1 Kgs 19) — God is NOT in the wind, earthquake, or **fire** (the very thing the story is famous for), but in a *gentle whisper*. Elijah repeats his “I alone am left” verbatim — unchanged. The rabbis link the whisper to the **sod** (secret), the lovers' whisper of Song of Solomon: *love* changes the human heart, and love is trained in **discipleship** (Elijah → Elisha is often called the first rabbi–disciple pair). God reserves **7,000** who haven't bowed to Baal.
- **the raven and the widow** — God feeds Elijah by *ravens* (unclean scavengers taking someone else's food in a famine) and at the widow of Zarephath's rationed table — God nudging Elijah that “slinging judgment” makes innocent bystanders pay the price, even when you're *right*.
- **zeal** (Reed's caution) — like nuclear energy: it can power a city or blow it up; have it “in moderation.”
