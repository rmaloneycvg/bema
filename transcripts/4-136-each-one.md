### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the early church pattern** — do the Kingdom / get persecuted, repeat: heal the lame beggar → Sanhedrin threatens; share everything → ; heal many → Sadducees jealous, apostles arrested; feed the widows → Stephen seized.
- **Jesus breathing the Spirit (John 20)** — "Receive the Holy Spirit" tied to *forgiveness*; the apostles are on a process, like us, not changed in a single instant.
- **a kingdom of priests** — the believers distributing to all in need fulfill a priestly role (Peter later calls them living stones / a royal priesthood).
- **Hellenistic (Herodian) vs. Hebraic Jews** — the widows-overlooked dispute; the community responds by *fixing it together* (choosing the Seven, with seemingly Hellenistic names) rather than dividing.
- **Ananias and Sapphira** — like the firewood-gatherer (Exodus) and Achan (Joshua), a severe story at the *start* of a new chapter: compromise/dishonesty can't be the DNA of what God is building (not a statement about eternal judgment).
- **Mashiach / Messiah** — Judaism largely awaits an *age* (olam haba), not primarily a person, and never God-in-the-flesh (that's blasphemy to the Jewish mind). A messianic figure would merely *usher in* the age.
- **Isaiah 32** — "a king (singular) will reign in righteousness and *rulers* (plural) will rule with justice; *each one* will be... the shadow of a great rock." Jesus is the king; *we* are the rulers. *Then* the blind see, the deaf hear, etc. — the signs follow the rulers doing justice.
- **Peter's shadow (Acts 5)** — a deliberate play on Isaiah 32: Peter = **Petros** (rock), his shadow = "the shadow of a great rock in a thirsty land." The messianic age is breaking in.
- **mishpat** — justice; the rulers setting things right.
- Moshe (Hasidic shopkeeper in Jerusalem): he awaits not a Messiah but the *Kingdom* — healing of the nations, peace to chaos. Making that visible is on *us*.
