### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the meta-narrative review** — Preface (Gen 1–11), Introduction (Gen 12–50), then “The Tale of Two Kingdoms: Empire vs. Shalom” — right-handed power (fear/coercion) vs. left-handed power (invitation/grace); Exodus → Sinai wedding (“a kingdom of priests”) → tabernacle → Leviticus (the priesthood manual, the “priestwich,” partying/caring for the oppressed) → Numbers (desert honeymoon) → Deuteronomy (remember — the alien, orphan, widow) → Joshua (crossroads of the earth) → Judges (the redemption cycle) → Ruth.
- **having a king was never the problem** — Deuteronomy 17:14-15 already anticipated “we want a king like the nations” and permitted it (“the king the Lord your God chooses … from among your own”). The problem in 1 Samuel 8 is the *motive*: “like all the other nations” — but Israel is a *kingdom of priests*, called to be *distinct* so they can bless the nations, not to blend in.
- **rejecting God as king** — “it is not you they have rejected, but me as their king” (1 Sam 8:7); and “a king to go out and fight our battles” — whereas *God* fought their battles before (undefeated).
- **sheep need a shepherd; Israel gets a donkey herder** — rabbinic tongue-in-cheek: Israel is meant to be *sheep* led by a *shepherd*-king, but God gives them **Saul**, a (bad) *donkey herder* who'd lost his donkeys — because they're acting like stubborn, stiff-necked donkeys. Saul is tall/impressive (“head and shoulders above” — the world's metric) and from **Benjamin** (the tribe of the Judges-ending atrocity).
- **God's metrics ≠ our metrics** — Samuel anoints **David**, Jesse's eighth/youngest son, a *shepherd* from **Judah**, not even brought in at first (“man looks at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart”). God loves to choose the *last* (Jesus: “the last will be first”) — as throughout Genesis (not the firstborn: Noah's son, Avram, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Ephraim over Manasseh with Jacob's crossed hands).
- **kiddush Hashem** (Hebrew, “hallow/sanctify the Name”) — David's defining commitment: not his ego but *God's reputation/place in the cosmos* thrust out front (developed next episode). God chooses the lowly because they remember their story.
- **Eli, his wicked sons, and Samuel** — 1 Samuel opens as a “Judges epilogue”: Eli's sons seize meat *before the fat is rendered* (violating Leviticus — the fat is God's portion) and defile the temple; Eli is “semi-righteous”; the adopted boy **Samuel** becomes a righteous judge (who also has disappointing sons).
