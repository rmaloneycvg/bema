### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Number symbolism** (reviewed): 1 = God; 2 = tablets of Moses; 3 = community; 4 = the Gentiles (four corners); 5 = books of Moses; 6 = sinful man; 7 = completion / the seven pagan nations (Joshua 3:10); 8 = new creation; 10 (3+7) = complete community; 12 = tribes of Israel; 40 = testing/trial; 50 = the groups Moses organized at Sinai (Jethro's counsel).
- **feeding of the 5,000 vs. 4,000** — The 5,000 (in the Jewish Triangle) uses Jewish numbers: 5 loaves + 2 fish (the complete Law: books + tablets), 12 baskets left over. The 4,000 (in the Gentile Decapolis) uses Gentile numbers: 7 loaves (completion / seven nations), fish count unspecified, 7 baskets left over. Point: Jesus is enough for Jews *and* Gentiles.
- **yeast = sin** — "the yeast of the Pharisees/Sadducees/Herod" = their sinful teaching (here, that one should *not* reach the mamzer / the Gentiles).
- **sign of Jonah** — again a Gentile reference (Jonah to Nineveh), not the resurrection.
- **the half-healed blind man** (Mark 8) — a living teaching prop: Jesus heals him partway ("people like trees walking") to dramatize "do you have eyes but fail to see?" before completing the healing.
- **mamzer / mumzer** — the outsider; Matthew's agenda.
