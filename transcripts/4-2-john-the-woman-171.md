### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the “chosen lady”** — three options for who John addresses: (1) a literal *woman and her children*; (2) a *church* personified as a woman (the church-as-bride; outlying villages of a city were called its *daughters*/“suburbs” — e.g., “daughters of Jerusalem” = surrounding towns, not literal women); or (3) *both* — a real woman who leads a house church, with the letter intended for her wider congregation. Clue: John uses the *masculine* Greek for the recipients later, which fits a church better than a lone woman.
- **truth and love** — the running Johannine pairing (as in 1 John): “walk in obedience to his commands … his command is that you walk in love.” Obedience *is* love; love is what makes it true. If there is no love, there is no truth (she'd be “a liar”).
- **the “deceivers” / Antichrist** — “many deceivers … who do not acknowledge Jesus Christ coming in the flesh” (v.7). Candidates: the **Docetist** Gnostics (deniers of Jesus's true humanity, cf. 1 John/Colossians), the legalistic Judean opponents who dogged Paul, a disengaged “holy-huddle” legalism, or the **Nicolaitans** of Revelation. Marty leans toward the Docetist Gnostics.
- **circulated correspondence** — even a personally addressed letter was expected to be shared among churches, supporting the church/both readings.

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- **Docetism** / the Docetists — the Gnostic denial of the incarnation, likely target of the “deceivers” (carried over from the 1 John / Colossians discussion).
- **The Nicolaitans** — flagged as a possible identity of the deceivers (to be met in Revelation).
- Note: 2 John may be the only NT letter addressed *directly* to a woman (contrast with Paul's letters to churches); the “woman and children / sister's children” framing.
