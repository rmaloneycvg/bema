### Hebrew Words Explained

- **mathetes** (Greek, μαθητής) — the single, broad Greek word used throughout Acts and Luke for “disciple.” Its weakness is that it covers everything from a casual *student* to a fully committed follower. Greek has only this one word for both ideas, so context must carry the distinction.
- **talmid** (plural **talmidim**) — Hebrew for a committed disciple: not merely a student who sits under a teacher, but one who follows the rabbi all day, every day, for years, with the goal of becoming exactly like the rabbi in every way (a “come, follow me” relationship). Timothy is a *talmid* of Paul; Priscilla and Aquila function as *talmidim* as well.
- **limud** (plural **limudim**) — Hebrew for *student* in the ordinary sense: one who attends, listens, and learns under a teacher without the total life-consuming imitation of a *talmid*. Hebrew authors could distinguish *limud* (student) from *talmid* (disciple); Greek collapses both into *mathetes*. Marty's whole question is whether Paul is producing *limudim* (students) or *talmidim* (disciples).
- **mumzer** / **mamzer** — in Jewish law, an illegitimate child or outsider who is barred from full inclusion (and from circumcision). Timothy, with a Jewish mother and Greek father, is a literal, legal *mumzer*, which is precisely why Judaism would not allow him to be circumcised — and why Paul circumcising him is the gospel reversing exclusion into inclusion.
- **theosabes** (Greek, “God-fearer”) — Gentiles who worshiped the God of Israel and attended synagogue without full conversion; e.g., Titius Justus in Corinth (Acts 18:7) and the “God-fearing Greeks” of Thessalonica.
