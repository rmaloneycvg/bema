### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Pharisees vs. Sadducees** — Jesus spent *three years* critiquing/partnering with the Pharisees; he spends *one week* confronting the corrupt chief priesthood (the Sadducees / Zadokim), and it gets him killed. The Pharisees did not kill Jesus; the priestly mafia did.
- **s'micha / smicha** — authority (the priests demand Jesus's credentials).
- **the parable of two sons** — likely a remez to **Zadok and Abiathar** (the "sons of Zadok" / Sadducees): which son actually does the father's will?
- **the parable of the tenants** — built on Isaiah 5's vineyard. The priests mishear it (tenants = Rome); Jesus turns it on them (tenants = the priesthood hoarding the fruit). The vineyard = God's people, now *bearing fruit*; this is a condemnation of the priesthood, not Judaism.
- **betushim** — the "stinking/wild grapes" Isaiah 5 found instead of good fruit.
- **the cornerstone** — Psalm 118:22–23, "the stone the builders rejected."
- **Isaiah 61** — strangers/foreigners tending the vineyard; the people becoming priests of the Lord. Jesus's point: you have the enemy backwards.
- **the wedding banquet / no wedding clothes** — the Kingdom is a party God throws; the firstborn RSVP first but refuse, so the hall is filled with outsiders (the mamzer). The guest without wedding clothes wants the party but not God's way — a remez to Zephaniah 1 (judgment on the idolatrous priests, "those clad in foreign/strange clothes," merchants wiped out, silver and gold unable to save).
- **mamzer / mumzer** — the outsider; Matthew's agenda.
