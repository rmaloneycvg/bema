### Hebrew Words Explained

- **remez** (Hebrew, “hint”) — the second level of Jewish *PaRDeS* interpretation: a subtle allusion embedded in the text that points the hearer to another passage. Marty argues Paul's retelling of the Damascus-road event is loaded with *remez*, deliberately echoing the prophetic call narratives so a Jewish listener will recognize what Paul claims happened to him.
- **goad** / “kick against the goads” — a *goad* is a cattle prod used to drive livestock; “to kick against the goads” is an idiom for fighting the direction God is driving you. In Jewish tradition this phrase is attached to the one disobedient prophet, **Jonah**, so Paul's use of it (Acts 26:14) signals, “I was being Jonah — running from my call.”
- **Son of man** (Hebrew *ben adam*) — God's repeated address to Ezekiel (“Son of man, stand up on your feet”). The “stand up on your feet” command, absent from Acts 9 but present in Paul's Acts 26 retelling, is lifted straight from Ezekiel's commissioning.
- **“set apart from my mother's womb”** — Paul's phrase in Galatians 1:15 deliberately quotes the prophetic call formula used of **Jeremiah** (“before you were born I set you apart”, Jer 1:5) and the Servant of **Isaiah** (“he who formed me in the womb to be his servant”, Isa 49:5), framing Paul's experience as a prophetic calling, not a conversion.
- **“a light for the Gentiles”** — Isaiah 49:6, the Servant's job description (“I will make you a light for the Gentiles that my salvation may reach the ends of the earth”) — which Paul adopts as the description of his own mission.

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- The theological danger of calling Paul's experience a “conversion” — Marty warns it is a seedbed for **replacement theology** (supersessionism), the mistaken idea that the church replaces Israel.
- **Gamaliel** — Paul's teacher, “one of the greatest teachers in all Judaism,” cited to argue Paul's altered details are intentional craft, not faulty memory.
- The four prophetic call narratives Paul echoes — **Jonah** (the disobedient prophet sent to Gentiles), **Ezekiel** (sent to a stubborn, rebellious people of God), **Jeremiah** (set apart from the womb), and **Isaiah's Servant** (a light to the Gentiles).
- The three-days-of-darkness / scales motif — Jonah three days in the fish (dark, covered in scales) paralleled with Saul three days blind until “scales” fell from his eyes (Acts 9:18).
- The BEMA **Turkey trip** — invitation to study the apostles' use of Scripture-in-context in person.
