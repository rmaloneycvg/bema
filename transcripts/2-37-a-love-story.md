### Hebrew Words Explained

- **translating the names** — Elle renders every Hebrew name by meaning (as Marty did with *Ya'akov*). Names carry identity and destiny. In Ruth 1: **Beyt Lechem** = “House of Bread” (ironic in a famine), **Yehuda** = “praise,” **Moav** = “from father” (Lot's incest, Gen 19 — a legacy of shame), **Elimelech** = “my God is king” (is he, in this tragedy?), **Na'ami** = “pleasant/good,” **Machlon** = “diseased,” **Khilion** = “weak,” **Ephrathah** = “fruitful,” **Rut** = “neighbor,” **Boaz** = “in fierce strength.”
- **dabaq** (Hebrew, “cling”) — Ruth *clings* to Na'ami (1:14): the first flourishing-in-trauma tool is to find something good and hold on with all your might (“ball, ball, ball!”).
- **Shaddai** — the name for God Na'ami invokes bitterly (1:20-21). Rooted in Avraham's calling: *Shaddai* evokes a great winged family-protector/guardian deity familiar from Avraham's home culture. Na'ami uses it ironically — “God was supposed to protect us.”
- **chen** (Hebrew, “favor / grace / patronage”) — Ruth seeks *chen* in Boaz's eyes (2:2,10): not vague “grace” but a *sponsor* who says “I choose you and will give you what you need to walk out your calling.”
- **chayil** / **gibor chayil** → **eshet chayil** — Boaz is a “hero of force” (*gibor chayil*, like David's mighty men); he later calls Ruth a “woman of force” (*eshet chayil*) — strength/valor/resourcefulness, the same word.
- **chutzpah / “fast feet” (courage)** — the Hebrew for courage connotes *quick feet*, willingness to run to the front lines. Ruth repeatedly refuses “autopilot” and pursues a changed destiny with *chutzpah*.
- **gala** (Hebrew, “uncover/denude”) — at the threshing floor (ch. 3), Elle translates “uncover *from* his feet” (not just “his feet”). *Gala* is the nakedness word (Noah's drunkenness; Leviticus's “do not uncover nakedness”); Elle (with most scholarship) reads the scene as a provocative proposition — while noting BEMA does not commend it as a strategy, and that God works through people who “color outside the lines.”
- **“spread your wing over me … you are my redeemer”** (3:9) — *wing* (*kanaf*) is both the corner of Boaz's garment/prayer shawl (the *tallit* wing placed over bride and groom) and the “wings” of refuge Boaz earlier blessed her under (2:12).
- **Shavuot** — the threshing-floor scene occurs at the barley/wheat-harvest holiday; Ruth is the scroll read in the Jewish community at *Shavuot*.
- **Genesis 12 echo** — Boaz describes Ruth (“you left your father, mother, and the land of your birth … and came to a people you did not know,” 2:11) in the exact language of God's call to Avraham — naming her as true family of Avraham.
- **Boaz = temple pillar** — the name *Boaz* appears elsewhere only as one of the two pillars of Solomon's temple (the other, **Yakin**, “He has established”). The outsider “neighbor” (Rut) flees to “fierce strength” (Boaz) — i.e., flees to the temple/worship/community of God for refuge.
- **the four tools for flourishing in trauma** — (1) catch something good and cling to it, (2) ignore the other voices, (3) seek a beacon of strength in a context you know, (4) pursue a changed destiny with chutzpah — plus a coda: flee to worship.
