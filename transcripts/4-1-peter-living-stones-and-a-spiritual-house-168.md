### Hebrew Words Explained

- **“spiritual house” = the temple** (1 Peter 2:5) — many Jews avoided saying “temple” (too holy a word) and instead said “spiritual house.” Peter says God is quarrying his chosen people as **living stones** and building them into *one* temple — the stones *plural*, the temple *singular* (cf. Paul: “you (plural) are the temple (singular)”). Perseverance is *corporate*: the community together tells the world what God is like.
- **the audience / places of refuge** — Peter writes to exiles in **Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia** — backwoods, off-the-beaten-path regions. **Cappadocia** especially: a place of hiding, with underground cities carved into soft **tufa** rock, where the persecuted fled. Marty speculates Peter ministered to these leaderless “Shammai-country” areas because James led Jerusalem, John (and Paul) led Asia, but the Gentile believers in the backwoods had no home synagogue.
- **Peter's transformation** — the same Peter who once drew a sword and swore to die for his rabbi, then denied him, was restored on a beach (John 21, “feed my sheep”). The letter is written by a *changed* man who chose the way of grace and “living hope” — hope for “a guy like me.”
- **suffering / persecution** — the dominant theme: “do not repay evil with evil … repay evil with blessing”; “do not be surprised at the fiery ordeal.” Marty draws a sharp contrast with American “culture wars,” which he calls “a spit in the face” of people who know real persecution — American Christianity (especially comfortable/privileged) is *not* a persecuted people; our missional call is to persist in doing good, not to fight for rights or restore a “Christian world.”
