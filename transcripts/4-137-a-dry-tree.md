### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Luke's literary device (history + speech)** — like Josephus (e.g., the Masada speech): a block of narrative, then a long speech (Peter's sermon, Stephen's speech, Paul before Agrippa). The speeches are presented as delivered, but it's a storytelling convention.
- **the "how" of the early church** — not pacifism and not retributive/"redemptive" violence, but confronting injustice by shining light on it (the Sermon on the Mount: turn the other cheek — the *right* cheek implies a demeaning backhand; give the inner cloak too; carry the pack a second mile). Stephen is stoned praying "do not hold this sin against them," not fighting back.
- **tallit / haluk** — outer garment / inner garment; **angaria** — a Roman soldier's right to compel one mile.
- **the Son of Man *standing*** — the only place Jesus is pictured *standing* (not sitting) at God's right hand: honoring Stephen, the first to take up his cross.
- **the martyrs** — Philip of Hierapolis and his daughters; Polycarp of Smyrna — zealous, but their zeal retrained toward forgiveness, not the Sicarii dagger.
- **the implications of the Kingdom = outsiders come in** — Samaria (Philip; cf. John 4), then the Ethiopian eunuch.
- **the Ethiopian eunuch** — a God-fearer with a *scroll of Isaiah* (great wealth/devotion), excluded from the temple assembly (Deuteronomy 23:1 bars eunuchs). His question about Isaiah 53 ("who is this about — himself or someone else?") is really "is there a place in this for *me*?" The suffering-servant discourse is primarily Israel as God's servant, an exhortation to suffer for the Lord — not first a messianic prophecy.
- **Isaiah 56** — just past Isaiah 53: to the *foreigner* and the *eunuch* ("a dry tree") who keep the covenant, God promises "within my temple... a name better than sons and daughters." Philip (knowing his Text) likely read right on to it.
- **Shaul / Saul** — enters holding the coats at Stephen's stoning (saved for the next episode).
