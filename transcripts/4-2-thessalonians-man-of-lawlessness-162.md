### Hebrew Words Explained

- **olam hazeh** (Hebrew עולם הזה, “this age”) — the present, broken era preceding the restoration of all things; contrasted with **olam haba** (עולם הבא, “the age to come” / eternal life). The “man of lawlessness” belongs to the *olam hazeh* — “the last days” of this worldly age, not a far-future dispensation.
- **two-part vs. three-part eschatology** — two-part: this broken age, then a hard break into the age made right. Three-part (Jesus's view): the two ages *overlap* — the age to come breaks in and grows while this age slowly dies out (a transition period). Relevant to how “the day of the Lord” is understood.
- **“man of lawlessness” / “Antichrist”** — Marty argues the Greek is *qualitative/descriptive* (the *kind* of person who thrives in the last days), not a single future individual. If it points to anyone concrete, it's **Caesar** — “the secret power of lawlessness is already at work” (2:7) = Rome, already present. The description echoes the Hellenistic pagan culture Paul indicts in Romans 1 (exalting self as God).
- **authorship doubt** — Marty (while fully accepting the canon) finds 2 Thessalonians' Greek uncharacteristically “messy” and its insistence on its own Pauline authenticity (“I, Paul, write this in my own hand … the distinguishing mark in all my letters”, 3:17; “not … by any letter supposed to have come from us”, 2:2) oddly heavy-handed — his one canonical reservation.
