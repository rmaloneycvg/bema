### Hebrew Words Explained

- **nephesh** (נֶפֶשׁ) — the soul; in the Hebraic mind the whole essence of a person — body and spirit together, emotions, consciousness, the sum total of what makes "you" you. The second test is a test of the *nephesh*: does God have you, or only when he meets your demands?
- **meod** (מְאֹד) — "very"; translated "might" or "strength." It means all your resources, whatever you have to offer. The third test is the test of the *meod* — will you use your strength to protect the weak? (cf. *tov meod*, "very good," in Genesis 1).
- **lev / levav** (לֵב / לֵבָב) — the heart, the seat of the will (the first test).
- **tsa'aqah** (צְעָקָה) — the cry of the oppressed, of those whose lives are threatened. Moses cries out a genuine *tsa'aqah* when the people are ready to stone him.
- **paniym** (פָּנִים) — "faces," the word behind "before." God says "I will stand *before* you by the rock" — standing between Moses and the rock, face to the rock.
- **nakah** (נָכָה) — to strike, to smite in order to kill. The same word used when Moses struck down the Egyptian and when God struck the gods of Egypt; now Moses is told to *nakah* the rock — in picture, striking God, who stands before it.
- **Adonai Nissi** (יְהוָה נִסִּי) — "The LORD is my Banner," the name Moses gives the altar; the banner points beyond itself to where God is.
- **Shema / Ve'ahavta** (שְׁמַע / וְאָהַבְתָּ) — "Hear, O Israel, the LORD is our God, the LORD alone. Love the LORD your God with all your heart (*levav*), with all your soul (*nephesh*), and with all your might (*meod*)" — the three tests map onto the three faculties of the Shema.
