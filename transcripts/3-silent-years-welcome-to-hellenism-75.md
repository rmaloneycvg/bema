### Hebrew Words Explained

- **euangelion** (εὐαγγέλιον, Greek) — "gospel / good news." The term predates the New Testament by centuries; Alexander the Great carried a *euangelion* — the good news that Hellenism had arrived.
- **Hellenism** — the Greek worldview (named after *Helles*) that, for the first time, systematically moved the gods out of the center and put *man* at the center ("man is the measure of all things"). Its anthem: "I did it my way."
- **gymnasium / Asclepion** (Greek) — education (think university) and healthcare (think hospital); two of Alexander's four cultural pillars (with entertainment/theater and athletics).
- **Decapolis** (δεκάπολις) — "ten cities"; the region of Greco-Roman cities built in and around Judea.
- **Hasmonean / Maccabean** — the Jewish dynasty (167-63 BC) that arose after the Maccabean revolt (the story of Hanukkah), led by Judah Maccabee ("Judah the Hammer").
- **Hasidim** (חֲסִידִים) — "pious ones"; the ultra-devoted Jews who rejected Hellenistic compromise and settled the Galilee; from them came the **Zealots** (devotion by the sword) and the **Pharisees** (devotion by obedience).
- **Sadducees / Herodians** — the Hellenized priestly class and their lay sympathizers who embraced both God and Greek leisure/luxury.
- **Essenes** — the priestly separatists who withdrew to the desert (e.g. Qumran), associated with the Dead Sea Scrolls.
