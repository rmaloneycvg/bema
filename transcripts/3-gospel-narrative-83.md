## Book of Bible

The Gospels (the meaning of "gospel")

This episode sets up the Gospel study and does not read a scripture passage as a block; instead it weaves in short phrases — John the Baptist's and Jesus's message "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand" (Matthew 3:2; Mark 1:15), the sending of the Twelve and the seventy-two to "proclaim the good news of the kingdom" (Luke 9-10), and the apostles preaching "the kingdom of God" in Acts. The one text quoted at length is extra-biblical: the Priene Calendar Inscription (c. 9 BC) honoring Caesar Augustus — "the birthday of the god was for the world the beginning of the gospel (euangelion)..."

### Hebrew Words Explained

- **euangelion** (εὐαγγέλιον, Greek) — "gospel / good news." Centuries before the New Testament, Alexander the Great and later Rome used it as the imperial proclamation that a new king and kingdom had arrived. Each Gospel writer's "gospel" is therefore a subversive counter-announcement: there is a *different* King and a *different* Kingdom.
- **parousia** (παρουσία, Greek) — "arrival / coming / presence"; a royal visit (and the word behind Christ's "second coming").
- **advent / epiphany** — the arrival/appearing of a Caesar (or his birth), proclaimed as good news.
- **Pax Romana** (Latin) — "the peace of Rome," part of the empire's gospel.
- **Kingdom of God / Kingdom of Heaven** — the content of the biblical gospel: a new King and Kingdom that *is here*. (Matthew's preference for "Kingdom of Heaven" is flagged for the next episode.)
