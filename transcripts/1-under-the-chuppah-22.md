### Hebrew Words Explained

- **chuppah** (חוּפָּה) — the wedding canopy (a *tallit*/prayer shawl held up on poles) under which the couple takes their vows; it symbolizes the presence of God hovering over the couple. At Sinai, Israel camps *under* the mountain as under a chuppah.
- **beit av** (בֵּית אָב) — "house of the father," the extended-family household unit.
- **mishpucha** (מִשְׁפָּחָה) — family; the larger extended family that arranges marriages together.
- **mikvah** (מִקְוֶה) — a ritual immersion/cleansing bath the bride undergoes to be consecrated before the ceremony.
- **tallit** (טַלִּית) — the prayer shawl, used to form the canopy of the chuppah.
- **ketubah** (כְּתוּבָּה) — the written marriage covenant defining the relationship (seven to twelve items). The Ten Commandments function as Israel's ketubah, written on stone.
- **mohar** (מֹהַר) — the bride price / wedding gift exchanged between the two fathers.
- **shofar** (שׁוֹפָר) — the ram's-horn trumpet whose blast begins the ceremony (the "loud trumpet blast" at Sinai).
- **neged / ezer k'negdo** (נֶגֶד / עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ) — "against / a helper corresponding to him" (from Adam and Eve, the first marriage); Israel comes *neged* against the mountain.
- **Decalogue / decalogos** (עֲשֶׂרֶת הַדְּבָרִים) — "ten words" / "ten sayings." The Bible calls them words, not commandments; the Jewish numbering differs from both Protestant and Catholic schemes.
