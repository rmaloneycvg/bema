### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Torah, Nevi'im, Ketuvim** — the three divisions of the Hebrew Scriptures (the *Tanakh*): the **Torah** (instruction/law), the **Nevi'im** (the Prophets), and the **Ketuvim** (the Writings). Marty maps the metanarrative onto them: God invites/defines/tests/fulfills the covenant in Torah, the covenant's breakdown unfolds in the Nevi'im, and the Ketuvim supplements the story — then the Gospels incarnate it and Acts is the epilogue.
- **midrash** — the Jewish practice of interpreting and applying Scripture to a new situation. Marty's central claim: the New Testament letters are *inspired, authoritative midrash* — God-breathed applications of the one narrative of God to specific, varying contexts (Corinth, Ephesus, Philippi, Colossae, etc.), not a single systematized “Pauline theology.”
- **ecclesia** (Greek *ἐκκλησία*, “called-out ones”) — the Greek word translated “church”; Marty uses it for the Spirit-filled community that the story has progressed to (from Abraham to an indwelt *ecclesia*).
- **chutzpah** — Yiddish/Hebrew for bold audacity or nerve; used to describe the stubborn, fiery, story-trusting character of the family God chooses to partner with.
- **PaRDeS / remez / drash** — the Jewish interpretive framework (*PaRDeS*: Peshat, Remez, Drash, Sod) and two of its levels, *remez* (hint) and *drash* (interpretation/seeking), which Marty says Western readers miss and therefore misinterpret both the “First Testament” and Jesus's rabbinic teaching.
