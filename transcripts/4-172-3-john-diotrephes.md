### Hebrew Words Explained

- **kiddush haShem** (Hebrew קידוש השם, “sanctifying/hallowing the Name”) — the rabbinic ideal of living so as to honor God's name. 3 John's “for the sake of the Name” (v.7) and the call to love and hospitality is how one does *kiddush haShem* — the same thing that marked **David** (a king “after God's own heart” who, for all his failures, sought to hallow the Name). Loving others *is* how we sanctify the Name.
- **truth = the way of love** — the Johannine flag Marty keeps planting: truth is not true because some abstract Platonic “absolute truth” exists out there; it is true because it is *the way of love*. “If it's not love, it just ain't truth.” Note the sequence with Gaius: his *love* is established in the church first, and “working together for the truth” follows — love is the foundation.
- **hospitality as the defining mark** — welcoming others (even strangers/traveling believers) is what sets believers apart from pagans (Sermon on the Mount: “don't even the pagans love their friends? Love your enemies”). **Diotrephes** refuses hospitality — “about as close to the anti-story as you can get.”
- **Diotrephes** (Greek, “nourished/loved by Zeus/Jupiter”) — Marty speculates (admittedly a stretch) the name hints at lingering pagan ways; Brent notes it may not even be his real name but a label (“the Jupiter-lover”). **Jupiter** = the Roman Zeus, chief god, closely tied to Caesar.
- **Demetrius** — the *good* counterpart (“well spoken of by everyone, and even by the truth itself”), a fellow worker known for generosity, love, and hospitality. “Gaius is not alone — those who follow God rarely are” (an Elijah callback).
- **the “elder”** — the author's self-title in 2–3 John; Marty takes it as John himself (others posit his disciples).
