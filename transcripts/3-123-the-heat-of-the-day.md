### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the parable of the workers in the vineyard** — God pays the last-hired first *on purpose*, so everyone sees his generosity. The Kingdom isn't fair; it's benevolent. God loves lavishing on those who "don't deserve it."
- **"bearing the burden in the heat of the day"** — a rabbinic phrase for the Jews carrying the weight of Torah on behalf of the world; it makes the parable explicitly about Jew and Gentile (the 5 p.m. workers = Gentiles).
- **denarius** — a day's wage; Torah requires paying a worker at day's end.
- **the heat of the day** — remez to Abraham under the tree (Genesis 18) showing radical hospitality to the three visitors.
- **bechor** — the firstborn, who bears more responsibility but receives a *double portion* (Isaiah 61:7). Jesus reframes the "double portion" as *everything set right* — which only delights those transformed to want it.
- **chutzpah** — bold audacity (the mother of Zebedee's sons; the two blind men shouting louder).
- **the cup** — James and John answer "we can" like true talmidim (the rabbi called them, so they can be like him); they will drink it in martyrdom.
- **Son of David** — from the blind men (fellow outsiders): a loaded affirmation of Jesus despite his culturally questioned, mamzer-like pedigree.
- **mamzer / mumzer** — the outsider; Matthew's agenda, possibly extending to Jesus himself.
- **God goggles** — Marty's shorthand: Jesus operates in his human nature, not omniscience (Philippians 2; Hebrews — he "learned obedience"). The hard case is foreseeing the resurrection "on the third day."
- **going up to Jerusalem** — always "up" (Mount Zion) regardless of elevation.
