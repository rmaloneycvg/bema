### Hebrew Words Explained

- **a prophet is a mouthpiece, not a future-teller** — a prophet brings a *present-day message for a present-day audience in a present-day situation*. They may occasionally forecast, but their role is to speak God's message *now* (Amos is a prophet because God sent him to speak to *his* Israel, not because he predicts centuries ahead). Abraham Joshua Heschel adds the prophet is *more* than a passive mouthpiece — an *active partner* who has leaned into the very *pathos* (heart) of God.
- **reading prophets contextually** — the prophets aren't *narrative* genre, but they are intensely *contextual*: always ask *when / where / to whom*. Marty cares about where a prophet's voice is meant to be *heard in the narrative*, not the academic debate over the actual date of composition or authorship.
- **Source A vs. Source B in the prophets** — some prophets carry the Samuel/Kings agenda (**immorality / idolatry**), others the Chronicles agenda (**justice / empire**). Marty was raised hearing the prophets as mostly *Source A* (idolatry/immorality) but found *far more Source B* (justice) dominates — and both are true; don't pick one.
- **the five historical periods (Marty's framework)** — (1) **Pre-Assyrian** (all is well; a word of *warning*): to Israel — **Amos, Hosea**; to Judah — **Micah, 1 Isaiah**. (2) **Assyrian** (army at the door): **Jonah, Nahum** (Nineveh/Israel) and **Zephaniah, 2 Isaiah** (Judah, still warned). (3) **Babylonian** (laying waste; a word of *woe*): **Jeremiah, Lamentations, Habakkuk, Obadiah, Joel**. (4) **Exile** (new address in Babylon): **Ezekiel, Daniel, Job, 3 Isaiah**. (5) **Remnant** (return/rebuild, or stay in Persia; a word of *hope*): **4 Isaiah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi**, plus the histories **Esther, Ezra–Nehemiah**. The arc moves **warning → woe → hope**.
- **multiple voices of Isaiah** — nearly all scholarship hears at least two voices (chs. 1–36/39 and 40–66 — “Deutero-Isaiah”); most hear three (“Trito-Isaiah”); Marty likes a *four*-voice reading, because Isaiah's content spans more time than one lifetime. (How exactly to divide it is debatable.)
- **Nevi'im / Tanakh** — the prophets occupy the *Nevi'im* section; the histories (Esther, Ezra–Nehemiah) are technically *Ketuvim* but placed here for the storyline. **Job** could sit in Wisdom literature but is placed in the Exile period for a reason (explained later).
