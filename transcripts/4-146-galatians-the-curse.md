**1** You foolish Galatians! Who has cast a spell on you? ### Galatians 4:1-7 (NET)

**1** Now I mean that the heir, as long as he is a minor, is no different from a slave, though he is the owner of everything. **2** But he is under guardians and managers until the date set by his father. **3** So also we, when we were minors, were enslaved under the basic forces of the world. **4** But when the appropriate time had come, God sent out his Son, born of a woman, born under the law, **5** to redeem those who were under the law, so that we may be adopted as sons with full rights. **6** And because you are sons, God sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, who calls “ Abba! ### Hebrew Words Explained

- **miqsat ma'ase haTorah** (“some of the works of the law”; Greek **erga nomou**, ἔργα νόμου) — the identity-marker works that make a person Jewish. “Cursed is everyone who relies on the works of the law” means: if you seek your *justification* by becoming Jewish, you live under the crushing curse of trying to keep it all perfectly — not a curse from God, but the weight of that reliance.
- **theosabes / theosebeis** (“God-fearers”) — the Galatian Gentile believers; tempted to convert to escape both Jewish-community persecution (Shammai Galatia) and Roman emperor-worship (they lack the Jewish exception).
- **b'nei Avraham** (“children of Abraham”) — full covenant members. In Jewish thought a *child of Abraham* was a proselyte who took on Torah/circumcision. Paul's radical move: a Gentile *with faith* is adopted as a full child of Abraham — faith, not circumcision, is the family marker.
- **emunah** (Hebrew, “faith / faithfulness”) — Habakkuk's “the righteous will live by *emunah*”: not mere belief but faith put into action. Quoted in BEMA's betrothal line *v'erastikh li l'olam … b'emunah* (“I will betroth you to myself … in faithfulness,” Hosea 2:19-20).
- **gezerah shavah** (Hebrew rabbinic rule, “equivalence of expressions”) — linking two passages that share a common phrase so each interprets the other. Paul chains them: Habakkuk (“live by faith”) + Leviticus (“live by them”), then Deuteronomy (“cursed is everyone hung on a pole/tree”) tied to Christ's crucifixion.
- **paidagogos** (Greek, “pedagogue / guardian”) — NOT a schoolmaster/teacher but a *guardian-protector* hired to safeguard an underage heir until he can legally claim his inheritance. The law is the *paidagogos* escorting God's people safely to the day the promise (the Seed) is realized — then its role is complete.
- **Seed** (singular) — Paul's grammatical argument: the promises were to Abraham's “Seed” (singular), meaning one person, Christ.
- **Abba** (Aramaic, “Father”) — the Spirit of the Son cries “Abba, Father,” marking adoption to sonship.

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- *The Holy Epistle to the Galatians* by **Thomas Lancaster** — cited again for its chapter on the *paidagogos*/pedagogue.
- **Hillel vs. Shammai** on justification; Paul uses a classic *Hillel* argument from **Genesis 15** (Abraham “credited as righteous”) — justified by trust two chapters *before* his circumcision (Genesis 17).
- The **“forivolous/foolish Galatians”** trope — ancient literature mocked Galatians as backwater and barbaric; Paul's “you foolish Galatians” is a pointed jab at a highly educated Shammai community that settled there to stay unbothered.
- The **Jewish exception** (Herod the Great's arrangement) — why excluded Gentile believers faced capital-offense emperor-worship.
- The **Deuteronomy covenant-renewal ceremony** on two mountains (Ebal/Gerizim) — the blessings-and-curses context Paul quotes.
- **Rabbi David Fohrman** — “give me the first four chapters of Genesis and I can teach you anything in the Bible”; supports treating Genesis as preface/introduction and Abraham as an imperfect early “fulfillment” of the story.
- The **“Pint o' Bacon”** running gag (Backyard Tap House) as shorthand for the kosher/curse question.
