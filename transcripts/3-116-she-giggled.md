### Hebrew Words Explained

- **talmid** (pl. **talmidim**) — a disciple; a student chosen by a rabbi because the rabbi believes "you can become just like me." To be called is itself the rabbi's vote of confidence in your ability.
- The reframed rebuke — Jesus's "you of little faith, why did you doubt?" is read not as Peter failing a task but as Jesus affirming Peter: *faith in whom?* Peter didn't lose faith in Jesus (who was fine) but in himself; the rabbi believes his disciple can do what he does.
- **faith like a child** — Marty's meditation (from a book he wrote for his kids) on watching his daughter fall and giggle while learning to walk uphill: innocence tied to confidence, getting up and trying again.
