### Romans 6:19-23 (NET)

**19** (I am speaking in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh.) For just as you once presented your members as slaves to impurity and lawlessness leading to more lawlessness, so now present your members as slaves to righteousness leading to sanctification. **20** For when you were slaves of sin, you were free with regard to righteousness. **21** So what benefit did you then reap from those things that you are now ashamed of? ### Romans 7:1-25 (NET)

**1** Or do you not know, brothers and sisters (for I am speaking to those who know the law), that the law is lord over a person as long as he lives? **2** For a married woman is bound by law to her husband as long as he lives, but if her husband dies, she is released from the law of the marriage. **3** So then, if she is joined to another man while her husband is alive, she will be called an adulteress. ### Hebrew Words Explained

- **“wages” thread** — “the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life” (Rom 6:23) is the *culmination* of the labor/wage argument begun in Romans 4 (Abraham got no “wage”; righteousness was credited as a gift). A paycheck is earned; the gift is not — so the famous verse is routinely quoted out of its multi-chapter context.
- **miqsat ma'ase haTorah** (“some of the works of the law”) — when Paul says the Jews “died to the law,” he does **not** mean they stopped observing Torah; they stopped seeking their *justification* in it (consistent with Galatians and Romans 3).
- **sarx** (Greek σάρξ, “flesh”) — Marty insists this means *animal appetites* / beast-nature, NOT “sinful nature” (an Augustinian gloss). We are *more than* our *sarx*; sin is an *intruder* — “sin living in me,” not the truest truth of who we are (we bear the image of God). Read “animal appetites” wherever “flesh” appears.
- **talaiporos** (Greek ταλαίπωρος) — “what a *wretched* man I am” (Rom 7:24) means *afflicted / enduring troubles / conflicted*, not “terrible at my core.” Brent's word study: Paul describes conflict, not depravity.
- **kal va'chomer** (“light and heavy,” lesser-to-greater) — Paul's “how much more” reappears: if nothing in first-century persecution can separate believers from God's love, *how much more* for us.
- **pistis Christou** (“faith of Christ”) — freedom comes by displaying the *faith of Jesus*, trusting the promises of God.
- **Abba** (Aramaic, “Father”) and **adoption to sonship** — the Spirit brings not slavery/fear but *adoption*; “adoption to sonship” (*huiothesia*) was a loaded Roman legal term. Echoes the Galatians theme of being adopted as *b'nei Avraham*.
- **mastering the beast** — Paul's inner war echoes Genesis 2–4: Adam, Eve, and Cain (“you must master it”) — the human task of knowing “when to say enough.”
- **tzedakah / self-sacrifice vs. self-preservation** — setting the mind on the Spirit frees us to lay down our lives for others (Session 1 Empire-vs-Shalom).
- **creation groaning** — the redemption hoped for is of *this* world (Genesis 1 goodness restored), not an escape to somewhere else.
