### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Caesarea Philippi / Banias (Panias)** — a pagan cult center at a huge spring from a cave, long tied to **Baal** (god of agricultural fertility; rain as his seed) and later to **Pan** (half-goat fertility god). Known in the Roman world as **the Gates of Hell** — where the underworld met this world. Site of the festival "Pandemonium."
- **sod** — the God-given mystery; Jesus says Peter's confession came by revelation ("not by flesh and blood"), not deduction.
- **the living God** — the phrase Peter uses ("Son of the living God") is a remez; it appears at Joshua 3:10 (the seven nations, "the living God is among you") and first at Deuteronomy 5 (hearing God's voice from the fire and living).
- **Petros vs. Petra** — Jesus renames Simon **Petros** ("Rocky"), but "on this **Petra** (bedrock/cliff face) I will build my church" — rabbinically pointing at the literal rock of the Gates of Hell: the church is built offensively, into the chaos, and hell will not prevail.
- **Simon / Shimon** — from **shema** ("to hear"); "the one who listens." After Peter rebukes Jesus and is told "get behind me, Satan [the opposer]," he must become less Peter and more Shimon — step back and listen (echoing Deuteronomy 5).
- **chutzpah** — bold fire (why some thought Jesus was Elijah).
- **the crowd** — Mark/Luke suggest the "deny yourself, take up your cross" words are shouted to the *pagan crowd* at Caesarea Philippi, not only the disciples.
