### Hebrew Words Explained

- **El Shaddai** (אֵל שַׁדַּי) — the name by which God was known to the patriarchs, often translated "God Almighty." The rabbis read *Shaddai* as a contraction of *Mi She'amar Leolamo Dai* — "the one who said to his world, enough" (Genesis Rabbah 5:8): the God who knows when to say enough.
- **YHWH** (יהוה, Yod-Heh-Vav-Heh) — the timeless, personal name God reveals in Exodus 6, emphasizing God's timelessness ("I am that I am"). Rendered LORD in English Bibles. "Jehovah" is a misnomer from mixing the vowels of *Adonai* into the name.
- **Elohim** (אֱלֹהִים) — the generic word for God/gods (with a plural feel), the concept of God Pharaoh recognizes, contrasted with the intimate covenant name Adonai/YHWH.
- **kavod** (כָּבֵד) — heavy, weighty, stubborn. When Pharaoh's heart is *kavod*-ed, it is made heavy so he cannot see — he does not perceive what is in front of him.
- **chazak** (חָזַק) — to be strong, to strengthen; a word of resolve. When a heart is *chazak*-ed, the person sees clearly and chooses to power through (cf. *chazak v'ematz*, "be strong and courageous").
- **bechor** (בְּכוֹר) — the firstborn, who carries not only a double portion of inheritance but a double portion of responsibility: to carry on the father's legacy and spread his values. God calls Israel to be his *bechor* — his message to the world.
- **Shema** (שְׁמַע) — "Hear, O Israel," the first command placed in the tefillin.
- **Ve'ahavta** (וְאָהַבְתָּ) — "Love the LORD your God with all your heart...," the second command in the tefillin.
- **ruach** (רוּחַ) — spirit/wind; the ruach that blows over the Red Sea to open the birth canal (water and spirit as Exodus language).
