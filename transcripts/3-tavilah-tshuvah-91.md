### Hebrew Words Explained

- **tavilat t'shuvah** (טְבִילַת תְּשׁוּבָה) — "baptism of repentance." *T'shuvah* = turning/returning (repentance). An **Essene** immersion: a once-decisive, self-performed turning from error to walking in the light — distinct from a daily ritual washing.
- **mikveh** (מִקְוֶה) — immersion/baptism. Two kinds: **Pharisee** mikveh (ritual cleansing before synagogue or eating, prayerful/liturgical) and **Essene** mikveh (repentance). In Jewish practice you immerse *yourself*; John doesn't dunk people — "baptized by him" refers to his authority/program.
- **Beelzebub** (בַּעַל זְבוּב, "lord of the flies," the god of Ekron) — the New Testament name surfacing in 2 Kings 1.
- John's **camel-hair garment and leather belt** — a direct quotation of Elijah's description (2 Kings 1:8); John shows up "in his Elijah costume," and even baptizes at three sites tied to major Elijah stories (Bethany beyond the Jordan, Aenon near Salim, the Judean wilderness).
- **winnowing fork / threshing floor** — judgment imagery; the temple was built on Araunah's threshing floor (bought by David), so "his winnowing fork is in his hand" aims fire at the priesthood.
