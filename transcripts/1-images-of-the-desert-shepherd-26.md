### Hebrew Words Explained

- **midbar** (מִדְבָּר) — desert / wilderness, literally the "place of speaking." Built on the root *d-b-r* with a *m-* prefix. The desert is where David, Jesus, and Israel go to hear the word of God.
- **davar / dabar** (דָּבָר / דִּבֶּר) — word / to speak; a weighty, enduring word you keep with you (not casual chatter). The title of Deuteronomy is *Devarim*, "Words/Things."
- **amar** (אָמַר) — to say, casual speech ("Elle talked to Brent"), contrasted with the weightier *davar*.
- Hebrew "word trees": every word is built on three root consonants; vowels and added letters branch out related meanings around one base image — making Hebrew picture-based and concrete rather than precise. From *d-b-r* come word, commandment, to come into agreement, even plague.
- The word for **goat** also carries the sense of **strength** — a lens on the sheep-and-goats parable: is God looking for the strong one who knows a better way, or the one who listens?
