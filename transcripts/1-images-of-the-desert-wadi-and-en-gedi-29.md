### Hebrew Words Explained

- **wadi** (also *vadi*) — a deep desert canyon carved by the rains; its bends block sight lines so you cannot see what lies ahead, teaching you to follow the shepherd's voice step by step. Flash floods in wadis are the number-one cause of death in the desert.
- **maim chaim** (מַיִם חַיִּים) — "living water": water that comes directly from God as a spring or rain, flowing and fresh — contrasted with stagnant, "dank" cistern water that has been caught and stored on our own behalf.
- **En Gedi / Ein Gedi** (עֵין גֶּדִי) — "spring of the deer/kid"; a desert oasis. *Ein* = spring, *gedi* = a kind of young deer/goat.
- **lulav** (לוּלָב) — the bundle waved at Sukkot, made of four elements: palm frond, myrtle, river willow, and *etrog* (a citrus fruit).
- **Sukkot** (סֻכּוֹת) — the Festival of Tabernacles, during which the water ceremony is held.
- **Hoshana** (הוֹשַׁע נָא) — "Lord, save us! / grant us success!" (English "Hosanna"), shouted while shaking the lulav; drawn from Psalm 118.
- **tenufa** (תְּנוּפָה) — the "wave" (or "heave") offering; the same word means to *brandish*, as in a military context — what are we brandishing before God: our force, or our ability to be shade and water for others?
