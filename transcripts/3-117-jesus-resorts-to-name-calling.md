### Hebrew Words Explained

- **halakha** (from **halakh**, "to walk") — the rabbinic rulings on how to live out Torah ("the tradition of the elders"); the fence around the 613 that becomes Mishnah, then Talmud. Jesus thinks critically about halakha and won't follow a tradition that nullifies the written Torah.
- **the Torah laws as commentary** — Exodus 21's case laws are read as commentary on the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20); "honor your father and mother" vs. the "Corban"/devoted-to-God loophole.
- **kidnapping / stealing a name** — the verse between the two Jesus quotes (Exod 21:16): to "steal" someone is to rob their essence/dignity, exactly what cursing one's parents does.
- **dogs / pigs** — Jewish slang for Gentiles (cf. Ezekiel's birds/fish for the nations), not as harsh as it sounds in English.
- **chutzpah** — bold audacity; the Canaanite/Syrophoenician woman's persistence.
- **master / rav** — the Hebrew for "master" shares the root of **rabbi**; Elijah is traditionally the first "master" with a disciple (Elisha). The woman's "crumbs from the master's table" is a remez to Elijah and the widow of Zarephath.
- **Jesus as learner** — per Philippians 2 (he did not grasp equality with God), John ("everything I have learned from my Father"), and Hebrews (he "learned obedience"): in his human nature Jesus learns — Ray Vander Laan reads this story as the moment Jesus realizes his mission extends to the Gentiles too (no replacement theology).
