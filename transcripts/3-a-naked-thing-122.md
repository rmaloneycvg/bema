### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the Eight Great Debates** — the rabbinic discussion topics (one is divorce) that revealed how a rabbi interpreted Scripture.
- **Shammai vs. Hillel** — Shammai's yoke = obedience (second commandment ≈ keep the Sabbath); Hillel's yoke = love ("love your neighbor"). Jesus almost always sides with Hillel — but on *divorce* he sides with Shammai, yet for a Hillel reason (protecting the woman).
- **get** — a certificate of divorce.
- **ervat davar vs. davar ervat** (Deuteronomy 24:1 textual variant): **davar ervat** = "a thing of nakedness" (sexual immorality) — Shammai's narrow grounds; **ervat davar** = "a naked thing" (any thing at all) — Hillel's broad grounds (famously, "if she burns your biscuits"). Jesus's point: divorce isn't about the man's convenience but about *protecting* the vulnerable woman.
- **eunuchs** — Jesus's hard saying about those who forgo marriage for the Kingdom.
- **the Ten Commandments as two parallel tablets of five** (per Rabbi Fohrman) — 1–6, 2–7, 3–8, 4–9, 5–10. "Honor your father and mother" (5) parallels "do not covet" (10): both are about being *satisfied with the life you have*. Jesus lists 6–9 then jumps to 5 (not 10) for the rich young man — whose real issue is coveting a different lot.
- **olam haba** — the age to come / eternal life.
- **shaphat / mishpat / shofetim** — to judge / justice / judges; the Twelve "judging" the tribes = joining God in *putting the world back together* (shalom to chaos), not condemnation.
- **mamzer** — the devalued outsider (women, children, the poor) the Kingdom is for.
- **camel through the eye of a needle** — means what it says (near-impossible); the "small gate" legend has no historical basis.
- Underneath it all: **trust the story**.
