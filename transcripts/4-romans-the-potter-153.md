### Hebrew Words Explained

- **predestination (first-century / oracle sense)** — NOT the Calvinist decree. In the Greco-Roman world you went to an *oracle* (e.g., Delphi, or Didyma in Turkey) and received a conditional formula: “If you offer X and if you do Y, then it is predestined that Z will happen.” Predestination assumed *your obedience*. So Romans 8:29-30 means: you are made for partnership with God; *if* you trust and walk faithfully, it is predestined you'll be conformed to the image of Jesus.
- **bechor** (Hebrew, “firstborn”) — the child who partners with the Father to spread the father's values to the other children. God predestines his people to be conformed to Christ, the *bechor* — and God loves to work through non-firstborns (Isaac over Ishmael, Jacob over Esau), the barren, and the outsider.
- **“Jacob I loved, Esau I hated”** — Marty's key move: the passage is about whom God *accepts* (mercy, compassion), not whom he rejects. The point is God's *compassion* and his freedom to show mercy to the unlikely — the “thousand to three” theme.
- **the Potter and the clay** — vessels “for special purposes” vs. “common use,” and vessels “prepared for destruction” that God “bore with great patience” — i.e., God's patience with the pagan Gentiles (like Pharaoh) to display his mercy, not a doctrine of reprobation.
- **remez** and **gezerah shavah** — Paul's buried allusions and his linking of two texts sharing a word: the “stone” gezerah shavah joining **Isaiah 8:14** (stone of stumbling) and **Isaiah 28:16** (whoever trusts will not be shamed), making Jesus the stone — both a warning and an invitation.
- **“Jesus is Lord” vs. imperial “Caesar is Lord”** — Romans 10:9 subverts the Roman “bread and circus” propaganda slogan (“if you proclaim Caesar is Lord, you will be saved”). Heart = *will/obedience* in the Jewish sense; mouth = proclamation.
- **the olive tree** — Israel is the *cultivated* olive tree; Gentiles are *wild* branches grafted in. God did NOT plant a new tree — he pruned and grafted into the *same original* tree. This refutes **replacement theology** (supersessionism).
- **“and so / in this manner all Israel will be saved”** (Rom 11:26) — Greek *houtōs* = “in this way,” not “and then,” guarding against a “new Israel” misreading. “Fullness” (NIV84/KJV), not “full inclusion” — the Jews aren't being *included* into anything; it's their story, and the *Gentiles* are the ones included.
- **kal va'chomer** (“how much more”) — recurs: if Gentile inclusion came through Israel's stumbling, *how much more* their full restoration.
- **Adonai** — “Lord,” the God Gentiles can come to know even through creation (Psalm 19).
