### Hebrew Words Explained

- **euangelion** (Greek, “good news / gospel”) — Paul writes “I am not ashamed of the euangelion” *to Rome itself*, the very origin point of the imperial “gospel”; he refuses to be ashamed of a rival, true gospel in the capital where the empire's gospel radiates from.
- **justification / “credited / reckoned”** — to be *justified* is to be *declared righteous*; Thomas Lancaster's helpful synonym is **exonerated** (not that you are innocent, but that the books are settled). “Credited” / “reckoned” (Gen 15:6, Rom 4) is accounting language: a *gift*, not a *wage* earned by work.
- **miqsat ma'ase haTorah / works of the law** — justification is “apart from the law”; if it came by the works of the law, Jew and Gentile would be justified differently — but there is one righteousness *from God*, by faith, for all.
- **mishpat** (Hebrew, “justice”) and **shofet** (“judge”) — God acts to be “just and the one who justifies” (Rom 3:26): a *shofet* not only *pronounces* judgment but *carries it out* — so God both declares and brings about the restoration/exoneration of those who trust him. (Not Western legal-penal theology.)
- **faith = trust (emunah)** — the Reformation elevated “faith,” but in the Jewish mind faith is **trust**: “trust the story” of Genesis 1 (God loves and values his good creation). This is the bedrock of Romans.
- **“wages” thread** — Paul's labor/wage metaphor begins in Romans 4 (“to the one who works, wages are not credited as a gift”) and only *closes* at Romans 6:23 (“the wages of sin is death”) — that famous verse is the end of an argument begun here, not a standalone.
- **“all have sinned … all are justified”** (Rom 3:23-24) — the famously half-quoted verse: the sentence does not stop at “fall short of the glory of God”; it continues “and all are justified freely by his grace.”

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- *Prototype* by **Jonathan Martin** — recommended again; quoted on having already “heard from God” and known oneself “infinitely loved.”
- **Genesis 15:6** (“Abraham believed God and it was credited to him as righteousness”) — Paul's proof, as in Galatians: Abraham was justified by faith *before* circumcision (Genesis 17), making him father of the faithful uncircumcised and circumcised alike.
- **David / Psalm 32:1-2** (“Blessed are those whose transgressions are forgiven”) — quoted in Romans 4:7-8 as a second witness to righteousness credited apart from works.
- Hermeneutical warning against reading Romans *backwards* through **Augustine, Luther, Calvin** and Reformation legal/penal categories; BEMA's method is to read *forward* through Sessions 1–3 and the primacy of Christ.
- Note: Romans is about **justification**, not **atonement** — Marty stresses the atoning work of the cross is treated elsewhere, not here.
