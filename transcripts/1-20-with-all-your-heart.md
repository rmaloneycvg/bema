### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Yam Suph** (יַם־סוּף) — literally the "Sea of Reeds," not "Red Sea." The Hebrew does not mean Red Sea; it refers to a reedy, marshy body of water on the way out of Egypt.
- **yada** (יָדַע) — to know experientially and intimately, not merely to know data. God tests Israel "to *know* what was in their heart" — an experiential knowing (as in "Adam *knew* his wife Eve, and she conceived").
- **Marah** (מָרָה) — "bitter," the name of the bitter well. The same root also carries the sense of "rebellious" — a play on words: will this be a bitter, rebellious people?
- **lev / levav** (לֵב / לֵבָב) — the heart, understood in the Hebraic mind as the seat of the will and volition — the place from which choice comes. The first test is a test of the heart: what will you choose?
- **Adonai yimloch l'olam va'ed** (יְהוָה יִמְלֹךְ לְעֹלָם וָעֶד) — "The LORD reigns forever and ever," sung at the sea; the first time Israel directly calls God *Adonai* with their own mouths.
