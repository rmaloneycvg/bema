### Hebrew Words Explained

- **zakar** (זָכַר) — to remember. It appears 14 times across Deuteronomy — "Remember that you were slaves in Egypt... that is why I command you to do this." Remembering is active and communal (ask your elders, your parents), not merely a private mental act.
- **shakach** (שָׁכַח) — to forget. "Do not forget" appears 9 times in Deuteronomy — including the striking note that God himself will *not* forget his covenant.
- The **foreigner, orphan, and widow** (the "AOW" / alien, orphan, widow; גֵּר, יָתוֹם, אַלְמָנָה) — the groups Israel is to remember *because* they were once foreigners, orphans, and widows in Egypt. Remembering who you were lets you see and care for who they are now.
- **suzerain-vassal covenant** — the ancient treaty form Deuteronomy follows: preamble, historical summary, stipulations, blessings-and-curses (light on reward, heavy on punishment), and a concluding song to aid memory. Torah as a whole mirrors this shape.
