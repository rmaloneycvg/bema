### Hebrew Words Explained

- **to "fulfill" vs. "abolish" Torah** — rabbinic idioms. To *fulfill* means to interpret Torah correctly *and* walk it out rightly with your life. To *abolish* is to interpret it wrongly, or to interpret rightly but fail to live it. Jesus isn't "beating the game" or ending Torah — he's showing how it was always meant to be read.
- **mitzvah / mitzvot** (מִצְוָה / מִצְוֹת) — commandment(s)/good deeds; not a 613-level video game to "beat."
- **halach** (הָלַךְ) — one's "walk": interpretation must be matched by behavior.
- **Ketuvim** (כְּתוּבִים) — the Writings. Jesus names only "the Law and the Prophets" here because the Writings weren't yet canonized; later (Luke 24:44) he adds "the Psalms."
- **s'micha** (סְמִיכָה) — rabbinic authority (literally "laying on of hands"), required to say "you have heard it said... but I say to you." It was conferred by two rabbis who already had it. Jesus receives it at his baptism — from John the Baptist (rogue/natural s'micha) and from the voice of God — and keeps all of it (the Great Commission: "all authority has been given to me").
