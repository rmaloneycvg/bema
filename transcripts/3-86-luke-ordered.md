### Hebrew Words Explained

- **proselyte** — a Gentile convert to Judaism; once circumcised, as fully Jewish as a born Jew. The hosts argue Luke is not a "Gentile author" but a Jewish proselyte (likely a former Asclepion-trained physician), so his Gospel is deeply Jewish.
- **Theophilus** (Θεόφιλος, Greek) — "friend of God"; the named recipient of Luke, possibly a Herodian Jew, a proselyte, or even a stand-in for "anyone who is a friend of God" (the church).
- **hazan / hazzanim** (חַזָּן) — synagogue custodian(s) responsible for *keeping* the text, including scrolls a village didn't physically own. Luke's "servants of the word" (Luke 1:2) renders the same Greek used for the hazzanim — he consulted them.
- **"ordered" / sequenced** — Luke 1:3's word is not "orderly/chronological" but *ordered/sequenced*; Goulder's theory: Luke composed a Gospel to be read as a companion to the weekly synagogue readings.
- **parashah** (פָּרָשָׁה) — the weekly Torah portion (whole Torah read yearly).
- **haftarah** (הַפְטָרָה) — the paired reading from the rest of the Tanakh (cycled over ~three years).
- **Meshiach / second Moshe** — one of Luke's themes: Jesus as the promised prophet "like Moses" (Deuteronomy 18) that second-temple Judaism awaited.
