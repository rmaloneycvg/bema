### Hebrew Words Explained

- **toldot** (תולדות) — "generations / account / legacy"; "this is the account of Jacob" frames the dysfunction (split family dynamics, Joseph only with Bilhah's and Zilpah's sons) as *Jacob's* unresolved legacy.
- **"bad report"** — in Hebrew more than tattling: Joseph brings "evil, creeping, whispered rumors" — more intent and darkness than the English suggests.
- **ketonet passim** (כתנת פסים) — the "ornate robe": a long-sleeved undergarment reaching to the palms, a leisure garment (not work clothes). The point is it is a *second* coat — a bechor marker.
- **bechor** (בכור) — "firstborn / chosen"; Joseph is the *bechor* of Rachel, so Jacob is bypassing Leah's firstborn — layers of family drama.
- **Tamar** (תמר) — "palm tree" (the upright Middle-Eastern palm) — a name evoking uprightness/righteousness; women are often named for trees (cf. Hadassah/Esther = myrtle).
- **yibbum** (יבום) — levirate marriage (Deuteronomy 25); the brother-in-law's duty to raise up offspring for a dead brother and keep the widow in the *beit av*.
- **nakar** (נכר) — "to scrutinize / recognize" (noun: outsider); the key word linking the Joseph and Judah/Tamar stories ("identify/recognize these" — the bloody tunic and the seal/cord/staff) and tracing the family's cycle of deception (Jacob/Isaac, Jacob/Laban, the brothers/Jacob, Judah/Tamar).
- **saris** (סריס) — "eunuch / castrated official"; Potiphar is a *saris*, which recontextualizes his wife's situation.
