### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Mount Hermon** — means "holy mountain"; Marty argues it (behind Caesarea Philippi), not Mount Tabor, is the Mount of Transfiguration. The seven shared details with Sinai (high mountain, three companions, cloud, glory, changed appearance, glory that *settles* — **shakan** — after six days, God speaks) make the story a replay of Moses at Sinai.
- **shakan** — to settle/dwell; the root of **mishkan** (tabernacle). Peter wanting to build **tabernacles** (sukkot) is the logical "next step" after Sinai, not a blunder.
- **Tanakh's three parts** — **Torah** (Moses), **Nevi'im** (Prophets), **Ketuvim** (Writings). A midrash held that all three would testify to Messiah; God's words at the Transfiguration quote all three: Psalm 2 (Writings), Isaiah 42 (Prophets), Deuteronomy 18 (Torah).
- **exodus** — in Luke, Moses and Elijah speak with Jesus about his "exodus."
- **light and truth** (Psalm 43) — a midrash on Psalm 42–43 identifies truth = Moses and Elijah, light = Messiah (Isaiah 42); the only non-Gospel midrash placing all three together, and it points to God's "holy mountain" where he dwells (shakan).
- **the living God** (Deuteronomy 5) — hearing God's voice from the fire and surviving; mirrors the Transfiguration.
- **a rabbi pays his talmid's taxes** — the rabbi–disciple bond; Jesus pays only two temple taxes (his and Peter's), implying the other disciples were under the tax age (teenagers).
- **chutzpah** — Elijah's fiery passion for truth.
