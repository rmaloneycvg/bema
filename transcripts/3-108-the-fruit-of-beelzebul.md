### Hebrew Words Explained

- **Beelzebul / Beelzebub** — from the Hebrew **Baal** ("lord") plus **zebub** ("flies") = "lord of the flies," or **zebul** ("heavens/exalted") = "lord of the heavens." In Philistine mythology Baal shooed away flies (sickness). The Pharisees accuse Jesus of driving out demons by this pagan power.
- **P'rushim** — the Pharisees.
- **blasphemeo** (Greek) — to speak ill of / slander / tear down; the "blasphemy against the Holy Spirit" is bearing counter-testimony against the clear work of God (the anti-story), not an unpardonable one-time act.
- **the finger / Spirit of God** — Luke says "finger," Matthew "Spirit": "if I drive out demons by the Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God has come upon you."
- **the anti-story** — God's people working against what God is doing.
- **Son of Man** — "three days and three nights... in the heart of the earth" (sign of Jonah).
- **beit av** — "house of the father" / household; Jesus points to a truer family ("whoever does the will of my Father").
- **remez / drash / p'shat** — levels of interpretation invoked around "three days and three nights" and the seven returning spirits.
