## Book of Bible

Proverbs 2:21-22; 10:3; 20:21; 15:22; 19:21; 26:4-5; 3:5-6; Ecclesiastes 7:13-14; 7:15; 7:1-4

### Proverbs 2:21-22 (NET)

**21** For the upright will reside in the land, and those with integrity will remain in it, **22** but the wicked will be removed from the land, and the treacherous will be torn away from it.

### Proverbs 10:3 (NET)

**3** The LORD satisfies the appetite of the righteous, but he thwarts the craving of the wicked.

### Proverbs 20:21 (NET)

**21** An inheritance gained easily in the beginning will not be blessed in the end.

### Proverbs 15:22 (NET)

**22** Plans fail when there is no counsel, but with abundant advisers they are established.

### Proverbs 19:21 (NET)

**21** There are many plans in a person’s mind, but it is the counsel of the LORD which will stand.

### Proverbs 26:4-5 (NET)

**4** Do not answer a fool according to his folly, lest you yourself also be like him. **5** Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own estimation.

### Proverbs 3:5-6 (NET)

**5** Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not rely on your own understanding. **6** Acknowledge him in all your ways, and he will make your paths straight.

### Ecclesiastes 7:13-14 (NET)

**13** Consider the work of God: For who can make straight what he has bent? **14** In times of prosperity be joyful, but in times of adversity consider this: God has made one as well as the other, so that no one can discover what the future holds.

### Ecclesiastes 7:15 (NET)

**15** During the days of my fleeting life I have seen both of these things: Sometimes a righteous person dies prematurely in spite of his righteousness, and sometimes a wicked person lives long in spite of his evil deeds.

### Ecclesiastes 7:1-4 (NET)

**1** A good reputation is better than precious perfume; likewise, the day of one’s death is better than the day of one’s birth. **2** It is better to go to a funeral than a feast. For death is the destiny of every person, and the living should take this to heart. **3** Sorrow is better than laughter, because sober reflection is good for the heart. **4** The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of fools is in the house of merrymaking.

### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the quotidian** — wisdom literature shifts from the grand, communal, systemic narrative (Torah/Samuel/Kings) to the *quotidian*: the daily, humdrum stuff — work, family, money, speech, emotions, dealing with people who annoy you. Proverbs says God cares about “the family caravan” along the journey; how you handle work/conflict/time/money is as much “spirituality” as prayer or worship.
- **Proverbs = wise sayings that are *generally* true** — the key word is *generally*. Reliable baseline cause-and-effect (not karma, but the ordered way God set things up), yet not *always* true (the righteous *sometimes* go hungry; a good person is sometimes poor).
- **“not what is true, but *when* it's true”** (Rachel Held Evans) — wisdom is dynamic/lively, not a static catalog. Classic case: Prov 26:4 (“do NOT answer a fool by his folly, or you'll be like him”) vs. 26:5 (“ANSWER a fool by his folly, lest he be wise in his own eyes”) — back-to-back. Which applies depends on *context* (who is this “dolt,” your history, the stakes). Also Prov 15:22 (“many advisors → plans succeed”) vs. 19:21 (“many are the plans … but the Lord's counsel prevails”).
- **wise vs. foolish, not just right vs. wrong** — Reed reframes students' “is it a sin?” questions as “is it *wise* or *foolish*?” — which opens up the surrounding context.
- **wisdom personified as a woman / a dance** — in Proverbs, Wisdom is a *woman* calling out, not a crystal ball or dusty tome. Wisdom is like a *dance* (or a soccer formation): the structure/rules are generally true, but mastery frees you to improvise and *live well*, not just “by the book.”
- **Kohelet / Ecclesiastes = “post-wisdom”** — “wisdom after wisdom”: having learned the baseline logic, you've lived long enough to see it *not* play out. Juxtaposes Proverbs' confidence (Prov 3:5-6, “he will make your paths straight”) with Eccl 7:13-14 (“who can straighten what God has made crooked? … you cannot discover anything about your future”), and Prov 2:21-22 (“upright live in the land”) with Eccl 7:15 (“the righteous perishing in their righteousness, the wicked living long in their wickedness”).
- **hevel** (Hebrew הבל, “meaningless / vapor / smoke / mist”) — Ecclesiastes' refrain. Not “nothing matters” but *smoke*: obscuring, impermanent, un-graspable (you can't catch it in your hand). If you think you can explain the meaning of everything, you're going the wrong way.
- **“better the house of mourning”** (Eccl 7:1-4) and **a good name** — wisdom comes from diving *into* the places where things fall apart and don't make sense, not avoiding them. The goal is not pleasure-as-highest-aim but **satisfaction** and **meaning** — which only come *through* difficulty (the “85-year-old on the porch” thought experiment: teleported there, it would mean nothing; the meaning is in the costly journey).
- **holding two opposites at once** — wisdom = holding grief *and* hope, despair *and* hope, trusting God to “make paths straight” *and* accepting the crooked — “living in the tension.”
