### Hebrew Words Explained

- **the three recurring themes** — Jude (like 2 Peter) attacks *false teachers* in groups and themes of **three**. The three themes, repeated throughout: (1) **idolatry**, (2) **sexual immorality**, (3) an underlying **lust for wealth / power / prestige / influence** (plus rebellion against authority). These describe compromise with **Hellenism** / the Roman **guild-feast** culture.
- **orthopraxy, not orthodoxy** — the “false teaching” is not doctrinal heresy but a *practice* heresy: the teachers (hungry for advancement) encourage God's people to engage pagan Greco-Roman culture, leading to idolatry and immorality. “A practice heresy.”
- **three triads of examples** — (a) **the Exodus/golden-calf idolaters, the fallen angels, Sodom and Gomorrah** (vv.5-7); (b) **Cain, Balaam, Korah** (v.11); each illustrates the themes. **Cain** (Hebrew *qayin*, “to acquire” — per Fohrman, Cain kills Abel over *acquiring*) = wealth; **Balaam** (per Midrash, told Balak to send Moabite women to seduce Israel — Numbers 25 follows his oracles) = idolatry + immorality; **Korah** = rebellion against authority.
- **Jude is full of Midrash / Pseudepigrapha** — Jude *quotes* **1 Enoch** (“Enoch, the seventh from Adam, prophesied …”, vv.14-15) and alludes to the **Testament/Assumption of Moses** (Michael disputing with the devil over Moses's body, v.9). These are *Pseudepigrapha* (non-canonical) Jewish Midrash. Marty's point: Jude — inspired, canonical — freely uses the Midrash, which answers both the “why study Midrash?” critics and the “why ignore the Apocrypha?” questioners.
- **the Nephilim / sons of God** (Genesis 6) — skipped in Session 1 to protect the metanarrative, they resurface here via 1 Enoch: fallen angels who sought to “advance their station” through disobedience (with parallels to Greek mythology).
- **sarx** (“flesh / animal appetites”) — “rational animals … following mere natural instinct” (vv.10, 19): humans *have* instincts but, unlike beasts, can rationally overcome them — the false teachers don't.
- **Sodom and Gomorrah** — Jude is the *only* biblical text linking Sodom directly to *sexual immorality*; the other ~19 OT references tie it to pride, wealth, and failing the poor (e.g., **Ezekiel 16:49**). Jude bridges that gap because of his *cultural* context, not the hermeneutical context of Scripture.
- **Jude “brother of James”** — opening self-identification, tying him to the Jerusalem/James circle.

## Other Book References and Recommendations

- **1 Enoch (the Book of Enoch)** — a non-canonical *Pseudepigrapha* that is Jewish *Midrash*; quoted directly in Jude 14-15 (NIV footnote dates it ~1st c. BC). Links suggested: the Wikipedia “Book of Enoch” article and a full-text site (“Academy of Ancient Texts” — Brent disclaims knowing the rest of that site).
- **The Testament of Moses** (a.k.a. **The Assumption of Moses**, ~1st c. AD) — the Midrash behind Jude 9 (Michael vs. the devil over Moses's body); NIV footnotes it.
- **Rabbi David Fohrman** — on *Cain* / *qayin* (“to acquire”) as the key to the Cain-and-Abel story (acquiring; Abel as a threat to Cain's gain).
- **The BibleProject** — Marty mentions wanting to check their material on Jude (hasn't yet).
- Canon note: Jude (with 2–3 John) was among the last books admitted to the canon — more in Session 5. Marty reaffirms “I like our canon” (his one reservation remains **2 Thessalonians**).
- Session callbacks: **Hellenism** and **guild feasts** (1 Corinthians, ep 156); **2 Peter** (ep 169), nearly identical in content and structure; the Genesis **Nephilim** (Session 1).
