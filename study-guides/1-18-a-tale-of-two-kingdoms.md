# A Tale of Two Kingdoms

BEMA 18 · Session 1
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/1-18-a-tale-of-two-kingdoms.md`
Book: Exodus / Genesis (context episode — Genesis 47 and Exodus 19; a walkthrough of three Ray Vander Laan "That the World May Know," Vol. 8 — *God Heard Their Cry* — video lessons)
Co-host: Brent Billings · Host: Marty Solomon · Guest: Chris Marshall (talmid)

> **Session 1 overarching arc: partnership.** This is the hinge episode where the *narrative of God* begins. Having finished Genesis (preface + the family-of-God introduction), the hosts pause to set a macro frame before Exodus: the whole story — "from Exodus all the way through Revelation, and into church history, and all the way to today" (Marty) — is **a tale of two kingdoms**: a kingdom of **shalom** (order spoken by a God intimately connected to creation, built on *trust*) versus a kingdom of **empire** (order maintained by Pharaoh in the cartouche, built on *fear*). Egypt is the first metaphor for empire. The partnership arc surfaces precisely here: once God's people have been seduced by empire, God's problem shifts from "how do I get my people out of Egypt?" to "how do I get Egypt out of my people?" — and God answers it by looking for a **partner** who "hears the cry," then by inviting the whole people to *choose* which narrative they will trust (the Passover goat). Every day, "you and I get to choose which narrative we buy into" (Marty).

## Key Lessons

- **There are two competing creation narratives, and they produce two opposite worlds.** Biblical order is spoken by a God both inside and outside creation; Egyptian order is *maintained* by Pharaoh or the vault collapses. Marty: *"There's a narrative built on fear and there's a narrative built on trust. These are these two competing narratives."* This is the frame the entire remaining story runs on.
- **Egypt is a metaphor, not merely a place — "empire" that recurs in every era.** Marty: *"Egypt will become our first metaphor for this idea of empire... The Exodus story is so true, no matter which era you look at."* Pharaoh is deliberately unnamed because he represents the *system*. Brent: *"The story of pharaoh in the exodus is the story of the entire Egyptian system. It's not about the individual, it doesn't matter."*
- **Israel didn't start out making bricks in misery — they started out with the best farmland on earth, and bought into empire anyway.** The land of Goshen was luscious, waist-deep barley; the real tragedy is that they "gave into the Egyptian narrative" (Marty, citing Joshua/Ezekiel's "put away the gods you worshiped in Egypt"). Empire looked like a comfortable middle-class life — until it turned and crushed them.
- **God's new problem is internal: "how do I get Egypt out of my people?"** Marty: *"it's not going to be 'how do I get my people out of Egypt?' God has this new question... how do I get Egypt out of my people?"* God's method is sobering: *"If you really want empire, I will give you empire until that empire turns around and crushes you."* Empire only ever leads one direction.
- **God works by seeking a partner who "hears the cry" — heart first, methods later.** Marty: *"God needs a partner... somebody who's going to help Him... he finds somebody who has His heart. He may not have His methods quite yet."* Moses has the heart (he sees injustice) but not the method (murder); he can be shaped — forty desert years to learn new methods.
- **The call is not just to carry the message but to *be* the message — a whole kingdom of priests.** Marty: *"He's not just looking for Moses to go give the message, He's looking for Moses to go be the message... a kingdom of people that can show the world what God is like."* Brent ties Exodus 19 to this: God moves from Pharaoh as the sole mediator to *all* his people representing him.
- **The plagues are a war of narratives — God against the gods of empire — ending in a forced, trusting choice.** Marty: God "is going to war against the Egyptian gods... another way we could say this is God is going to war against the narrative of empire." The tenth plague demands buy-in: slaughter a goat (symbol of Amun-Ra) and paint the blood on the doorpost — a public declaration of which story you trust.
- **You cannot hold both kingdoms at once.** Marty: *"Empire and shalom are built on fundamentally different principles, and Jesus will tell us we can't serve both... both God and Mammon."* The "Godly empire" compromise is an illusion; the choice is real and daily.

## East/West Lens

- **Order as *relationship/presence* (Hebraic) vs. order *maintained by a strongman* (Egyptian/empire).** The biblical God speaks order and remains "intimately connected, both inside and outside this world" (Marty); the Egyptian system places Pharaoh in the cartouche doing the work "or else the world would come crashing down." Two cosmologies, two postures of the heart: trust vs. fear.
- **Knowing as experiential, not cerebral (*yada*).** The plagues aim that Israel, the Egyptians, *and* Pharaoh will *yada* — "experience" — that he is the LORD. Chris: *"To know experientially... to know its deepest most known truths."* Marty: *"It's not just a cerebral knowing... but it's an experiential knowing."* An Eastern category of knowledge-by-encounter.
- **God named by concrete action-in-relationship, not abstraction — "a God who hears the cry."** Chris notes Israel "never cried out specifically to Adonai. They just cried out and God still heard them." Marty: *"This is not a God that's waiting for you to get the formula right or the incantation correct or the magical spell worked out."* Hebraic covenant faithfulness over Western ritual-mechanics.
- **Metaphor and word-picture carry the theology (Egypt, the cartouche, the stick).** The hosts read Egypt "not just in its academic history, not just in its physicality, but... as a metaphor" (Marty). The shepherd's-crook/stick is a concrete, visual sign of coercive power — meaning rides on the image, not a propositional definition.
- **Communal/missional identity over individualized salvation (a *kingdom* of priests).** Salvation is corporate vocation: the whole people become God's representatives (Exodus 19), not a collection of privately-rescued individuals. Brent: God moves from the few who "can go" into the sanctuary to "I want my entire people to represent me."
- **Reading the Text in its own ancient world (archaeology, Egyptian mythology, the Ibiru).** The lens leans on Ray Vander Laan's visuals, Egyptian creation myth (Ra/Amun-Ra, the barge on the watery chaos), the Ibiru work records, and metallurgy — truth excavated in its own setting rather than flattened into a modern frame.
- **Avoiding presentism while still letting the text indict the present.** Marty is emphatic the lesson is not partisan ("I taught this exact same lesson in the last administration") — the ancient narrative is read on its own terms, *then* allowed to interrogate "which story we've bought into" today.

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Partnership (Session 1 arc) — God seeks a human partner who "hears the cry."** The arc's clearest statement in Exodus's opening: Marty corrects his own phrasing from "a leader" to *"God needs a partner."* God starts with Moses (heart before method) in order to make a whole people who can "be the message." The episode frames the entire covenant project as God refusing to act alone.
- **Trust the story vs. the narrative of fear (empire vs. shalom).** The anchor motif of the episode and the named spine of all the teaching to come: *"we find ourselves in a tale of two kingdoms. There's a kingdom of shalom and there's a kingdom of empire and every day you and I get to choose which narrative we buy into"* (Marty). Directly extends the Genesis "trust the story" through-line into the national narrative.
- **Shalom — order, the outsider brought in, everything in its proper place.** Marty's test for which narrative you've bought into: *"there should be the outsider being brought in, there should be everything being put back in its proper place, that would be shalom"* — vs. empire's expendable "poor, the refugee, the alien, the orphan, the widow, the elderly."
- **"When life is cheap, you have the wrong story."** The cost-of-empire motif: Brent (quoting Ray) — *"if you see a culture where life is cheap, and sacrifice of others is acceptable to sustain your own obsession with pleasure... you have the wrong story."* Dramatized by the slaves dying "like flies" at the 600–800° furnaces.
- **God hears the cry (covenant memory).** Explicit callback-thread: this is "the same God we encountered in Genesis... a God who would remember the covenant, the rainbow, Noah. The God who had walked the blood path" (Marty). Points directly back to 1-4 (bow in the clouds) and 1-10 (the blood path), and forward to 1-17's title.
- **Choosing the narrative / costly buy-in (the Passover goat).** The arc's recurring demand that trust become a public, risky act: painting Amun-Ra's blood on the doorpost is "God saying, 'I want you to trust the story. It's time for you to step out, it's time for you to tell me where you stand.'"
- **Be the message, not just bring it (kingdom of priests).** Vocation-as-witness: a motif that will run forward through Sinai and beyond — "a kingdom of Moseses" who "show the world what God is like."

## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

A sequential walkthrough of every teaching beat, in order. Used to verify full coverage.

1. **Welcome & "talmid" defined.** Brent introduces guest Chris Marshall; Marty defines *talmidim* = "disciples," *talmid* singular — "Talmid Chris."
2. **Review of Genesis & last week.** Marty recaps the preface (Genesis 1–11, "trust the story"), the family-of-God introduction (Avram, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph; Judah's cycle-breaking confession), and the pause episode on the "God of wrath" / insecurity before entering Exodus.
3. **Framing the macro move: Egypt as metaphor.** The plan to "take a macro look at the story" and read Egypt as the first metaphor for *empire* vs. *shalom*; the DVD methodology explained (Ray Vander Laan, *That the World May Know* Vol. 8, *God Heard Their Cry*, lessons 1–3; copyright-honored, not played).
4. **Video 1 — "How Big Is Our God?": two creation narratives.** Egyptian creation myth: Ra hovering/accidentally creating over a murky swamp (Chris), paralleling — and contrasting — God hovering over the chaotic waters but creating *intentionally*. The cartouche: the god pictured *within* the space you also occupy (Brent).
5. **Pharaoh as priest-king who maintains the vault.** Pharaoh is the chief priest, the human link to the gods, who "does the work" to keep the cartouche/vault of order against the watery chaos above and below. Fear-order vs. trust-order named.
6. **Pharaoh deliberately unnamed = the system.** Chris: the Bible doesn't say *which* Pharaoh; "each pharaoh is represented by the character of Pharaoh." Brent: it's "the story of the entire Egyptian system."
7. **Video 1's closing question — "Have we bought into the wrong story?"** Marty's extended non-partisan disclaimer (taught identically under Obama and Trump); the diagnostic: shalom brings the outsider in and puts things in their proper place; the wrong story makes the poor/refugee/alien/orphan/widow/elderly expendable. Brent quotes Ray: "if... life is cheap... you have the wrong story." The two kingdoms are named **empire** and **shalom** — the spine "from Exodus all the way through Revelation."
8. **Video 2 — "Israel in Bondage: God Heard Their Cry": the land of Goshen.** The lush, waist-deep barley; Ray's two-step from green harvest into "absolutely barren desert." *National Geographic* topsoil study — Goshen/Nile Delta ~100 feet of topsoil (vs. ~6 feet in the U.S. Great Plains/Northwest). Israel's nomadism made the flood-cycle workable; Genesis 47 cited.
9. **Chris's "one foot in barley, one foot in desert" image.** A priest of the Nile asking "which God brings you order?" — a near-unanswerable empire narrative when you can *see* the straight line between harvest and wasteland.
10. **Israel bought into the narrative.** Joshua/Ezekiel: "put away the gods you worshiped in Egypt"; they "gave in to the Egyptian narrative." The comfortable middle-class life (Ibiru work records: 10-day week with a weekend, three-room apartments, provided food/water) — empire as seduction.
11. **The cost of empire: metallurgy and expendable lives.** Egypt's leading iron/metal technology (bellows → hotter fire → stronger metal; the Iron/Bronze Age framing — "metal was the oil of their day"). Tools for the Israelite farmers, built at furnaces of 600–800° where "slaves would just drop like flies."
12. **The turn: a Pharaoh who "knew not Joseph," and the stick.** Empire turns on its beneficiaries. Pharaoh pictured over and over holding the shepherd's-crook/stick — held *over* people (Brent), a temple scene (Karnak?) of captives held by the hair under the stick of coercion.
13. **God goes looking for a partner.** Marty's correction: not just "a leader" — "God needs a partner." Moses has God's heart (sees injustice) but not the method (murder); he can be shaped (40 desert years). Ray: "be people that hear the cry."
14. **Chris's comfort note & Marty on the God of Genesis.** Israel "never cried out specifically to Adonai... God still heard them." Marty: the same covenant-remembering God (rainbow/Noah, the blood path) — "when the cry goes up, He hears it"; good news to the crying, warning to the cry-causer.
15. **Brent's Exodus 19 move — from Pharaoh-mediator to a kingdom of priests.** "I have made you like God to pharaoh" → "I will make you a kingdom of priests." Marty: the missional point — go *be* the message, "a kingdom of Moseses."
16. **Video 3 — "Finger of God: The Plagues": a competition of the gods.** Ray frames the plagues as the gods of Egypt (in Pharaoh) vs. the creator God. The sticks-to-snakes sign; the Text says Moses's *stick* (not snake) swallowed the magicians' sticks — "make no mistake... this is about sticks" (the crook of empire vs. the shepherd's staff of God).
17. **The plagues attack the hierarchy of deities + *yada*.** God knocks out Egyptian gods "like bowling pins" from the bottom; the goal that Israel, the Egyptians, *and* Pharaoh will all *yada* (experientially know) that he is the LORD → therefore God wars against *empire itself*.
18. **Plague 10 and the forced choice (Passover goat).** Israel has only watched so far; now God demands buy-in — slaughter a goat/lamb (Amun-Ra's animal) and paint the blood on the doorpost: a public declaration against Egypt's chief god. "They get an opportunity to choose which narrative."
19. **Closing: you cannot serve both kingdoms.** Empire and shalom run on fundamentally different principles; no "Godly empire" compromise ("God and Mammon"). Marty's provocation to compare two inauguration speeches and ask: cartouche-and-strongman, or the way of Jesus? What does our security/comfort/leisure cost?
20. **Sign-off.** Socials; the "Chris-tofu" running gag.

## Terminology

- **talmid / talmidim** (תלמיד / תלמידים) — "disciple(s)," singular/plural; a learner who follows a rabbi. Chris is introduced as a *talmid*. Marty: *"Talmidim means disciples, plural. Talmid, singular. Chris is a talmid."*
- **tohu va'vohu** (תהו ובהו) — "chaos / formless void"; the pre-creation state out of which God "spoke order... and He called this order good." Marty: *"out of what we call tohu va-vohu in the Hebrew. Out of chaos God spoke order."* The biblical order-from-chaos contrasted with the Egyptian narrative.
- **yada** (ידע) — "to know" experientially and intimately, not merely cerebrally; the aim of the plagues is that Israel, the Egyptians, and Pharaoh will all *yada* that he is the LORD. Chris: *"To know experientially... to know its deepest most known truths."* Marty: *"it's not just a cerebral knowing... but it's an experiential knowing."*
- **mammon** — "wealth" personified as a (later) rival god; Jesus' "you cannot serve both God and mammon" is read as "you cannot serve both empire and shalom." Marty: *"both God and Mammon — mammon being a much later god in the story. You can't have both."*
- **cartouche / "the vault"** — the Egyptian image of ordered space in which the god is pictured, bounded above and below by watery chaos; Pharaoh does the work to keep it in order. Brent: *"in that cartouche imagery their god is actually within the space that you are also in."* Marty: Pharaoh "sits in this vault of order and maintains that order because of the work that he does."
- **Goshen / the Nile Delta** — the lush, waist-deep-barley farmland where Israel settled (Genesis 47); "the best topsoil on the face of planet Earth" (~100 feet). Marty: *"the place where the Israelites were asked to settle was the best farmland on planet Earth."*
- **the stick / shepherd's-crook (of Pharaoh)** — the recurring hieroglyphic symbol of Pharaoh's coercive power, held *over* people; set against the true shepherd's staff of God. Chris: *"He's holding a shepherd's crook."* Brent: *"He's usually holding it over people."* Marty: "the threatening stick of power, the threatening stick of empire."
- **Ra / Amun-Ra** — the Egyptian creator/chief god; Ra "hovering over a murky swamp" in the creation myth; Amun-Ra's animal is the ram/lamb/goat, which is why the Passover animal is a direct affront. Marty: *"The chief god of the Egyptian system symbolized by a ram or a lamb or a goat."*
- **Ibiru** (he-be-roo) — a people-group named in Egyptian records (possibly cognate to "Hebrew") who did seasonal labor for Pharaoh under comfortable middle-class conditions. Marty: *"We found lots of Egyptian records that talk about the Ibiru... 'Boy, that sounds like an awful lot like "Hebrew."'"*
- **empire vs. shalom** — the two named "kingdoms": empire = order maintained by Pharaoh, built on fear, with expendable people; shalom = order spoken by God, built on trust, the outsider brought in and everything in its proper place. Marty: *"There's a kingdom of shalom and there's a kingdom of empire."*

## Illustrations & Stories

- **The cartouche with the god pictured *inside* the space (Brent).** Unlike gods who are "out there," the Egyptian god is depicted within the ordered vault "that you are also in" — a visual for an order that must be actively maintained from the inside. *"in that cartouche imagery their god is actually within the space that you are also in."*
- **Ray's two steps from barley into barren desert (Marty).** The signature visual: he introduces the video standing in waist-deep green harvest, then "takes two steps and... you go from this amazing green background... into absolutely barren desert" — later a straight line where "on the left... waist-deep barley. On the right, there's not a green leaf at all."
- **"One foot in barley, one foot in the desert" (Chris).** The living metaphor of a Nile priest standing astride the line asking "which God brings you order?" — turned on the modern believer: we'd assume God is "over here in the lush wonderful barley fields," not the desert.
- **The *National Geographic* topsoil cutaways (Marty).** The U.S. Great Plains / volcanic Northwest ranked #2 and #3 at ~6 feet of topsoil; Goshen/Nile Delta so deep (~100 feet) "they couldn't even do the cutaway... It was too deep."
- **The furnaces where slaves "drop like flies" (Marty).** Egypt's metallurgical furnaces burning "somewhere between... 600–800 degrees," worked by Israelite laborers — the concrete cost of empire where "life is cheap and people are expendable."
- **Pharaoh holding captives by the hair under the stick (Marty).** A temple scene (possibly Karnak) with Pharaoh gripping "all these people groups and the nations by the hair," the "threatening stick of power" in hand — empire's coercion rendered in stone, "every time you turn the corner."
- **Metal as "the oil of their day" (Marty).** History classified by metal (bronze age, iron age) because "metal ruled the world"; Egypt's bellows-driven hotter fires gave it the leading technology — the economic engine of its empire.
- **Compare two inauguration speeches (Marty, closing provocation).** A homework image: pull up the last two U.S. inauguration speeches ("an equal opportunity offender") and ask whether you're hearing "a cartouche with a man in the middle saying, 'Don't you worry, we'll keep the vault in order'" — or the way of Jesus.

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty says we each "get to choose which narrative we buy into" *every day* — empire or shalom. Where in your ordinary week do you feel the pull of empire's "order built on fear," and what would a small, concrete act of shalom look like in that exact spot?
2. The hard reframe of this episode is that Israel's deepest problem wasn't being *in* Egypt but having Egypt *in them*. What is a "narrative of empire" you've internalized so thoroughly that getting out of the situation wouldn't actually free you — and how might God go about getting it "out of you"?
3. Ray's test: shalom brings the outsider in and puts things in their proper place; the wrong story makes "the poor, the refugee, the alien, the orphan, the widow, the elderly" expendable. Looking honestly at the culture you live in (and your own habits), which story do the evidence point to — and where specifically?
4. Marty draws the line hard: you can't build a "Godly empire"; shalom and empire run on fundamentally different principles. Where are you most tempted to believe you can have both — God's heart *and* empire's security, comfort, or control — and what does Jesus' "you cannot serve both God and Mammon" expose about that?
5. God looks for a *partner who hears the cry* — someone with his heart even before they have his methods (Moses had the heart but reached for murder). Where do you sense God's heart stirring in you toward some injustice, and what "40 years in the desert" of method-reshaping might he be inviting you into before you act?
6. The Passover goat was Amun-Ra's own animal — painting its blood on the doorpost was a public, costly declaration of allegiance. What would be *your* equivalent: a visible, risky act that tells the watching "Egyptians" around you which kingdom you've actually chosen?
