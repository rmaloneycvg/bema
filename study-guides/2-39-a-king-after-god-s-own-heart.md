# A King After God's Own Heart

BEMA 39 · Session 2
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/2-39-a-king-after-god-s-own-heart.md`
Book: 1 Samuel 17:1-54 (David & Goliath); with Judges 20:15-17 (Benjamin's left-handed slingers) and 1 Samuel 13:13-15 (Saul's rebuke / 600 men)
Hosts: Marty Solomon & Brent Billings

> This is a near-direct retelling of the Ray Vander Laan "Valley of Elah" lesson, read through the Hebraic lens. On the surface it's the Sunday-school David-and-Goliath story; underneath it is a verdict on the two kings of Session 2's opening. Saul — tall, impressive, from the tribe of elite slingers (Benjamin), equipped with the right résumé — sits in his tent for forty days of testing and does *nothing*. David — the overlooked eighth son, a shepherd boy from Judah who should have no business on that field — is simply *willing*, because he cares about God's reputation (*kiddush Hashem*) and knows where his help comes from. The Session 2 arc stage is right here: Israel has rejected God as king and demanded a human one; the partner is unfaithful, yet God *refuses to quit* — He keeps steering the story, raising up "a man after his own heart" to carry the mission (bless all nations, deal the death-blow to the serpent) that the nation keeps abandoning.

## Episode Flow

1. **Cold open / Marty's ambivalence** — the kings section ("Saul, David, Solomon") has "never been a strong suit"; "the prophets turn my crank more than David."
2. **Indebtedness to Ray Vander Laan + a Gladwell aside** — this lesson is "pretty much a direct lesson" from RVL in the Valley of Elah; Malcolm Gladwell's TED Talk flagged as a "secular, unbiblical" alternate reading.
3. **Geographic setup** — the Valley of Elah as one of four *shephelah* valleys "snaking" from Philistia toward Jerusalem; Philistines on one hill, Israel on the other.
4. **Champion warfare (*ish habeinayim*)** — winner-take-all duel instead of mass slaughter; Goliath "roughly about nine feet tall," makes "Brock Lesnar look teeny weeny."
5. **Morning-and-evening defiance** — Goliath comes out at the exact times of the daily sacrifice (shofar ~9am/3pm), insulting God during Israel's worship.
6. **David the errand boy** — the eighth son, so young his sisters "babysit" him with the flocks; sent with grain, bread, and cheese; "David *heard* it" (shema, not casual overhearing).
7. **The reward confusion + Eliab's anger** — the soldiers obsess over the king's reward; David is scandalized that no one acts; the *bechor* brother burns with jealousy.
8. **David before Saul / the lion and the bear** — "You're just a boy" vs. David's shepherd résumé; a "backhanded reference" to the donkey-herder Saul.
9. **Saul's armor and *nasah*** — David "not used to" (untested/unproven) the armor; Brent's first-mention catch: *nasah* = "God *tested* Abraham" (Genesis 22), "the Lord will provide."
10. **The duel and David's speech** — "the battle is the Lord's"; "the whole world will know there is a God in Israel"; the stone sinks into Goliath's forehead; David beheads him.
11. **666 + scales = the serpent** — six cubits, six hundred shekels, a six-fingered brother, "scale" armor; David deals the "death blow to the head" (Genesis 3:15).
12. **Judges 20 — Benjamin's 700 slingers** — an elite "Navy SEALs" sniper unit; Saul is from Benjamin, so *Saul* is the one who should be walking down the hill with a sling.
13. **1 Samuel 13 — Saul's 600** — "incomplete" (six without the seventh/rest); Saul relies on "his own strength," the *ar'ar* bush, "cursed is the one who trusts in his own strength."
14. **Conclusion 1 — "throw your stone"** — God can do nothing with a stone that stays in your pocket; be willing (David), not equipped-but-idle (Saul).
15. **"Israel without weapons" / every vocation is holy** — no blacksmith in Israel because the Philistines monopolized metal to *oppress*; be the best at your calling to *bless* — the Impact Campus Ministries vocational-mentorship appeal.
16. **Conclusion 2 — "a mess after God's own heart"** — David's blood-stained résumé (too bloody to build the temple), yet a heart for the oppressed/outsider (the Gibeonite insult → war); empire critique.
17. **Resources closeout** — Feiler, Bell/Golden, Brother Lawrence, Reed Dent (wisdom lit coming up).

## Key Lessons

- **Willingness beats equipment — "throw your stone."** The episode's headline lesson, and Marty's favorite line for college students: *"you've got to throw your stone. God can do nothing with a stone that stays in your pocket."* Saul "may have been the one that was supposed to march down the field with a sling... but never left his tent." David "is not logically the person who should be doing anything, but he's willing." In the Session 2 arc, Israel has a *king* now — the equipped, impressive one — and he does nothing; God's story advances through the one who is simply available.
- **God tests the heart, and Saul's forty days expose it.** The forty days aren't filler: *"40 is a time of testing... Not a pass-fail test as much as a — I want to know what's in your heart."* Saul "has had 40 days and has done nothing." Then: *"David, you're up."* The king Israel demanded "to fight our battles" (ep. 38) cannot even leave his tent — the unfaithfulness of the partnership laid bare.
- **The right kind of king cares about God's reputation, not his own safety — *kiddush Hashem*.** *"David is concerned about God's reputation. He's not concerned about his own safety... It's about who God is and how God's being represented to the world."* His battle cry: *"the whole world will know that there is a God in Israel... the battle is the Lord's."* Marty guards the nuance: not that *"God needs us to go protect his reputation,"* but that we *"steward what the world sees as God's representatives."*
- **This story is bigger than David and Goliath — it's the serpent getting its death-blow.** The 6-6-6-and-scales reading turns the duel into the Genesis 3:15 promise: *"he will deal a death blow to your head... How does David kill Goliath?"* — *"A death blow to the head."* Marty: *"this story is also about things beyond, more than just David and Goliath."* The one story is being carried forward: the offspring of the woman crushing the serpent.
- **Saul looks complete and alive but trusts his own strength — the *ar'ar* bush.** Saul's 600 (vs. the complete 700) is *"incomplete,"* and *"Saul's trying to do this on his own strength."* Marty reaches back to Session 1: *"Saul is the ar'ar bush. He looks really good on the outside... but it's got the empty, poisonous fruit"* — *"cursed is the man who relies on his own strength."* The world's impressive king is life-less; God's overlooked king is life-giving.
- **Every vocation is a holy, sacred calling — because the mission is to bless, not oppress.** From "Israel without weapons": the Philistines held the leading metal technology and used it *"not to bless all nations, but to oppress all nations."* Marty's charge: *"go be the best blacksmith so that you can do the blessing... every vocation is a part of bringing order, shalom, to chaos."* This re-grounds the Session 1 mission (bless all nations at the crossroads) exactly where Session 2's Israel is losing it.
- **David is "a mess after God's own heart" — honored for his heart for the bottom of the heap, not his record.** Brent names it: *"a mess after God's own heart."* Marty: too much blood to build the temple, *"adultery and some murder... pretty gross stuff on his resume"* — *and yet* *"David understands God's heart for the outsider... David is fighting for those who are being abused."* The arc stage holds the tension: God works through a flawed partner without pretending the flaws away.

## East/West Lens

- **Numbers (quality/symbol over quantity).** The whole climax turns on symbolic numbers, read exactly as the Hebraic lens teaches. Forty: *"40 is a time of testing."* Six: *"Six is that number of beastly sinfulness"*; Goliath is "six, six, six" and Saul's 600 is "incomplete" without the seventh. Brent even ties it to creation: *"After Day 6 of creation... it's not truly complete without the rest portion."*
- **Words (word-pictures/images over definitions).** The hosts read the text as layered imagery, not flat report: *"this story is about David and Goliath, but this story is also about things beyond."* Goliath's "scale" armor + 666 is a *picture* of the serpent; David's sling evokes Benjamin's slingers; the ar'ar bush is a desert word-picture carried in from Session 1.
- **Faith as relational experience / testimony over intellectual proof.** David's confidence is a remembered story, not a calculation: *"The Lord who rescued me from the paw of the lion and the paw of the bear will rescue me from the hand of this Philistine."* Marty: *"I know where my help comes from — he'll write a song about that later."* Faith here is testimony and relationship, not odds.
- **God's description (concrete relationship over abstract attributes).** David defends not an idea of God but God's felt presence and reputation among the nations — *"the whole world will know that there is a God in Israel"* — a relational, public, concrete concern (*kiddush Hashem*) rather than a doctrinal defense.
- **Truth over time (dynamic/unfolding).** Meaning is deferred and accretive: Brent's *nasah* catch links David's armor-refusal to Genesis 22 ("the Lord will provide"); Marty keeps pointing ahead — the Psalms/Proverbs *"we're not there yet,"* David's "mess" to be wrestled later. The story's sense grows across the arc rather than resolving in one episode.

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story / the one meta-narrative (the arc itself)** *(explicit).* The David-Goliath duel is explicitly folded into the single story — "about things beyond, more than just David and Goliath" — reaching back to Genesis 3:15 and forward to the Messiah. *Advances the arc:* it shows God still driving the one plot (crush the serpent, bless the nations) even while Israel's monarchy wobbles — unfaithful partner, unquitting God.
- **God chooses the last / not the firstborn** *(explicit).* David is "the eighth son," so young his sisters watch him; the overlooked runt, not the *bechor* Eliab, becomes God's champion. *Advances the arc:* the Session-1 pattern (Isaac, Jacob, Joseph) now becomes the standard for kingship itself — God's king comes from the bottom, against Saul the impressive one.
- **Sheep & shepherd** *(explicit).* David defends his readiness as a *shepherd* ("a lion or a bear came and carried off a sheep... I went after it"), and Marty contrasts it with Saul the donkey-herder. *Advances the arc:* the shepherd-king motif, inverted by the donkey-herder gag in ep. 38, is now vindicated — the true shepherd steps onto the field the hired/impressive king abandoned.
- **Empire vs. Shalom / blessing vs. oppression** *(explicit).* The "Israel without weapons" riff names it directly: the Philistines use the leading technology *"to oppress all nations,"* while God wanted Israel "at the crossroads to bless all nations." *Advances the arc:* it exposes the stakes of the king crisis — a kingdom modeled on the nations (empire, coercion) vs. the vocation to bless; David's cause is the oppressed, not conquest.
- **Bless all nations / kingdom of priests** *(explicit).* Brent states the mission plainly — *"He wanted them to be at the crossroads to bless all nations"* — and Marty extends it to every vocation as shalom-bringing. *Advances the arc:* the mission betrayed in ep. 38 (wanting to be "like the nations") is here re-preached as the measure of faithful kingship and ordinary life.
- **God's reputation / *kiddush Hashem*** *(explicit).* Promised at the end of ep. 38, delivered here as David's defining orientation: *"it's not his ego, it's God's reputation"* embodied in "the whole world will know there is a God in Israel." *Advances the arc:* it names the heart-difference between the two kings — the thing that makes David "after God's own heart."
- **Remember where you came from** *(implied).* David's power is that he knows his smallness and his story — "I know where my help comes from" — the shepherd boy who hasn't forgotten the lion, the bear, and the God who delivered him; set against Saul's self-reliance. *Advances the arc:* the Deuteronomic logic (the lowly remember) is shown as *why* God's king can be trusted where the impressive one cannot.

## Chiasms & Structure

No clearly-developed chiasm is presented in this episode. The hosts build symbolic contrasts (Saul/David, 600/700, equipped/willing, ar'ar/acacia) and intertextual echoes (Genesis 3:15; Judges 20; Genesis 22) rather than a mirrored center-weighted structure, so this section is omitted per SOP.

## Passages Walked Through

- **[1 Samuel 17:1-11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2017:1-11&version=NIV)** — The geographic/champion-warfare setup. *Point:* champion warfare (*ish habeinayim*) is winner-take-all, and Goliath's morning-and-evening defiance lands "the exact time where your morning and evening sacrifices are taking place" — he insults God during Israel's worship.
- **[1 Samuel 17:12-24](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2017:12-24&version=NIV)** — David the errand-boy arrives. *Point:* the eighth son, young enough that his sisters "babysit" him with the flocks, "heard" (shema) the defiance — a heeding, not casual overhearing.
- **[1 Samuel 17:25-30](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2017:25-30&version=NIV)** — The reward talk and Eliab's anger. *Point:* the army fixates on the king's reward while David is scandalized no one defends "the armies of the living God"; the *bechor* brother burns with jealousy.
- **[1 Samuel 17:31-37](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2017:31-37&version=NIV)** — David before Saul; the lion and the bear. *Point:* David's confidence is remembered testimony — "the Lord who rescued me... will rescue me" — and a backhanded jab at the shepherd-less Saul.
- **[1 Samuel 17:38-40](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2017:38-40&version=NIV)** — The armor refused. *Point:* "All I need essentially is me and God. God needs me to be me"; the *nasah*/"not tested" wording maybe echoes Genesis 22's "God tested Abraham."
- **[1 Samuel 17:41-54](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2017:41-54&version=NIV)** — The duel. *Point:* "the battle is the Lord's" and "the whole world will know there is a God in Israel" (*kiddush Hashem*); the stone to the forehead is the serpent's "death blow to the head."
- **[Genesis 3:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%203:15&version=NIV)** — The serpent curse. *Point:* 666 + scales marks Goliath as the serpent; David crushes the head while the serpent "strikes the heel" — the one story's enmity-promise.
- **[Judges 20:15-17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Judges%2020:15-17&version=NIV)** — Benjamin's 700 left-handed slingers. *Point:* an elite "special forces" unit "could sling a stone at a hair and not miss" — so Saul of Benjamin, not David, should have been the slinger.
- **[1 Samuel 13:13-15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2013:13-15&version=NIV)** — Samuel's rebuke; Saul's 600. *Point:* God "has sought out a man after his own heart"; Saul's "about six hundred" is "incomplete" — he trusts his own strength (the *ar'ar* bush).
- **[Genesis 22:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%2022:1&version=NIV)** — "God tested (*nasah*) Abraham." *Point:* Brent's first-mention link — David refusing untested armor may trust that "the Lord will provide."

## Illustrations & Stories

- **Goliath vs. Brock Lesnar** (Marty) — scaling Goliath's size for a modern audience: "this guy makes Brock Lesnar look teeny weeny," building on the Saul/Brock image from the prior episode.
- **The S-curve valley** (Marty) — the Valley of Elah pictured as an S snaking through the shephelah "from the land of the Philistines... towards Jerusalem," so the geography carries the stakes of encroachment.
- **Goliath's defiance timed to the daily sacrifice** (Marty) — "whenever the shofar sounds, you kind of fall into this prayerful reverence... and during your prayer time, Goliath is down in the valley shouting his defiance" — the insult aimed squarely at worship.
- **Sisters babysitting the shepherd boy** (Marty) — a vivid read of David's youth: "If you're taking care of the flocks with your older sisters, it's essentially like your sisters are babysitting you... you're eight, nine, maybe ten years old."
- **"Throw your stone"** (Marty) — the signature teaching image for graduates: "God will use a stone that you put in the air... if you stay back and you keep the stone in your pocket, God can't do anything with that particular stone."
- **The blacksmith as holy calling** (Marty) — "I don't think too many people wake up in the morning and go, you know what a holy sacred spiritual calling is? Blacksmithing" — used to argue every vocation (CPA, artist, stay-at-home parent) brings shalom to chaos.
- **The spreadsheet panic attack** (Marty) — a self-deprecating aside — "I see a spreadsheet and my eyes glaze over. I kind of have a panic attack" — to make the point that the body needs people with gifts he lacks (the mentorship network).
- **The naked-from-the-waist Gibeonite envoys** (Marty) — David's heart for the insulted outsider: when a neighbor humiliates his well-wishers ("they shave their beards, they cut off their robes... send them back naked from the waist down"), "David's like: Oh no, you didn't."
- **The *ar'ar* bush (Saul) vs. the acacia/rotem (David)** (Marty) — the Session-1 desert image reused: Saul "looks really good on the outside... but it's got the empty, poisonous fruit."

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty says Saul "may have had all the goods he needed, but never left his tent," while David was simply willing. Where in your life are you waiting to be "more educated, more experienced" before you "throw your stone" — and what might God do with the stone if you threw it now?
2. The forty days were a test of "what's in your heart." When you've faced a defiance you kept avoiding, what did your inaction reveal — and what would acting out of concern for "God's reputation" (rather than your own safety) have looked like?
3. David defends not his odds but God's name — "the whole world will know there is a God in Israel." How is *kiddush Hashem* (caring about God's reputation) different from the subtle trap of thinking "God needs us to protect him"?
4. The hosts read Goliath as the serpent and David's blow as Genesis 3:15. How does seeing David-and-Goliath as part of the *one story* (not an isolated hero tale) change what the victory means?
5. Marty insists "every vocation is a part of bringing order, shalom, to chaos" — even blacksmithing or accounting. What is your "blacksmithing," and are you using it to bless at the crossroads or (unintentionally) to oppress?
6. David is called "a mess after God's own heart." How do you hold together his real sin and his real heart for the outsider — and where does that tension show up in the leaders (or the self) you're tempted to either idolize or write off?
7. Saul is the *ar'ar* bush — impressive outside, "trusts his own strength," ultimately empty. Where are you tempted to rely on your own strength and look good doing it, instead of being the overlooked-but-rooted one who "knows where my help comes from"?
