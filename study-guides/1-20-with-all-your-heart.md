# With All Your Heart

BEMA 20 · Session 1
Source transcript: `transcripts/1-20-with-all-your-heart.md`

## Overview

Marty Solomon, Brent Billings, and returning guest Reed Dent walk through the crossing of the Sea of Reeds (Exodus 14–15) and the first of Israel's three desert tests on the way to Sinai — the **test of the heart** (Exodus 15–16). The episode reframes "testing" away from a Western pass/fail exam into an experiential, relational measurement, and reframes the grumbling Israelites away from caricature into people genuinely learning (and showing God) what is in their hearts. It sits squarely inside the **Session 1 arc of partnership**: God is not only getting Israel out of Egypt but getting Egypt out of Israel, forming a *bechor* people in the refining desert.

## Key Lessons

- **"Stand and watch" is a stage-appropriate command — faith grows into action.** At the sea, the lesson is passivity-before-God; later lessons will require Israel to move first. Marty: *"today, as they're learning about who this God is, God says, 'I just want you to watch'... The next time I'm going to ask you to put your faith in what you've seen. But this time, on this day, it's just stand and watch."* The very next verse undercuts any over-spiritualizing of stillness — Reed: *"it's literally the next verse where God is like, 'Why are you crying to me? Move. Tell them to go.'"* God meets people where they are and then grows them (truth/relationship unfolding over time).

- **The Kingdom of God comes when three things happen together: the finger of God moves, people call him Lord, and people respond in obedience.** Marty: *"The first one is that the finger of God has to move... The second thing... is that people have to call Him Lord... But there's a third piece, and that is that the people have to respond in obedience. And when all three of those things happen, you get the Kingdom of God."* Each piece alone is still a genuine taste of Kingdom — but not the fullness. This directly feeds the Session 1 partnership arc: God's rescue (finger) and Israel's worship (Lord) must mature into obedient response.

- **A biblical "test" is not pass/fail — it is a measurement, a chance to *show* and to *grow*.** Marty: *"We think of the test that a teacher puts down in front of us at school, rather than the test strips that I put in the hot tub to see where the water is."* And: *"every test is an opportunity to grow, and every test is also an opportunity to show. To show God and to grow in God."* Deuteronomy 8 says God tested Israel "to know" (*yada*) their heart — intimate, experiential knowing, not data collection.

- **The first test is a test of the *heart* — the Hebraic seat of the will and choice.** Marty: *"the heart... is the seat of the will. It's where your choice comes from. It's a place of volition... The test of the heart is what are you going to choose?"* At bitter Marah, at the one well, and in the daily manna, the single question is: *will you do what God invites you to do?*

- **God's provision runs on "every word," not on bread alone — and it rewards waiting on God's instruction over anxious self-provision.** Hoarded manna breeds maggots; Sabbath manna keeps. Marty, confessing he'd have failed it: *"I would have been one of those anxiety ridden worry warts... 'But what if it doesn't come tomorrow?'... And it would have been all full of maggots."* Deuteronomy 8:3: *"man does not live on bread alone, but on every word that comes from the mouth of the Lord."*

- **The real "miracle" of the manna is communal generosity, not magic math.** The rabbis read "the one who gathered much did not have too much" not as supernatural leveling but as the community caring for those who could not gather. Marty: *"everybody went out and gathered. Some people gathered more. Some people couldn't gather at all. But by the time they all got back home that night, everybody always had exactly what they needed... a beautiful picture of generosity."* The hidden Marah statute (per the Midrash) — *the weak and marginalized drink first* — is being lived out.

## East/West Lens

- **Testing: relational/experiential knowing over abstract pass-fail judgment.** The Western instinct hears "test" as exam; the Hebraic sense is a measurement that God *yada*s. Marty: *"this word for 'know' in the Hebrew is not the data 'know'... God wants to know what's in our heart. God wants to experience our growth."* God already has the data; he wants the shared experience.

- **Heart as will/volition over heart as emotions/feelings.** Where the West locates the heart in sentiment, the Hebraic *lev/levav* is the seat of choice. Marty: *"the heart, the vav... is the seat of the will. It's where your choice comes from. It's a place of volition."* The test of the heart is therefore a test of what you will *choose*, not what you *feel*.

- **Truth conveyed as "imaginative remembering" over flat historiography.** Reed, drawing on Sternberg and Buber/Brueggemann, holds historiographic, ideological, and aesthetic registers together as "levers," not opposing forces: *"it's a both/and, it's not an either/or."* Buber: *"From the biblical viewpoint, history always contains an element of wonder."* Chapter 14's "strong east wind" and Chapter 15's "breath from the nostrils of God" are two faithful tellings of one real event — the poetic telling makes it *more* believable, not less.

- **Salvation/Kingdom as something to be lived and embodied over a one-time transactional moment.** Marty explicitly de-couples "Kingdom of God" from "going to heaven": *"I'm not talking about salvation... I'm talking about experiencing the intention of God, the reign of the Kingdom of God on earth today."* Kingdom is participatory and present-tense — finger, Lord, and obedience together.

- **Community/corporate solidarity over individual self-provision.** The manna lesson lands communally: the strong gather extra so the weak lack nothing. Reed ties it forward to Paul: *"Go look at 2 Corinthians 8 sometime and just maybe see what you find there."* The marginalized-go-first ethic is a corporate formation, not a private virtue.

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story (the arc itself).** The episode opens by rehearsing the whole arc — Genesis preface/introduction, the tale of two kingdoms, God pursuing even Pharaoh's heart, the call to be *bechor* — and frames the desert as the refining place where that story keeps unfolding. Marty: *"He's got to get Egypt out of them. And the only way He's going to get Egypt out of them... is He's got to take them to the desert. The desert is going to be this refining fire."* The partnership God wants does not change; the story runs on.

- **Partnership / Israel as *bechor* (Session 1 overarching arc).** Explicitly named in the recap: *"I need you to be my firstborn son that shares my values with the rest of my kids, that helps me accomplish my goals as the father."* The heart-test is the first concrete formation of that partner — God and Israel learning to live life together, each test "an opportunity to show God... and to grow in God."

- **The two kingdoms: Empire vs. Shalom / fear vs. trust / coercion vs. invitation.** Carried in from Episode 18 and named directly: Marty: *"these two kingdoms, Empire versus Shalom... Pharaoh versus God... fear versus trust, coercion versus invitation, domination versus service."* Reed adds his "right-handed power versus left-handed power" frame. The sea crossing is the hinge where Empire's pursuit ends and Shalom's formation begins.

- **A God who hears the cry (continuing the Episode 17 thread).** The manna narrative repeatedly grounds God's provision in his *hearing*: "he has heard your grumbling." Rather than punishing the grumble, God answers it with abundance — Reed: *"instead of any number of reactions that I could imagine, God says, 'Okay, I'll take care of you.'... a very kind and compassionate and long suffering kind of response."* The God who heard Israel in Egypt keeps hearing them in the desert.

- *(Note: the "master the beast" / sin-as-failing-to-master-the-animal motif from `1-3-master-the-beast.md` is not engaged this episode — Israel's struggle is framed through the heart/will test and communal generosity, not the beast/instinct lens, so the motif is not claimed. The El Shaddai "God who knows when to say enough" motif from Episode 2 is likewise not invoked here.)*

## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

1. **Recap of the arc so far** — Genesis preface/introduction; the Exodus as the start of God's narrative; two kingdoms; God pursuing Pharaoh's heart; Israel called to be *bechor*; the desert as refining fire. *(Marty: "He's got to get Egypt out of them... take them to the desert... this refining fire.")*
2. **Map orientation (Exodus 14:1-4)** — Goshen's green abundance vs. the desert; the counterintuitive southward route into a dead end; *Yam Suph* = Sea of Reeds, not Red Sea; where the crossing likely happened. *(Marty on the map and the Hebrew.)*
3. **"Stand and watch" (Exodus 14:10-16)** — Israel's terror and complaint; Moses' "be still"; God's immediate "Why are you crying to me? Move." Stage-appropriate faith. *(Reed: "it's literally the next verse where God is like... Move.")*
4. **The crossing (Exodus 14:19-22)** — the angel/pillar moving from front to rear; the east wind; walls of water. *(Brent reads; "There's those walls of water.")*
5. **Chapter 14 vs. 15 — the three levers** — historiographic/ideological/aesthetic; Buber and Brueggemann's "imaginative remembering"; creation language (waters separated, dry ground, breath). *(Reed's Sternberg/Buber/Plaut material; Marty's Midrash of fruit trees in the water walls.)*
6. **The song at the sea** — Exodus Rabbah: the first time anyone "busts out in song"; "I have been waiting for these"; gratitude as the completion of the saving act. *(Reed on the Midrash.)*
7. **The threefold coming of the Kingdom** — finger of God moves (even "the little finger" to the magicians), people call him Lord (*Adonai yimloch l'olam va'ed*), people respond in obedience. *(Marty.)*
8. **Reframing "test"** — not pass/fail; hot-tub test strips vs. school exam; *yada* knowing (Deut 8); show-and-grow. *(Marty, Reed, Brent "rewrite the Bible.")*
9. **First test — the heart / Marah (Exodus 15:22-27; Deut 8:1-3)** — *lev* as seat of will; *Marah* = bitter / rebellious wordplay; the stick (a stick, not *the* staff); the missing statute (Midrash: weak go first); 12 springs + 70 palm trees (people/community numerology); spring vs. well. *(Marty; Reed on spring vs. well, "super abundant.")*
10. **The test continues — manna (Exodus 16:1-30)** — the humble-pie check against caricaturing Israel; the wadi "baking oven" illustration; God hears the grumble and provides; Moses adds meat; the omer debate; hoarded manna rots; Sabbath manna keeps; some still disobey. *(Reed on condescension/the wadi; Marty on the omer and his own "worry wart" confession.)*
11. **Closing — communal generosity (Exodus 16:17-18)** — the rabbis' reading of "much/little"; the marginalized cared for; 2 Corinthians 8 as a later echo; lifting the "veil of cynicism" about the Israelites. *(Reed: "maybe they did get it... maybe I can get it too.")*

## Chiasm / Structure

The hosts do not identify a formal chiasm in this episode; none is asserted here. The episode's own organizing structure is the **three-part journey framework** (finger → Lord → obedience) overlaid on the **three desert tests** (heart → soul → might), of which only the first — the test of the **heart** — is covered in this episode. *(Marty: "They're gonna be tested three times... God's gonna test their heart, God's gonna test their soul, and God's gonna test their might.")*

## Hebrew / Terms

- **Yam Suph** (יַם־סוּף) — literally "Sea of Reeds," not "Red Sea." Marty: *"the word in the Hebrew doesn't mean Red Sea... It is literally the Sea of Reeds. It is the Reed Sea."*
- **yada** (יָדַע) — to know experientially and intimately, not merely cognitively. Marty: *"this knowing is a very, we shall say, experiential knowing... God wants to actually know—yada—it. God wants to experience it."* Used both of God testing "to know" Israel's heart (Deut 8) and of Israel coming to "know" the Lord through the meat/manna (Ex 16).
- **Marah** (מָרָה) — "bitter," the name of the bitter well; the same root also means "rebellious." Marty: *"marah can be bitter, and marah can be rebellious... Where are the people at? They're in danger of being in this rebellious state."*
- **lev / levav** (לֵב / לֵבָב) — the heart as the Hebraic seat of the will and volition. Marty: *"the heart... is the seat of the will. It's where your choice comes from. It's a place of volition."*
- **Adonai yimloch l'olam va'ed** (יְהוָה יִמְלֹךְ לְעֹלָם וָעֶד) — "The LORD reigns forever and ever," sung at the sea; the first time Israel directly calls God *Adonai* with their own mouths. Marty: *"It's the first time in the story... that we're told they directly, from their own mouth, call Him Adonai."*
- **omer** (עֹמֶר) — the daily measure of manna, "a tenth of an ephah" (Ex 16:36); debated as somewhere between an Egyptian *homer* (~a cup) and a biblical omer (~just under a liter). Marty: *"we have somewhere between a cup and just under a liter. We're not talking about a ton."*

## Illustrations & Images

- **Hot-tub test strips vs. a school exam** — Marty's central reframe of "test": *"the test that a teacher puts down in front of us at school, rather than the test strips that I put in the hot tub to see where the water is... a testing that's not about pass and fail, it's just getting a measurement."*
- **The wadi "baking oven"** — Reed's firsthand desert experience grounding Israel's complaint in reality: *"I could feel the heat actually radiating off of that wall and it literally felt like I was baking in an oven... 'Did Marty bring me out here to die?'"*
- **The Alabama pastor's drawl** — Marty: *"'Why did Ray bring us down to this desert to die?'"* — a self-deprecating echo of Israel's grumble that lifts the caricature.
- **Fruit trees growing in the walls of water** — Marty's Midrash: *"when they walk through the walls of water, there were fruit trees growing in the walls of the water, and they were picking fruit as they walked"* — tying the crossing to creation/abundance.
- **The "little finger" of God** — Marty: the magicians warn Pharaoh he is only feeling *"the little finger of God... not even a thumb or an index finger."*
- **Marty's own "worry wart" confession** — Marty admits he'd have hoarded manna: *"I would have been one of those anxiety ridden worry warts... And it would have been all full of maggots."*
- **Reed's "crumb on the treasure map"** — Reed teasing the 2 Corinthians 8 connection: *"I'll drop a little crumb, a little hint on the treasure map."*

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty distinguishes "stand and watch" faith (do nothing, let God act) from a later faith that must "move first." Where are you being asked to *be still*, and where might God actually be saying "Why are you crying to me? Move"? How do you tell the difference? *(Ex 14:13-15.)*
2. Reconsider the "test" image: a hot-tub test strip measures where the water *is*, not whether it passes or fails. If God's tests are opportunities to *show* and to *grow* rather than to pass or fail, how does that change the way you read your own hard seasons? *(Deut 8:2-3; Marty: "an opportunity to show God... and to grow in God.")*
3. The rabbis read the manna miracle as *communal generosity* — the strong gathering extra so the weak lack nothing — and the hidden Marah statute as "the weak and the marginalized go first." Who in your community "cannot go out to gather," and what would it look like for everyone to end the day "with exactly what they needed"? Compare 2 Corinthians 8. *(Ex 16:17-18.)*
4. Reed and Marty both confess they would likely have hoarded manna "just to be safe." Where does anxious self-provision show up in your life, and what would "living by every word" instead of "bread alone" look like concretely? *(Ex 16:19-20; Deut 8:3.)*
5. Marty names three things that must come together for the Kingdom to be experienced — the finger of God moving, people calling him Lord, and people responding in obedience — and notes communities that have one or two but not all three. Which do you/your community tend to emphasize, and which is most often missing? *(Marty's threefold Kingdom teaching.)*
6. Chapters 14 and 15 tell the same crossing with different "levers" (plain narrative vs. poetic song). Does holding history and poetry together as "both/and" strengthen or weaken your trust in the story — and why? *(Reed/Sternberg/Buber.)*
