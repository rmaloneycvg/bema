# Images of the Desert — Wadi and En Gedi

BEMA 29 · Session 1
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/1-29-images-of-the-desert-wadi-and-en-gedi.md`
Book: Numbers (the desert / wilderness), with Psalm 63; Jeremiah 2; Isaiah 32 & 58; Psalm 118; John 7; Matthew 7; Psalm 40
Hosts: Marty Solomon (host) · Brent Billings (co-host) · with guest Elle Grover Fricks

> The final "Images of the Desert" episode gathers three more desert pictures and reads them through a Hebraic, place-first lens. The **wadi** (a blind desert canyon) teaches you to trust the shepherd's voice step by step because you cannot see around the next bend. **Maim chaim** ("living water") at the oasis of **En Gedi** reframes God as the flowing source of life — and reframes *us* as streams meant to carry that water to others, a partnership sealed at the Sukkot water ceremony and declared by Jesus in John 7. Finally the **wadi flood** — the number-one killer in the desert — turns the Sermon on the Mount's house-on-the-rock into a lesson about *location*, not building material. The spine throughout is the Session-1 arc: God invites people into partnership — to be shade and water on his behalf.

## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

1. **Cold open / the lost recording.** Marty reveals he "never hit record on that last episode," so they re-record over Zoom. Marty: *"I never hit record on that last episode."* Elle returns for "our last images of the desert episode."
2. **The three deserts and reserved lessons.** Marty names the biblical wildernesses — the Negev, the Zin, and the Paran — and notes some teachings (e.g., "a lesson on rocks") are kept for in-person trips. Elle corrects the pronunciation of *Zin*: *"There are no short i sounds in Hebrew, so it's [pronounced like] 'seen.'"*
3. **Image 1 — the wadi.** Deep desert canyons carved by rain out of the Judean hills; their bends block sight lines. Marty: *"you can't see two hundred yards in front of you... you really have no idea where you're going."* Getting lost (an extra seven miles) becomes the lesson: the desert "teaches you to trust."
4. **Trusting the shepherd (little "s").** Elle on switching into "a different mode"; George DeJong's mantra *"Let's see what the Lord provides around the next bend."* The blind itinerary of the study tours is the applied version — "the desert teaches us how to be people of the ears."
5. **Leader vs participant anxiety.** Marty: being a participant was harder — *"my anxiety level was through the roof"* — because he didn't know what was next; the leader's lower anxiety becomes "a great lesson to learn about with life."
6. **Image 2 — maim chaim at En Gedi.** "Spring of the deer," an oasis encountered "out of nowhere." The sensational guide claim that the water fell near Bethlehem ~1800 years ago; Marty's "can you prove it's not?" teaching posture.
7. **God as living water (Psalm 63; Jeremiah 2).** David in "the desert of Judah"; the two sins of Jeremiah 2 — forsaking the spring of living water and digging "broken cisterns." Elle's word for cistern water: *"dank."*
8. **We are living water too (Isaiah 32; Isaiah 58).** The "king" (singular) and "rulers" (plural) each called to be streams; Isaiah 58's "spring whose waters never fail." The partnership move: God is the source; his people carry it.
9. **What counts as living water.** Two legitimate sources — springs or rain — vs. caught/stored cistern water; the Talmudic debate Elle describes ("Ditch water is fine"), landing on the essential idea: living water comes from God and flows.
10. **Sukkot, the lulav, and the water ceremony.** The four species of the lulav; shaking palm fronds that "sound like rain"; shouting *"Hoshana!"*; Elle's reframing of the wave offering (*tenufa* = "brandish") — what do we brandish before God? The priest's empty-then-full pitcher procession from the altar to the Pool of Siloam.
11. **John 7 — "rivers of living water."** Jesus stands and shouts on the last/greatest day; Marty's teacher's claim that he shouts in "the quiet part"; the syntactic surprise that the living water flows "from within *us*," not Jesus — enabled by the Spirit (John 7:39, Elle's catch).
12. **The En Gedi photo assignment.** Ray's charge to name the people who have been "En Gedi" to you — and to ask whether anyone would name you. Marty's hard admission that his En Gedi people had mostly been "unbelievers," and the challenge that "the least safe place to be is in a room full of God's people."
13. **Image 3 — the wadi flood.** Flash floods as the "number one killer in the desert" (Elle: *"I am the number one killer in the desert"*); the "sound of a train" and 40 seconds to two minutes to escape.
14. **Matthew 7 reread as location.** Building on sand = building at the *bottom* of the wadi; rock = the top. The point is *where* you build, not the material. Psalm 40 as the rescue image — "lifted out of the mud and mire," feet set on a rock.
15. **Close.** "That does it for our Images of the Desert series"; Brent will try to find a wadi-flood video.

## Key Lessons

- **The desert teaches trust because you can only see your next step.** The wadi's blind bends strip away the Western illusion of control. Marty: *"It teaches you to just worry about the next step, because you don't know what the next ten steps look like. You know what your next step is. It teaches you to trust in the intentions of the shepherd."* Elle names the posture: *"I'm just going to do what I'm told... that's not my job... I'm just there."*
- **Knowing vs. not-knowing changes everything — and following is harder than leading.** Marty contrasts his two roles: as a participant *"my anxiety level was through the roof... it made me more thirsty... the oppressive nature of that anxiety,"* but as a leader *"because I know, my anxiety level is so much lower, and I think that is such a great lesson to learn about with life."*
- **God is living water — and his people are called to be living water too.** The scriptures do a double move: God is the spring (Psalm 63, Jeremiah 2), and then his people are commissioned as streams (Isaiah 32, 58). Marty: *"God is our source of living water, and we're called to be a source of that on His behalf as well."* This is the partnership motif in liquid form.
- **Living water must come from God and must flow; stored water goes dank.** Marty: *"If you've taken water and you've moved it from one place to another, it's no longer living water."* Elle generalizes the rabbinic debate: *"living water is something that comes from God... it's not stagnant, it has a flowing element to it."* Jeremiah's indictment is choosing broken cisterns over the spring.
- **The living water flows from within *us*, not from Jesus.** The climactic John 7 reading: come to Jesus, and *"streams of living water flow from within us — not Jesus, but us. We come to Jesus as our sustenance... and because of that, streams of living water flow from within us, which fits everything we've learned in the Hebrew scriptures."* Elle adds the enabling agent: *"we're able to do that because of the Spirit."*
- **Be shade and water, not merely "right."** Elle's closing challenge: *"How much time are we investing in being people who have everything right... versus how much time have we been cultivating being shade, like God is shade, and water, like God is water."*
- **The Sermon on the Mount is about location, not building material.** Marty: *"The driving part of this teaching is not about building material... It's about location... where the person's building the house."* Sand is a fine material (contractors use it); building at the *bottom of the wadi* is the folly. Rescue (Psalm 40) is being pulled from the mud and set "on the rock."

## East/West Lens

- **Place-first / concrete over abstract.** The entire episode insists meaning is learned on the land, not in propositions. Marty: *"there are some things I feel like you can only learn in person."* And: *"the desert is a crucible."* The images (wadi, oasis, flood) *are* the theology.
- **People of the ears, not the eyes.** A signature Hebraic contrast. Marty: *"The desert teaches us how to be people of the ears... not people of the eyes. Because we have to listen for the voice."* The wadi makes "following the voice" visceral rather than cerebral.
- **Truth over time / trust the unseen next bend.** Rather than a mapped-out Western itinerary, the disciple trusts a guide dynamically. George DeJong's mantra: *"Let's see what the Lord provides around the next bend."* Marty: the blind itinerary "is going to be blank. And that's part of the whole joy of teaching in this way."
- **Community vs. individual (partnership).** God does not keep living water to himself; he enlists a plurality — the "rulers" (plural) of Isaiah 32, the "us" of John 7 — to carry it. Marty: *"we have the image of us being that source of water, not just God, but also us."*
- **Worship as embodied action (brandishing shade), not words-only.** Elle reframes the wave offering: *tenufa* can mean *brandish*, as in a military context — *"when we come before God, what are we brandishing? Are we brandishing our military power... or... are we bringing our shade with us?... mirroring what God does for us."* Elle also skewers rote worship: *"I'm sure it's just random, Marty. We just say stuff at church. It doesn't matter what it means."* — the Hebraic reply is: it's in the Text, it means "Lord, save us."
- **Word/structure sensitivity.** The lesson turns on reading Hebrew carefully — *maim chaim*, *En Gedi*, *lulav*, *Hoshana*, *tenufa* — and on noticing the Greek syntax of John 7 ("some debate about how you would do the Greek syntax here") that relocates the living water into the believer.

## Chiasms & Structure

No formal chiasm is claimed by the hosts. The episode's organizing structure is a deliberate **three-image progression** that builds toward the partnership thesis and then a warning:

| Movement | Image | Hebrew / key text | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wadi** (blind canyon) | Numbers / wilderness | Trust the shepherd's voice one step at a time |
| 2 | **Maim chaim** at **En Gedi** (oasis) | Psalm 63; Jeremiah 2; Isaiah 32 & 58; Psalm 118; John 7 | God is living water — and so are we (partnership) |
| 3 | **Wadi flood** | Matthew 7; Psalm 40 | Location, not material: build on the rock, not the wadi bottom |

Marty signals the intentional ordering: *"The second image I want to talk about is going to build into the third."* The water ceremony itself has a clear processional shape the hosts narrate (altar → down the ramp → Pool of Siloam → back up the ramp → the pour), climaxing in Jesus' shout in John 7.

## Terminology

- **wadi** (also **vadi**) — a deep desert canyon carved by the rains, descending from the Judean hills; its bends block sight lines so you "can't see two hundred yards in front of you," teaching step-by-step trust in the shepherd's voice. Marty: *"these deep desert canyons carved out by the rains."*
- **Negev / Zin / Paran** — the three biblical wildernesses. Elle on *Zin*: *"There are no short i sounds in Hebrew, so it's [pronounced like] 'seen.'"* (and notes it's "the name of an Akkadian moon goddess").
- **maim chaim** (מַיִם חַיִּים) — "living water": water that comes directly from God as a spring or rain, flowing and fresh. Marty: *"Maim being water, chaim being life and living. So living water."* Contrasted with stored "dank" cistern water.
- **En Gedi / Ein Gedi** (עֵין גֶּדִי) — "spring of the deer"; a desert oasis. *En/ein* = spring; *gedi* = a kind of young deer/goat. Marty: *"En Gedi is 'Spring of the Deer'. It's an oasis in the middle of the desert."*
- **cistern (broken cistern)** — a dug, water-storage pit; its water is stagnant and non-living. Elle: *"'dank' probably comes to mind... Lots of stuff growing in them, lots of bugs."* The image of Jeremiah 2:13.
- **lulav** (לוּלָב) — the Sukkot bundle made of four elements: a palm frond, a myrtle branch, a river willow, and an **etrog** (a citrus fruit) used as a handle. Shaken, many together "sound like rain."
- **Sukkot** (סֻכּוֹת) — the Festival of Tabernacles, a week-long festival whose last/greatest day holds the **water ceremony**.
- **Hoshana** (הוֹשַׁע נָא) — "Lord, save us! / Lord, grant us success!" (English "Hosanna"), shouted while shaking the lulav; drawn from Psalm 118. Marty: *"we shout in the Hebrew, 'Hoshanah!'"*
- **tenufa** (תְּנוּפָה) — the "wave" (or "heave") offering; the same word means to **brandish**, as in a military context. Elle: *"what are we brandishing?... are we bringing our shade with us?"*
- **wadi flood (flash flood)** — the sudden torrent that rushes down a canyon from rains in the mountains; the desert's number-one cause of death. Marty: *"if you're in a wadi and you hear the sound of a train, you have 40 seconds to two minutes to get out."*

## Illustrations & Stories (from the hosts)

- **Getting lost in the wadi (an extra seven miles).** Marty recounts being lost with his teacher Ray and later getting one of his own groups "slightly lost" — the embodied origin of "worry about the next step." Marty: *"Somebody did the math before we found a way to get out of that wadi."*
- **George DeJong's mantra.** The GTI teacher's refrain that became Marty's own: *"Let's see what the Lord provides around the next bend"* — the picture of desert following. (Elle lightens it with Pocahontas: *"What's around the riverbend?"*)
- **The blind itinerary.** Marty answers prospective participants who ask for a schedule by sending a blank one: *"It is not going to do you any good because it's going to be blank."* The tour itself trains "people of the ears."
- **Finding shade in the wadi.** Brent's memory of a tiny shaded outcropping big enough for everyone except Marty, and how the entry path was invisible "like twenty feet away" — the sight-lines lesson made visceral: *"the desert was just this very visceral and unrelenting experience."*
- **Heat exhaustion / heat stroke.** Marty's later heat stroke lets him finally understand what the group endured — the desert as crucible.
- **Encountering En Gedi "out of nowhere."** Coming in the back way with Ray Vander Laan ("interesting permissions and/or just disobeyed the rules") and stumbling onto the oasis: *"all of a sudden, boom, a spring."*
- **The scorpion in the cistern.** Elle's 2016 memory of a scorpion down a cistern (more than once), reinforcing how un-living cistern water is; Brent's deadpan: *"Would have been nice to know when I was getting a close-up picture of it, Elle."*
- **Shaking 300 palm fronds in a church room.** Marty's lesson at a church where a quiet crowd shook palm fronds and it "sounds like rain" — the sensory logic of asking God for rain at Sukkot.
- **The priest's empty-then-full pitcher.** The narrated water-ceremony procession: an empty pitcher poured at the altar, then a filled pitcher carried up from the Pool of Siloam, water hitting the "steaming altar" as the crowd erupts.
- **The En Gedi photo assignment.** Ray's charge to photograph yourself at the spring and send it to those who have been "En Gedi" to you — then to ask whether anyone would send *you* one. Marty's confession that, until recently, his En Gedi people had almost all been "unbelievers," and that *"the least safe place to be is in a room full of God's people."*
- **"I am the number one killer in the desert."** Elle's instantly-quotable line about flash floods; Marty jokes it's "our newest BEMA t-shirt." Brent: a tagline "for a Jason Statham movie."
- **The pickup truck caught in a flash flood.** Brent's note that wadi-flood videos keep disappearing, but he'll try to find the pickup-truck one.

## Discussion Questions

1. The wadi teaches you to "just worry about the next step, because you don't know what the next ten steps look like." Where in your life are you demanding to see the whole map before you'll take a step — and what would trusting "the next bend" look like this week?
2. Marty found that *following* produced far more anxiety than *leading*, because the follower doesn't know what's next. When has not-knowing spiked your anxiety, and how might trusting "the intentions of the shepherd" change that?
3. Jeremiah says God's people traded the flowing spring for "broken cisterns." What are the "dank cisterns" you've settled for instead of living water — and what would it look like to return to the source?
4. The John 7 twist is that living water flows "from within *us*, not Jesus." Who has been "En Gedi" — a source of living water in your desert — and have you told them? And would anyone name *you* as theirs?
5. Elle asks how much time we spend "being people who have everything right" versus "cultivating being shade... and water." Which have you been prioritizing lately, and why?
6. Jesus' house-on-the-rock lesson is about *location*, not material — don't build at the bottom of a flood-prone wadi. Where might you be building something good in a dangerous *location*, and what would moving to "the rock" require?
7. At Sukkot, worshipers "brandish" the lulav before God. Elle asks what we brandish — force, or our capacity to be shade and water. What are you actually brandishing in your life right now?
