# Jewish Hermeneutics

BEMA 110 · Session 3
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/3-110-jewish-hermeneutics.md`
Book: Matthew (center text Matthew 13:24–30, the weeds and the wheat; with Matthew 13:11; Genesis 26:12; Isaiah 6; Matthew 16:16–17 referenced)
Co-host: Brent Billings · Host: Marty Solomon

> Jesus is in Galilee teaching a string of "the Kingdom of heaven is like…" parables, and Marty pauses the narrative to teach the *method* beneath them: **PaRDeS** — p'shat, remez, drash, sod. A parable is not a simplifier but a complexifier; a rabbi *buries* truth so students will **discover** it, catering not to the lowest common denominator but to those who actually want the Kingdom. In the Gospels arc — the King embodying the one story — this episode hands the listener the interpretive keys to hear *how* the Messiah teaches: everything He says is rooted in and points back to the Hebrew Scriptures (Session 1's partnership text), so that the Kingdom He proclaims is continuous with, not a replacement of, the story Israel was always in.

## Key Lessons

- **A parable is designed to make learning *harder*, not easier — on purpose.** When the disciples ask why He teaches in parables, Jesus says, in effect, "So they won't understand." Marty: *"a rabbi is using a parable to make the teaching harder, more difficult, not easier… which is exactly why Jesus told his disciples, 'I teach them parables so that people won't understand me.'"* The point is not obfuscation for its own sake but filtering for those willing to do the work.
- **Eastern teaching runs on *discovery*, not explanation.** The one-word engine of a rabbi's method is *discovery*. Marty: *"The Easterner wants you to discover truth. Everything hinges on the idea of discovery… a good Eastern teacher is not going to just teach something flat out. They're going to try to bury the truth into the process of discovery."* Buried truth "does so much more work" than a stated proposition.
- **Jesus caters to the highest common denominator, not the lowest.** Parables sort the crowd between those who merely "want to be a part of the Jesus thing" and those who "actually want to do the work of the Kingdom." Marty: *"I want to cater to the highest common denominator because those are the people that actually want to engage… those are the people that are actually going to bring Kingdom."*
- **PaRDeS names four levels of Jewish interpretation — and the deeper levels are not more true or more inspired than the surface.** *P'shat* (surface) can be "incredibly profound"; *remez* (a hint linking to Hebrew Scripture) requires knowing the Text; *drash* (truth hidden in story) is wrestled out in community; *sod* (mystery) can only be given by God. Marty: *"The deeper levels are not more true, they're not more inspired than p'shat. The different levels will not contradict each other."*
- **Everything Jesus says has a remez — so you must know your Text.** Marty: *"whenever Jesus opens his mouth, there's a remez. You need to find the remez because that's going to link you to something deeper that Jesus is driving at."* The tool for noticing it is to notice "the problems," the things that "seem weird" and don't fit — exactly the reading habit built in Session 1's Genesis study.
- **"A hundredfold" is the worked example: it points to Isaac (Genesis 26:12), so the good soil's drash is *persevere in the land and the mission of God will work*.** "Hundredfold" occurs only one other place in all of Scripture. Marty: *"if you'll stay here and persevere, persevere with faithfulness in the thing that God has called you to, the mission of God will work."*
- **Discovery is a *group* act — the havurah — not a Google search.** Marty: *"Jewish hermeneutics demands the interaction of more than one student… the remez and the drash have to be discussed, examined, critiqued as a group in the process of group discovery."* The answer matters less than the wrestling: *"the answer really isn't that blasted important… wrestling with the Text is, because that's where the power lies."*

## East/West Lens

- **Truth over time / discovered vs. told (process vs. proposition).** The whole episode is an argument for discovery as the mode of knowing. Marty: *"An Easterner believes that you understand a thesis… so much better than you would have if I just explained it to you… I experience the truth. That's the discovery portion."*
- **Mystery vs. mastery (comfort with the unresolved).** The highest level, *sod*, resists capture entirely. Marty: *"No matter how hard I try, I can't teach you sod… Sod is a super supernatural gift from God… the Holy Spirit."* The Western impulse to a "formula" is named and resisted.
- **Community vs. individual (the havurah as the site of understanding).** Interpretation is corporate. Marty: *"This is why learning is done in the context of a havurah… Jesus has 12 disciples formed a havurah,"* picturing them arguing the parable around the campfire while Jesus sleeps with "one eye open."
- **Western self-awareness (the hosts naming their own bias).** Marty flags the Western love of the "straight lines… the practicality… the formulaic nature of these things," confessing *"I am just as Western as any of our listeners… we love to make things [solid and] complete."* The lens is named, not pretended away.
- **Concrete/agrarian imagery carrying the abstract.** The Kingdom is taught through soils, seeds, weeds, wheat, and wells — material pictures doing conceptual work, consistent with the Eastern preference for the concrete.

- **The Kingdom is for those who *want* it (the explosive-sheep / John the Baptist image).** Marty: *"This is an explosive Kingdom. ## Terminology

- **PaRDeS** (פרדס, "orchard/paradise") — an acronym for the four levels of Jewish interpretation: **p'shat, remez, drash, sod**. Marty: *"This PaRDeS word references these four levels of Jewish hermeneutic."*
- **p'shat** (פשט) — the surface or plain reading; the "easiest to discern," shallowest in depth but not shallow in value. Marty: *"90% of Christian teaching, exegetical interpretation, and expository preaching are based on nothing more than p'shat,"* and it "can be incredibly profound."
- **remez** (רמז) — literally "hint"; a word/phrase deliberately buried in the teaching that links the student to a Hebrew Scripture, unlocking deeper meaning (requires knowing the Text). Marty: *"the remez is going to link that student to a passage in the Hebrew scriptures that will give context, the deeper meaning that the teacher is driving at."*
- **drash** (דרש) — "truth hidden in story"; the deeper meaning the remez unlocks, "never a simple idea," meant to be debated in community. Marty: *"Drash is the idea of truth hidden in story."*
- **sod** (סוד) — "mystery"; the fourth and deepest level, which "cannot be learned or taught" and is given by God / the Holy Spirit. Marty: *"Sod is connected to mystery and cannot be learned or taught."*
- **havurah** (חבורה) — a learning fellowship/group; the setting in which remez and drash are wrestled out together. Marty: *"This is why learning is done in the context of a havurah… Jesus has 12 disciples formed a havurah."*
- **discovery** — the engine of Eastern teaching: the rabbi buries truth so the student experiences/discovers it, which is why parables make learning harder on purpose. Marty: *"Everything hinges on the idea of discovery."*
- **chutzpah** — bold fire/audacity; the Kingdom belongs to those who want it. Marty: *"God wants a Jacob, who's willing to have a little fire and a little chutzpah."*
- **exegesis / expository preaching** — the Western Bible-college process of gleaning a proposition directly from the surface of a passage; Marty classes it as p'shat-level work. Marty: *"you're trying to glean the proposition directly from the passage… That's all p'shat."*

## Illustrations & Images

- **The escape/puzzle room.** Marty's central image for a parable: *"A parable is like a gigantic textual puzzle room… The whole point of the room's to find these keys… You're trying to make all these discoveries to unlock a parable."* He and Brent compare notes on rooms in Missoula and Lewiston, Idaho.
- **The 12 disciples arguing around the campfire while Jesus sleeps.** Marty pictures group discovery in action: *"I picture Him rolling over one eye open with a smirk on his face as he listens to His 12 students who wrestle with… 'Why did He tell it? What do you think the remez was?' Judas thinks one thing, and Peter thinks something else."*
- **"Swimming in the shallow end."** The image for p'shat that insists shallow ≠ worthless: *"Even though the learner on this level is swimming in the shallow end, there's nothing wrong with the meaning that can be mined from p'shat."*
- **"One of these things is not like the other, like a song."** The Sesame-Street-style cue for spotting a remez by noticing what doesn't fit (e.g., the backwards "100, 60, 30"). Brent: the numbers "seem specific, maybe grandiose, and also… are backwards. It seems weird."
- **Ray Vander Laan weeping at the back of the Yeshiva class.** An Orthodox rabbi tells Ray, "your Rabbi Jesus, he had a phenomenal teaching on this scripture," and Ray realizes he doesn't know his own Rabbi's teachings. Marty: *"'I would sit in the back of the class, and I would just cry because I didn't really know the teachings of my Jesus.'"*
- **Jesus's teaching "goes for the juggler [jugular]."** The conviction (not condemnation) that comes when you finally grasp what Jesus was doing. Marty: *"His teachings… just say, 'Really? Because if you really understood what I was teaching, you would do this.'"*

A sequential walkthrough of every teaching beat, in order. Used to verify full coverage.

4. **Why parables make it *harder*.** The first assumption of Jewish hermeneutics: a rabbi uses a parable to make teaching more difficult; "so they won't understand."

9. **Level 2 — remez.** "Hint" linking to Hebrew Scripture; the treasure-hunt/puzzle-room; "whenever Jesus opens his mouth, there's a remez"; find it by noticing what "seems weird." The soils each had a remez (path→Hosea 10, rocky→Isaiah 5, thorns→Jeremiah 4); the good soil left for the listener.
10. **The hundredfold worked example.** "100, 60, 30" stands out (and is "backwards"); "hundredfold" occurs only one other time — Genesis 26:12, Isaac. Review of the Isaac story (Gerar, Abimelech, reopening wells, yielding rights, "we see that the Lord is with you").
11. **Level 3 — drash.** "Truth hidden in story"; the remez unlocks it; the good-soil drash = persevere faithfully in the land and the mission of God will work. The remez "is always up for debate" (Brent: once-only words are strongest); not "Bible code"; single-but-complex intent; requires a havurah; the 12 arguing around the campfire; the answer matters less than the wrestling.
12. **Level 4 — sod.** "Mystery"; cannot be taught or learned; a supernatural gift of God/the Spirit; Matthew 13:11 ("the secrets of the kingdom… given to you"); Jesus chose the Twelve because He saw God at work in them; forward pointer to Peter's confession (Matthew 16:16–17).
13. **Why this matters.** We "act like experts on Jesus" without knowing what He was doing as a Jewish rabbi; Ray Vander Laan weeping in the Yeshiva class; the promise that Matthew 13 will change how you hear everything Jesus says; conviction, not condemnation ("goes for the jugular").
14. **The assignment.** The weeds parable is left hanging on purpose; listeners now have the tools — find the p'shat, hunt the remez on biblegateway.com, wrestle the drash, and maybe God gives sod — best done in a havurah. ## Discussion Questions

1. Jesus teaches in parables "so they won't understand." Where in your own faith life have you preferred an easy explanation over the harder work of discovery — and what might change if you let a text stay difficult?
2. Marty insists p'shat is not shallow in value and that the deeper levels are "not more true, not more inspired." How does that reframe the way you've been taught to read the Bible — and does it relieve or unsettle you?
3. The remez for the good soil ("a hundredfold") points to Isaac persevering in the land (Genesis 26). What is one teaching of Jesus you could take to biblegateway.com this week to hunt its remez — and what Hebrew Scripture might it be pointing you back to?
4. Drash "has to be discussed, examined, critiqued as a group." Who is your havurah — and if you don't have one, what would it take to find even one other person to wrestle the Text with?
5. Marty says "the answer really isn't that blasted important… wrestling with the Text is, because that's where the power lies." Is that freeing or frustrating to you, and why?
6. In the Session 3 arc, the King is embodying Israel's story — "everything's based on the Hebrew Scriptures." How does hearing that Jesus is *continuing* the Session 1 partnership story (not starting a new one) change how you read the Gospels?
