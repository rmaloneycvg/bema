# BEMA 67: Haggai — Build! — Study Guide

**Session 2 (The Prophets) · Book of Haggai**
Primary texts: [Haggai 1:1–15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Haggai%201%3A1-15&version=NIV) · [Haggai 2:1–23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Haggai%202%3A1-23&version=NIV)
Hosts: Marty Solomon (host), Brent Billings (co-host)

---

## Overview

This episode walks the whole short prophecy of Haggai — two chapters, "about the size of a good long blog post" — as the hosts near the end of Session 2's trek through the Prophets. Brent frames the takeaway up front: "the real issues rarely change — past, present, or future." Haggai is a **remnant-period** prophet, centered in Jerusalem among the people who have returned home under **Zerubbabel** (whom the previous episode on Ezra and Nehemiah barely mentioned). His ministry kicks off the rebuild and spans roughly nine months in the second year of **King Darius**. The surface message, Marty says, is blunt: it is "time to get off your butts and build God's house" while the people sit in their own "paneled houses."

But the hosts quickly turn the surface inside out. If God rejected David's house-building impulse earlier in Session 2 — because God "loved his tent" — why is God now worked up about his house? The answer: it was never about the house at all; it is about the **hearts of the people**. Haggai's refrain, "give careful thought to your ways," is essentially the diagnostic question *how is that working out for you?* — you eat but are never full, you earn wages only to drop them in "a purse with holes in it." The episode's review image is a single imperative: **Build!** And the promise attached to it reorders everything the returnees assume about glory: the rebuilt second temple, a fraction of Solomon's in silver and stone, will carry a *greater* glory — because glory was never about bricks and mortar but about the **presence of the LORD**.

The guide tracks how this advances the one story. Session 1 established the **partnership**: God plants a people to be a blessing. Session 2 (the Prophets) watches the partner — Israel — fail repeatedly, and watches God refuse to quit. Haggai sits at the far, low end of that arc: a humbled remnant, back from exile, dispirited and distracted, trying to rebuild a culture on a ruined foundation. God's word through Haggai is not a verdict but a summons back into the mission — "I am with you," "be strong," "I will bless you" — insisting that the unfaithful-yet-not-abandoned partner's small, unimpressive obedience is exactly where God still intends to dwell.

## East / West Lens

Only the interpretive categories the hosts actually engage are tagged below; each is cited.

- **Heart/posture over architecture (East) vs. the thing itself (West).** The hosts refuse to read Haggai as a building-project book. Marty: *"It's really not about God's house at all. It's about the hearts of the people."* The Western instinct to measure the project by its materials is set against a God who weighs motivation and priority.
- **Fulfillment-in-mission over transactional formula.** Marty explicitly blocks the quid-pro-quo reading of the drought language: *"Not so much in the literal formulaic, if we do this, then God will make it rain... but more along the lines of, if we were to do these things, I wonder if we would find the fulfillment."* Covenant blessing is relational and vocational, not mechanical.
- **Glory as divine presence, not grandeur.** When the second house is promised greater glory than Solomon's, the hosts locate glory in God's presence rather than money or masonry. Marty: *"when I hear glory I'm going to think of the presence of the Lord... This must not be about bricks and mortar."*
- **Purity dynamics read from Leviticus (ritual logic as moral teaching).** The consecration riddle is read through the Levitical rule that impurity transfers but holiness does not. Marty: *"It's so much easier to pass on impurity than purity. You have to be very careful."*
- **Hebrew word-sense (chazak).** Marty hears the Hebrew behind "be strong," the Joshua echo, rather than a generic English pep talk: God's charge to *"be strong... work, for I am with you"* carries the resonance of *chazak*, "be strengthened."

## Episode Flow (Coverage Backbone)

1. **Framing: the real issues rarely change.** Brent sets the thesis — "we will read through the prophecy of Haggai and find that the real issues rarely change — past, present, or future." Marty situates it: "wrapping up the last section of the prophets," in the remnant period.
2. **Placing Haggai in the remnant diagram.** The hosts recall which remnant voices they've covered — 4 Isaiah, then Ezra and Nehemiah (treated, with the Jewish perspective, as "one real volume"). Haggai comes next, with Zechariah to follow.
3. **Setting and scope.** Centered in Jerusalem (the critique concerns the LORD's house / the temple); the returned remnant under **Zerubbabel**, Ezra, and Nehemiah. The book is short — two chapters, ~nine months in the second year of **King Darius** — and Marty urges listeners to just read it.
4. **The blunt surface message (1:1–4).** Brent reads the opening. Marty: "it's time to get off your butts and build God's house." Brent sharpens it: "Not only nothing, they're building their own fancy houses" — the "paneled houses" of verse 4.
5. **The David tension raised.** "I thought God didn't want a house... Why does God want a house now?" Marty tells the listener to "hold on to that tension," then names the crux: it's "not about God's house at all. It's about the hearts of the people." David at least had a bothered conscience; these people aren't even motivated.
6. **"Give careful thought to your ways" (1:5–6).** Brent reads the refrain and the futility list. Marty: Haggai is asking "how's that working out for you?" — "You're eating, but you're never full... This isn't really working out for you."
7. **Build the house; the drought explained (1:7–11).** "Go up into the mountains and bring down timber and build my house." Marty connects the withheld dew to **Deuteronomy**'s closed-sky language from Session 1 — but refuses the formula, reframing it as fulfillment found in God's mission, not transaction.
8. **The people respond — and leadership matters.** The remnant, stirred up, begin work (within about three and a half weeks). Marty relates it to being a church leader: "Walking in obedience month after month after month is difficult."
9. **"Does it not seem like nothing?" (2:1–5).** God addresses discouragement over the humble rebuild versus the remembered **former glory** of Solomon's temple. God's charge: "Be strong... work, for I am with you" — Marty hears **chazak** and the Joshua echo, plus the covenant-from-Egypt reassurance "do not fear."
10. **"Greater glory" and the shaking (2:6–9).** Brent reads the promise to "shake the heavens and the earth" and fill the house with glory. Marty: a prophetic figure for God moving — "shake them out like a rug." Since the second house is poorer in money and masonry, glory "has nothing to do with the money"; it's the presence of the LORD.
11. **Brent's structure question.** Brent notices the repeated "says the LORD / declares the LORD" and asks whether it signals gaps like the Genesis narrative markers. Marty candidly: "I don't know if I have the expertise to comment on that... Good question, I don't know." (Parked below.)
12. **The consecration riddle (2:10–19).** Brent reads the priest questions. Marty draws the **Leviticus** lesson: holiness is not passed on by contact, but impurity is — "It's so much easier to pass on impurity than purity." A less-than-obedient path contaminates everything; persevere on the narrow way. Haggai again: "give careful thought."
13. **The signet ring (2:20–23).** God promises to shake kingdoms and make **Zerubbabel** "like my signet ring, for I have chosen you." Marty: Zerubbabel "will carry my authority... This is all headed somewhere positive and redemptive."
14. **Closing thoughts: priorities and humble temples.** Marty: Haggai still speaks "today" about priorities — our agendas or God's — and what truly matters: not "the opulence of our church buildings... the impressive nature of our Bible knowledge... the academic credentials." God "is still able to dwell in even the humblest of temples. In fact, he seems to prefer it" — the swing back to David's tent, and the promise of greater things through the "pathetic rebuilt temple."

**Coverage verification:** Both read passages are covered — Haggai 1:1–15 (segments 4–8) and Haggai 2:1–23 (segments 9–13). Every stated unit is represented: the David tension (5), the "give careful thought" refrain (6, 12), the drought/Deuteronomy reframe (7), the people's response and discouragement (8–9), the greater-glory promise and the "shaking" figure (10), the consecration/Leviticus lesson (12), the signet ring (13), and the closing priorities/humble-temple synthesis (14). The stray structural-markers exchange (11) is parked in **Other Talking Points**.

## Key Lessons

Framed against the Session 2 arc — the partner's unfaithfulness and God's refusal to quit.

1. **It was never about the house; it is about the heart.** The whole episode pivots on this. Marty: *"It's really not about God's house at all. It's about the hearts of the people."* **Arc link:** the returned partner is distracted and self-absorbed ("paneled houses"), yet God does not walk away — he diagnoses the heart and re-summons it into the mission.

2. **"Give careful thought to your ways" — consider how your self-focus is actually working out.** The refrain is a mirror, not a threat. Marty: *"Haggai is asking them to consider... 'How's that working out for you?'... You're eating, but you're never full."* **Arc link:** judgment language in the Prophets keeps turning out to be invitation; God exposes the emptiness of unfaithfulness so the partner will return.

3. **Covenant blessing is fulfillment in God's mission, not a transaction.** Marty blocks the formula even while citing the drought. *"Not so much in the literal formulaic... but more along the lines of, if we were to do these things, I wonder if we would find the fulfillment."* **Arc link:** this re-sounds Session 1's partnership — the people were planted to live God's story, and life "works" only inside that vocation.

4. **Glory is the presence of the LORD, not grandeur — so the humble rebuild can outshine Solomon's temple.** Marty: *"This must not be about bricks and mortar and building projects, this must be about something bigger and better."* **Arc link:** at the lowest point of the partnership, a diminished remnant, God insists the small and unimpressive is precisely where he will do *greater* things — he refuses to measure the partner by its ruins.

5. **Impurity spreads more easily than holiness — so the narrow, consecrated way takes deliberate effort.** From the Leviticus riddle. Marty: *"It's so much easier to pass on impurity than purity. You have to be very careful... persevere through the work that we're called to do."* **Arc link:** restoration is not automatic; the faithful partner must work harder to stay consecrated than to drift, which is why God keeps sending prophets to steady the rebuild.

6. **God prefers to dwell in humble temples — ducks need not be in a row.** The closing swing back to David's tent. Marty: *"God is still able to dwell in even the humblest of temples. In fact, he seems to prefer it... We don't have to have all our ducks in a row."* **Arc link:** God's refusal to quit lands as grace — the unfaithful partner's inadequate offering is still a dwelling place for God's presence.

## Narrative Tensions

Each tension: the surfaced problem, a classification tag, the cited quote, a verse link, and a Hebraic resolution (or "Open — dance with it").

- **Why does God suddenly want a house when earlier he refused one?** *(Contradiction / expectation tension.)* Marty: *"I thought God didn't want a house. Now God, all of a sudden is worked up about his house. Why does God want a house now, when he didn't want a house earlier?... Hold on to that tension."* Text: [Haggai 1:4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Haggai%201%3A4&version=NIV). **Hebraic resolution:** the apparent contradiction dissolves once you see the issue was always the heart, not the architecture — David's impulse came from a troubled conscience while God "sat in a tent"; the returnees aren't even motivated, having built their own paneled homes and given no thought to God. The constant is priority and posture, not whether a building exists.

- **Is this quid pro quo — build the temple and God turns the rain back on?** *(Theological / character-of-God tension.)* Marty: *"Not so much in the literal formulaic, if we do this, then God will make it rain... but more along the lines of, if we were to do these things, I wonder if we would find the fulfillment."* Text: [Haggai 1:9–11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Haggai%201%3A9-11&version=NIV). **Hebraic resolution:** the Deuteronomy echo is covenantal, not mechanical — the point is that self-serving pursuit cannot satisfy, while living God's story brings fulfillment in the ordinary (food, drink, labor). Blessing is relational, not a vending machine.

- **How can a smaller, poorer temple have greater glory than Solomon's?** *(Logic / value tension.)* Marty: *"the glory of this second house is going to be greater than the glory of the first house. That must mean that the glory has nothing to do with... the money... the quality of building."* Text: [Haggai 2:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Haggai%202%3A9&version=NIV). **Hebraic resolution:** redefine glory. If glory meant silver and masonry, the claim is false; since glory is the *presence* of the LORD and what God does through the house, the humble rebuild can far surpass the grand one.

- **Do the repeated "says the LORD / declares the LORD" markers signal structural gaps like Genesis?** *(Translation / structure tension.)* Brent: *"Is that the same kind of thing where there's some kind of a gap between there?... It seems like it's constructed a little bit differently."* Text: [Haggai 2:6–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Haggai%202%3A6-9&version=NIV). **Open — dance with it.** Marty declines to claim expertise: *"I'm not sure if the rules of the Hebrew here apply in the same way... I don't know if the prophetic genre changes in that regard... Good question, I don't know."* An honest unresolved question left open.

- **Why does obedience get harder a couple of months in, right after a good start?** *(Experiential / discipleship tension.)* Marty: *"Walking in obedience month after month after month is difficult, and life has the seasons that you go through... now a couple of months later, we're kind of drifting."* Text: [Haggai 2:1–3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Haggai%202%3A1-3&version=NIV). **Hebraic resolution:** the consecration lesson answers it — impurity and discouragement spread far more easily than holiness, so perseverance on the narrow way requires more intention than drifting; God sends the prophetic word precisely to re-strengthen (*chazak*) the flagging remnant.

## Terminology

- **"Give careful thought to your ways"** — Haggai's repeated refrain; functionally the diagnostic question *how is that working out for you?* Marty: *"Again, Haggai uses that same phrase, 'Give careful thought to your ways.'"*
- **Remnant** — the people who returned to Jerusalem (not those left behind in Persia); the period of the prophetic diagram Haggai belongs to. Marty: *"this is definitely the remnant that has returned back home under Zerubbabel."*
- **Zerubbabel** — governor of Judah leading the returnees; given prophetic help by Haggai and Zechariah; promised to become God's "signet ring." Marty: *"Zerubbabel gets some help in this time period from a couple of prophetic friends."*
- **Paneled houses** — the people's own finished homes, contrasted with the LORD's house left "a ruin"; the image of misplaced priority. Brent/Haggai 1:4: *"Is it a time for you yourselves to be living in your paneled houses while this house remains a ruin?"*
- **Chazak ("be strong" / "be strengthened")** — the Hebrew Marty hears behind God's charge to Zerubbabel and Joshua, echoing Joshua. (Lens-tagged above.) God: *"Be strong... work, for I am with you."*
- **Glory** — reframed as the presence of the LORD, not grandeur or materials. Marty: *"when I hear glory I'm going to think of the presence of the Lord."*
- **Consecrated / defiled (clean and unclean)** — the Levitical categories in the priest-riddle; holiness does not transfer by contact, impurity does. Marty: *"It's so much easier to pass on impurity than purity."*
- **Signet ring** — the royal seal of authority; Zerubbabel made God's signet, carrying God's authority into the world. Marty: *"He's going to be my signet ring. He's going to carry my authority."*
- **"Shake the heavens and the earth"** — a prophetic figure of speech for God moving/acting, not a literal event. Marty: *"shake them out like a rug... It's a figure of speech for God's going to do something."*

## Illustrations

- **The purse with holes in it.** The futility of self-serving labor: you work and the earnings leak away. Haggai 1:6 (read by Brent): *"You earn wages only to put them in a purse with holes in it."* Marty frames the whole list as "how's that working out for you?"
- **Dr. Phil's "How's that working out for you?"** Marty's modern analogy for Haggai's refrain. *"how well Dr. Phil would say, 'How's that working out for you?'"*
- **Shaking the heavens like a rug.** The domestic image for God's cosmic action — beating out a carpet. Marty: *"I'm going to shake them out like a rug, or I'm going to shake them like a carpet."*
- **Church leadership month after month.** Marty reads the mid-book discouragement through his own pastoral experience: obedience is hard to sustain over seasons, which is why the people drift a couple of months in. *"I really see something that I can relate to as a church leader."*
- **God's tent over Solomon's palace.** The closing contrast: God "loved his tent," and prefers the humble rebuilt temple to Solomon's glory — doing "greater things with that humble project." *"I love that swing back and connection to David, where God says, 'I don't need, I love my tent.'"*

## Discussion Questions

1. Haggai's refrain is **"give careful thought to your ways"** — essentially *how is that working out for you?* Where in your own life have you worked hard, "eaten but never been full," and avoided asking that question honestly?
2. Marty insists Haggai is "not about God's house at all" but about the **heart**. What are the "paneled houses" in your life — the things you've finished and furnished while something God has asked of you "remains a ruin"?
3. God promises the humble second temple a **greater glory** than Solomon's because glory is presence, not grandeur. How does defining glory as God's presence rather than impressiveness change how you measure a church, a ministry, or your own faith?
4. The consecration riddle teaches that **impurity spreads more easily than holiness**. Where have you seen that asymmetry at work — and what does it mean that the "narrow way" takes more intention than drifting?
5. Marty says God "seems to prefer" the **humblest of temples** and that "we don't have to have all our ducks in a row." Is that comforting or uncomfortable for you? Why?
6. Placed near the low end of Session 2's arc — a dispirited remnant rebuilding on ruins — Haggai still hears God say "I am with you" and "be strong." How does reading Haggai as a word to the **unfaithful-yet-not-abandoned partner** change it from a motivational building sermon into part of the one larger story?
