# Creating a Space

BEMA 24 · Session 1
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/1-24-creating-a-space.md`
Book: Exodus (the Tabernacle) — but this is a *practical* episode, not a verse-by-verse exegesis. It steps back from the Tabernacle story to teach spiritual practices, with passing reflection on Genesis 1–3, the woman at the well (John 4), and the Shema (Deuteronomy 6).
Co-host: Brent Billings · Host: Marty Solomon · Guest: Josh Bossé (Impact's BEMA Student Minister)
Core image leaned on throughout: Ray Vander Laan's "Creating a Space" lesson — the reversal "the first time, God created the space and said *you fill it*; this time, God says *you create the space, and I'll fill it*."

> **Session 1 overarching arc: partnership.** This episode makes the partnership explicit and *personal*. The Tabernacle (Episode 23's "mobile Genesis 1–3") becomes a template for how a human being partners with God: God no longer says "I made the space, you fill it" (and we made a mess of that), but "you make the space, and I'll fill it." Marty: *"If we will create space for God, God will fill that space. He may not fill it the way we want... but if we create space for God, God will fill it."* The whole episode is the human half of the covenant partnership — the disciplines by which a person (and a community) builds a Mishkan God can inhabit. Josh keeps pulling it back to relationship: ignoring God's voice through the noise "isn't a relationship" — like earbuds in while your spouse is talking. Partnership here is intimacy, not production.

## Key Lessons

- **The Tabernacle teaches a reversal: *we* now create the space, and God fills it.** Marty relays Ray Vander Laan: *"It's almost as if the first time in creation, God created the space, and He said, 'You fill it.'... And so this time, it's almost as if God says, 'You create the space, and I'll fill it.'"* The disciplines of this episode are how we build that space.
- **If we build no space, we have no grounds to expect to hear God — because that's just not a relationship.** Marty: *"Why would God shout through all the noise... all of our busyness... If we're not going to take any time, any space in our life... why would we expect Him to do that?"* Josh sharpens it: *"if that was happening between you and your friend, we would just acknowledge that's not a good relationship... Like, that isn't a relationship."*
- **Success is intimacy with God, not fruit or impact — those are God's job to supernaturally produce.** Marty reads Impact's definition: *"Success is developing intimacy with God and community with each other through a living relationship with Jesus... We must not allow ministry for God to crowd intimacy with God out of our lives."* We control the space; God controls the fruit.
- **Our culture's drive to produce and "double it every year" is not how God's kingdom works.** Brent: *"Our timing is not God's timing... that's not how God's kingdom works."* Josh connects it to the previous Empire/Shalom episode — the compulsion to produce "wasn't coming from anyone else but my own heart."
- **The goal is a *balanced handful* of practices for a season — not the whole list.** Marty: *"The goal is not to do this whole list. That'd be crazy... have a few disciplines... for a season."* Have some life-giving ones you gravitate toward and one or two that stretch you.
- **Keep it small and genuinely restful — don't quiet your mind only to fill it with the pressure of doing all of this.** Brent: *"don't make it unachievable. Keep it simple... And don't fill it with all the... pressures of doing everything that we're talking about on this episode, that's not really the point."* (He names his own over-reach: "I'm going to memorize Torah." Too big a bite.)
- **The practices co-inform and deepen one another.** Josh: *"they all co-inform each other... practices can deepen other practices"* — e.g., adding a breath of silence before prayer, or pairing simplicity with Sabbath so the Shabbat meal is "nice, but also simple."
- **Spiritual practices are not a power-up or a badge of being "hardcore" — they draw us toward our own humility.** Josh: *"They aren't here to be ways to be spiritually hardcore or next level... not try and get around our own humanity and our own humility."* Fasting especially "breaks you down and makes you feel less spiritual" — and that brokenness is where you finally hear God.

## East/West Lens

- **Intimacy over production (Eastern relationship vs. Western metrics).** The definition of success explicitly refuses Western ministry math — baptisms, flyers, event size — in favor of relationship. Josh: *"that wasn't coming from anyone else but my own heart, and the parts of my heart that aren't able to slow down and be in God's presence."*
- **Praying the *text* — a very Jewish instinct that God has already spoken.** Marty: *"this would be a very Jewish concept. They would say God's already spoken."* The Psalms, the Shema, and (in a high-church key) *Lectio Divina* turn Scripture itself into the words of prayer.
- **The text "talks back" when you slow down and repeat it (Eastern meditation vs. Western information-transfer).** Josh prayed the Shema in Hebrew slowly "so my brain was trying to translate it," and *"the words taking on new depth and new meaning... there were always new levels and new ways for that text to speak."* Marty: a centering prayer, said ~30 times, "will start talking back to you."
- **Gratitude as *blessing the Creator* for a good creation, not guilt-driven thankfulness.** The Jewish practice of up to 70 blessings a day (*Baruch atah Adonai...*) is naming creation good. Josh: *"it isn't that kind of punitive, 'You better be thankful.' It's more like, what are the moments of genuine appreciation that just rise up in daily life?"*
- **Sabbath as counter-Empire rhythm.** Via Brueggemann, Sabbath is "the perfect counter to a culture that is bent on the military industrial complex, the consumption" — rest as resistance, an Eastern rhythm against the Western machine.
- **Feasting (divine indulgence) as a *practice*, recovering a goodness Western piety often forbids.** Marty: *"If self-restraint is creating space for God, so is the proper level of... divine indulgence... some of us... have been taught that any level of self-indulgence is bad and sinful."* The party only "has the ability to speak" within a right rhythm.
- **Fasting as *response*, not transaction.** Against the Western "fast to get an answer," Scot McKnight's claim: *"Fasting is not something we do for something. It's something we do in response to something"* — mourning, humility, breaking down pride, so we can hear.

## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

A sequential walkthrough of every teaching beat, in order. Used to verify full coverage.

1. **Framing.** Brent: today has no particular text — it's "the general story of the Tabernacle," a continuation of last week, turned toward "a study of spiritual practices" with a presentation. A "more practical episode."
2. **The reversal (Ray Vander Laan's "Creating a Space").** Marty: last week the Tabernacle was a "mobile Genesis 1, 2, and 3," a "mobile invitation to trust the story." RVL's line: God once said "you fill it" (it didn't go well); now "you create the space, and I'll fill it." The promise: if we make space, God fills it — maybe not how/when/as we expect.
3. **Josh sharpens the relational stakes.** Ignoring God through the noise "isn't a relationship" — earbuds in while your spouse talks is "a sign of a deeper problem." Josh shares that the Mishkan is deeply personal: he named his firstborn **Ronan** after the first "joyful shout" (*ronan*) in the Hebrew Bible, the root behind Episode 23's title.
4. **The woman at the well — "worship in spirit and in truth" (John 4).** Josh frames the whole episode as that question: *"What does it look like to worship God in spirit and in truth?... create that space where it is just truly intimacy with God?"*
5. **The story of Bill and the lake.** Marty's mentor Bill "challenged" him (a setup) to sit by a lake for eight hours with a Bible and a sack lunch, opening the Bible only if God said to. It took three hours for his mind to quiet; then "intimacy... I couldn't get enough." Bill then began handing him books and contemplative practices.
6. **Impact's definition of success.** Bill was living out Impact's "sauce." Marty reads it in full: success = intimacy with God + community, through a living relationship with Jesus; impact and fruitful ministry occur "supernaturally" and are *not* aimed at; "we must not allow ministry for God to crowd intimacy with God out of our lives."
7. **Josh and Brent on the definition.** Josh: it's why he signed up; it reframes "success" away from numbers and (the miracle) it's actually *practiced*, reshaping community so numbers "have no bearing on who is respected." Brent: it sets aside the culture's exponential-growth drive; "our timing is not God's timing," and forward-points to the 40 desert years.
8. **Community, not just individual intimacy.** Josh: sitting in a room of ministers where numbers don't weight anyone's voice reinforces the intimacy they're building with God.
9. **The question of the episode + the plan.** Marty: will *we* make a (non-physical) space God can fill? He'll walk ~15–18 practices. Goal: a *handful* for a season — some life-giving, one or two that stretch.
10. **Prayer practices (four).** (a) **Prayer journaling** — Marty "hates" it; Leanne Dent's "it's like flossing"; its power is seeing, in hindsight, how God has grown you. (b) **Fixed-hour prayer / the daily office** — alarms at 9/12/3/6/9; stop and pray, even just the Lord's Prayer. (c) **Contemplative prayer** — e.g., the centering/Jesus Prayer ("Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner"), which "starts talking back" after ~30 repetitions. (d) **Praying the text** — "a very Jewish concept"; the Psalms, *Lectio Divina*; turn each line into prayer.
11. **Josh and Brent on prayer.** Josh: fixed-hour prayer is "super cool"; praying the Shema in Hebrew morning/night, slowly translating, the text speaking back "new levels." The **Ninefold Prayer** — the same prayer prayed nine times across people (liked/loved/neutral/enemy) and groups, then yourself — stretches you to pray for enemies what you'd pray for family. Brent: childhood Catholic practice he couldn't appreciate then, but "when you actually do that with intentionality, it does do a work on you."
12. **Transition.** Marty: take just one prayer practice; toy with it for ~90 days.
13. **Practices of presence (three) — Marty's favorites.** **Solitude, Sabbath, and silence.** Definition: "creating space to become more aware of the love, grace, and companionship of God than the companionship, demands, and duties of life and others." The lake day was this. The monk's **balloon illustration**: release them one by one until you hold "the one balloon that God wanted you to see." Marty's crystal-clear realization that day: "I have been neglecting my wife." Brueggemann's Sabbath video as counter-Empire. (Brent confirms it's not paywalled.)
14. **Practicing the presence of God.** Brother Lawrence's classic *The Practice of the Presence of God* — God as constant companion ("God's with me" doing dishes, driving, playing video games); Paul's "pray without ceasing"; a rubber band / string-around-the-finger reminder.
15. **Simplicity.** "Resisting the pull of acquisition, complexity, consumption and hurry through the intentional pursuit of a simple life." Not the minimalism fad — a spiritual practice of stripping down a cluttered area of life. Chris McNaught's *Less Is More Than Enough* (first discussed in Episode 45).
16. **Josh and Brent on presence.** Josh: the practices "co-inform each other"; his over-elaborate 18-course Shabbat meal stopped feeling like Shabbat — so now, "nice, but also simple." Practices can deepen other practices (a breath of silence before prayer). "You will hear things more powerful than anything this podcast will ever put into your ears." Brent: start tiny (a five-item bullet list, not five pages); keep it restful; don't fill the quiet with new pressure.
17. **Generosity (and secrecy).** Marty loves *seasons* of generosity — always carry cash to give; tip extra big — as a *practice*, not a transaction (not about the waiter, about God working in him). The church's tragic reputation as the worst Sunday tippers. Often paired with **secrecy** (anonymous giving; left hand doesn't know the right). Brent notes tipping is culturally specific — find your own form of generosity.
18. **Submission.** Not abusive or moral compromise — leaning into a season under leadership that "grinds your gears" (personality/values mismatch), letting them lead. One of Marty's most formative seasons: "they taught me more... about leadership than any other season of my life."
19. **Gratitude.** The Jewish tradition of up to **70 blessings a day** (*Baruch atah Adonai Eloheinu melech ha'olam*). Marty's honest ceiling (~40s; ~22 tweeted one Thanksgiving). Ann Voskamp's *One Thousand Gifts*. Josh: gratitude as naming creation good (not punitive), powerful in a humbling season — "I'm glad I can hear that bird singing" — found "in the mundanity of life."
20. **Meditating and memorizing.** Meditating on a text all day (Marty's recent personal retreat day, a passage from John). Memorizing: Ray Vander Laan's "quit trying to stack" — memorize your weekly portion, set it aside; retention is "the Holy Spirit's job." Marty has worked through nearly all the Gospels, most of Torah, prophets. Brent: his earliest spiritual practice — hallway memory verses in a K–3 Christian school.
21. **Spiritual direction.** A trained "spiritual companion" (often paid, like a counselor) who listens "with a spiritual ear" for "what God is up to." Distinct from therapy, sometimes held alongside it.
22. **Seasonal practices (three).** (a) **Fasting and self-denial** — food or media; Marty's 40-day and 28-day food fasts (planned entry/exit, nutrients, V8 Fusion, smoothies); Brian McLaren's *Finding Our Way Again* reframed it; Scot McKnight's *Fasting*: a *response* to something, breaking down pride so we can hear. Josh: fasting too hard (no food/water while doing construction, nearly passing out on a ladder) — not a "Mario mushroom boost"; now mostly medical, so he gets creative on Yom Kippur. (b) **Feasting** — "divine indulgence," saying yes to the party / the good bottle of wine / the filet mignon, in right rhythm so the party keeps its power (to be developed in Leviticus). (c) **The church calendar** — Advent (leaning into the dying/darkness where the Christ child is born) and Lent (Ash Wednesday → resurrection), yearly spaces for God to fill.
23. **Closing exhortation.** Marty: don't do them all — a "balanced meal," a doable handful, "spiritual practice wins under your belt." Brent: Session 7 revisits all of this in depth (don't jump ahead); the practices cycle like the church calendar — revisit and re-evaluate yearly.
24. **Josh's Olam ministry.** Josh is Impact's BEMA Student Minister; college students can reach him. **Olam** ("world/eternity") is intimacy-with-God-in-community + creating space, worked out *communally* (not just individually). Contact: midrashjosh@gmail.com.

## Terminology

- **Mishkan** (מִשְׁכָּן) — the Tabernacle, the "dwelling place"; the mobile space God's people built and God filled with His presence — the template for this episode's "create a space, God fills it." Josh: *"the Mishkan itself, the Tabernacle, is very important to me."*
- **ronan / rinnah** (רִנָּה) — a joyful shout; "the first time that word is used in the Hebrew Bible," the root behind Episode 23's "falling on joyful faces" and the name of Josh's firstborn son. Josh: *"my firstborn son is named Ronan because that is the first time that word is used in the Hebrew Bible. It's a word for a joyful shout."*
- **Shema** (שְׁמַע) — "Hear, O Israel..." (Deuteronomy 6); prayed morning and night as a text-centered prayer practice. Josh: *"I would say the Shema morning and night, and talk about the text coming back to you."*
- **Baruch atah Adonai Eloheinu melech ha'olam** (בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם) — "Blessed are you, LORD our God, King of the universe," the opening of a Jewish blessing; a *blessing* is "an expression of gratitude," with a tradition of up to 70 a day. Marty: *"'Blessed are you, Lord, our God, King of the universe' for whatever it is."*
- **Olam** (עוֹלָם) — "world / eternity"; the name of Josh's communal spiritual-practice ministry, built from the definition of success plus the idea of making space. Josh: *"Olam was just me sticking those two things together."*

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty frames the whole episode on Ray Vander Laan's reversal: in creation God made the space and said "you fill it"; now God says "you create the space, and I'll fill it." Where in your life right now are you waiting for God to "fill" something you haven't actually made space for?
2. Josh says ignoring God through the noise "isn't a relationship" — like earbuds in while your spouse is talking. What is the "noise" that most reliably keeps you from hearing God, and what one practice from this episode could begin to turn it down?
3. Impact's definition of success refuses to measure fruit, impact, or numbers — only "intimacy with God and community with each other." If you quietly measured your spiritual life that way for a season, what would you stop doing, and what would you start?
4. Marty counsels a *handful* of practices for a season — some life-giving, one or two that stretch you — not the whole list. Which practice here are you most drawn to (comes easily), and which one scares or bores you enough that it might be exactly the stretch you need?
5. Scot McKnight reframes fasting as a *response* to something, not a transaction to get something; the practices draw us toward humility, not spiritual "hardcore" status. Where might a practice of being broken down (fasting, submission, silence) be more honest for your current season than a practice of producing?
6. Josh describes practices that "co-inform each other" (a breath of silence before prayer; a Shabbat that's "nice, but also simple"). Pick two practices from this episode — how could one deepen the other for you over the next 90 days?
