# Creating a Space

BEMA 24 · Session 1
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/1-creating-a-space-24.md`
Book: Exodus (the Tabernacle) — a **practical episode on spiritual practices**, not a verse-by-verse study
Guest: Josh Bossé (BEMA teaching team; Impact campus ministry; Olam)

> A deliberate pause 24 episodes in: instead of studying the Tabernacle *in* the story, the hosts hold it up as a metaphor for our own lives. In Genesis, God created the space and said "you fill it" (it didn't go well); with the Tabernacle, God flips the roles — **"you create the space, and I will fill it."** The whole episode is a practical tour of ways to create space for God, with the invitation to pick just a few for a season.

## Key Lessons

- **The Tabernacle is a promise: create space for God, and God will fill it.** Via Ray Vander Laan: in creation "God created the space... 'You fill it'"; now "God says, 'You create the space, and I'll fill it.'" Marty: *"if we create space for God, God will fill it. He may not fill it the way we want... but if we create space for God, God will fill it."* God's desire for relationship is constant — the burden on us is only to make room.
- **Success is intimacy with God, not measurable output.** Impact's "sauce": *"Success is developing intimacy with God and community with each other... We must not allow ministry for God to crowd intimacy with God out of our lives."* We control the space and the intimacy; God bears the fruit in God's timing. Josh ties this straight to empire-vs-shalom (ep. 18): the pressure to produce "wasn't coming from anyone else but my own heart."
- **Pick a few practices, not the whole list — a balanced meal, not a gorge.** Marty: have "a few disciplines... for a season" — some life-giving, one or two a stretch. Brent: don't bite off "memorize Torah"; keep it "actually restful." Overloading sabotages the very space you're trying to create.
- **Practices form us; they don't earn us anything (and they humble us).** On fasting especially — it doesn't make you "more spiritual," it breaks you down to your humanity so you can finally hear God. Josh: these aren't a way "to be spiritually hardcore"; beware the "aesthetic of being a spiritual person."

## East/West Lens

- **Faith (relational experience vs intellectual creeds)** — The whole episode is faith as *practice and encounter*, not doctrine. Marty's eight hours by the lake and Josh's "feel it in your bones" presence are experiential, relational knowing — the Eastern mode.
- **Community vs individual** — Intimacy with God is pursued "in the context of Christian community"; Josh's Olam experiments with spiritual practices *communally*, not just privately.
- **Truth over time (dynamic/unfolding)** — Growth is seasonal and slow (the desert's 40 years, the church calendar's annual cycle); "our timing is not God's timing." Practices deepen over years, not in a sprint.
- **Numbers / symbol** — The Jewish aim of *70 blessings a day* as a gratitude practice (70 as fullness/community, echoing earlier episodes).
- **God's description (concrete relationship)** — God is pursued as a present companion ("practicing the presence of God"), not an abstract concept to be proven.

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story (the arc itself).** Creating space *is* trusting the story — believing God will show up and fill it, in God's way and timing. The Tabernacle is "a mobile invitation to trust the story," a mobile Genesis 1–3. God's priorities don't change; the practices are how we posture ourselves toward them. Recurs across the corpus; condensed at the Session 1 capstone (episode 32).
- **Sin as failing to master the animal instinct ("master the beast").** Present as the empire-driven *compulsion to produce and hurry* — an un-mastered impulse Josh locates "in my own heart." The practices (Sabbath, simplicity, submission, fasting) are precisely how one masters it. Same dynamic anchored in `1-master-the-beast-3.md`. Flag for cross-reference; do not re-explain there.
- **Sabbath (callback to eps. 1, 20, 22).** Returns as a core practice of presence and the counter to a consumption/production culture (Brueggemann); explicitly tied to the shalom/empire frame.

## Chiasms & Structure

No chiasm or narrative structure is present — this is a practical episode. In its place, here is the **catalog of practices** the hosts walk through, grouped as they present them. (Guidance: choose a *few* for a season — mostly life-giving, one or two stretching.)

| Group | Practices |
|---|---|
| **Prayer** | prayer journaling ("like flossing"); fixed-hour prayer / the daily office (alarms at 9/12/3/6/9); contemplative / centering prayer (the Jesus Prayer); praying the text (Psalms, Lectio Divina); the Ninefold Prayer (same prayer for someone you like/love/feel neutral toward/suffer to think of — then groups — then yourself) |
| **Presence** | solitude, Sabbath, and silence (Marty's eight hours by the lake; the "balloons" image); practicing the presence of God (Brother Lawrence); simplicity (resisting acquisition/complexity/consumption/hurry) |
| **Heart-formation** | generosity (+ secrecy/anonymity); submission (non-abusive; leaning into seasons you can't control); gratitude (the 70-blessings challenge; Voskamp's *One Thousand Gifts*) |
| **The Text** | meditating on a passage; memorization (Ray's rule: *don't stack* — do your weekly portion, set it aside, move on) |
| **Relational** | spiritual direction (a trained "spiritual companion") |
| **Seasonal** | fasting & self-denial (a *response*, not a transaction); feasting ("say yes to the party"); the church calendar (Advent → the Christ child in the dark; Lent/Ash Wednesday → resurrection) |

## Terminology

- **Mishkan** (מִשְׁכָּן) — the Tabernacle / dwelling place; the space God's people built and God filled with his presence.
- **ronan / rinnah** (רִנָּה) — a joyful shout; the first such word in the Hebrew Bible (behind the prior episode's title and Josh's son's name).
- **Shema** (שְׁמַע) — "Hear, O Israel..."; prayed morning and night as a text-centered practice (Deuteronomy 6).
- **Baruch atah Adonai Eloheinu melech ha'olam** — "Blessed are you, LORD our God, King of the universe"; the opening of a Jewish blessing; tradition encourages ~70 blessings a day.
- **Olam** (עוֹלָם) — "world / eternity"; the name of Josh's communal spiritual-practice ministry.

## Discussion Questions

1. "You create the space, and I'll fill it." Where in your life is there genuinely *no* space right now for God to fill — and what would you have to clear to make some?
2. Impact defines success as intimacy, not output, and refuses to measure the "fruit." Where do you measure your spiritual life by numbers/production, and how does that mirror the empire story?
3. Marty needed hours by a lake before his mind slowed to "one balloon." What would it take for you to create a chunk of solitude long enough to actually get quiet?
4. From the practice catalog, pick *one* life-giving practice and *one* stretching practice to try for 90 days. Why those two?
5. Fasting humbles rather than elevates; feasting ("say yes to the party") is equally a practice. Which do you resist more — self-denial or genuine celebration — and what does that reveal?
6. The 70-blessings challenge measures "how aware are you of how good creation is." Try it for a day. What did noticing (or failing to notice) teach you?
