# Introductory Lesson

BEMA 0 · Session 1
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/1-introductory-lesson-0.md`

> This is the anchor episode for the entire BEMA interpretive approach. It is a "context episode" (no single book of the Bible), so instead of narrative tensions in a text, the whole episode lays out the **East/West interpretive lens** that every later guide tags against. Treat this guide as the reference key for the lens.

## Key Lessons

- **The Bible was written *for* us but not *to* us — read it on its own (Eastern/Hebraic) terms.** Marty: *"The Bible wasn't written to us as modern readers. The Bible was written to an ancient audience and that ancient audience had an ancient context."* When we drag our Western questions onto the text, we "get even more lost." This is the ground floor of trusting the story: the story is constant; our job is to stop distorting it with questions it never set out to answer.
- **The goal is to add the right hand, not amputate the left.** The piano illustration: playing "Old McDonald" with only the left hand (Western base chords), *"I don't think... you would even have recognized that as 'Old McDonald Had a Farm.'"* Both hands together give *"this beautiful, robust, full expression of what the scripture is trying to do."* We are not rejecting our Western identity — Marty: *"It's not a sin to be Western."* God loves **"unity, not uniformity."** The story God is telling has always been for all nations; recovering the Eastern lens lets us hear the song, not change it.
- **Ask the questions the text is asking (who/why), not the ones it isn't (how/what).** Marty quotes a friend: *"When we ask questions the Bible isn't asking, we always get the wrong answers."* Because God's priorities are constant, the fruitful posture is to listen for authorial/communicative intent: *"What did the author mean when they wrote it? What did the audience hear when they heard it?"*
- **Hold truth as unfolding, and dance with what you don't know.** Truth doesn't move; our understanding grows. Marty: *"Truth is not relative. Truth is unfolding."* And the Jewish posture Ray Vander Laan taught him: *"when the Jews don't know something, they will often dance... I dance for the things that I don't know today, but I dance for the things that I will know later."* Trusting the story means trusting its Author across the parts we can't yet see.

## East/West Lens

This episode defines the lens, so it engages nearly every category directly. Each is cited.

- **Words** — Western: abstract definitions, prose, lists. Eastern: word-pictures, images, story. Marty: *"that Eastern Hebrew thinker expresses truth using word pictures and stories... The word is actually a picture, an image."* Example contrast — Western says God is *"omniscient, omnipotent, omnipresent"* (Brent); Eastern says *"God is a strong tower. God is a rock. God is living water."*
- **Numbers** — Western: quantity. Eastern: quality/symbol (no numerals, only letters). Marty: *"If you were to put five apples in a line on the table for the Eastern Jewish thinker, they're going to see Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy."* Also: *"Books of Moses, Tablets of Moses, Days of Creation... five plus two equals seven"* learned qualitatively.
- **Eternal life** — Western: linear, quantitative, begins after death. Eastern: qualitative, *"life lived in harmony with God"* now. Marty: *"Eternal life is in this world... It's not something that begins when my life is over."* (`olam haba`; `aionos zoe` vs `bios`.)
- **Community vs individual** — Western starts with the individual; Eastern starts with the community. Marty: *"It starts with the community and then goes to the individual. And that will come into play with things like sin."*
- **Error & sin** — Western: wrong belief / wrong thinking (`metanoia` as change of mind). Eastern: wrong behavior, *"what a person does."* Marty: *"for the Eastern Hebrew thinker, it's about wrong behavior and it emphasizes what a person does."*
- **God's existence** — Western tries to prove it; Eastern assumes it. Marty: *"The Bible assumes the existence of God. But if we try to make the Bible do what we want it to do, we end up twisting the Bible."*
- **God's description** — Western: nature of the being / abstract attributes. Eastern: nature of the relationship / concrete experience. Marty: *"How does this God relate? How have I literally experienced this God? So abstract versus concrete."*
- **Faith** — Western: intellectual, creeds/doctrines/proof-texts. Eastern: relational, experiences/testimony/story. Marty: *"faith is relational. It's not about creeds and doctrines. It's about experiences with God... There's no attempt to explain or to rationalize, only to give testimony."*
- **Truth** — Western: rational & scientific (how/what). Eastern: religious & experiential (who/why). Marty on the creation story: *"the focus... is on who created, why was it created?... not what happened and how did sin enter the world."*
- **Truth over time** — Western: static/unchanging (absolute vs relative). Eastern: dynamic/unfolding. Marty: *"Truth is not relative. Truth is unfolding... truth just is. And yet we learn more."*

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story (the arc itself).** This episode plants the arc without the exact phrase: God's constancy across the whole narrative. Marty: *"God has always been interested in all nations... God has always been interested in unity, not uniformity."* Everything downstream is an invitation to trust that this God's desires don't change. This through-line recurs in every later episode and is condensed in the Session 1 capstone (episode 32).
- **Sin as failing to master the animal instinct ("master the beast").** Not yet named here, but this episode seeds its soil with the **error & sin** category — Eastern sin is about *behavior*, "what a person does," and about a *communal* reality, not just individual wrong belief. That framing is the setup the "master the beast" episode (`1-master-the-beast-3.md`, its anchor) will build on. Flag for cross-reference; do not re-explain there.

## Terminology

- **olam haba** (עולם הבא) — "the world/age to come"; used of eternal life. Can be used quantitatively, but the Hebrew reference point is qualitative. (From `### Hebrew Words Explained` and Marty: *"in the Hebrew, the phrase is olam haba... their assumption in their mind, their reference point, is qualitative."*)
- **aionos zoe** (αἰώνιος ζωή) — "eternal life"; both words qualitative in Greek — a *quality* of life, not mere endless duration. Marty: *"eternal life is not bios. Eternal life is aionos zoe. And aionos is also a qualitative Greek term."*
- **bios** (βίος) — quantitative, physical/material life (root of "biology"). Marty: *"Bios, we get the word biology from bios. That's quantitative life."*
- **zoe** (ζωή) — qualitative life, the word behind "eternal life." Marty: *"Zoe is a qualitative word in the Greek."*
- **metanoia** (μετάνοια) — "repentance," literally a change of mind/thinking; often preached in a very Western (intellectual) way; Eastern emphasis is changed behavior. Marty: *"the word for repent in the Greek metanoia, a change of mind, a change of thinking. And we talk about it in a very Western way."*
- **hermeneutics** — the discipline of interpretation; Marty names it as the "fancy word" for why BEMA approaches the Bible as it does: *"a more historically informed hermeneutic."*

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty says, *"When we ask questions the Bible isn't asking, we always get the wrong answers."* Where have you personally brought a "how/what" question to a text that was really answering "who/why"? What changed when you tried the other question?
2. The piano illustration claims you might not even *recognize the song* when it's played with only the Western "left hand." Pick a familiar passage — what might you have been missing by hearing only the bass chords?
3. God is described as loving *"unity, not uniformity,"* and as *"always been interested in all nations."* How does trusting that God's priorities never change reshape the way you read a hard or strange passage?
4. The episode ends with "dancing" when you don't know something, versus getting angry or frustrated. What's one unanswered question you could choose to *dance with* rather than force to a conclusion?
5. Eastern thought starts with the **community** and then the individual, including on sin. How might that reorder the way your group reads passages you've always read individually?
