# Introductory Lesson

BEMA 0 · Session 1
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/1-0-introductory-lesson.md`

> This is the anchor episode for the entire BEMA interpretive approach. It is a **context episode** (no single book of the Bible), so instead of narrative tensions in a text, the whole episode lays out the **East/West interpretive lens** that every later guide tags against. Treat this guide as the reference key for the lens. Session 1's overarching arc is **partnership** — recovering the Bible's own (Eastern/Hebraic) terms so we can hear what God has always been inviting humanity into.

## Key Lessons

- **The Bible was written *for* us but not *to* us — read it on its own (Eastern/Hebraic) terms.** Marty: *"The Bible wasn't written to us as modern readers. The Bible was written to an ancient audience and that ancient audience had an ancient context."* When we drag Western questions and assumptions onto the text, Marty says, *"we just tend to get even more lost."* The goal is to *"learn how to think Hebrew"* — not to become Hebrew, but to hear the inspired conversation on its own ground.
- **The goal is to add the right hand, not amputate the left.** The piano illustration: played "Old McDonald" with only the left hand (Western base chords), *"I don't think... you would even have recognized that as 'Old McDonald Had a Farm.'"* Both hands together give *"this beautiful, robust, full expression of what the scripture is trying to do."* We are not rejecting our Western identity — Marty: *"It's not a sin to be Western or to make Western assumptions or to think in a Western way."* God loves *"unity, not uniformity."*
- **Ask the questions the text is asking (who/why), not the ones it isn't (how/what).** Marty quotes a friend: *"When we ask questions the Bible isn't asking, we always get the wrong answers."* The fruitful posture is to listen for authorial/communicative intent: *"What did the author mean when they wrote it? What did the audience hear when they heard it? That conversation is the inspired one."*
- **Hold truth as unfolding, and dance with what you don't know.** Truth doesn't move; our understanding grows. Marty: *"Truth is not relative. Truth is unfolding."* And the Jewish posture Ray Vander Laan taught him: *"when the Jewish mind doesn't know something... they have this positive reaction to it... I dance for the things that I don't know today, but I dance for the things that I will know later."*
- **Give yourself time and grace — new wine bursts old wineskins.** This re-formation is slow and, at first, disorienting. Marty: *"you're taking new wine... and putting it into old wineskins and you will try to hold on to both... at some point those skins will probably burst."* Brent's companion encouragement: *"give yourself grace and lots of time... don't get stuck."*

## East/West Lens

This is the episode that *defines* the lens, so it engages the full slate of categories directly. Each is tagged with a cited quote from the transcript.

- **Words** — Western: abstract definitions, prose, lists/bullet points. Eastern: word-pictures, images, story. Marty: *"that Eastern Hebrew thinker expresses truth using word pictures and stories... The word is actually a picture, an image."* Example contrast — Western says God is *"omniscient, omnipotent, omnipresent"* (Brent); Eastern says *"God is a strong tower. God is a rock. God is living water."*
- **Numbers** — Western: quantity. Eastern: quality/symbol (Hebrew has no numerals, only letters with numeric values). Marty: *"If you were to put five apples in a line on the table for the Eastern Jewish thinker, they're going to see Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy."* Also learned qualitatively: *"Books of Moses, Tablets of Moses, Days of Creation. Five plus two equals seven."*
- **Eternal life** — Western: linear, quantitative, begins after death and is *"detached from this world."* Eastern: qualitative, *"life lived in harmony with God"* now. Marty: *"Eternal life is in this world... It's not something that begins when my life is over."* (`olam haba`; `aionos zoe` vs `bios`.)
- **Community vs individual** — Western starts with the individual (*"It's about me... I'm the center of gravity"*); Eastern starts with the community. Marty: *"It starts with the community and then goes to the individual. And that will come into play with things like sin."*
- **Error & sin** — Western: wrong belief / incorrect thinking, emphasizing *"what a person knows"* (`metanoia` as a change of mind). Eastern: wrong behavior, emphasizing *"what a person does."* Marty: *"for the Eastern Hebrew thinker, it's about wrong behavior and it emphasizes what a person does."*
- **God's existence** — Western tries to prove it; Eastern assumes it. Marty: *"The Westerner, the Greek thinker tries to prove the existence of God. The Hebrew Eastern thinker assumes the existence of God... if we try to make the Bible do what we want it to do, we end up twisting the Bible."*
- **God's description** — Western: the nature of the *being* / abstract attributes. Eastern: the nature of the *relationship* / concrete experience. Marty: *"How does this God relate? How have I literally experienced this God? So abstract versus concrete."*
- **Faith** — Western: intellectual, documented in creeds/doctrines/belief-statements and proof-texted. Eastern: relational, carried in experiences, testimony, and story. Marty: *"faith is relational. It's not about creeds and doctrines. It's about experiences with God... There's no attempt to explain or to rationalize, only to give testimony."*
- **Truth (ultimate truth)** — Western: rational & scientific, focused on *how* and *what*. Eastern: religious & experiential, focused on *who* and *why*. Marty on creation: *"the focus... is on who created, why was it created?... not what happened and how did sin enter the world... Who is humanity? Who is God? And why do we sin in the first place?"*
- **Truth over time** — Western: static/unchanging (framed as absolute vs. relative). Eastern: dynamic/unfolding. Marty: *"Truth is not relative. Truth is unfolding... truth just is. And yet we learn more... Truth doesn't change... but truth is dynamic."*

## Terminology

- **hermeneutics** — the discipline of interpretation; Marty names it as the *"fancy word"* taught in Bible college for why BEMA approaches the Bible as it does, and later calls the aim *"a more historically informed hermeneutic."*
- **olam haba** (עולם הבא) — "the world/age to come"; used of eternal life. Can be used quantitatively, but the Hebrew reference point is qualitative. Marty: *"in the Hebrew, the phrase is olam haba... their assumption in their mind, their reference point, is qualitative."*
- **aionos zoe** (αἰώνιος ζωή) — "eternal life"; both words qualitative in Greek — a *quality* of life, not mere endless duration. Marty: *"eternal life is not bios. Eternal life is aionos zoe. And aionos is also a qualitative Greek term."*
- **bios** (βίος) — quantitative, physical/material life (root of "biology"). Marty: *"Bios, we get the word biology from bios. That's quantitative life."*
- **zoe** (ζωή) — qualitative life, the word behind "eternal life." Marty: *"Zoe is a qualitative word in the Greek."*
- **metanoia** (μετάνοια) — "repentance," literally a change of mind/thinking; often preached in a very Western (intellectual) way, whereas the Eastern emphasis is changed behavior. Marty: *"the word for repent in the Greek metanoia, a change of mind, a change of thinking. And we talk about it in a very Western way."*
- **unity, not uniformity** — Marty's shorthand for God's posture toward the nations: *"God has always been interested in unity, not uniformity... God's not trying to make everyone the same."*

## Illustrations

- **The two windows (from Ray Vander Laan).** Standing 20 years at one window of a room, your view is accurate but incomplete; walked to a doorway on the far side, you see the same room — and a piano you never knew was there. Marty: *"both views are somewhat incomplete. Both views are limited. And Ray said, 'That's the difference between Eastern and Western.'"*
- **The piano / "Old McDonald Had a Farm" (Logan Daily playing).** Left hand = Western foundation/bass chords; right hand = the Eastern melody *"the part that you're going to hum"* (Brent). Left-hand-only is barely recognizable; right-hand-only is *"tinny... a little empty"*; both hands give *"this beautiful, robust, full expression."* Marty notes most of us have only been *"trained to read the Bible through that left-handed perspective."*
- **The Super Bowl (Brent).** A reference everyone gets today would baffle someone 200 years ago — illustrating how far removed we are from the text's shared cultural assumptions. Brent: *"go back 200 years and make a reference to the Super Bowl. Wouldn't work."*
- **"In the beginning, God" (the rabbi and the young student).** The rabbi stops the child three times and says, *"in the beginning, God, if you can remember this, the study of Torah will go well with you"* — the *who*, not the *how/what*.
- **Dancing with the unknown (Jewish posture, via Ray Vander Laan).** *"when the Jewish mind doesn't know something... they have this positive reaction to it... I dance for the things that I will know later."*
- **New wine in old wineskins (Marty, quoting Jesus).** The felt experience of re-forming your hermeneutic: *"you're taking new wine... and putting it into old wineskins... at some point those skins will probably burst."*

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty says, *"When we ask questions the Bible isn't asking, we always get the wrong answers."* Where have you personally brought a "how/what" question to a text that was really answering "who/why"? What changed when you tried the other question?
2. The piano illustration claims you might not even *recognize the song* when it's played with only the Western "left hand." Pick a familiar passage — what might you have been missing by hearing only the bass chords?
3. God is described as loving *"unity, not uniformity"* and as *"always been interested in all nations."* How does trusting that God's priorities never change reshape the way you read a hard or strange passage?
4. The episode ends with "dancing" when you don't know something, versus getting angry or frustrated. What is one unanswered question you could choose to *dance with* rather than force to a conclusion?
5. Eastern thought starts with the **community** and then the individual — including on sin. How might that reorder the way your group reads passages you have always read individually?
6. Marty warns that pouring "new wine into old wineskins" eventually bursts the skins. What is one "old wineskin" (a long-held reading or assumption) you suspect this journey may stretch?
