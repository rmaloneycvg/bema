# Betrayal

BEMA 131 · Session 3
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/3-131-betrayal.md`
Book: Zechariah 11:9–17 and Mark 16:4–8 — a thematic study of two characters (Judas and Peter) in Jesus's final week, not a single linear passage
Hosts: Marty Solomon (host) · Brent Billings (co-host)

> This is a character study of the "betrayals — plural" of Jesus. The hosts rebuild Judas from his cultural setting (a Zealot "man from Keriot") to argue he *misread* Jesus and tried to force a revolution, then set his story against Peter's. The Jewish-reader twist: hand this story to an Orthodox Jew with the labels removed and they name **Peter** as the betrayer, not Judas — because a talmid publicly disassociating from his rabbi is the ultimate offense. The resolution reframes "betrayal" entirely: the one real betrayal of Jesus is believing there is no tomorrow. Judas lets his story end; Peter lets himself be reinstated. In the Session-3 arc — the king/Messiah embodying the whole story — this episode shows the Messiah living out, in the darkest hours of his final week, the exact "trust the story / new tomorrows" grace that ran through Session 1 (partnership) and endured through Session 2 (the partner's unfaithfulness and God's refusal to quit).

## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

1. **Framing: betrayals, plural.** Brent teases "the betrayals — yes, plural — of Jesus." Marty plays it up (*"Plural betrayals? ... It's one betrayal. I know the story of Jesus. Or do we?"*). Brent: *"I think we do, we just don't realize it."*
2. **Why we study: to be changed, not to collect feelings.** Marty: *"we study the Bible to be provoked ... to learn from the story, not just form opinions and get feelings from the story."*
3. **What we know about Judas before the last week.** He's in charge of the money; the Gospel writers insistently brand him "the betrayer"; John hints he dips into the money bag. Marty: *"John's Gospel seems to suggest that Judas is dipping into the money bags. Little thief stealing off the top."*
4. **The name: Judas Iscariot = "man from Keriot."** *"Iscariot is a combination of the Hebrew, ish ... and Keriot ... 'Judas, man from Keriot.'"* Keriot likely a Zealot compound; Zealots sit under the Hasidim with the Pharisees.
5. **The Robin Hood read of the stolen money.** Zealots as *"thieves at heart, believing in a 'Robin Hood mentality.'"* Brent's addition: the ministry is bankrolled by Herod's money (via Chuza), so Judas may be funneling it "back to his brothers."
6. **The Joanna / Chuza / Luke 8:3 funding backstory.** Three women bankroll Jesus; Joanna is wife of Chuza, Herod Antipas's treasurer — so Herod indirectly funds Jesus. Marty: *"Herod Antipas ends up funding Jesus's ministry."*
7. **Judas misreads Jesus — and so do the rest.** After a week of Jesus confronting the chief priests, Judas (a Zealot) is "champing at the bit." Marty: *"what he's trying to do in the betrayal of Jesus is Judas is trying to kickstart the revolution ... to force Jesus's hand."* The disciples broadly think Jesus is a Zealot.
8. **Peter Rollins and *The Fidelity of Betrayal*.** The not-wholly-endorsed theory of a prearranged Jesus–Judas agreement. Marty proposes a *middle ground*: *"Jesus knows where Judas is at."* (Link episode 79 — Zealots.)
9. **Judas's end is scripted by the Text.** Why throw the silver *into the temple* (breaking into a sacred space at night — a very Zealot act)? Because Zechariah 11 says so. Brent reads Zech 11; Marty: *"The story was already written in Zechariah."*
10. **The point of Judas: how often do *we* misread Jesus?** Marty: *"It makes me wonder how often I misread the intent of Jesus"* — the lens-driven mishearing of a teacher who keeps saying the opposite.
11. **The second betrayer: Peter.** Hand the story to an Orthodox Jew with labels removed and they name Peter. The denial in Caiaphas's courtyard (an informal, "mafia-style" Sanhedrin at the high priest's house) is a true betrayal: *"a talmid to publicly disassociate with the rabbi ... the truest form of a slap of the face of his teacher."*
12. **Peter goes back to fishing — assuming he's done.** *"Peter understands that his time as a disciple is over. He has forsaken the rabbi ... He went back to fishing because that was his trade."*
13. **"Go, tell his disciples and Peter" (Mark 16:7).** The angel names Peter so the women won't leave him out. Only John's Gospel records the beach reinstatement. Marty: *"If the angel doesn't specify Peter's name, the women are going to leave him out because Peter is not a disciple anymore."*
14. **The reframe: the only real betrayal is believing there's no tomorrow.** Judas decides his story ends; Peter trusts the God of second (and third) chances. Marty: *"The only true betrayal of Jesus ... would be to believe that there's no tomorrow."* (With an explicit, careful pastoral caveat about suicide.)
15. **This is Session 1 again.** Brent: *"It's Cain and Abel."* Marty ties it to Abraham, Adam and Eve, Jacob, "almost any character in Genesis," plus Lamech and the 70-times-7 forgiveness Peter himself raised.

## Key Lessons

- **"Betrayal" is redefined by how a story *ends*, not by the size of the mistake.** Judas and Peter both fail catastrophically; the difference is the ending. Marty: *"one chooses to let a story end there, and the other chooses to let himself be reinstated."* In the Session-3 arc, the embodied-king is the one who keeps the ending open — the Messiah lives out the "new tomorrows" grace in person.
- **The one true betrayal of Jesus is believing there is no tomorrow.** Marty: *"The only true betrayal of Jesus and what he stood for would be to believe that there's no tomorrow, to believe that there's no coming back from this."* This is the clearest Session-3 restatement that the whole story (Session 1's partnership, Session 2's refusal-to-quit God) culminates in a Messiah who *is* bottomless grace.
- **Rabbinically, honest mistakes aren't betrayal — disassociation is.** Marty: *"if your intent is that you're trying to do what the rabbi's asking you to do, that's not a betrayal ... you're just making a mistake."* Judas misjudged the mission; Peter denied the relationship — the deeper offense.
- **We constantly misread Jesus through our own lens.** Marty: *"It makes me examine how often I might misunderstand what Jesus is trying to do in the world because of how I choose to hear some of his teachings while ignoring the text."* The disciples heard a Zealot revolution in a rabbi who spent three years teaching enemy-love.
- **Knowing the Text changes what you see — Judas's end is quoting Scripture.** Marty: *"How badly we need to know our Bibles in order to truly understand the parts that we do read."* The thirty-pieces-into-the-temple is Zechariah 11 enacted, not an improvised gesture.
- **Reinstatement requires trusting that the rabbi means it.** Marty: *"Peter is willing to trust that when a rabbi says he's still worth saving and still fit to feed sheep, he means it."* The embodied king doesn't just forgive in principle; he walks a beach and does it.
- **Identity isn't defined by your worst moment.** Marty: *"you aren't defined by your biggest mistakes, your deepest insecurities, or your worst fears."* This is the Session-1 Genesis gospel ("trust the story even in failure") now embodied by Jesus himself.

## East/West Lens

- **Word-pictures over abstractions (name as data).** The whole Judas reconstruction hangs on decoding a name rather than defining a doctrine: *"what might help us the most for today ... is to look at his name ... 'Judas, man from Keriot.'"*
- **Community/relational honor over individual conscience (the rabbi–talmid bond).** "Betrayal" is measured against the discipleship relationship, not inner sincerity: *"For a talmid to publicly disassociate with the rabbi is unheard of in rabbinical discipleship, the truest form of a slap of the face of his teacher."*
- **Living in the Text (Scripture as script, not proof-text).** A Hebraic actor behaves *out of* the Text in real time: *"Judas responds to his plight with great statements from the Text ... The story was already written in Zechariah."*
- **Mystery/tension held open rather than resolved (both/and).** On the Rollins prearrangement theory, Marty refuses a clean binary: *"maybe there's a middle ground here. It's not necessarily a flat-out prearrangement ... Yet, Jesus knows where Judas is at."*
- **The story is one continuous story (Jesus embodies Genesis).** Brent: *"It's Cain and Abel."* Marty: *"It's the Gospel that has been there from the very beginning."* Eastern reading reads the Gospels backward into Torah rather than treating them as a new, detached message.

## Recurring Through-lines

*(Motifs genuinely present in this episode, each backed by a citation. No registry consulted.)*

- **Trust the story / new tomorrows (the Session-1 spine, now embodied by Jesus).** Explicitly named: *"This story has always been a story about new tomorrows. This story has always been about allowing God to write incredible endings."* Peter trusts it; Judas doesn't.
- **The God of second chances / bottomless grace.** Marty: *"the God of second chances, the God of third chances, the God of endless hope, bottomless grace, and wasteful love."* The reinstatement of Peter is this through-line made flesh.
- **You are not defined by your worst mistake (identity before performance).** *"you aren't defined by your biggest mistakes, your deepest insecurities, or your worst fears."* The Session-1 Genesis-identity motif carried into the Messiah's final week.
- **Zealots and the misread revolution.** The recurring Session-3 backdrop: *"most of the disciples think that he is a zealot ... they're shocked that he turns himself over."* Judas is the sharpest case of the whole group's misreading.
- **Forgiveness / 70-times-7 (Cain, Lamech).** Brent and Marty close by linking back to Cain's line: *"the whole connection to Lamech and Cain's line"* and *"'I've killed a man for wounding me' — that whole thing."*

## Terminology

- **Judas Iscariot / "man from Keriot"** — *ish* (man) + *Keriot*, likely a Zealot compound. Marty: *"'Judas, man from Keriot.' That's what his name means."*
- **Zealot** — the sword-carrying wing of the Hasidim who despised Rome and the chief-priest collaborators; *"thieves at heart, believing in a 'Robin Hood mentality' of taking what was needed for the greater good."*
- **Hasidim** — the larger movement under which Zealots and (non-sword-carrying but sympathetic) Pharisees sit. Brent names it; Marty: *"The Hasidim, right."*
- **talmid** — a disciple of a rabbi; to publicly disassociate is the cardinal offense. Marty: *"For a talmid to publicly disassociate with the rabbi is unheard of."*
- **havurah** — the fellowship/band of disciples; Peter was *"the ringleader of the havurah."*
- **remez** — a hint pointing back to another passage; Jesus's "sword" sayings are *"usually remezes hinting back at other prophecies."*
- **the thirty pieces of silver / "thrown to the potter"** — Zechariah 11's shepherd's wages; the script behind Judas throwing the coins into the temple.
- **"God-Goggle statement"** — Marty's shorthand for a line that *could* be read as divine foreknowledge (e.g., "do quickly") versus plain rabbinic awareness.

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty says the real difference between Judas and Peter is that "one chooses to let a story end there, and the other chooses to let himself be reinstated." Where in your own life are you tempted to decide a story is over — and what would "trusting a new tomorrow" look like there?
2. The disciples heard a Zealot revolution in a rabbi who spent three years teaching enemy-love. Marty asks, "how often I misread the intent of Jesus ... because of how I choose to hear some of his teachings while ignoring the text." Where might your lens be making Jesus say what you want to hear?
3. Rabbinically, an honest mistake isn't betrayal, but public disassociation from the rabbi is. How does that reframe which of your failures actually damage your relationship with God — and which are "just" the learning process?
4. Judas acts "out of the Text" even at his end, quoting Zechariah with his body. What does it look like for *you* to know the Text well enough that it scripts your instincts — for better or worse?
5. The angel says "and Peter" so the women won't leave him out. Who in your community has quietly assumed they're "no longer a disciple," and what would it mean to name them back in?
6. Marty insists "the only true betrayal of Jesus ... would be to believe that there's no tomorrow." Is there a place where believing in no coming-back has become, for you, the actual betrayal of the story?
