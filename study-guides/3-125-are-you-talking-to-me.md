# Are You Talking to Me?

**BEMA 125 · Session 3** — Matthew (Jesus's final week: three parables aimed at the corrupt priesthood — Matthew 21:28–22:14)
Source transcript: `transcripts/3-125-are-you-talking-to-me.md`

> **Session 3 arc — the king/Messiah embodying the story.** In Session 1 God found a partner and charged him to bless the world; in Session 2 the partner proved unfaithful and God refused to quit, pursuing with "bottomless patience." Here, in Jesus's final week, that same God shows up *in person* and keeps doing the exact same thing — Marty names it directly: "Jesus looks an awful lot like the God of Session 2, or the God of Session 1... the redemption cycle and this God's never-ending patience." Jesus has spent three years partnering with and deconstructing the Pharisaical world; now he spends one week going on the offensive against the corrupt chief priesthood (the Sadducees), "serenading the priests with parables." The three parables are the Messiah embodying the story and advancing the plot toward the cross: the partner's unfaithfulness has concentrated in a priestly mafia hoarding God's fruit, and the king comes to confront it, foretell its doom, and throw his party open to the outsiders — the mamzer.

---

## Key Lessons

Read for the setting: Jesus is not raging at Judaism or at the Pharisees but at a specific, corrupt priestly power, and he does it with three parables built on the Hebrew prophets. The "weird" turns (the un-dressed wedding guest, the mishearing of the tenants) are clues, not clutter — they point back to Isaiah, the Psalms, and Zephaniah.

- **Jesus confronts religious corruption, not Judaism — and that distinction is everything.** The partner's unfaithfulness in Session 2 has crystallized into a corrupt priesthood; the Messiah targets *that*, not the people and not the Pharisees. Cited: *Marty* — "A very, very corrupt religious mafia... that seven family mafia that made up the chief priesthood in that second temple period, they are the ones that killed Jesus. It's total corruption. It's not Judaism. It's not the Pharisees." This is how the one story advances: God-in-person goes after the thing that keeps God's people "from the mission of God."

- **Three years of partnership, one week of confrontation.** The arc of patient partnership (Sessions 1–2) runs straight through Jesus's ministry with the Pharisees; the one week against the priesthood is the exception that gets him killed, and he knows it. Cited: *Marty* — "Jesus is going to spend three years with the Pharisees. He'll spend one week—one week!—with the Sadducees, and it's going to get him crucified and he knows it... 'Nobody is going to do what I'm about to do and live to tell the tale.'" The Messiah embodies the story by walking knowingly into death.

- **The vineyard is still bearing fruit — the problem is the tenants hoarding it.** Isaiah 5's vineyard produced sour grapes; now the people *are* producing fruit, but the priesthood won't hand it over. Cited: *Marty* — "The Jewish people are bearing fruit, and God's come to get it so that the people—this isn't a condemnation of Jewish people. This is a condemnation of the priesthood." The partner is finally fruitful; the corrupt middlemen are the obstacle.

- **You've got the enemy backwards.** The priests expect God to evict the Romans so they can return to luxury; Jesus (via Isaiah 61) says foreigners tending the vineyard is God's *good* design, not the enemy. Cited: *Marty* — "You think the foreigners are the tenants? The foreigners are supposed to be the tenants in God's good world... you've got it all backwards. You've got the enemy wrong." The Messiah redefines who the mission is *for*.

- **The Kingdom is a party, and God will have it one way or another.** The firstborn RSVP first but refuse, so the hall fills with outsiders — "insiders, outsiders, mamzers." Cited: *Marty* — "God will have his party, Brent Billings... he fills his banquet hall with anyone who is willing to come and be a part of a celebration, insiders, outsiders, mamzers." God's relentless, never-quitting pursuit (Session 2) becomes an open invitation to the least likely.

- **It is possible to want God's party but refuse God's way.** The guest without wedding clothes wants in on the celebration but won't be consecrated/dressed by the king. Cited: *Marty* — "a person who wants to be a part of the celebration but not recognize God's desire, design, or authority. He wants to be a part of God's will, he just doesn't want to do it God's way." Marty turns this on the religious listener: "I think there are far too many of us that probably fit that category."

- **God's party may not look like the party I would throw.** The haunting self-examination of the episode: the religious miss the party precisely because it comes at the wrong time, place, and in the wrong way for their expectations. Cited: *Marty* — "Am I so distracted by the worries of this world? My own personal agenda and the idolatry of self to hear the invitation to God's party? Do I even see the party that God's throwing?"

## East/West Lens

- **Words (word-pictures / idiom over definition).** The whole episode turns on Hebrew word-pictures the English flattens — *betushim*, *mamzer*, *s'micha*, "strange clothes." Cited: *Marty* — "I found betushim. God's people weren't producing fruit, but now, Brent, they are." The parables *mean* by image and allusion, not by abstract definition.

- **Scripture interpreted by scripture (remez / allusion over proof-text).** Jesus teaches by hinting back to known texts; the hosts track the layers — *p'shat* then *remez* then *drash*. Cited: *Marty* — "We're just on p'shat. I think Jesus could also be planting a remez... I think he could be referencing us back to Isaiah 61." The meaning is built by *linking* passages (Isaiah 5, Psalm 118, Isaiah 61, Zephaniah 1), the Hebraic way of reading.

- **Community over individual (five people groups, not individuals).** Understanding the week requires knowing the *groups* — Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes — and their corporate stories, not just individual actors. Cited: *Marty* — "we got to go back and we have to review those five people groups, because this is critical to understanding what's happening in Jesus's final week." Even John is placed by his group: *Brent* — "Being from the Essenes."

- **Error & sin (corrupt *doing* over wrong believing).** The indictment is about what the priesthood *does* — hoarding, exploiting, mistreating — not a doctrinal error. Cited: *Marty* — "they're not giving God what is rightfully His... They continue to take advantage of those who are trying to find and worship God."

- **God's description (relentless, concrete pursuit over abstract attribute).** God is pictured as a landowner who keeps sending servants and a king who keeps sending invitations — relational persistence, not an abstract trait. Cited: *Marty* — "He paints a picture of God's relentless pursuit of his people and an invitation that keeps going out but is ignored, disrespected, and despised."

## Recurring Through-lines

- **The redemption cycle / God's bottomless patience (the Session 2 backbone, now embodied).** The landowner who sends servant after servant, the king who sends invitation after invitation — this is Judges' redemption cycle walking around in Jesus. Cited: *Marty* — "The redemption cycle and this God's never-ending patience. Bottomless patience. This is what Jesus has been portraying... Over and over again in Judges and then over and over again through the Gospels." The arc's constant — God refuses to quit on the partner — is incarnate here.

- **The mamzer / outsider welcomed (Matthew's agenda).** The banquet hall filled with "the bad as well as the good" is Matthew's recurring insistence that the outsider gets in. Cited: *Brent* — "The mumzer." / *Marty* — "The mamzer. It's seen again here in full effect in this parable, God will have his party... insiders, outsiders, mamzers." The blessing promised to the partner (Session 1) is now flung open to everyone willing to come.

- **Trust the story / the mission doesn't change.** Jesus insists the priests have inverted the mission — foreigners in the fields was always the good design (Isaiah 61), and remembering the mission is the way back. Cited: *Marty* — "When you finally remember the mission that God sent you here, you will have Romans tending your fields... because that's the world that God is building." The priority God set at the start is the standard the Messiah holds the priesthood to.

- **Weird is a clue (the author links stories on purpose).** The jarring wedding-clothes turn is a deliberate signal pointing to Zephaniah 1. Cited: *Brent* — "Weird is a clue." / *Marty* — "Weird's a clue... we call it a remez. This remez, I believe, comes from Zephaniah 1." The same reading method that runs through the whole corpus.

## Terminology

- **s'micha / smicha (סמיכה)** — rabbinic ordination / authority; what the priests demand of Jesus to challenge his credibility and sniff out who backs him. Cited: *Brent* — "Smicha." / *Marty* — "they may be trying to sway public opinion about his credibility, as well as find out who else is behind this young upstart's ministry."
- **Pharisees (P'rushim) vs. Sadducees (Zadokim) vs. Essenes** — the people groups essential to the week. Jesus works *within* the Pharisaical world for three years; the corrupt chief priesthood are the Sadducees ("sons of Zadok"); John is an Essene. Cited: *Marty* — "Jesus has been operating as a Pharisee. He's been operating as a completely different kind of Pharisee."
- **betushim** — the "sour / stinking / wild grapes" Isaiah 5's vineyard produced instead of good fruit. Cited: *Brent* — "Betushim." / *Marty* — "I found betushim. God's people weren't producing fruit."
- **mamzer / mumzer** — the outsider / one of questionable birth; "Matthew's agenda" — the banquet fills with mamzers when the invited refuse. Cited: *Marty* — "he fills his banquet hall with anyone who is willing to come... insiders, outsiders, mamzers."
- **p'shat / remez / drash** — levels of reading: the plain sense (*p'shat*), the hinted allusion (*remez*), the deeper exposition (*drash*). The hosts name each as they move through the parables. Cited: *Marty* — "We're just on p'shat. I think Jesus could also be planting a remez." / "The drash of this parable turns into a scathing retort of the Sadducees."
- **sons of Zadok (Zadok & Abiathar)** — the two Aaronic descendants in David/Solomon's day from whom the priesthood was selected (Zadok chosen); Marty's proposed remez behind the Parable of Two Sons. Cited: *Marty* — "They are sons of Zadok. In my opinion, I think Jesus is calling back to Abiathar and Zadok. Which son is going to get the priesthood?"
- **"strange / foreign clothes"** — Zephaniah 1's phrase for those under judgment; the hook linking the un-dressed wedding guest to the prophecy. Cited: *Marty* — "There's the reference, foreign or 'strange clothes.' I will punish the officials and the king's sons and all those clad in strange clothes."
- **consecrate** — to set apart, to bathe/prepare (as a bride bathes before her wedding); the king "consecrating" his invited guests in Zephaniah mirrors providing wedding attire. Cited: *Marty* — "that means to set apart, to bathe, to remember a bride bathes before her wedding."

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty insists it was a corrupt priesthood — not Judaism or the Pharisees — that killed Jesus. Where do we flatten "the enemy" in a story (or in our own church world) into a whole group, and what changes when we name the actual corruption instead?
2. Jesus spent "three years" partnering and only "one week" confronting. In your own relationships and communities, what is the ratio of your patience to your confrontation — and does it look like "bottomless patience"?
3. The priests had "the enemy backwards," expecting God to evict the outsiders when outsiders in the vineyard were God's good design. Who are the "foreigners in the field" you assume are the problem but who might be part of God's intended world?
4. Marty's reflection questions, posed directly to listeners: *Am I insistent that God throw the kind of party I would throw? Am I blinded by the fact that God's definition of a party isn't my definition? Am I so distracted by my own agenda and the idolatry of self that I don't even see the party God is throwing?* Sit with one of these.
5. The guest wanted the party but not the king's wedding clothes — he "wants to be a part of God's will, he just doesn't want to do it God's way." Where are you trying to attend God's party on your own terms?
6. Brent's Easter dinner became "just Kingdom" when the invited canceled and a stranger came. When have you watched a plan collapse and the Kingdom show up through the unexpected guest instead?
7. "Weird is a clue." Where in Scripture (or in your own life) have you been tempted to skip past something that doesn't fit — and what might it be pointing you toward if you treated it as a remez?
