# A Strengthened Heart

BEMA 19 · Session 1
Source transcript: `transcripts/1-19-a-strengthened-heart.md`

## Key Lessons

- **The ten plagues are God's relentless pursuit of Pharaoh's heart — not just the rescue of Israel.** Marty: *"God's not just trying to get His people rescued. He's also trying to get Pharaoh rescued. Why ten plagues? ... If He was just trying to rescue His people, He'd probably do it in one plague."* This is the arc itself — **trust the story; God does not change what he wants or what matters.** The God who hears the cry and loves the outsider loves even Pharaoh; his desire to redeem is constant, extending to his enemies.

- **There are two "hardenings," not one — and they change the whole Calvinist/Arminian debate.** *kavod* (heavy — Pharaoh cannot *see*) vs *chazak* (strengthened — Pharaoh *sees* and is given resolve). Marty: *"all of that conversation was void of a God really pursuing Pharaoh's heart."* God even *strengthens* Pharaoh so he can face the real choice; only when Pharaoh finally *chazak*s his own heart does God respect that decision and move on.

- **God's name shift (El Shaddai → YHWH) marks a shift from a God known for restraint to a God known for what he does.** Marty: *"God wants to be known not for what He doesn't do, but for what He does do."* The patriarchs knew "the one who said to his world, enough" (restraint); now God reveals the timeless, personal name and acts to redeem — the same God, a fuller revelation (truth unfolding over time).

- **God calls Israel to be his *bechor* — his firstborn — meaning a double portion of *responsibility*, to be his message to the world.** Marty: *"I don't need you to have a message to give to the world. I need you to go be the message."* What God has always wanted — a partner who carries his values to everyone else — now scales from one man (Abraham) to a whole nation.

- **The Exodus is a story of being "born again" — out through a bloody door, through the Red Sea birth canal.** Marty, via the rabbinic reading: *"they wanted the readers... to see a birth image. They were leaving in haste... through a bloody door."* Water and spirit (*ruach*) are Exodus language that reappears in Jesus' words to Nicodemus — one continuous story.

## East/West Lens

- **Truth over time — dynamic/unfolding understanding** — God's self-revelation grows from El Shaddai to YHWH without the God changing. Marty: *"by my name 'the LORD' I was not known to them... now God gives Himself a different name."* Our understanding deepens; the God does not move.

- **God's description — nature of the relationship over abstract attributes** — The Monopoly/Parker Brothers image frames God relationally and by presence, not by abstract definition. Marty: *"how do we comprehend somebody who is outside the world that He created? ... I'm timeless, God might say."* Monotheism means God is *intimately present on the board with you* — a relationship, not a disconnected pantheon.

- **Faith as relational over intellectual (creeds/proof-texts)** — Pharaoh literally cannot grasp a God you would *love* and who loves you. Marty, voicing Pharaoh: *"I don't understand a concept where you would love a God and a God would love you... the gods... they're pretty much angry all the time."* The covenant God is known by love-relationship, not by the transactional terms of polytheism.

- **Error & sin as perception/behavior over wrong belief** — The hardening language is about *seeing* and *resolve* (what Pharaoh perceives and does), not about holding a wrong doctrine. *kavod* = "he can't see it"; *chazak* = "I do see it and I choose to power through."

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story (the arc itself).** Named directly: the whole episode reframes a "crazy story of God wheeling and dealing plagues" as God pursuing hearts — his constant priority. Marty closes: *"we see the heart of God, not just for His people, but with all those outside of His people... a God really pursuing, really chasing down... I want all of you to say yes to my love."* This continues the Episode 17 "God who hears the cry" thread — God runs the story out "to shalom's end," and his desire to redeem (even enemies) never changes. The through-line recurs across the whole corpus.

- **The God who knows when to say "enough" (El Shaddai).** An explicitly recurring BEMA motif: Marty ties this name back to Episode 2 ("the God who knows when to say enough"), reading *Shaddai* via the midrash as *Mi She'amar Leolamo Dai*. It links God's restraint in creation and in the Flood to the restraint-vs-action contrast here. (Noted as a cross-episode recurrence per the hosts' own callback; added to the running motif list.)

- *(Note: the "sin as failing to master the animal instinct" / master-the-beast motif, anchored in `1-3-master-the-beast.md`, is not engaged this episode. Pharaoh's resistance is framed through the *kavod/chazak* seeing-and-resolve lens, not the beast/instinct lens, so the motif is not claimed.)*

## Chiasms & Structure

The hosts do not present a chiastic or mirrored structure this episode. The organizing device is Fohrman's **question-cascade** method (stacking ~10-20 unanswered questions, then unwinding them in reverse) and the **two-word hardening study** (*kavod* / *chazak*). Because there is no clearly mirrored, center-weighted structure described, this section is omitted rather than fabricated.

## Terminology

- **El Shaddai** (אֵל שַׁדַּי) — the patriarchs' name for God, usually "God Almighty." The rabbis (Genesis Rabbah 5:8) read *Shaddai* as *Mi She'amar Leolamo Dai* — *"the one who said to his world, enough."* Marty: *"the God who knows when to say enough."*
- **YHWH** (יהוה, Yod-Heh-Vav-Heh) — the timeless, personal name revealed in Exodus 6 ("I am that I am"), rendered LORD. Marty: *"the focus seems to be on its timelessness, God's timelessness."* ("Jehovah" is a misnomer from mixing in the vowels of *Adonai*.)
- **Elohim** (אֱלֹהִים) — the generic word for God/gods (plural feel); the concept Pharaoh recognizes, contrasted with the intimate covenant name. Marty: *"Moses is dancing with those names."*
- **kavod** (כָּבֵד) — heavy, weighty, stubborn. Marty: *"when Pharaoh's heart is kavod-ed, it means it's made heavy... he can't see it."*
- **chazak** (חָזַק) — to be strong / to strengthen; resolve. Marty: *"chazak is when I do see it and I choose to power through."* (cf. *chazak v'ematz*, "be strong and courageous.")
- **bechor** (בְּכוֹר) — the firstborn; not just a double portion of inheritance but a *double portion of responsibility*. Marty: *"the bechor has this calling to carry on the legacy... God is asking His people to be the bechor."*
- **Shema** (שְׁמַע) — "Hear, O Israel," the first command in the tefillin.
- **Ve'ahavta** (וְאָהַבְתָּ) — "Love the LORD your God...," the second command in the tefillin.
- **ruach** (רוּחַ) — spirit/wind; the *ruach* over the Red Sea opening the "birth canal" — water and spirit as Exodus language echoed in John 3 (Nicodemus).

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty says the ten plagues are God's pursuit of *Pharaoh's* heart, not just Israel's rescue — "this is how much God loves even our enemies." How does that reframing change the way you read the Exodus? Who is the "Pharaoh" you'd struggle to believe God is pursuing?
2. The two hardenings — *kavod* (can't see) and *chazak* (sees and resolves) — reframe the free-will debate. Where in your own life have you been *kavod*-hearted (unable to see what's right in front of you) versus *chazak*-hearted (seeing clearly but choosing to resist)?
3. God shifts from being known for restraint (El Shaddai) to being known for action (YHWH). What does it mean that the same God is revealed more fully over time, and where might your own understanding of God still be growing?
4. To be God's *bechor* is to *be* the message, not just carry one. What would it look like for your community to live as a "firstborn" — embodying God's values rather than merely talking about them?
5. The hosts read the Exodus as being "born again" through a bloody door and a Red Sea birth canal. How does seeing liberation as *rebirth* (water and spirit) reshape what deliverance means to you?
