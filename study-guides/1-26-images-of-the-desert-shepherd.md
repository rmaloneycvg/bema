# Images of the Desert - Shepherd

BEMA 26 · Session 1
Source transcript: `transcripts/1-images-of-the-desert-shepherd-26.md`

## Key Lessons

The arc of Session 1 holds steady here: **trust the story — God does not change what he wants or what matters.** The desert is where Israel learns experientially the very thing God has wanted all along — a people who will trust his provision. Marty frames the whole episode as the next step after the wedding (Sinai) and honeymoon suite (the tabernacle): "now they have to learn experientially. They have to *yada*... that's what they're going to do. And they're going to do that in the desert."

- **The desert is the place of the word, not the place of things seen.** The lesson is to become "people of the ears and not people of the eyes." Marty: "God will use the desert to teach us how to become people of the ears and not people of the eyes... It's a 'place of word' and it's a 'place of voice.'" This is the constant priority: God wants a people who hear and follow his voice, exactly as he has from creation onward.

- **"Just enough" is the shepherd's provision, and that is the point — not scarcity, but trust.** Marty rereads Psalm 23 against American abundance: "All my needs are met because I have just enough... exactly what I need and nothing more." He links it to Deuteronomy: "Man doesn't live by bread alone, but on every word — every *dabar* — that comes from the mouth of God... you learned how to wait on God's provision, the manna, the daily." God's desire that his people trust his care has never changed.

- **The desert is a season to pass through, not a destiny — and God is in the middle of it.** Elle: "the desert isn't where they live forever... they're on their way, they're going somewhere else, but they have to learn lessons in the desert first... you can know that God is in the middle of it with us." Trusting the story means trusting that the God who leads into the desert is the same faithful God leading out of it.

- **God is looking for sheep, not goats — those who listen over those who rely on their own strength.** Marty: "God's looking for sheep, not goats. Seems to be one of the clear things that we pull out of those teachings." Elle sharpens it: the word for goat carries the sense of strength — "Is that who God's really looking for? Or is he looking for those who are able to listen?"

## East/West Lens

- **Words** (abstract definitions vs. word-pictures/images/story). The central engagement of the episode. Elle on Hebrew word trees: "in Hebrew, every word is built using three consonants... those come out like different branches from this tree... always related meaning to whatever the base image... is." Marty: "Hebrew is built off of images and pictures, not precise definitions... you're packing a lot more into a Hebrew word because of its picture and its ability to speak... not because of its precision, but because of its illustration." Elle confirms English holds "a lot more abstract concepts... love" while Hebrew is "more picture-based and therefore more concrete."

- **Truth / knowing (how/what vs. who/why; rational vs. experiential).** The desert teaches by lived experience, not information. Marty: "It's one thing to know about it... It's another thing to actually be shaped and formed and experience that relationship." Israel must "*yada*" — know relationally and experientially — not merely identify the pieces.

- **God's description (abstract attributes vs. nature of the relationship/concrete experience).** God is described not with propositions but through the concrete shepherd image. Marty: "it's such a dominant idea in your scriptures that God led us through the desert, that God was a shepherd leading us like a flock through the desert... following the voice of our shepherd."

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story (the arc itself).** Named explicitly as the spine of Session 1. Marty's long review: "we're trusting the goodness of the God behind the creation... to trust in his provision and his care." The desert is the training ground for that trust — "I'm going to trust that my shepherd will lead me to my next bite." This through-line runs across the whole corpus; here it takes the concrete form of waiting on daily provision.

- **Strength as a thing to be surrendered in the desert (a motif the episode seeds forward).** Elle's observation that "the word for goat means strength" prompts Marty to name an emerging desert theme: "What is our strength actually able to do for us? And what is God inviting us to learn about our strength, our own strength in the desert?" He flags it as recurring: "That's going to come up more than a couple times in our images of the desert." (Candidate addition to the seeded through-line list: *human strength vs. dependence on God* across the desert episodes.)

## Terminology

- **midbar** (מִדְבָּר) — desert / wilderness, literally the "place of speaking." Root *d-b-r* with an *m-* prefix. Elle: "What they are literally saying is place of speaking... that is the etymological — the Hebraic background to every scene that's in the desert."
- **davar / dabar** (דָּבָר / דִּבֶּר) — word / to speak; a weighty, enduring word. Elle: "if Marty Solomon is giving me a word... that I should keep with me for the next five years, that is a *davar*... it has a little bit more weight to it than *amar*." The book of Deuteronomy is *Devarim*, "the Book of Words... the Book of Things."
- **amar** (אָמַר) — to say; casual speech. Elle: "'I'm just talking to...', 'Elle talked to Brent'" — contrasted with the weightier *davar*.
- **Hebrew "word trees"** — every word built on three root consonants, with vowels/added letters branching related meanings around one base image. From *d-b-r* branch: word, commandment, "to come into agreement or discuss," and even "plague." Illustrates the picture-based, concrete nature of Hebrew.
- **goat = strength** — Elle: "The word for goat means strength... Are you trying to be the strong person? Is that who God's really looking for? Or is he looking for those who are able to listen?" A lens on the sheep-and-goats parable.

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty rereads Psalm 23 as a psalm of "just enough" rather than abundance. Where in your life have you confused God's provision with American-style comfort — and how would "just enough" change the way you pray or wait?
2. The desert is the "place of the word" — a place of the ears, not the eyes, and a disorienting place where you "could get lost." When has a disorienting, hard season actually made you better at *hearing* God? What did the loss of sight (clarity, control) teach you?
3. Elle notes the desert "isn't where they live forever... they're on their way." How does believing a desert season is *temporary and purposeful* — rather than a punishment or a destiny — change how you move through it?
4. Sheep follow the shepherd's voice; goats "know a better way" and rely on their strength, yet both are in the same flock. Honestly, which are you right now, and in what specific area is God inviting you to listen rather than lean on your own strength?
5. The shepherds in the biblical world were often teenage girls with little brothers in tow — not the figures we imagine. Where else might your mental picture of a familiar Bible story carry an unexamined Western or cultural assumption, and how could checking that reshape the meaning?
