# Images of the Desert - Shepherd

BEMA 26 · Session 1
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/1-26-images-of-the-desert-shepherd.md`
Book: Numbers (the desert / wilderness; read passage: Psalm 23)
Co-host: Brent Billings · Host: Marty Solomon · Guest: Elle Grover Fricks (BEMA teaching team; Hebrew / ancient Near East)

> **Session 1 overarching arc: partnership.** This is the first of a short series on the **images of the desert**, and it marks the story's arrival at the book of Numbers — framed as the "desert honeymoon period" after the wedding at Sinai and the honeymoon suite of the tabernacle. Having learned *who* God is, *who* they are, and their mission ("a kingdom of priests"), God's people now have to learn the relationship **experientially** — they have to *yada*. The desert is the classroom for that formation. Through the image of the shepherd, the episode teaches that the desert is the *midbar*, the "place of the word," built on the same root as *dabar* — so to walk the desert is to learn to live by "every word that comes from the mouth of God." The shepherd leads not with a beating staff but with *voice*; the sheep get "just enough" and must trust the shepherd for the next bite. The partnership question lands as a choice between sheep and goats: will God's people listen and follow the shepherd's voice, or rely on their own strength and "know a better way"? Marty: "now they have to learn experientially. They have to *yada*… And they're going to do that in the desert."

## Key Lessons

- **The desert is the place of the word, not the place of things seen — God is forming "people of the ears, not people of the eyes."** The lesson of the *midbar* is to listen and follow the shepherd's voice through a disorienting landscape. Marty: *"God will use the desert to teach us how to become people of the ears and not people of the eyes… It's a 'place of word' and it's a 'place of voice.'"* The desert is "actually a really disorienting place… It is not a place of the eyes. The desert is a place of the ears."
- **"Just enough" is the shepherd's provision — the point is trust, not scarcity.** Marty rereads Psalm 23 against American abundance: *"All my needs are met because I have just enough… exactly what I need and nothing more."* The desert "teaches you just enough… I just need the one bite right now, and I'm going to trust that my shepherd will lead me to my next bite." He links it to Deuteronomy: *"Man doesn't live by bread alone, but on every word — every dabar — that comes from the mouth of God… you learned how to wait on God's provision, the manna, the daily."*
- **The biblical shepherd leads with voice, not force — "rod and staff" comfort because they mean protection, not discipline.** Marty dismantles the common "beating/leg-breaking" sermon images: *"these are just not biblical images… The shepherd leads with their voice."* On the rod and staff: *"if a stick is going to be used to hold violently over your head, it's not going to comfort you… This rod and staff of the shepherd is about leadership, about protection."* Sheep follow voice commands so completely that a shepherd can draw his own flock out of a mixed sheepfold with a single call.
- **The desert is a season to pass through, not a destiny — and God is in the middle of it.** Elle: *"the desert isn't where they live forever… they're on their way, they're going somewhere else, but they have to learn lessons in the desert first… you can know that God is in the middle of it with us and that there are things to be learned and ways that we might be able to hear God"* that the lush "kelly-green pasture of our imagination" would never teach.
- **God is looking for sheep, not goats — those who listen over those who trust their own strength.** Both sheep and goats are in the flock and both reach the fold, but they behave differently. Marty: *"God's looking for sheep, not goats. Seems to be one of the clear things that we pull out of those teachings."* Elle sharpens it with Hebrew: the word for goat carries the sense of *strength* — *"Are you trying to be the strong person? Is that who God's really looking for? Or is he looking for those who are able to listen?"*
- **The desert builds character, interdependence, community, and nations.** Marty opens with Bruce Feiler as the thesis for the whole desert series: *"Because the place is demanding, it builds character. Because it is destructive, it builds interdependence. Because it's isolating, it builds community. Because it is the desert, it builds nations."* The hardship is the formation, not an obstacle to it.

## East/West Lens

- **Words as word-pictures and images vs. abstract precise definitions.** The central engagement of the episode. Elle on Hebrew "word trees": *"in Hebrew, every word is built using three consonants… those come out like different branches from this tree… always related meaning to whatever the base image… is."* Marty: *"Hebrew is built off of images and pictures, not precise definitions… you're packing a lot more into a Hebrew word because of its picture and its ability to speak… not because of its precision, but because of its illustration."* Elle confirms English holds "a lot more abstract concepts like love" while Hebrew is "more picture-based and therefore more concrete."
- **Knowing as experiential (yada / who·how) vs. informational (what·facts).** The desert teaches by lived experience, not data. Marty: *"It's one thing to know about it… It's another thing to actually be shaped and formed and experience that relationship."* Israel must *yada* — know relationally — not merely "identify the pieces and understand how it works."
- **God described through concrete relationship (the shepherd) rather than abstract attributes.** God is pictured, not propositionally defined. Marty: *"it's such a dominant idea in your scriptures that God led us through the desert, that God was a shepherd leading us like a flock through the desert… following the voice of our shepherd."*
- **A hearing culture (ears) vs. a seeing culture (eyes).** The Hebraic ideal is the listener who follows a voice through the unseen, over the Western confidence of the eye that must see the whole horizon first. Marty: *"people of the ears and not people of the eyes… The desert is a place of the ears."*
- **Provision as "just enough" / daily dependence vs. abundance and self-sufficiency.** The Eastern reading of Psalm 23 is sufficiency and trust; the Western hearing defaults to comfort and surplus. Marty: *"I don't have any more than I need. I don't have abundance… All my needs are met because I have just enough."*
- **Communal, family-based vocation vs. the individual specialist.** Shepherding is a whole-family enterprise, which reframes who "the shepherds" are. Elle: *"everything is definitely a family enterprise… this occupation, like so many others, would be run by every member of the family, no matter the age and the gender."*

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Partnership (Session 1 arc) — God forms a people who will listen and follow, and invites their trust.** The desert is the training ground for the covenant partnership: God leads, the people learn to depend. Marty's long review names the thread from creation forward — "to trust in his provision and his care" — and the shepherd image makes it concrete: "I'm going to trust that my shepherd will lead me to my next bite." The sheep-vs-goat question *is* the partnership question: listen and follow, or go your own way. (Anchor arc of Session 1; flag for cross-reference, do not re-explain.)
- **Trust the story / trusting God's provision.** Named explicitly as the spine of Session 1 in the review, and embodied here as waiting on daily, "just enough" provision (the manna, "every dabar"). Marty: *"we're trusting the goodness of the God behind the creation… to trust in his provision and his care."*
- **Human strength surrendered in the desert (a motif seeded forward).** Elle's note that "the word for goat means strength" prompts Marty to name an emerging desert theme and flag it as recurring: *"What is our strength actually able to do for us? And what is God inviting us to learn about our strength, our own strength in the desert?… That's going to come up more than a couple times in our images of the desert."*
- **The desert as formation (midbar / place of the word).** The *midbar*=*dabar* word-tree makes "hearing the word" the governing image of the whole desert series — where David, Jesus, and Israel go to be shaped by God's voice. (Seeds the images-of-the-desert run that follows.)

## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

A sequential walkthrough of every teaching beat in the episode, in order. Used to verify full coverage.

1. **Framing & review — arrival at Numbers, the "desert honeymoon."** Marty recaps Session 1: the preface (good creation, trusting the God behind it), the introduction (Genesis 12–50, the family who learns "what's possible when we do trust"), the Exodus deliverance and the "wedding" at Sinai, and the tabernacle as both "honeymoon suite" and a retelling of Genesis 1–3, framed by mission ("a kingdom of priests") and last episode's look at priesthood. Now the people "have to learn experientially. They have to *yada*… And they're going to do that in the desert." Flags Ray Vander Laan's *That the World May Know*, Vol. 12, "Walking with God in the Desert," and possible Focus on the Family resource links.
2. **Why the desert matters — Feiler quote.** Marty and Elle discuss the desert's pull on people; Brent notes the concentration of national parks in the Utah desert. Marty reads Bruce Feiler (*Walking the Bible*): the desert builds "character… interdependence… community… nations."
3. **Hebrew word trees — midbar / dabar / amar (with Elle).** Elle teaches that Hebrew words are built on three root consonants with branching vowels/letters; *midbar* ("desert/wilderness") shares the root *d-b-r* ("speak"), so the desert is literally the "place of speaking." She contrasts the weighty *davar* with the casual *amar*, notes *Devarim* (Deuteronomy) as "the Book of Words/Things," and other branches of *d-b-r* (commandment, to come into agreement, even "plague"). Marty draws the lesson: Hebrew is "built off of images and pictures, not precise definitions."
4. **The flock on the hillside — leading with the voice.** Marty recounts bailing off the bus on his first RVL trip (2014) to watch a shepherd and flock for nearly an hour. Lessons learned: the hillside ruts the Bedouin call "paths of righteousness" ("if you follow those paths, you will eventually find the shepherd"); the shepherd "never leads with their staff," never beats the sheep, but leads from behind with voice and command; sheep respond to voice ("My sheep know my voice"), so a shepherd can draw his own flock from a mixed sheepfold with one call.
5. **Walking through Psalm 23 (Brent reads; Marty teaches image by image).**
   - *"Green pastures"* — not lush fields but tiny tufts of dry desert grass, visible as "green" only when "the sun hits it just right at twilight" (Brent's sarcastic "What grass, Marty?!" and the single old brown M&M illustration).
   - *"I shall not want / I lack nothing"* — "just enough," not abundance; the desert lesson of sufficiency and daily dependence (manna; Deuteronomy 8, "man does not live by bread alone… but on every dabar").
   - *"Quiet waters"* — still, "muddy little pools at the bottom of a desert wadi," not bubbling brooks.
   - *"Right paths / paths of righteousness"* — the Bedouin shepherd paths.
   - *"Valley of the shadow of death"* — at nightfall the shepherd moves into the *middle* of the flock; a 2006 story of a flock nearly walking off a chasm edge following the shepherd's voice.
   - *"Rod and staff, they comfort me"* — leadership and protection, not a beating.
   - *"You prepare a table… in the presence of my enemies"* — a desert hospitality image (Marty's earlier Hadija story), tied to shepherd families.
6. **Elle's pastoral application — the desert is temporary and purposeful.** Elle: the desert "isn't where they live forever… they're on their way," so a hard "scrap of bread" season is not failure or final destiny — "God is in the middle of it with us," teaching things the "kelly-green pasture of our imagination" can't.
7. **Sheep vs. goats — behavior, resources, and the parable.** Brent raises that sheep and goats eat different things and behave differently. Marty: a typical Bedouin flock keeps both (sheep eat the grass → wool; goats eat everything else → dairy), so they don't compete. On behavior: sheep "blob" together following the shepherd's voice; goats "do their own thing," speckled all over the mountainside. Ray's challenge: "Which one are you?" Brent's backyard goat story (picky eaters). Elle: the word for goat carries the sense of *strength* — "Is that who God's really looking for? Or… those who are able to listen?" Marty flags this strength-motif as recurring across the desert episodes.
8. **Who are the shepherds? — a family enterprise.** Brent asks who the shepherds really were. Marty: the 2014 flock was led by two teenage girls with their two younger brothers tagging along — "very typical in their culture," the older daughters watch the flocks while patriarchs run family business. One boy held a sling (rock-throwing to steer the flock). This reframes David leaving his flock (sisters likely watched it) and the female shepherds at the wells of Jacob and Moses; even the Bethlehem shepherds could be male or female. Elle confirms the whole-family-enterprise pattern from living there.
9. **Closing image — the shepherd of early Christianity.** Elle notes that before the cross/crucifix, one of the earliest Christian images "in the catacombs and art, jewelry" was "a young shepherd boy with a sheep slung across his back."

## Terminology

- **midbar** (מִדְבָּר) — desert / wilderness, literally the "place of speaking." Root *d-b-r* with an *m-* prefix. Elle: *"What they are literally saying is place of speaking… that is the etymological — the Hebraic background to every scene that's in the desert."*
- **davar / dabar** (דָּבָר / דִּבֶּר) — word / to speak; a weighty, enduring word you keep with you. Elle: *"if Marty Solomon is giving me a word… that I should keep with me for the next five years, that is a davar… it has a little bit more weight to it than amar."* The book of Deuteronomy is *Devarim*, "the Book of Words… the Book of Things."
- **amar** (אָמַר) — to say; casual speech. Elle: *"'I'm just talking to…', 'Elle talked to Brent'"* — contrasted with the weightier *davar*.
- **Hebrew "word trees"** — every word built on three root consonants, with vowels/added letters branching related meanings around one base image. From *d-b-r* branch: word, commandment, "to come into agreement or discuss," and even "plague." Illustrates the picture-based, concrete nature of Hebrew. Elle: *"those come out like different branches from this tree, and they have different meanings, but always related meaning to whatever the base image… is."*
- **goat = strength** — the Hebrew word for goat carries the sense of strength, a lens on the sheep-and-goats parable. Elle: *"The word for goat means strength… Are you trying to be the strong person? Is that who God's really looking for? Or is he looking for those who are able to listen?"*
- **"paths of righteousness"** — the hillside ruts worn by millennia of flocks, which the Bedouin shepherds call paths of righteousness. Marty: *"if you follow those paths, you will eventually find the shepherd. The shepherd is on the paths of righteousness."*

## Illustrations & Stories

- **The single brown M&M (Brent).** Updating his original "shareable bag" bit, Brent brings a family-size bag that holds just "one M&M, and it's a brown one… You can't even see it, it gets lost in the desert" — dramatizing the "just enough," one-bite tuft of desert grass.
- **The camcorder flock on the hillside (Marty).** Marty's first-trip (2014) memory of bailing off the bus and watching a shepherd and flock for nearly an hour — the "blob" of obedient sheep, the shepherd appearing a hundred yards behind, "just talking to the sheep… sing-song," and the goats "speckled" all over the mountainside. The experiential anchor for the whole lesson.
- **The chasm story, 2006 (Marty).** A shepherd separated from his flock after nightfall gave a command from across a chasm turn and "almost his whole flock walked right off the edge" — because "the sheep follow the shepherd's voice," illustrating total dependence on voice in the "valley of the shadow of death."
- **The backyard goats (Brent).** Brent invited a neighbor's goats to clear his weedy yard expecting a "clear cut," but the time-lapse showed everything still growing — "they are eating things, but they're just picky" — showing goats "do their own thing."
- **Hadija's table in the desert (Marty, recalled).** Marty's earlier Session 1 story of extravagant desert hospitality from shepherd families, now read into "You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies" as a concrete desert image.
- **The two teenage girls and a sling (Marty).** The photo of two teenage girl shepherds with two younger brothers (one holding a sling) that "reorder[ed] my mental picture of who shepherds can be."

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty rereads Psalm 23 as a psalm of "just enough" rather than abundance. Where in your life have you confused God's provision with American-style comfort — and how would "just enough" change the way you pray or wait?
2. The desert is the "place of the word" — a place of the ears, not the eyes, and a disorienting place where you "could get lost." When has a hard, disorienting season actually made you better at *hearing* God? What did losing clarity or control teach you?
3. Elle insists the desert "isn't where they live forever… they're on their way." How does believing a desert season is *temporary and purposeful* — rather than punishment or destiny — change how you move through it?
4. Sheep follow the shepherd's voice; goats "know a better way" and rely on their strength, yet both are in the same flock. Honestly, which are you right now, and in what specific area is God inviting you to listen rather than lean on your own strength?
5. The biblical shepherd leads with *voice*, not a beating staff, and the rod and staff "comfort" because they mean protection, not discipline. Where have you pictured God's leadership as mostly correction or punishment — and how would hearing it as a guiding voice reshape that relationship?
6. The shepherds of the biblical world were often teenage girls with little brothers in tow — not who we imagine. Where else might your mental picture of a familiar Bible story carry an unexamined cultural assumption, and how could checking it reshape the meaning?
