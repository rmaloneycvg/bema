# BEMA 112: Explanations That Don't Explain — Study Guide

**Session 3 (The Gospels) · Episode 112**
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon & Brent Billings
**Primary texts:** Matthew 13:24–30, 36–43 · with Genesis 3:15, Judges 12:5–6

---

## Overview

Having first used the parable of the weeds (back in the parable-mechanics episode) to teach PaRDeS, Marty and Brent circle back to actually sit with the parable itself — and discover that Jesus's famous "explanation" in Matthew 13:36–43 does not explain. It clarifies the variables of the story while *compounding* the puzzle: adding fresh remez and drash rather than resolving them. The episode is a sustained lesson in Eastern teaching: the rabbi leads disciples toward truth without handing it over, and the faithful response to an unsolved text is to dance, not to despair.

For the Session 3 arc — the king/Messiah embodying Israel's story — this episode shows the Messiah *teaching the way the Kingdom actually comes*. Where Session 1 established God's partnership with humanity and Session 2 traced the partner's unfaithfulness and God's refusal to quit, here the embodying King declines to be the crashing judge who rips the weeds out now. He insists the Kingdom grows like wheat-among-tares toward a future harvest — the three-part eschatology that redefines what kind of Messiah is standing in front of them.

## Episode Flow (Coverage Backbone)

1. **Framing the callback.** The parable was "left hanging" when first used only for parable mechanics; today they deal with the parable itself. (Marty: "We used it to talk about parable mechanics, introduced a lot of our listeners to PaRDeS... but we never really dealt with the parable itself.")
2. **The literary setup of Matthew 13.** Marty reads the whole chapter as one intentional conversation, not a catalog of scattered sayings — four public parables, then the move into the house, then more. (Marty: "I do believe that this is one conversation that Jesus is intentionally tying together.")
3. **Soils as the opening frame.** The parable of the soils sets up the whole discourse by asking "which soil are you?" before Jesus launches the harder teaching. (Marty: "I want you to wonder about which soil you are because I'm about to really challenge people to think.")
4. **Observation 1 — the explanation that doesn't explain.** Against the art of discovery, an Eastern teacher is the last to simply explain; Jesus only clarifies variables. (Marty: "all the rabbi does is further clarify the variables and elements of the parable... Jesus doesn't explain anything.")
5. **Observation 2 — the explanation as a *second* teaching.** No mention of p'shat/remez/drash; the explanation compounds, adding more layers. (Marty: "Is Jesus actually taking his p'shat, his remez, his drash and actually adding additional p'shats, additional remezes, and additional drashes to the conversation?")
6. **P'shat work: tares and the bowing wheat.** The "bastard wheat" (shibbolet) is indistinguishable from wheat until harvest, when real wheat bows and the tare stands straight. (Marty: "you can't tell that it's not wheat... the wheat actually bows... and the tare stands straight up.")
7. **P'shat takeaway — discern, don't uproot.** Our job is to discern light from darkness, but not to do God's uprooting job. (Marty: "do not try to do God's job because only God at the time of the harvest... will be able to identify what is truly weeds and truly wheat.")
8. **Discernment vs. judgment.** Brent distinguishes identifying fruit from wholesale elimination. (Brent: "You're making an identification, you're not making a judgment.")
9. **Remez hunting.** Son of Man, fiery furnace, end of the age, weeping and gnashing of teeth, "shine like the sun" — each a potential hint back into the Text (Daniel, Jeremiah). (Brent: "blazing furnace, that definitely feels like Daniel.")
10. **The Orthodox Jewish Bible tool.** Marty demonstrates reading the OJB to surface Hebrew terms and bracketed OT cross-references (e.g., Genesis 3:15), while candidly rating the translation. (Marty: "What I do love about the Orthodox Jewish Bible is it will leave a lot of the words untranslated.")
11. **Observation 3 — three-part eschatology.** This is the clearest parable affirming that weeds and wheat grow together until a *future* harvest, not a single crashing judgment now. (Marty: "This is probably the clearest parable... that gives the clearest affirmation of three-part Jewish eschatology.")
12. **The 4-and-4 structure.** Four public parables, then explanation plus three more private ones — a deliberate parallelism reinforcing the same teaching point. (Marty: "there is a parallelism just intentionally because he's going to make the exact same teaching points.")
13. **The honest teacher.** Marty admits he hasn't nailed the remez/drash; hunches, not conclusions. (Marty: "I'm trying to avoid the fact that I, as a teacher, don't really feel like I've nailed down the parable.")
14. **Dancing at the unknown.** The Jewish posture toward an un-understood passage: delight at a future "aha," versus Western fist-shaking. (Marty: "they dance... because there's a future day where they will celebrate.")
15. **Closing challenge — "Which soil are you?"** The episode ends by handing the opening question back to listeners, with a pastoral guess that many are rocky soil. (Brent: "Which soil are you?")

## Key Lessons

1. **An Eastern "explanation" compounds the mystery rather than dissolving it.** Jesus clarifies who the characters are but refuses to flatten the parable into a point. This is the Messiah teaching *in his own idiom* — the art of discovery. (Marty: "There is some clarity... but he still doesn't actually explain the parable at all. He doesn't explain the mechanics. He doesn't actually explain the point.")

2. **Discernment is commanded; uprooting is forbidden.** The disciple's job is to tell weeds from wheat, but the gathering belongs to God and his harvesters at the end of the age — because premature uprooting destroys real wheat. (Marty: "no matter how discerning we are, we will misidentify some of them and uproot some of the wheat that God has planted.")

3. **The parable redefines the Messiah's agenda — three-part eschatology.** A two-part eschatology expects the Messiah to arrive and immediately purge evil. Jesus plants seed and tells the servants to let both grow together, which means *the harvest is not yet*. This is precisely how this episode advances the Session 3 arc: the embodying King is not the crashing judge the disciples want, but the sower whose Kingdom grows amid the weeds. (Marty: "In two-part eschatology, there are no weeds and wheat growing.")

4. **The right response to an unsolved text is to dance.** Faithful study is not conditioned on having the answer; a passage you cannot crack is a gift awaiting a future opening. (Marty: "God has given them this passage that they don't understand for some future date when it will finally open their eyes.")

5. **Discernment is not judgment.** Identifying fruit is not the same as condemning and eliminating people wholesale — a distinction that guards the previous lesson from becoming an excuse for passivity. (Brent: "You're making an identification, you're not making a judgment.")

## East/West Lens

*Only categories actually engaged by this episode are tagged; each is cited.*

- **Teaching method — discovery over explanation (West: explain; East: lead and let them dig).** The whole episode turns on the Western instinct to demand a point-by-point explanation versus the Eastern teacher's refusal to give one. (Brent: "Art of discovery is like you are led into the general vicinity of the truth and you have to explore to find it." / Marty: "The last thing that an Eastern teacher is going to do is explain something.")

- **Posture toward the unknown (West: frustration; East: dancing).** The explicit contrast of how each tradition reacts to a passage it does not understand. (Marty: "Westerners, when we bump into a passage that we don't know, we often get frustrated and perplexed and shake our fists... When an Easterner... runs across something they don't understand... they dance.")

- **Eschatology (West/modern two-part; Jewish three-part).** The two-age-overlap reading is drawn directly from the Jewish framework the series has been building. (Marty: "If it's a three-part eschatology, it means... we now have to live in a world where weeds and wheat grow together.")

- **Scriptural literacy / formation (East: total memorization under a rabbi).** Brent frames the disciples' interpretive capacity in terms of a lifetime steeped in the Text. (Brent: "Age five, you start memorizing Torah... They have the entire scriptures memorized.")

## Narrative Tensions

1. **Tension — the "explanation" explains nothing.**
   - *Classification:* **Logic gap / genre expectation clash.**
   - *Surfacing quote:* Brent: "His disciples came to him and said, 'Explain to us the parable of the weeds in the field.'" followed by Marty: "Jesus doesn't explain much of anything and the disciples don't seem to balk at that."
   - *Verse:* [Matthew 13:36–43 (NIV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+13%3A36-43&version=NIV)
   - *Hebraic resolution:* In the art of discovery, clarifying the variables while adding more remez/drash *is* the teaching; the disciples don't object because they expect to keep digging, not to receive a lecture. (Marty: "all the rabbi does is further clarify the variables.")

2. **Tension — if the Messiah is here, why not pull the weeds now?**
   - *Classification:* **Unstated cultural assumption (messianic expectation).**
   - *Surfacing quote:* Marty: "I'm betting the disciples were like, 'Okay. We understand the teaching, but are you telling us that you are not here to bring the Kingdom of God?' Like crashing in two-part eschatology."
   - *Verse:* [Matthew 13:28–30 (NIV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+13%3A28-30&version=NIV)
   - *Hebraic resolution:* Three-part eschatology — the Kingdom is sown now and harvested later; the King's refusal to uproot is not weakness but the shape of the age-overlap. (Marty: "the harvest is not yet here yet.")

3. **Tension — are the "angels" angels, or messengers?**
   - *Classification:* **Translation disagreement.**
   - *Surfacing quote:* Marty: "melochim, angels, that's also connected to messengers... Is this like angels, angels?... I don't know. I don't know."
   - *Verse:* [Matthew 13:39 (NIV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+13%3A39&version=NIV)
   - *Resolution:* **Open — dance with it.** Marty explicitly leaves it unresolved and models delighting in the question rather than forcing an answer.

4. **Tension — the teacher himself hasn't solved the parable.**
   - *Classification:* **Logic gap (interpretive incompleteness).**
   - *Surfacing quote:* Marty: "I don't really feel like I've nailed down the parable of the weeds and the wheat. Not in the original parable and not even in the explanation. There are some hunches, but nothing that I've put together that I would present in a podcast."
   - *Verse:* [Matthew 13:24–30 (NIV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+13%3A24-30&version=NIV)
   - *Hebraic resolution:* Admitted incompleteness is not failure; it is the dancing posture lived out in real time — hunches held, answers awaited.

## Terminology

- **PaRDeS** — the four-level interpretive framework: *p'shat* (surface meaning), *remez* (a hint pointing to another Hebrew text), *drash* (a derived truth), *sod* (God-given mystery). (Marty: "There is no mention of p'shat, remez or drash.")
- **shibbolet / shabbolet** — "ear of grain"; here the "bastard wheat" (tares) indistinguishable from wheat until harvest, when wheat bows and the tare stands straight. Also the Judges 12 password. (Marty: "there is what's called 'Shibbolet'... that is the kind of tares that they would have encountered.")
- **two-part vs. three-part eschatology** — two-part expects a single crashing judgment; three-part has seed sown now and harvest later, with the two ages overlapping. (Marty: "If it's a three-part eschatology, it means the harvest is not yet here yet.")
- **Rebbe Melech HaMoshiach** — "Rabbi, King Messiah" (the OJB's rendering of Jesus). (Transcript, OJB reading.)
- **Ben haAdam** — "Son of Man." (Marty, reading the OJB.)
- **HaOlam Hazeh** — "this world / this age." (Marty, reading the OJB.)
- **B'nei HaMalchut / b'nei haRah** — "children of the Kingdom" / "children of the evil one." (Marty: "b'nei haMalchut, which is 'children of the Kingdom,' malchut is kingdom.")
- **HaSatan** — "the satan / the accuser / the adversary," with the definite article "Ha." (Marty: "That means 'the satan, the accuser, the adversary.' It has a definite article with it.")
- **katzir / katzirim** — "harvest" / "harvesters." (Marty, reading the OJB.)
- **melochim** — "angels," also "messengers." (Marty: "melochim, angels, that's also connected to messengers.")
- **mashal** — "parable." (Marty, reading the OJB.)
- **beit / beit av** — "house / household." (Marty: "Remember, it's talking about the beit av, the house.")
- **talmidim** — "disciples." (Marty, reading the OJB.)
- **ayin tovah / ayin ra'ah** — "good eye / bad eye." (Marty: "things like ayin tovah, and ayin ra'ah.")

- **Bastard wheat that bows at harvest.** The field looks uniform until maturity, when true wheat bows under its grain and the tare stands straight — grounding the parable's "let both grow together" in agronomic reality. ## Discussion Questions

1. Marty insists Jesus's "explanation" doesn't actually explain — it compounds the parable with more remez and drash. Where in your own Bible reading have you demanded a clean "point" from a text that was designed to make you keep digging? What changes if you let it stay open?
2. The p'shat teaching is *discern but do not uproot*. Brent sharpens this: identification is not judgment. In practice, how do you tell the difference between discerning fruit and appointing yourself the field's weed-puller?
3. Three-part eschatology means the Messiah plants seed now and harvests later, so weeds and wheat grow together in the meantime. How does that reshape what you expect God to be doing about evil right now — and how is this a different kind of King than the "crashing" Messiah the disciples wanted?
4. Marty contrasts Western fist-shaking with the Eastern practice of *dancing* at a passage you can't understand. Name one text you've been frustrated by. What would it look like to dance with it instead?
5. The episode ends by handing the opening question back: *Which soil are you?* Be honest — path, rocky, thorny, or good soil in this season — and what would "grabbing a shovel to build deeper roots" concretely require of you?
