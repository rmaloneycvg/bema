# Images of the Desert — Ar'ar and Tamarisk

BEMA 28 · Session 1
Source transcript: `transcripts/1-28-images-of-the-desert-arar-and-tamarisk.md`

## Key Lessons

- **Outward vibrance can be inward poison; apparent deadness can be the gift of the desert.** The *ar'ar* looks bright and lush but is toxic and hollow inside; the acacia looks dead but is "the gift of the desert." Marty: *"You can look unbelievably dead on the outside, but be unbelievably rich on the inside."* This serves the arc — **trust the story; God does not change what he wants or what matters** — by naming the difference between trusting in flesh (the *ar'ar*/cursed) and trusting in God (the water-planted tree). Jeremiah 17 ties the two directly.

- **Trusting in "mere flesh" makes you an *ar'ar*; trusting in God makes you a tree planted by water that never fails to bear fruit.** Jeremiah 17's wordplay (*arur / ar'ar / arava*) binds "cursed" to the toxic bush to the wilderness. The constant invitation of the whole story — trust God, not your own strength — is pictured botanically.

- **The tamarisk is planted for people you'll never see benefit from it — "How many tamarisk trees did you plant today?"** It takes ~80 years to mature. Marty: *"You plant a tamarisk tree for your grandkids... essentially for others. You will never benefit."* Abraham planting one at Beersheba is a statement of faith in what God will keep doing for future generations.

- **Abraham's tamarisk is a witness to *El Olam*, the Eternal God — the tree embodies God's "in perpetuity" faithfulness.** Elle: *"God is going to continue to work in this place because He is Adonai Olam. Just like this tree is going to continue in the future to bless future generations."* God's ongoing covenant commitment (the arc) is planted as a living sign.

- **A priest (*kohen*) is an "opulent delegate" — one who undertakes another's cause, dressed to evoke God's abundance.** Elle's cognate-language reading reframes the Leviticus priesthood: a two-directional representative pleading God's cause to the people and the people's cause to God, radiating the richness of God's realm.

## East/West Lens

- **Words as word-pictures over abstract definitions** — The entire lesson is taught through *images*, not propositions. Brent: *"our culture being built around words and trying to fit all of these conceptual things into these letters. But if you just look at this tree and then look at this tree, you totally get it."* The Hebrew puns (*arur/ar'ar/arava*) carry meaning by sound and sight.

- **Truth over time — dynamic/unfolding understanding** — This "reboot" episode layers new scholarship (Elle's cognate work, the Akkadian poem, the *olam* connection) onto a lesson Marty taught for years. Marty: *"it's even better after today."* Understanding grows while the images hold still.

- **Community vs individual** — The tamarisk lesson is explicitly intergenerational and communal: plant for grandchildren and "spiritual grandkids." Marty ties it to investing in college students — planting "kingdom tamarisk trees" for a community that outlives you.

- **God's description — relationship over abstraction** — God is named *El Olam* ("Eternal God") in relational, horizon-image terms, and the *kohen* is defined by undertaking another's *cause* (relationship), not by abstract office.

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story (the arc itself).** Jeremiah 17 is the hinge: the *ar'ar* is "the one who trusts in man," the fruitful tree is "the one who trusts in the LORD." The desert images dramatize the arc's core invitation — trust God, not flesh — and Abraham's tamarisk declares trust in God's enduring (*olam*) faithfulness to future generations. Recurs across the whole corpus.

- **Honeymoon in the desert / learning to *yada* God.** A recurring Session 1 framing (Sinai wedding → tabernacle honeymoon → wilderness as the place the bride comes to *know* her husband). Invoked again to set up the desert-images series. Noted as cross-episode recurrence.

- **Empire vs Shalom.** A recurring BEMA contrast (named in the prior shepherd episode: leading "with our voice rather than a stick"), referenced here as the backdrop for the desert-image series. Noted as cross-episode recurrence.

## Chiasms & Structure

No chiasm is present. The episode's structure is a set of **paired desert images** taught side by side — the *ar'ar* (vibrant-but-toxic) set against the acacia (dead-looking-but-the-gift-of-the-desert) via Jeremiah 17, followed by the tamarisk/legacy image via Genesis 21. Since there is no mirrored, center-weighted structure, this section is omitted rather than fabricated.

## Terminology

- **kohen** (כֹּהֵן) — priest. Elle (via cognates): *"one who undertakes anyone's cause is a deputy or delegate"* plus *"opulence or abundance"* — an "opulent delegate" of God's realm.
- **ar'ar** (עַרְעָר) — the desert bush: bright, vibrant, soft-looking — but toxic, with a hollow, cobwebby interior and a milky toxic coating. "You look for something that looks good in the desert, then it ends up being an *arur* or an *ar'ar*."
- **arur** (אָרוּר) — "cursed"; the strongest curse-word (execration / "to bind someone to a curse"), imaged as razing/stripping down. An audible pun with *ar'ar* in Jeremiah 17.
- **arava** (עֲרָבָה) — the desert/wilderness (distinct from *midbar*); the third pun — *arur / ar'ar / arava*.
- **acacia** (שִׁטָּה, *shittah*) — looks dead but is "the gift of the desert"; the wood of the Ark, poles, Mishkan boards, and altars (mostly gold-overlaid). Elle links it to the "opulent delegate" and the inverse of Jesus' whitewashed tombs.
- **tamarisk** (אֵשֶׁל, *eshel*) — a cultivated "luxury" tree (salt cedar) whose shade is ~15% cooler because it draws moisture from the air; ~80 years to mature. "How many tamarisk trees did you plant today?"
- **olam** (עוֹלָם) — eternal / forever / in perpetuity; "the vanishing line of the horizon." Abraham calls on *El Olam* at the tamarisk.

## Discussion Questions

1. The *ar'ar* looks vibrant but is toxic; the acacia looks dead but is the gift of the desert. Where in your life are you tempted to prize the *ar'ar* look — impressive on the outside — over the acacia reality of quiet, durable fruitfulness?
2. Jeremiah 17 contrasts trusting "mere flesh" with trusting God. Name a specific place you're currently drawing strength from your own abilities. What would it look like to be "planted by the water" there instead?
3. "How many tamarisk trees did you plant today?" What have you done recently purely for people who come after you — things you'll never see bear fruit? What could you plant this week?
4. Abraham's tamarisk was a witness to *El Olam*, the Eternal God who keeps working across generations. Where do you need to trust that God's work in a situation isn't finished — that this is "not the end of the story"?
5. Elle reframes the priest as an "opulent delegate" who undertakes another's cause in both directions. Whose cause are you shouldering before God right now — and whose should you be?
