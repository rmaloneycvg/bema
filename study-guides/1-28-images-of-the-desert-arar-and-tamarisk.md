# Images of the Desert — Ar'ar and Tamarisk

BEMA 28 · Session 1
Source transcript: `transcripts/1-28-images-of-the-desert-arar-and-tamarisk.md`

> **Session 1 arc — partnership.** Session 1 traces God forming a people to partner with him. This episode sits in the "honeymoon in the desert" stretch: Sinai was the wedding, the tabernacle the honeymoon suite, and the wilderness is where the bride learns to *yada* (know) her husband. The desert trees are lessons the bride learns about trusting God rather than flesh, and about partnering with God's enduring purposes across generations.

## Key Lessons

- **Outward vibrance can be inward poison; apparent deadness can be the gift of the desert.** The *ar'ar* looks bright and lush but is toxic and hollow inside; the acacia looks dead but is "the gift of the desert." Marty: *"You can look unbelievably dead on the outside, but be unbelievably rich on the inside."* This serves the partnership arc by naming the real difference between trusting in flesh (the *ar'ar*/cursed) and trusting in God (the water-planted tree) — the posture that makes genuine partnership with God possible. Jeremiah 17 ties the two images directly.

- **Trusting in "mere flesh" makes you an *ar'ar*; trusting in God makes you a tree planted by water that never fails to bear fruit.** Jeremiah 17's wordplay (*arur / ar'ar / arava*) binds "cursed" to the toxic bush to the wilderness. Marty: *"the person who trusts in flesh, who rests on our own abilities, man, we can look real great on the outside... But at the end of the day, what it provides and what's inside is toxic."* The whole story's invitation — trust God, not your own strength — is pictured botanically.

- **The tamarisk is planted for people you'll never see benefit from it — "How many tamarisk trees did you plant today?"** It takes ~80 years to mature. Marty: *"You plant a tamarisk tree for your grandkids. You don't plant it for yourself. You will never benefit. You plant a tamarisk tree essentially for others."* Partnership with God is intergenerational: you labor for a future you won't live to enjoy.

- **Abraham's tamarisk is a witness to *El Olam*, the Eternal God — the tree embodies God's "in perpetuity" faithfulness.** Elle: *"God is going to continue to work in this place because He is Adonai Olam. Just like this tree is going to continue in the future to bless future generations."* Abraham's act of planting is itself a statement of partnership — a bet on what God will keep doing for descendants.

- **A priest (*kohen*) is an "opulent delegate" — one who undertakes another's cause, dressed to evoke God's abundance.** Elle's cognate-language reading reframes the Leviticus priesthood: Elle: *"one who undertakes anyone's cause is a deputy or delegate"* combined with *"opulence or abundance or richness."* A two-directional representative — Brent: *"pleading God's cause to the people"* as well as the people's cause to God — which is partnership made into a vocation.

## East/West Lens

- **Words as word-pictures over abstract definitions** — The entire lesson is taught through *images*, not propositions. Brent: *"our culture being built around words and trying to fit all of these conceptual things into these letters. But if you just look at this tree and then look at this tree, you totally get it."* The Hebrew puns (*arur / ar'ar / arava*) carry meaning by sound and sight, not by definition.

- **Truth over time — dynamic, unfolding understanding** — This "reboot" episode layers new scholarship (Elle's cognate work on *kohen*, the Akkadian poem, the *olam* connection) onto a lesson Marty taught for years. Marty: *"it was one of my favorite desert lessons, and now I just feel like it's even better after today."* The Eastern posture lets understanding grow while the images hold still.

- **Community over the isolated individual** — The tamarisk lesson is explicitly intergenerational and communal: plant for grandchildren and "spiritual grandkids." Marty: *"we're trying to plant kingdom tamarisk trees... give them away to young adults who will take these things and do something with it that we may never even see or benefit from."* Meaning is measured by what outlives you, not by individual payoff.

- **God known relationally, not abstractly** — God is named *El Olam* ("Eternal God") through a concrete horizon-image (Elle: *"the vanishing line of the horizon is the picture for olam"*), and the *kohen* is defined by undertaking another's *cause* — relationship and action — rather than by an abstract office.

## Chiasms & Structure

No chiasm is present. The episode's structure is a set of **paired desert images** taught side by side — the *ar'ar* (vibrant-but-toxic) set against the acacia (dead-looking-but-the-gift-of-the-desert) via Jeremiah 17, followed by the tamarisk/legacy image via Genesis 21. Because there is no mirrored, center-weighted structure, the mermaid diagram is omitted rather than fabricated.

## Terminology

- **kohen** (כֹּהֵן) — priest. Elle (via cognate languages): *"one who undertakes anyone's cause is a deputy or delegate"* plus *"opulence or abundance or richness"* — an "opulent delegate" who represents God's realm to the people and the people's cause to God.
- **ar'ar** (עַרְעָר) — the desert bush: bright, vibrant, soft-looking — but toxic, with a hollow, "cobwebby emptiness" interior and a milky, toxic coating. Marty: *"this bush is toxic. It's poisonous."*
- **arur** (אָרוּר) — "cursed"; Elle: *"the strongest one, to bind someone to a curse,"* imaged as razing/stripping down. An audible pun with *ar'ar* in Jeremiah 17.
- **arava** (עֲרָבָה) — the desert/wilderness (distinct from *midbar*); Elle's "third pun" — *arur / ar'ar / arava*.
- **acacia** (שִׁטָּה, *shittah*) — looks dead but is "the gift of the desert"; Elle: the wood of *"the Ark of the Covenant... the poles for the ark, the boards on the Mishkan, the incense altar, the burnt offering altar,"* mostly gold-overlaid — the inverse of Jesus' whitewashed tombs.
- **tamarisk** (אֵשֶׁל, *eshel*) — a cultivated "luxury" tree (also called salt cedar); its shade is ~15% cooler because it draws moisture from the air. Marty: *"it takes 80 years to grow to complete full maturity... You plant a tamarisk tree for your grandkids."*
- **olam** (עוֹלָם) — eternal / forever / in perpetuity. Elle: *"the vanishing line of the horizon is the picture for olam."* Abraham calls on *El Olam* at the tamarisk.
- **yada** (יָדַע) — to know (relationally/experientially). Marty: the bride goes to the desert *"to know this God that they are in a relationship with."*
- **midbar** (מִדְבָּר) — the desert/wilderness where the "honeymoon" happens; the place of learning to *yada* God.

## Illustrations & Stories

- **Brent seeing the ar'ar in person (2016 trip).** Brent: *"There's no color anywhere else around, and it's just this bright green banner standing there in the desert. It's amazing."* The vivid outward beauty is the whole setup for the "toxic inside" reversal.
- **Marty popping open the ar'ar fruit for students.** Marty: *"you peel it open, and then it pops open. There's this popping sound. [POP] And the whole thing is empty. It's got this cobwebby emptiness... There is a milky substance on the outside... the part that is most toxic about it."* The hollow, poisonous fruit dramatizes "trusting in flesh."
- **Elle's heat exhaustion — "the arur of the ar'ar."** Elle: *"it was the first day, and Marty was teaching about this bush, and I got heat exhaustion... So I experienced the arur of the ar'ar."* A lived (and self-deprecating) enactment of the curse-word pun.
- **Elle sitting in Noga Hareuveni's garden at Hebrew University.** Elle: *"I sat in the garden that he worked on every day during my lunch break and enjoyed the fruit of his labor, nearly literally, the shade of his labor."* A living image of the tamarisk principle — benefiting from another's long-ago planting.
- **"Kingdom tamarisk trees" — investing in college students.** Marty: *"we're trying to plant kingdom tamarisk trees... give them away to young adults who will take these things and do something with it that we may never even see or benefit from."* The desert image applied to BEMA's own ministry.
- **The Mercedes-Benz / "luxury tree."** Marty: *"It's like the Mercedes-Benz of trees,"* validated by Elle's Akkadian poem — a playful handle for the tamarisk's cultural prestige.

## Discussion Questions

1. **Ar'ar or acacia?** Where in your own life are you tempted to look like the *ar'ar* — vibrant and impressive on the outside while hollow or toxic inside? What would it look like to be more like the acacia: unremarkable, even "dead-looking," but genuinely "the gift of the desert"? (Jeremiah 17:5-8)
2. **"How many tamarisk trees did you plant today?"** Name one thing you could invest in now whose benefit you will likely never see yourself. What makes that kind of long-horizon work hard — and what makes it worth doing anyway? (Genesis 21:33)
3. **Trusting flesh vs. trusting God.** Jeremiah 17 contrasts the one who "draws strength from mere flesh" with the one "whose confidence is in" the LORD. Practically, how do you tell the difference in yourself between self-reliance and genuine trust? What "roots toward the water" are you putting down?
4. **El Olam.** Abraham names God "the Eternal God" at the moment he plants for the future. How does believing God works "in perpetuity" change the way you make decisions today — about work, family, or faith community?
5. **Dancing with the open question.** The hosts leave Brent's question unresolved: why does Jesus use some images (whitewashed tombs, the cup) and not the *ar'ar*/acacia contrast? Sit with it — what might Jesus have been doing with the images he *did* choose? (Matthew 23)
