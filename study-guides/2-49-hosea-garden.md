# Hosea - Garden

BEMA 49 · Session 2
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/2-49-hosea-garden.md`
Book: Hosea (passages read: 1:1-5; 2:14-23; 6:1-4; 10:12; 11:1-4; 14:1-9 — the transcript prints the NET; Brent reads aloud from the RSV). Narrated at length: 2 Kings 9 (Jehu). Brought in: Isaiah (swords into plowshares).
Host: Brent Billings · Co-host: Elle Grover Fricks
Core move throughout: read the *whole* scroll, not a zoomed-in "unfaithful woman" sermon. The name *Hosea* is from *yasha* ("rescue") — so the scroll asks **what is the rescue, and what are the people being rescued from?** Answer: a faith that makes rampant violence (Jehu's bloodshed at Jezreel) feel "kosher." God's *yasha* (chapter 2) is a sevenfold **transformation** — the overgrown garden weeded, so God can plant his seed (*Yizreel*, "God sows").

> **Session 2 overarching arc: the prophets — the partner's (Israel's) unfaithfulness and God's refusal to quit.** Session 1 built the *partnership*: God courting and marrying a people out of Egypt, "you create the space and I'll fill it." This episode lives squarely in the collapse of that partnership — the monarchy's violence (Jehu) curdling the people's faith — and names God's response in the plainest marital terms the prophets use. Hosea advances the one story by showing *how* God refuses to quit: not with a contract or a divine thumbs-up from "outer space," but as a *person who reacts personally* — torn, grieving, alluring the unfaithful partner "back into the wilderness" to speak tenderly, back to "the days of her youth... when she came up from the land of Egypt." Where Session 1 asked whether the partner would say *yes* at Sinai, Hosea holds that same courtship up in the age of betrayal: the people have dragged a *Ba'al* (master/slave) relationship into their life with God, and God's rescue is to transform it back into "my husband" (*ishi*) — to re-name "Not My People" as "You are my people." The arc is the partnership failing and God, grieving, refusing to let the marriage die.

## Key Lessons

- **The scroll is named *rescue* — so the first question is "rescue from *what*?"** Elle: *"the name of the scroll is Hosea... It comes from the root yasha... the kind of rescue that's bringing God's people out into an open, wide place... a liberation-focused rescue."* Cross out *Hosea*, write *rescue* beside it, and the whole book becomes a question.
- **What the people are rescued *from* is a faith that baptizes violence.** The "blood of Jezreel" (Jehu's rampage, 2 Kings 9) is the point. Elle: *"They're being rescued from a kind of faith that makes them believe that that kind of rampant violence is okay... taking by force and slaughter and conquest, that's actually... all kosher."* Might-plus-rightness-makes-right is the sin.
- **The way of Avraham and David is *waiting* for God, not seizing by force.** Elle: *"Avraham walked in the land. He did not consider himself... a conquistador. He waited... King David also... does not kill Shaul when he has the chance to. That's the man after God's own heart... when we're willing to wait and let God give his supernatural instructions."*
- **The God of the Bible is a *person who feels and reacts* — not a benevolent force floating in outer space.** Elle: *"even if our theology isn't deistic, we end up applying this deism to God... God is a person and he reacts personally to the things that we do, which puts a lot more onus of responsibility in our relationship with God."*
- **Repentance requires *both* A1 (acknowledge your guilt) *and* A2 (seek my face) — not one or the other.** Elle: *"you won't even be able to find the path unless you do two things. First, acknowledge your guilt and seek my face."* Some of us camp in self-blame; some want only the encounter. God asks for both before "you will break forth like the dawn."
- **Seeking God's face *without* acknowledging guilt fails the "vibe check."** On Hosea 6, Elle: *"you didn't pass the vibe check... I don't trust you. I don't believe you, because you're trying to seek my face, but you haven't acknowledged your guilt."* The three-and-a-half dark chapters are God *draining the abscess* — ugly but necessary work.
- **The hard chapters are God doing the priests' own work *for* them.** The people were "disqualified from the priesthood"; so God, through the prophet, does the intercessory work of walking them through guilt. Elle: *"he's doing that work through his prophet that they're supposed to be doing as priests."*
- **God's love was never earned and never conditional — he taught them to walk.** On Hosea 11, Elle: *"No, I've always loved you. I'm the one who was there at the beginning. I'm the one who taught you to walk... Of course I've always loved you."* And yet love still asks for change — the "OnlyFans / college fund" analogy: "because I love you... I'll stop."
- **God's *yasha* is transformation at every layer — his "divine alchemy."** Elle: *"God takes one thing, and through his divine alchemy, his resurrection... He's the God of renewing life, always."* The sevenfold rescue of chapter 2: alluring/relationship first, free gifts (vineyards), Valley of Trouble → door of hope, restored first love, *Ba'al* → "my husband," creation's suffering ended / bow and sword abolished, and identity renewed.
- **The relationship comes *first*, not last — knowing God is the whole point.** Elle: *"We'd like to put that last... God does it right at the front... knowing God is the defining feature between 'depart from me, I never knew you' and 'the sheep hearing his voice.'"*
- **God refuses the master/slave ("*Ba'al*") frame and insists on marriage.** Elle: *"the word Ba'al actually means master... you have dragged into your relationship with me this slavery, master mentality... I'm going to remove that out of your mouth."* The biggest lie is "that God is like Satan" — the far-off, angry, vengeance-seeking master.
- **The rescue of Empire "looks nothing like" God's rescue — God abolishes the bow and the sword.** Hosea 2's covenant with the beasts and the ending of war reaches back to Hosea 1's "I will break the bow." Elle: *"we are often too willing to pick up the weapons of this world... And God says, nope... I do things like taking down Jericho... through worship."*

## East/West Lens

*(Only categories the episode actually engages, each with a citation.)*

- **Names carry meaning and drive interpretation (Eastern naming vs. Western label).** The hermeneutic Elle names outright: start with the name. *Hosea* = rescue (*yasha*); *Jezreel/Yizreel* puns on *Yisrael* and means "God sows"; *Ephraim* carries *pri* ("fruit"); *Ba'al* = master. Elle: *"if we start with the name... the name of the scroll is Hosea... It's all about the names."*
- **God as emotive person vs. the impassible/deistic abstraction (Eastern relational God vs. Western philosophical one).** Elle contrasts "the impassibility of God" debated in philosophy with "the God of the Bible... constantly having feelings." Elle: *"God is a person and he reacts personally to the things that we do."*
- **Hebraic narrative order (A/B up front) vs. Western climax-at-the-end structure.** Elle: *"the part A and part B in this text presents itself in a way that we don't usually see in Western texts: where they are both going to be right at the front... In Western literature, we like to have the nice resolution at the end."* The guide must *reorder* the scroll to feel resolved.
- **Yada (experiential/relational knowing) vs. knowing-about (Eastern relational knowledge vs. Western information).** Hosea 4: the people are "destroyed from lack of *yada*." Elle: *"They might know the things about God, but they're lacking in their relationship with God and it's destroying them."* (Greek *gnosis* set alongside.)
- **Embodied, grounded, visual imagery vs. abstract concept (Eastern concreteness).** Elle prefers "garden" over "transformation" precisely because *"it's not embodied, it's not grounded on the planet, and Hebrew is always like something very visual and a garden is very visual."*
- **Reading the whole canon as one interlocking story (Eastern intertext vs. Western proof-text).** You cannot read Hosea 1 without 2 Kings 9; Jehu's cloaks-on-the-road is "a remez from the story of Palm Sunday"; the bow/sword refashioned points to Isaiah. Elle: *"if we don't know our text, we have like no way to engage with this scroll."*
- **Translation as interpretation — read multiple translations (Eastern attention to the word).** *Achor* left untranslated hides "roiling trouble"; the NIV footnotes it, the NET renders "Valley of Trouble." Brent: *"the case for reading multiple translations."*

## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

A sequential walkthrough of every teaching beat, in order. Used to verify full coverage.

1. **Framing / pastoral warning.** Elle warns against the common "unfaithful woman" sermon: zooming in on Gomer and making "why are these women so unfaithful?" the central question manufactures "a recipe for violence against women... intimate partner violence." She points to the domestic-violence statistics link in the show notes.
2. **The hermeneutic: start with the name.** *Hosea* ← *yasha* ("rescue... out into an open, wide place") — the root behind Jesus/Yeshua. Cross out *Hosea*, write *rescue*: so **what is the rescue, from what, how, why?**
3. **Hosea 1 read (RSV) and the hinge most sermons skip.** The "blood of Jezreel" and the "house of Jehu." Elle: don't let "the lullaby effect" float you past it to "the really interesting reality show stuff."
4. **2 Kings 9 narrated.** Elisha's disciple anoints Jehu but *adds his own ideas* (desolate Ahab's house, "you will avenge"); the cloaks-on-the-road (a *remez* to Palm Sunday — "Jesus also rides into town for people who are excited about violence"); Jehu's escalating slaughter (Judah's king at Megiddo, Ahab, Jezebel to the dogs, seventy sons, forty-two relatives in a pit, the Ba'al worshippers), the skulls piled at the gate of **Jezreel**, and "What do I have to do with peace?"
5. **The diagnosis: a faith that makes violence "kosher."** The rescue is *from* "might and rightness makes right." The counter-model: Avraham and David *wait* for God.
6. **God is an emotive person, not a deistic force.** The transition into structure flags the "emotive quality of God" — against the cultural training that feelings/reactivity are bad.
7. **Structure: Part A (problem) + Part B (God's solution), both up front — then reordered.** A Hebraic shape, not the Western climax-at-the-end.
8. **Part A walked chapter by chapter.** Ch. 3 = a picture of what has happened ("raisin cakes," "no fear of me"). Ch. 4 = what is happening: destroyed from lack of *yada*; disqualified from the priesthood ("a Kingdom of Priests"); "you have forgotten my teachings... I will forget you." Ch. 5 = what they refuse to do → the subsets **A1 acknowledge guilt** and **A2 seek my face**.
9. **Hosea 6:1-4 read — the failed A2-without-A1.** The lovely "let us return / he will revive us... on the third day" followed by God's verse-4 refusal: "Your love is like a morning cloud." The "vibe check" they fail.
10. **The abscess / three-and-a-half dark chapters (7-10).** God drains the abscess — ugly but necessary; he does the priests' own intercessory work for them.
11. **Hosea 10:12 read.** "Break up your fallow ground... it is time to seek the Lord" — the agricultural metaphor: weed the overgrown garden *first*, or the spring rains just grow more weeds. The *Yizreel* pun ("God sows," the seed of God) and *Ephraim/pri* ("fruit").
12. **Hosea 11:1-4 read.** "When Israel was a child, I loved him... I taught Ephraim to walk." God's love was always there and never earned — balanced by the "OnlyFans / college fund" analogy that love still calls for change.
13. **Hosea 14 read — the Western-style resolution (A2 fulfilled).** Return, healing, blossoming "like the lily," dwelling "beneath my shadow," flourishing "as a garden."
14. **Part B: Hosea 2:14-23 read — God's *yasha*/solution.** The sevenfold **transformation**: (1) **allure**/relationship first; (2) **free gifts** (vineyards); (3) **Valley of Achor → door of hope** (*Achor* = "roiling trouble"; NIV footnote; NET "Valley of Trouble" — read multiple translations); (4) **first love restored** ("days of her youth... out of Egypt"); (5) ***Ba'al* → "my husband"** (master/slave frame removed; "the biggest lie... that God is like Satan"); (6) **creation's suffering ended / bow, sword, war abolished** (covenant with the beasts — "back in Eden"; echo of 1:5 "I will break the bow"; Isaiah's swords refashioned; "Guns to Gardens"); (7) **identity renewed** ("Not Pitied" / "Not My People" → "You are my people" / "Thou art my God") — "the last is the first."
15. **Spiritual discipline.** Say Hosea 2:19-20 in Hebrew alongside the Shema (Episode 21) — the betrothal verses: "I will betroth you to me forever..."
16. **The prophet's one-word image.** Brent proposes *transformation* (replacing the original "prostitute"); Elle prefers *garden* (embodied, visual, Edenic). They settle on **"a garden of transformation."**
17. **Benediction.** "May we be people who are willing to acknowledge our guilt and seek the face of God, that he might do his salvific work and rescue us from the part of our heart which longs for vengeance."

## Terminology

- **yasha** (יָשַׁע, "to rescue, save, deliver") — the root behind the name *Hosea* and behind *Jesus/Yeshua*; a liberation-rescue "bringing God's people out into an open, wide place." Elle: *"the name of the scroll is Hosea... It comes from the root yasha."* The scroll's whole shape is the question: what is the rescue?
- **yada** (יָדַע, "to know") — experiential, *relational* knowledge (set beside Greek *gnosis*), as opposed to knowing facts *about* God. Hosea 4: the people are "destroyed from lack of *yada*." Elle: *"They might know the things about God, but they're lacking in their relationship with God and it's destroying them."*
- **Jezreel / Yizreel** (יִזְרְעֶאל) — a place name that is also a pun: it echoes *Yisrael* but carries a Z and means "God sows / the seed of God." The valley of Jehu's bloodshed becomes the seedbed of God's new garden. Elle: *"it's a pun... it's the seed of God. God's trying to plant something new."*
- **Ephraim / pri** (אֶפְרַיִם / פְּרִי) — God repeatedly addresses *Ephraim* (technically Joseph's son, not one of Jacob's original twelve); the word *pri* ("fruit") sits inside the name, meaning "I will be fruitful." Elle: *"pri is in the middle of Ephraim's name, fruit. 'I will be fruitful,' is what that name means."*
- **Achor** (עָכוֹר, "roiling trouble") — trouble boiling over; often left untranslated ("Valley of Achor"), footnoted "trouble" in the NIV, rendered "Valley of Trouble" in the NET. God transforms it into "a door of hope." Elle: *"It means roiling trouble. Like trouble that's boiling water, that's boiling over."*
- **Ba'al / baalim** (בַּעַל) — not merely a Canaanite deity's name but the word for "master / lord" (still the word for "husband" in modern Hebrew). The people dragged a slavery/master mentality into their life with God; God promises to remove it and be called *ishi* ("my husband"). Elle: *"the word Ba'al actually means master... you have dragged into your relationship with me this slavery, master mentality... I'm going to remove that out of your mouth."*
- **A1 / A2 (acknowledge guilt / seek my face)** — Elle's shorthand for the two necessary movements of return in Hosea 5-6. Elle: *"First, acknowledge your guilt and seek my face... You must do A1 and you must do A2."*
- **Part A / Part B (problem / solution)** — Elle's structural outline: Part A (chs. 3-14 as walked) is the problem of violence/unfaithfulness; Part B (ch. 2) is God's *yasha*. Both appear "right at the front" in Hebraic fashion and are reordered for a Western-style resolution.
- **the prophet's image: "a garden of transformation"** — the one-word tag for Hosea. Brent proposes *transformation* (over the original "prostitute"); Elle prefers *garden* ("embodied... grounded... very visual"); they combine them. Brent: *"A garden of transformation, if you want to think of it that way."*

## Illustrations & Stories

- **Elle's narration of 2 Kings 9 (Jehu).** The episode's governing backstory, told "bad audiobook" style: the disciple who *adds his own ideas* to the prophecy ("you will avenge"), the cloaks laid on the road (a *remez* to Palm Sunday), the escalating slaughter, the skulls piled at Jezreel's gate, and Jehu's "What do I have to do with peace?" — the picture of a faith that makes violence "kosher."
- **Draining the abscess.** Elle's image for the three-and-a-half dark chapters: *"it's not fully healed if you just let the abscess sit there... We've got three and a half chapters of draining that abscess."* Brent: "I don't know how I feel about how graphic this image is."
- **The overgrown garden and the spring rains.** On Hosea 10:12: if your garden is "completely overgrown" and you just invite the spring rains, "you're not going to end up with anything edible... just more weeds." You must weed first — break up the fallow ground — so God can grow good fruit.
- **The "OnlyFans / college fund" marriage.** Elle's analogy for cheap grace: dumping the kids' college funds "into OnlyFans or into sports gambling" and telling your spouse "our love isn't based on money, babe... why are you being so transactional?" — exactly what we do to God when we say "you love me no matter what, thanks" while living destructively.
- **"Let's dunk in the comments."** Brent and Elle on our culture's appetite for vengeance: "Let's get on social media. Let's do some vengeance... that lights up something" — the modern form of Jehu's "you will avenge."
- **The regrown war trenches.** Elle on how violence always "destroys the land": "good luck trying to find pictures of a war where the land stayed pristine... you can still see the scars of the land where the trenches were" — pointing toward "Guns to Gardens" and Isaiah's refashioned weapons.
- **"You didn't pass the vibe check."** Elle dramatizes God's verse-4 rejection in Hosea 6 — the people's lovely words met with "Whoa... I don't trust you. I don't believe you."

## Discussion Questions

1. Elle reframes the whole scroll around its name: *Hosea* means **rescue** (*yasha*). If you cross out "Hosea" and write "rescue," the book asks *what are the people being rescued from?* Her answer is a faith that makes violence feel "kosher." Where do you see that same baptizing-of-force in your own circles or culture?
2. The hosts insist the "unfaithful woman" reading, taught in isolation, becomes "a recipe for violence against women." How does reading the *whole* scroll (Jehu, the garden, the betrothal) change what you thought Hosea was about?
3. God asks for **both** A1 (acknowledge your guilt) **and** A2 (seek my face). Which one comes easily to you, and which one do you tend to skip — and what would it look like to do the harder one?
4. Elle says the dark chapters are God "draining the abscess," and that you can't just invite the spring rains onto an overgrown garden. Is there "fallow ground" in your life you've been inviting God to grow things in without first doing the weeding?
5. Elle argues the God of the Bible is "a person [who] reacts personally" — not a benevolent force floating in outer space. How does your working picture of God's *emotions* shape the "onus of responsibility" you feel in the relationship?
6. God refuses the *Ba'al* (master/slave) frame and insists on "my husband." Where have you dragged a "keep your head down and do what you're told" posture into your relationship with God, and what would trading it for intimacy require?
7. In Session 1 the question was whether the partner would say *yes* at Sinai. Here, in the age of betrayal, God alludes the unfaithful partner "back into the wilderness... to the days of her youth." What does it mean to you that God's response to unfaithfulness is to *court the partner again* rather than walk away?
