# Go

BEMA 132 · Session 3
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/3-132-go.md`
Book: Matthew (Matthew 28:1-20) — the resurrection and the Great Commission; with 1 Corinthians 15:12-14 (if there is no resurrection, faith is useless)
Co-host: Brent Billings · Host: Marty Solomon

> **Session 3 overarching arc: the king/Messiah embodying the story.** Session 1 built the vision of *partnership* — God seeking a people to carry the message and restore shalom. Session 2 traced the partner's unfaithfulness and God's refusal to quit — monarchy, corrupt priesthood, and prophets pleading a people back to the order of life. Session 3 is the Gospels: the king/Messiah stepping into Israel's story to embody it himself. This episode brings that embodiment to its climax — the empty tomb and the Great Commission — and then, explicitly, loops the whole thing back to the beginning. Marty names it outright: "we didn't actually get away from Session 1." The resurrection is God's final answer to the question the whole narrative has posed since Genesis — *which kingdom gets the last word, Empire or Shalom, the order of death or the order of life?* Ezekiel was shown dry bones and asked "can these bones live?"; the empty tomb answers "yes," and so the story *really can be trusted*. Then the risen king re-commissions the partnership: the disciples (doubters included, Peter reinstated) are sent to *make disciples, baptize, and teach* — carrying the embodied story to all nations. The episode advances the one story by turning the Messiah's own life-over-death into the mission of his people: the king has embodied the story, and now he keeps all authority for himself and sends partners to go embody it everywhere — "surely I am with you always, to the very end of the age." It also deliberately points forward to Session 4 (Paul, baptism of all nations, the "scandal" of the Gentiles) without resolving it here.

## Key Lessons

- **The resurrection is the lens that changes everything — it answers Ezekiel's question.** Of incarnation, crucifixion, and resurrection (all equal), the one you give preeminence shapes your theology; Marty chooses resurrection. On the empty-tomb morning, "God finally answered the question that He had allowed only Ezekiel to see in a vision. Apparently, death isn't as real as we thought it was and that changes everything." (Marty)
- **"Living was His reason for dying," not the reverse.** Against *Passion of the Christ*'s "dying was His reason for living," Marty flips it: "Jesus was more interested in our life before death. That's why He went to the cross." The resurrection is not a magic trick tacked on the end but the point.
- **The whole Jesus story is Session 1 in flesh — a Tale of Two Kingdoms.** "This whole Jesus thing has been a plain out—taking the story and putting it in flesh, of Empire and Shalom, of order and of disorder, of life and of death." The resurrection is God's affirmation that "the order of life really does get the last word." (Marty)
- **Resurrection makes hope rational — the empty tombs prove the claim.** Every tomb Marty has stood in — even ones claiming to be *the* tomb — has "nothing in them." "Resurrection is God's way of saying hope is worth it, and the story really can be trusted. I choose to be a person of hope." (Marty)
- **The Great Commission and the Ascension are two different events in two different places.** Marty's teacher Ray showed him the commission is given on *a mountain in Galilee*, while the Ascension happens at the Mount of Olives outside Jerusalem (Acts). "The great commission and the ascension of Jesus were not the same event... I couldn't believe it when he taught me that." (Marty)
- **The command to go *to the mountain* is a brilliant rabbinic field trip.** Marty's hunch (admittedly a hunch) is Mount Hermon — the Transfiguration site near Caesarea Philippi — which would force the disciples to walk "right past almost literally every moment of Jesus's ministry," unpacking his teaching post-resurrection, and to receive the commission where Jesus said "I will build my church." (Marty)
- **"Some doubted" — and Jesus commissions them anyway.** The text refuses to separate believers from doubters before the greatest command. "He does not separate the sheep from the goats for the great commission... apparently, doubt is acceptable for commissioned disciple-makers of Jesus." Peter is reinstated; it is still "the eleven." (Marty)
- **Teach — so you must know the Text.** "We are to teach others about God as seen in the person of Jesus Christ... I'm not sure how we're going to fulfill this command if we do not know his teachings," so we had better get "the Text" in us. (Marty) Teaching is helping people "see God everywhere," not bringing them to one holy mountain.
- **Baptize — an act of t'shuvah, inviting people home into a new Kingdom.** "The act of baptism is an act of repentance. We are inviting people to return home... a new King and a new Kingdom." The commission's baptism "of all nations" is tied to the Holy Spirit and Jesus's Kingdom — something new, not merely John's baptism of repentance. (Marty)
- **Make disciples — rabbinic "come, follow me," a call for *someone*, not necessarily everyone.** Marty reads the commission not as making converts or mentoring but as rabbinic discipleship: "find people who are willing and able to spend their whole selves becoming just like... their rabbi." He admits it is his most controversial point and that he "could be wrong." (Marty)
- **The risen rabbi keeps *all* authority for himself.** Unusually, Jesus does not pass his authority to disciples the way rabbis normally did. "He ain't given it away... All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me." We go "in the living authority of Jesus," with the closing promise "surely I am with you always, to the very end of the age." (Marty)

## East/West Lens

- **Narrative-over-doctrine instinct (Eastern): the resurrection is read as the climax of one unified *story*, not a freestanding proposition.** Marty insists the point of the empty tomb is that "the story really can be trusted," folding Christology back into the Genesis-to-Revelation narrative. Marty: *"we didn't actually get away from Session 1. We didn't get away from our core principles."*
- **Comfort with mystery and the unresolved (Eastern): doubt is left inside the commissioned community.** Rather than tidying the doubters out of the picture, the Hebraic instinct lets the tension stand. Marty: *"I love that we're told that some of the disciples doubted... apparently, doubt is acceptable for commissioned disciple-makers of Jesus."*
- **Context-first reading (Eastern): the commission is heard "through the ears of context."** Marty reads "make disciples" against the first-century rabbinic world rather than a modern evangelistic grid. Marty: *"I need to hear this command through the ears of context. The call to make disciples is a call to rabbinical, come-follow-me discipleship."*
- **God-meets-people-where-they-are (Eastern): the new baptism uses familiar cultural language.** Mikveh for Jews, ritual pagan washings (Asclepius, Demeter) for Gentiles — a known form filled with new meaning. Marty: *"it's God meeting people where they're at using a language that they're familiar with, but also doing something unique and different."*

Marty: *"In the beginning, we were invited to trust the story. At its core, the invitation of Genesis was an invitation to believe in the reality of the order of life."* — the empty tomb proves "the story really can be trusted."

- **Partnership re-commissioned — God's people sent to carry the story (Session 1 arc).** The risen king sends the disciples (doubters included) to make disciples of all nations. Marty: *"He says, 'No, you've seen me. Now go and help other people see me everywhere.'"*
- **Hope in the order of life over the order of death (continuation of the life/death choice motif).** Deuteronomy and Joshua set life and death before the people; the resurrection declares which wins. ## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

A sequential walkthrough of every teaching beat, in order. Used to verify full coverage.

1. **Cold open — bringing the Gospels to a close.** Brent frames the episode as closing the Gospels discussion with "a brief look at the resurrection and the great commission." Marty notes it is the end of Session 3, "almost" — a capstone lesson still remains.
2. **Marty's discomfort with studying the resurrection "cold."** He admits the crucifixion/resurrection material is hard for him to teach academically; he links two Real Life "resurrection Sunday" sermons in the show notes for the "more preachy... more poetic... more art" treatment. "I hate to study this story because it just feels so cold and academic."
3. **Matthew 28:1-4 read.** The earthquake, the angel rolling the stone, the terrified guards. "The greatest event in all human history."
4. **Three lenses of Christology (incarnation, crucifixion, resurrection).** All equal, but the one given preeminence shapes theology. Evangelical penal substitution centers the crucifixion ("Jesus died your death" — deferred to Session 4).
5. **"Dying was His reason for living" (Passion of the Christ) — flipped.** Marty's counter: "living was his reason for dying." The resurrection is not a "neat magic trick at the end."
6. **Modern theologians named.** Greg Boyd (centrality of the cross — not for substitutionary reasons but because God's nature is revealed on the cross; Marty has traded Twitter exchanges with him). Tony Jones (solidarity Christology, preeminence of the incarnation — God joining the struggle). Brent flags Hebrews/Session 4 for more.
7. **Marty's choice — preeminence of the resurrection, via the Tale of Two Kingdoms.** The whole Bible (Exodus to Revelation) is Empire vs. Shalom; the order of death (cancer, greed, fear, death) vs. the order of life (love, mercy, forgiveness, hope). It *looks* like death wins.
8. **The resurrection as God's answer to the life/death question.** Deuteronomy (life and death set before the people), Joshua (choose), the prophets pleading repentance, and Ezekiel's dry bones ("can these bones live?" — "Sovereign Lord, only you know"). The empty tomb answers it.
9. **1 Corinthians 15:12-14 read.** Paul does not say the crucifixion or incarnation makes faith vain — he says *without the resurrection* "your faith is useless." (Brent notes it's verses 12-14.)
10. **The empty tombs.** Marty has stood in tombs in Israel and Turkey — some claiming to be *the* tomb — and "they're all empty." Resurrection affirms the order of life, generosity, forgiveness, and love get the last word. "I choose to be a person of hope. He is not here. He is risen."
11. **Matthew 28:5-10 read.** The angel's message to the women; "do not be afraid"; Jesus meets them; "go and tell my brothers to go to Galilee."
12. **The commission is NOT the ascension — Ray's insight.** Marty had always assumed Jesus gave the commission just before ascending; his teacher Ray showed him otherwise. The Ascension is at the Mount of Olives (Acts); the commission is on a Galilee mountain. (Mark 16 after v.8 dismissed as unreliable — "garbage," softened.)
13. **"To Galilee" — a week-long journey.** Jerusalem to Galilee is a three-day run or six-/seven-day walk. Brent jokes it would be "a good bucket list item."
14. **Matthew 28:11-15 read — the guards bribed.** The chief priests pay the soldiers to say the disciples stole the body; "this story has been widely circulated among the Jews to this very day."
15. **Which mountain? The rabbinic field-trip hunch.** Jesus says only "the mountain," never naming it, yet the disciples understand. Mount Arbel is the only real "mountain mountain" by the Sea of Galilee; other peaks (Sermon on the Mount is just a rise; Gamla a knoll) are far off. Marty's hunch: Mount Hermon ("holy mountain"), the Transfiguration site (traditional guess Mount Tabor, which Marty rejects), tied to the phrase "there you will see him."
16. **Why Hermon would be brilliant (two reasons).** (1) The disciples must walk from Jerusalem all the way to the northernmost point of Jesus's ministry (Caesarea Philippi / Golan Heights), passing "almost literally every moment of Jesus's ministry," then walk all the way back to the Mount of Olives to receive the Ascension — a field trip to unpack his teaching post-resurrection. (2) Caesarea Philippi is where Jesus said he would "build my church" — fitting to utter the commission to "all nations... even to the very gates of hell" in that same place.
17. **Matthew 28:16-17 read — "but some doubted."** Marty loves that the authors leave doubt in; if you were inventing the story you'd have everyone on board. Jesus does not separate believers from doubters; Peter is reinstated ("the eleven"). "Doubt is acceptable for commissioned disciple-makers of Jesus" — a core BEMA passion.
18. **Matthew 28:18-20 read — the Great Commission.** "All authority... go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing... teaching... and surely I am with you always."
19. **Unpacking backwards — "teach them."** Jesus wants his followers to be teachers who help people "see God everywhere," passing on his teachings — which requires knowing "the Text."
20. **"Baptize them" — t'shuvah and a new Kingdom.** Baptism as repentance/return, marking entry into the new Kingdom under a new King; "a putting on of a new self... and a washing away of the old."
21. **Brent's question — what about the Gentiles and mikveh?** Jews already have mikveh everywhere; what of Gentiles? Pagan ritual washings existed (Asclepius in Turkey, Demeter), so the idea is relatable — God meeting people in familiar language while doing "something unique and different."
22. **This baptism is not merely John's baptism.** Foreshadowing Paul and Apollos: John's was *tavilat t'shuvah* (Essene baptism of repentance); the new baptism is "connected to the Holy Spirit and the Kingdom that Jesus has ushered in." Marty admits he hadn't made this strong enough before.
23. **"Make disciples" — Marty's most controversial reading.** Not making converts (which he loves) nor spiritual-formation mentorship, but rabbinic "come, follow me" discipleship — becoming just like the rabbi. Read "through the ears of context."
24. **A calling for *someone*, not everyone.** Only ~1% were disciples in Jesus's day; the rest were faithful Torah-keepers, not failures. Marty admits "I could be wrong"; Brent offers Paul's gifts list (apostles, teachers) as support; Session 4 (Paul's life) will test it.
25. **Why campus ministry.** Marty got into campus ministry because college students have "a certain level of freedom" (no spouse, mortgage) to be told "come, follow me" the way a rabbi would — eat, live, do life together.
26. **Jesus keeps all the authority.** Unlike normal rabbis who pass authority to disciples so the story continues, the risen Jesus retains "all authority... has been given to me." We are commissioned in his *living* authority.
27. **Closing words — "to the very end of the age (olam)."** The final promise for the tough moments: "surely I am with you always, to the very end of the age." Brent: "the end of the olam?" Marty: "the end of this olam."
28. **Wrap — "every verse of Matthew."** They note they fulfilled the "crazy idea" of reading every verse of Matthew; Session 3 ran months long; a capstone lesson is next.

## Terminology

- **incarnation / crucifixion / resurrection (Christology)** — the three lenses through which Jesus's life is read: incarnation (the birth, "God becoming a man"), crucifixion (his death), resurrection (his new life). All equal, but the one given preeminence shapes one's theology. Marty: *"our theology is shaped by which of these three we choose to view it through."*
- **substitutionary / penal substitutionary atonement** — the evangelical theory that "Jesus died your death," which naturally centers the crucifixion; raised here and deferred to Session 4. Marty: *"Much of evangelical Christianity clings to the idea of substitutionary atonement... Jesus died your death."*
- **solidarity Christology** — Tony Jones's school emphasizing the preeminence of the incarnation: "God came to join us in solidarity... the cross being the ultimate end of that work of solidarity." (Marty)
- **Empire / Shalom (the Two Kingdoms)** — the two orders the whole Bible dramatizes: the order of death (cancer, greed, fear, death) vs. the order of life (love, mercy, forgiveness, hope). Marty: *"Empire and Shalom... the order of death and the order of life."*
- **t'shuvah** (Hebrew תְּשׁוּבָה, "return / repentance") — the meaning Marty gives baptism: an invitation to "return home... come back to where God originally created them to be."
- **mikveh** (Hebrew מִקְוֶה, ritual immersion bath) — the Jewish ritual immersion already woven through Jewish culture and "readily available all over the place," which Jews would bring to baptism. Marty: *"they're going to have mikveh as part of their culture already."*
- **tavilat t'shuvah** (Hebrew טְבִילַת תְּשׁוּבָה, "immersion of repentance") — John's / the Essene baptism of repentance, which Marty distinguishes from the Spirit-and-Kingdom baptism of the commission. Marty: *"John was doing a tavilat t'shuvah... Essene mikvah... baptism of repentance."*
- **make disciples (rabbinic "come, follow me" discipleship)** — the process in which "a disciple wanted to know what the rabbi knows in order to do what the rabbi does, in order to be just like the rabbi in his walk with God." (Marty) Marty's controverted reading of the commission's central verb.
- **all authority** — the risen rabbi's retention of authority rather than passing it to disciples (the normal rabbinic move). Marty: *"He ain't given it away... All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me."*
- **olam** (Hebrew עוֹלָם, "age / world / long duration") — "age," as in "to the very end of the age." Brent: *"The end of the olam?"* / Marty: *"The end of this olam."*

## Illustrations & Stories

- **"Dying was His reason for living" — Passion of the Christ (Marty).** The opening/closing slide of Mel Gibson's film, used as a foil: Marty flips it to "living was his reason for dying" to argue the resurrection, not the cross alone, is the point.
- **The empty tombs (Marty).** Marty has stood in multiple tombs in Israel and Turkey — some claiming directly or indirectly to be *the* tomb — and the one true thing about all of them is "there's nothing in them... they're all empty." A tangible argument that "the order of life really does get the last word."
- **Ezekiel's valley of dry bones (Marty/Brent).** The prophet shown dry bones and asked "can these bones live?", answering "Sovereign Lord, only you know." The resurrection is God finally answering that question — the one he "had allowed only Ezekiel to see in a vision."
- **The rabbinic field trip to the mountain (Marty).** If the commission mountain is Mount Hermon, the disciples' journey from Jerusalem to the northern tip of Jesus's ministry (Caesarea Philippi / Golan Heights) and back to the Mount of Olives becomes a walking review "right past almost literally every moment of Jesus's ministry."
- **Jerusalem-to-Galilee as a "bucket list" walk (Brent).** Brent's aside that walking the three-to-seven-day route from Jerusalem to Galilee "sounds like a good bucket list item."
- **Pagan ritual cleansings — Asclepius and Demeter (Marty/Brent).** From their study in Turkey: before entering the Asclepion you underwent a ritual cleansing, and Demeter's cult had washings too — showing Gentiles already had a relatable concept of cleansing for baptism to meet.
- **Campus ministry as rabbinic discipleship (Marty).** Marty's autobiographical reason for campus ministry: college students have "a certain level of freedom" (no spouse, no mortgage) that lets him say "come, follow me" — "get up when I get up, eat breakfast with me... live life with me."
- **"Every verse of Matthew" (Brent/Marty).** The running project payoff — they fulfilled Marty's "crazy idea" of reading every verse of Matthew, stretching Session 3 "a few months longer."

## Discussion Questions

1. Of incarnation, crucifixion, and resurrection — all equal — which one do you *practically* give preeminence to as your "first filter"? How has that choice (perhaps unconsciously) shaped the way you read Jesus, and what would change if you shifted the lens?
2. Marty flips "dying was His reason for living" into "living was His reason for dying," arguing Jesus "was more interested in our life before death." Where in your faith has the cross overshadowed the resurrection — and what would it look like to let the empty tomb reframe your hope?
3. The resurrection is cast as God's answer to Ezekiel's "can these bones live?" — a declaration that "the order of life really does get the last word." Where does the order of death currently *look* like it's winning in your life or world, and what would it mean to "choose to be a person of hope" there?
4. Jesus commissions the Eleven even though "some doubted," refusing to separate believers from doubters (Peter among them). How does it change your sense of calling to know that "doubt is acceptable for commissioned disciple-makers of Jesus"? Where are you waiting to be certain before you'll go?
5. Marty's "make disciples" is rabbinic "come, follow me" — becoming just like the rabbi — and he thinks it's a call for *someone*, not everyone, while openly admitting "I could be wrong." Do you find his reading compelling? Who, concretely, could you say "come, follow me" to — or who might be saying it to you?
6. Teaching the commission presupposes knowing Jesus's teaching: "we better be working on getting the Text in us." Be honest about how well you actually know his words. What is one practice that would get more of the Text in you this year?
7. The risen rabbi keeps *all* authority for himself rather than handing it off, yet sends his people out "in the living authority of Jesus" with "surely I am with you always, to the very end of the age." How does going in *his* authority (not your own) change the way you'd approach the work you feel sent to do?
