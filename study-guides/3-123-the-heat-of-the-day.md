# BEMA 123 — The Heat of the Day — Study Guide

**Session 3 (The Gospels) · Episode 123**
**Speakers:** Marty Solomon, Brent Billings
**Primary texts:** Matthew 20:1–34; Isaiah 61:1–7
**Source transcript:** `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/3-123-the-heat-of-the-day.md`

---

## Overview

This episode works through Matthew 20 on the road up to Jerusalem: the parable of the workers in the vineyard, Jesus's third passion prediction, the request of the mother of James and John, the teaching on servant greatness, and the healing of the two blind men outside Jericho. The anchor is the parable — read not as a lesson about deathbed conversions but, through the rabbinic phrase "bearing the burden in the heat of the day," as an explicitly Jew-and-Gentile story about a God who is not fair but outrageously generous.

Within the Session 3 arc — the king/Messiah embodying the one story — this episode shows the King teaching and enacting the shape of the Kingdom he is about to die for. Session 1 established the partnership: Israel agreeing "to work for a denarius" so that all nations could be blessed. Session 2 traced the partner's unfaithfulness and God's refusal to quit. Here the King reframes that partnership's *reward*: the "double portion" of the firstborn turns out to be *everything set right* and *everyone blessed*, and the King embodies it by refusing power ("not to be served but to serve") on the very road to his own death. The episode advances the one story by exposing how badly even the inner circle misreads the Kingdom — jockeying for thrones right after a passion prediction — while two culturally-rejected blind men see the King most clearly of all.

> **Marty:** "God is not fair. God is horribly unfair in a beautifully, generous, and benevolent way."

## Episode Flow (Coverage Backbone)

Segment-by-segment list of what the hosts actually cover, in order. Coverage is verified against this list below; strays are parked in **Other Talking Points**.

1. **Setup of the parable (Matthew 20:1–8).** The landowner hires workers through the day and pays the last-hired first.
2. **"Don't race past the assumptions" — why pay the least first?** Marty presses the group to wrestle: God *could* have avoided the whole conflict; he stages it on purpose so the generosity is *seen*.
3. **The parable's resolution (Matthew 20:9–16).** Equal pay, grumbling, "are you envious because I am generous?", "the last will be first."
4. **PaRDeS exercise — four p'shat conclusions.** God loves undeserved generosity; God wants our selfishness transformed; the Kingdom isn't fair but benevolent; it is this way for people "we think don't deserve it."
5. **"Bearing the burden in the heat of the day."** The rabbinic phrase for Jews carrying the weight of Torah on behalf of the world; it makes the parable about Jew and Gentile (5 p.m. workers = Gentiles). The "how much is it / we'll take two" story.
6. **Finding the remez — Genesis 18 (Abraham in the heat of the day).** Radical hospitality to the three visitors as the key to "why the last got paid first."
7. **Finding the remez — Isaiah 61:1–7.** Is "foreigners will work your vineyards" a curse or a blessing? Resolved via 4 Isaiah's theme (hope): it is blessing, the year of the Lord's favor.
8. **The double portion / bechor.** The firstborn's double portion (Isaiah 61:7) reframed as *everything in its proper place*; being the conduit of blessing to the nations is itself the blessing.
9. **Brent's reward tension.** Cherry-picked "reward" verses vs. long passages; the reward is different than we think, and you must read the whole of Scripture.
10. **Going up to Jerusalem (Matthew 20:17–19).** Always "up" to Mount Zion; the third passion prediction.
11. **"God goggles."** Jesus foreseeing his death as a human reading a corrupt situation; the hard case is foreseeing resurrection "on the third day." Philippians 2 / Hebrews — set aside divine advantage, "learned obedience."
12. **The mother of Zebedee's sons (Matthew 20:20–23).** "Mommy chutzpah"; the cup; James and John answer "we can" like true talmidim.
13. **Servant greatness (Matthew 20:24–28).** The ten are indignant; "not so with you"; the Son of Man came to serve and give his life as a ransom. Forward glance to Session 4 (these same men lead an inclusive movement).
14. **The two blind men at Jericho (Matthew 20:29–34).** "Son of David" from fellow outsiders (mamzer theme); "What do you want me to do for you?"; compassion and sight.

## Key Lessons

Framed against the Session 3 arc (the King embodying the story), with the explicit move from Session 1 (partnership) and Session 2 (unfaithfulness / God refusing to quit).

- **The Kingdom isn't fair — it's benevolent — and God stages his generosity to be *seen*.** Marty: *"The Kingdom of God is not fair. A lot of us have this American concept of the Kingdom of Heaven, God is fair. No, God is not fair. God is horribly unfair in a beautifully, generous, and benevolent way."* The owner pays the last first *on purpose*: Marty: *"Why would God purposely pay the least first in front of everybody else? ... Almost like God is wanting to show something. The God character here almost wants it to be seen."* The King is publicly dramatizing the character of the God whose story he embodies.

- **The parable is about Jew and Gentile, not deathbed conversions.** The phrase "heat of the day" locks the meaning. Marty: *"when Jesus tells this parable, He very intentionally drops that phrase in there and it makes this parable very deliberately about Jew and Gentile... The 5:00 PM workers are quite literally Gentiles."* This is the Session 1 partnership cashed out: *"we have always agreed to work for a denarius so to speak. The whole reason we agreed to work for a denarius was so that all the Gentiles could get a denarius."*

- **The "double portion" of the firstborn is *everything set right*, and only the transformed heart wants it.** From Isaiah 61:7 and the *bechor* concept, Marty: *"become the type of people where your heart leaps and rejoices when you get told your double portion is everything in its proper place. Everybody being blessed. Everybody having enough, that will be our double portion."* Brent names the shape: *"we get the wealth of nations and we become the conduit that blesses all the other nations... Being that conduit is the blessing."* This is the partnership vocation (bless all nations) reframed as reward.

- **Heaven is only "heaven" for those transformed to love its generosity.** Marty (via a British theologian's "flames of heaven"): *"Heaven is more like hell to you because you don't actually like what heaven is, you don't want unadulterated generosity... you're the elder brother in that prodigal son story, and heaven isn't heaven for you."* The King is inviting the religious ("the ones that have been there the whole time") into a converted heart, not a payout.

- **The King's greatness is service unto death — and he embodies it on the road to the cross.** Marty on Matthew 20:28: Jesus teaches *"this upside-down wacky"* kingdom where *"whoever wants to be first must be your slave."* The passion prediction and the throne-request collide to expose how the disciples keep *"spinning these words into words of revolution."* The King embodies the story the partner never could; this is Session 2's "God refuses to quit" taken all the way into the King's own body.

- **Outsiders see the King most clearly.** The blind men's "Son of David" is a loaded confession from fellow rejected ones. Marty: *"The world may see you as a mamzer, the world may not respect your pedigree, but we believe in who you are... They're making a bigger statement than that."* The King who will be *"handed over to the Gentiles"* is first truly named by the marginalized.

## East/West Lens

BEMA's recurring Hebraic (Eastern, concrete, relational, story-shaped) vs. Greek-Western (abstract, systematized) contrast. Only categories actually engaged in this episode are tagged, each with a citation.

- **Discovery over information distribution (Hebraic).** The whole parable segment is a staged refusal to hand over the answer; Marty keeps the group in the question. Marty: *"don't race past the immediate assumptions we make about the story, they are worth wrestling with. Ask more questions about the story... really, really wrestle with that."*

- **Layered reading / PaRDeS (Hebraic).** Meaning is pursued through levels — p'shat then remez — rather than a single flat moral. Marty: *"let's do our PaRDeS exercise here... There's a lot of just p'shat conclusions,"* then *"We have to find the remez."*

- **Text in conversation with text / intertextuality (Hebraic).** "Heat of the day" and "foreigners in your vineyards" are read through Genesis 18 and Isaiah 61; the double portion is read back through the *bechor* of Session 1. Marty: *"when I say heat of the day, what is the first story you think of... Abraham sitting in the shade of a tree."*

- **Fairness vs. generosity (Hebraic justice as benevolence).** The Western instinct toward proportional fairness is named and overturned. Marty: *"A lot of us have this American concept of the Kingdom of Heaven, God is fair. No, God is not fair."*

- **Jesus as fully human, not viewing through omniscience (Hebraic).** The "God goggles" discussion insists Jesus operates within his human nature. Marty: *"I don't believe that Jesus ever looked at things through his... God goggles,"* reinforced by Brent with Philippians 2 and Hebrews: *"even though Jesus was God, He did not use that to his advantage. He set it aside."*

- **Reading the whole scroll vs. proof-texting (Hebraic).** Brent's reward tension is resolved by refusing to isolate verses. Brent: *"you have to know the whole Scriptures and put it together. You can't just pick and choose to get what you want."*

## Recurring Through-lines

Derived from *this episode only* (the registry is not consulted). Every explicit motif is captured with a cited quote; genuinely-implied ones are tagged `implied`.

- **Partnership for the sake of the nations (Session 1 arc, explicit).** The denarius-agreement *is* the Abrahamic call to bless all nations. Marty: *"The whole reason we agreed to work for a denarius was so that all the Gentiles could get a denarius. This is our calling at play."*

- **Radical hospitality / blessing all nations (explicit).** Genesis 18 as the remez. Marty: *"we have to be radically generous because that's the call of Abraham to bless all nations."*

- **The Gentile inclusion thread running through these chapters (explicit).** Marty: *"Jesus is still on this Gentile kick we've encountered for the last few chapters... this whole thing's about the Gentiles, and you don't want it to be."*

- **The bechor / double portion from Session 1 (explicit callback).** Marty: *"The firstborn, bechor, right? Going back to Session 1,"* and the double portion means more responsibility *and* more blessing.

- **Jesus as mamzer / the outsider (explicit, Matthew's agenda).** Marty: *"Chilton makes a case that I agree with... that he thinks Jesus would've been seen culturally as a mamzer,"* and the blind men's confession reads against that stigma.

- **Being a true talmid — "you can be like me" (explicit callback to Peter on the water).** Marty: *"Let's just give them the same props we gave Peter... Jesus, by calling them, said that you can be like me."*

- **The upside-down Kingdom / first-last reversal (explicit).** Marty: *"this upside-down wacky"* kingdom; *"So the last will be first, and the first will be last."*

- **God refuses to quit on an un-getting partner (Session 2 arc, `implied`).** The disciples plainly don't understand ("they don't get it now in this verse, but they end up getting it"), yet the King keeps teaching and heading to the cross for them — the Session 2 refusal-to-quit carried into the King's body. Marty: *"I'm just moved as I look at the young, immature, untrained disciple, knowing where it is that they're heading."*

## Terminology

- **p'shat** — the plain/surface meaning (first level of PaRDeS). Marty: *"There's a lot of just p'shat conclusions. I have four written down in front of me."*
- **remez** — the "hint": a deeper allusion pointing to another passage. Marty: *"We have to find the remez. There's a couple of options."*
- **PaRDeS** — the layered Jewish interpretive method (p'shat / remez / and further levels). Marty: *"let's do our PaRDeS exercise here, Brent."*
- **denarius** — a standard day's wage; Torah requires paying a worker at day's end. Marty: *"Torah said you have to pay a worker at the end of every single day."*
- **"bearing the burden in the heat of the day"** — a rabbinic phrase for Jews carrying the weight of Torah on behalf of the world, making the parable about Jew and Gentile. Marty: *"it was a phrase that was used commonly to talk about the Jew's relationship with the Gentile."*
- **bechor** (בכור) — the firstborn, who bears more responsibility and receives a *double portion*. Marty: *"The firstborn, bechor, right? Going back to Session 1."*
- **double portion** — the firstborn's inheritance; reframed in Isaiah 61 as *everything set right*. Marty: *"your double portion is everything in its proper place. Everybody being blessed."*
- **chutzpah** — bold audacity. Marty: *"We call that chutzpah. Mommy chutzpah."*
- **the cup** — the suffering Jesus will drink; James and John will drink of it in martyrdom. Marty: *"They'll at least take some sips of that cup that Jesus is going to drink from."*
- **talmid / talmidim** — disciple(s) who follow a rabbi believing they can become like him. Marty: *"isn't that the right answer, Brent, if they're talmidim?"*
- **Son of David** — a loaded messianic confession, here from culturally-rejected outsiders. Marty: *"That is a statement about, 'The world may see you as a mamzer... but we believe in who you are.'"*
- **mamzer / mumzer** — the outsider / one of questioned legitimacy; part of Matthew's agenda and possibly applied to Jesus himself. Marty: *"he thinks Jesus would've been seen culturally as a mamzer."*
- **God goggles** — Marty's shorthand for the (rejected) idea that Jesus viewed everything through divine omniscience. Marty: *"I don't believe that Jesus ever looked at things through his... God goggles."*
- **going up to Jerusalem** — always "up" to Mount Zion regardless of literal elevation. Marty: *"it's always up because Mount Zion is the highest of all mountains... Spiritually."*
- **4 Isaiah** — the fourth section of Isaiah, whose theme word is *hope*; the context that turns Isaiah 61:5 from curse to blessing. Marty: *"4 Isaiah's theme is hope."*

## Illustrations & Stories

- **"How much is it? We'll take two."** The rabbinic joke about God offering the covenant: the Greeks refuse the 613 commandments, the Americans "bow the knee to nobody, #Merica," and the Jews ask the price — "It's free" — and say "We'll take two." Marty uses it to picture Israel *gladly* carrying the burden on behalf of the world. Marty: *"the Jews looked at God and said, 'How much is it?' and God said, 'It's free,' and they said, 'We'll take two.'"*
- **The "flames of heaven."** A British theologian's picture that heaven becomes hell for the untransformed heart. Marty: *"You get to heaven and heaven is not really heaven. Heaven is more like hell to you because you don't actually like what heaven is."*
- **Imagining "those people" ushered to a better seat.** A thought-exercise: picture whoever "those people" are for you getting the same (or better) reward, and let your heart learn to say *"Oh, yes!"* instead of *"that's not fair."* Marty: *"imagine them getting ushered in, getting the same reward, sitting in a better seat than you got."*
- **Ray on 2,000 years of Torah-keeping.** Marty recalls Ray teaching that Jews kept kosher, cleanliness, and clothing laws *"so that the world could know who God was... They did that for you and for me as Gentiles."*
- **Marty and the homeless in campus ministry.** Four years of ministry taught him he was *"just totally dead wrong"* about assuming he knew what people needed — a lens on Jesus asking "What do you want me to do for you?" Marty: *"I went into that thinking I knew what they needed... only to find out... What I really needed to do is engage with them in relationship."*

## Other Talking Points

Minor strays parked here so the coverage backbone stays clean:

- **Brent's self-study on "reward" verses.** Brent describes having collected many individual verses promising rewards "based on this or that," then realizing they ignore longer passages like Isaiah 61. Brent: *"you can find all these individual verses that talk about your reward... but it completely ignores longer passages like this that don't speak directly to that."* Resolution: read the whole of Scripture, not cherry-picked proof texts (ties back to the Sermon on the Mount wrestling earlier in the session).
- **Jesus "learning" and a process of discovery.** Marty clarifies that affirming Jesus learned/discovered does not imply he learned through sin: *"It doesn't mean that there was a sinful... there is a process of learning, there's a process of illumination."*

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty insists "God is not fair... horribly unfair in a beautifully, generous, and benevolent way." Where have you quietly assumed the Kingdom runs on fairness, and what in you resists a God who lavishes generosity on people "who don't deserve it"?
2. The owner pays the last-hired *first, in front of everyone,* on purpose. Why might God *want* his generosity to be seen and provoke us, rather than quietly avoiding the offense?
3. Run Marty's thought-exercise: picture whoever "those people" are for you being ushered into a better seat than yours and given the same reward. What does your gut reaction reveal about the transformation this parable is inviting?
4. The "double portion" turns out to be *everything set right / everyone blessed* — and only the transformed heart wants it. Is that a reward you actually long for? What would have to change in you for it to feel like good news?
5. Marty reads the parable through "bearing the burden in the heat of the day" as a Jew-and-Gentile story. How does the Session 1 idea — Israel "agreeing to work for a denarius so that all the Gentiles could get a denarius" — reshape what you think the people of God are *for*?
6. James and John answer "we can" drink the cup, and Marty defends it as a faithful talmid's response. When has believing "my rabbi called me, so I can be like him" led you to say "we can" to something costly?
7. The blind men — fellow outsiders — are the ones who clearly name Jesus "Son of David." Why do you think the marginalized so often see the King most clearly, and where might that pattern be true in your own community?
8. Marty leaves "raised on the third day" as an open question he can't resolve. How do you hold a text that genuinely puzzles you without either forcing an answer or dismissing it?
