# Images of the Desert — Rotem and Acacia

BEMA 27 · Session 1
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/1-27-images-of-the-desert-rotem-and-acacia.md`
Hosts: Marty Solomon & Brent Billings
Book: Numbers (the desert / wilderness) — an **image-driven devotional episode** in the "Images of the Desert" mini-series, drawing on scattered shade/tree references (Judges 9; Psalms 80, 121, 1; Song of Songs 2; Isaiah 4, 25, 30, 32; Jeremiah 17).

> **Session 1 arc — partnership.** Marty frames Numbers as the wilderness wandering, and the mini-series as "the things you would experience and see in that desert." Two trees carry today's lesson: the **rotem** (broom bush), the desert image of *shade* — small, solitary, "just enough"; and the **acacia**, "the gift of the desert" that can look dead for a decade and then spring to life. The partnership move: God is our shade and gift *first*, and then invites us to *be* shade and gift for others in their deserts. As Marty puts it, "It's not just that God is our shade. It's that God invites us to be shade."

## Key Lessons

- **God is "the shade at your right hand" — just-enough refuge, never farther than you can reach.** The rotem is not luxury comfort but a solitary bush with shade for one. On Psalm 121, Marty: *"That is as far as God is ever from you... The relief and the refuge you need for this moment, just enough, is at your right hand. It's never further away than that."* God's constant accessibility is the fixed point in every desert.
- **We are not only shaded — we are called to *be* shade for others.** Isaiah 32 has one King reign in righteousness, but *plural* rulers who *each* become "a shelter and a shade" — the kingdom-of-priests theme again. Marty: *"It's not just that God is our shade. It's that God invites us to be shade. Each one of us... is supposed to be a refuge in the storm."* You needn't be a mighty oak with every answer — *"I can show up and be a rotem bush."*
- **Faithfulness in drought bears fruit in its season — the acacia.** The acacia can "sit dormant for a decade" and look dead, then spring to life with the rains. Read against Psalm 1 (per Noga Hareuveni), "yields its fruit in season" means fruit on God's unknown timing. Marty: *"You keep showing up. You keep being faithful... when the time comes, you will be known as the gift of the desert. Do not lose heart."*
- **To "meditate" on the word (hagah) is to *growl* over it like a lion over its prey.** Marty: Psalm 1 is *"not talking about a quiet morning on the front porch with a sunrise and a cappuccino and an Instagram post that says #blessed."* *Hagah* is the low, rumbling growl of a hungry lion hunched over its kill — ravenous delight in the Text.
- **This learning is meant to be handed on — disciple one or two young adults.** Marty closes by urging listeners to "pursue, model, and teach" (Impact's discipleship language): *"You don't have to have this mastered to disciple somebody in it. You can actually learn it along with them."*

## East/West Lens

- **Meaning carried by word-pictures (over definitions).** The whole episode teaches through *images* — shade, the gift-tree — rather than propositions. Marty on the shade texts: *"we read over the shade references too easily... now they jump off the page to me."* Experiencing the desert makes the picture do the theological work.
- **Onomatopoeia — the word *sounds like* the thing.** *Hagah* is Hebrew that "sounds like the thing it's talking about": not the roar of a lion but "the low, rumbling growl of a lion." The Eastern text engages the ear and body, not just the intellect.
- **Provision as "just enough" (sufficiency over Western abundance).** The rotem's shade is deliberately meager — "just enough shade... not an abundance." Marty names the contrast with "my Western American mind and heart. I think of everything in abundance. I think of shade and I want comfort. I want luxury."
- **Truth unfolding over time (dynamic, not scheduled).** The acacia's "season" is unknown — "it could be years of drought" — so fruitfulness unfolds on God's timeline, not a predictable Western calendar of annual harvests.
- **Community over the solitary individual.** Isaiah 32's *each one* becomes shade/refuge, and the closing call to disciple one or two young adults ("pursue, model, teach") makes the lesson communal rather than private.

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story.** Both trees are "trust the story" images for the desert: trust that God is your just-enough shade at your right hand, and trust that faithful waiting through drought will bear fruit in its season. God's priorities — presence, provision, fruitfulness — don't change even when your life "can look quite dead on the outside." Marty: *"Do not lose heart."*
- **Kingdom of priests (callback to the Sinai covenant, eps. 18 & 22).** Isaiah 32's "one King / plural rulers who each become shade" is explicitly flagged as the kingdom-of-priests idea — Marty: *"Oh, a kingdom of priests throwback. Woo!"* God's people are deputized to *be* God's refuge to the world.
- **Master the beast (redirected animal instinct).** The *hagah* image carries the same ravenous animal drive seen in "master the beast," now *redirected* toward devouring God's word rather than one's own appetite — "a lion hunched over its prey, wanting to devour." Isaiah 30's warning (running to "Pharaoh's shade" / empire for refuge instead of God) is the same un-mastered impulse to secure yourself the wrong way. Flag for cross-reference; do not re-explain here.
- **Just enough / daily provision (callback to the shepherd image, ep. 26, and manna, ep. 20).** The rotem's shade is explicitly tied back to the shepherd episode's "just enough": Marty — *"What's the word we talked about with the shepherds in our last episode, Brent?... Just enough."* God provides sufficiency for *this* moment, not stockpiled abundance.

## Chiasms & Structure

No chiasm or narrative structure — this is a two-image devotional. The organizing shape is the pairing of the two trees, each teaching a God-first lesson that becomes a be-this-for-others call:

| Tree | Image | Lesson (God for us → us for others) |
|---|---|---|
| **rotem** (broom bush) | *shade* — small, solitary, "just enough" | God is just-enough refuge at your right hand (Psalm 121) → *you* can be a rotem-bush of shade for others; you needn't be a mighty oak with all the answers (Isaiah 32, "each one") |
| **acacia** (*shittah*) | *the gift of the desert* — looks dead for years, then blooms | Faithfulness through drought bears fruit "in its season" (Psalm 1 / Jeremiah 17) → keep showing up and *hagah* the Text so that, in God's timing, you become "the gift of the desert" |

## Terminology

- **rotem** (רֹתֶם) — the broom tree / broom bush, the desert image of *shade*: small (no more than ~three feet tall), solitary ("you're not going to find 40 rotem bushes together... It's a solitary bush"), offering only "just enough" shade for one person. Elijah collapses under one after Mount Carmel (1 Kings 19); rabbinic tradition places Ishmael under one (Genesis 21). Marty: *"the picture of shade in the desert."*
- **acacia** (שִׁטָּה, *shittah*) — "the gift of the desert" (so named by the Bedouins): its wood is strong for building and burns "longer and hotter," its sap/"pitch" is used medicinally, and its pods boiled in water feed livestock ("one kilo... will feed a camel for a month"). It can "sit dormant for a decade," looking dead, then spring to life with the rains. Marty leaves the sap-vs-pitch wording an open, affectionate disagreement with his wife.
- **hagah** (הָגָה) — to "meditate," but onomatopoeically the low, rumbling *growl* of a hungry lion hunched over its prey (the growl of Jeremiah / Isaiah 31:4). Psalm 1's "meditate on his law day and night" is not placid quiet time but a ravenous growling-over of the Text.
- **wadi** — a desert canyon/ravine, dry most of the year but prone to flash floods ("a number one killer in the desert"). The acacia grows at the end of wadis, where the floodwater spreads out "and eventually just kind of seeps in."

## Illustrations & Stories

- **RVL's 40-minute shade lesson.** Marty recalls learning the rotem lesson first with Ray Vander Laan in the desert: *"there's 54 of us. He wanted everybody to have a chance to sit in the shade of a rotem bush. And so he taught for 40 minutes while we stood out in the blazing hot heat... and everybody got like 30 seconds in the shade."* Embodied proof that the shade is "just enough" — "you couldn't fit two people underneath there."
- **Christi Pedrow's photograph.** The show-notes image of the rotem both hosts "have loved for years" — "the perfect representation of what we're going to talk about today." Brent, deadpan: *"that's a very full rotem tree."*
- **Marty avoiding grief, lament, and hospital calls.** His confession that he resisted being present in others' pain because *"I felt like I had to be an oak tree of shade... I had to have all the answers."* The rotem reframes the call: *"I can show up and be a rotem bush."*
- **Bethel University / South Bend (Brent).** A team "had been trying to get something going at Bethel University for such a long time and just nothing... striking out year after year," then "a little flash flood, and all of a sudden they had 50 people in a commuter Bible study." An acacia season arriving.
- **Mitch in Florida (Brent).** One of the earliest BEMA groups: Mitch "sat there in Florida for two and a half years, didn't hear from a single person. And then over the course of two weeks, he had a dozen people and a thriving discussion group." Brent: *"those two and a half years, that's a long time to wait."*

## Discussion Questions

1. The rotem offers only "just enough" shade for one. Where are you waiting for abundant, comfortable relief when God is offering just-enough refuge for *this* moment, "never further away than you can reach"?
2. Marty admits he avoided grief, lament, and hospital visits because he thought he had to be "an oak tree of shade" with all the answers. Where could you show up as a "rotem bush" — just-enough shade — for someone in their desert right now?
3. The acacia can look dead for a decade before it blooms. What area of your life or ministry feels dormant, and what would faithful "showing up" look like while you wait for the season (think of Mitch's two-and-a-half years, or South Bend's long drought)?
4. *Hagah* means to growl over the word like a hungry lion over its kill — not "a cappuccino and an Instagram post that says #blessed." How does that reframe your picture of "meditating on Scripture," and what would ravenous engagement with the Text look like for you?
5. Isaiah 30 warns against running to "Pharaoh's shade" (empire) for refuge instead of to God. Where are you tempted to find your security in the wrong shade?
6. Isaiah 32 says *each one* of God's people becomes shade and refuge — the kingdom of priests. Who are the one or two young adults you could "pursue, model, and teach," discipling them in these desert lessons even while you are still learning them yourself?
