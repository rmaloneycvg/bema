# His Bow in the Clouds

**BEMA 4 · Session 1** — Genesis (Noah and the Flood: Genesis 6-9)
Source transcript: `transcripts/1-his-bow-in-the-clouds-4.md`
Speakers: Marty Solomon, Brent Billings, **Elle Grover Fricks** (guest — M.A., Bible in its ancient Near Eastern context, Hebrew University of Jerusalem)

---

## Key Lessons

The flood story is a second creation account, and the God revealed in it holds the same priorities — redemption over destruction, relationship over fear.

- **God hangs up his war-bow — he disarms himself rather than rule by fear.** The *keshet* is a weapon, and it is set in the clouds pointed *upward*, back toward God, not aimed at the earth. Cited: *Marty* — "this God in Genesis doesn't try to leverage fear... he gives away all of his leverage because he doesn't want that kind of relationship." / *Elle* — "the implement of war—same word." This is who God has always been — trust the story.

- **God's concern is violence/evil, not human "annoyance" — the opposite of the pagan gods.** Reframing Gilgamesh: the gods there exterminate humanity for making noise; the God of Genesis grieves violence and immediately moves to save. Cited: *Elle* — "The people are annoying... God's problem, when we read Genesis 6, is evil... violence being done against the earth, being done against people."

- **"Even though" — God commits to creation despite humanity, not because of its merit.** The "every inclination... only evil" phrase bookends the story: it *prompts* the flood at the start and is *overruled* at the end. Cited: *Marty* — "even though every inclination of the human heart... even if it is true... I will not destroy creation." God's commitment is constant and one-directional in grace.

- **This is a re-creation: God Sabbaths from destroying.** The ark narrative replays the creation days (ruach over water, light through the window, waters above/below) and lands on a Sabbath — this time a rest from destroying. Cited: *Marty* — "God's going to Sabbath again, but this time he's going to Sabbath from destroying."

- **The author reframes the folk stories his audience already knew.** Scripture isn't inventing a flood tale from scratch; it's retelling the common ancient Near East deluge to say "God is not who you thought." Cited: *Marty* — "the author is purposely retelling and reframing the folk stories that they are used to... 'Let me invite you to reframe what you assume about the world, about who God is.'"

## East/West Lens

Only categories the episode actually engages, each with a cited quote.

- **Words (word-pictures, sound, repetition over abstract definition).** The lesson lives in Hebrew word-counts, onomatopoeia, and a weapon-word for "bow." Cited: *Elle* — "Ruach is onomatopoeic for breath... kh-kh-kh... that end is kind of like an exhale."

- **Numbers (quality/symbol over quantity).** The counts 7/7/40/150 and 7-7-5-3 are structural and symbolic, not merely tallies — odd numbers create a center. Cited: *Marty* — "odd numbers are kind of like little mini chiasms... You have a center."

- **God's description (nature of the relationship over abstract attributes).** "God remembered Noah" and "I will remember my covenant" describe relationship/fidelity, not literal divine forgetting. Cited: *Elle* — "Surely if God is omniscient, He doesn't forget anything, and thus how can He remember something?"

- **God's existence / portrayal (monotheism assumed; one God in control vs. a frightened pantheon).** Cited: *Elle* — contrasting Gilgamesh, "they seem to have the situation really out of control... cowering like curs... there's no sense of that happening in the biblical account."

- **Truth (religious/experiential 'who/why' over scientific 'how/what').** The hosts refuse the "did it literally happen?" question in favor of what the story reveals about God. Cited: *Marty* — "this story is inviting us to learn something about who God is, not just tell us about what happened."

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story (the arc).** The flood retelling exists precisely to say the pagan picture of God is wrong — God is in control, set against violence, and quick to redeem. Cited: *Elle* — "a God who is in control, a God who is set against violence, and a God who is quick to want to get things back on the right track." Recurrence: builds on the "good creation God loves and keeps trying to redeem" thread from the earlier Session 1 episodes; the "even though" grace deepens the "God's posture never changes" point from the Cain/Abel and Eden studies.

- **Re-creation / Sabbath pattern (structural through-line to Genesis 1).** The ark story reruns the creation sequence and lands on Sabbath. Cited: *Marty* — "Everything in the Ark story happens again in the exact same order as the creation story... at the end we should be waiting for Sabbath." Recurs: directly ties to the Genesis 1 creation episode (ruach over the waters).

- **The lullaby effect (method through-line).** Over-familiarity numbs us to the problems that are the doorway in. Cited: *Elle* — "there's a lot of lullaby effect going on... I go, 'Uh-huh, uh-huh, uh-huh.'" Recurs across the series as the hosts' core reading posture.

Note: the "master the beast / knowing when to say enough" motif is **not** a focus of this episode; it is not forced here.

## Chiasms & Structure

The episode identifies **two nested structures**: the large flood chiasm (found via the number pattern 7-7-40-150 // 150-40-7-7), and a smaller "mini-chiasm" formed by the middle mentions of four counted keywords in Genesis 9.

### 1. The flood chiasm (centered on Genesis 8:1)

```mermaid
flowchart TB
    A1["7 days"]
    B1["7 days"]
    C1["40 days (rain)"]
    D1["150 days (waters prevail)"]
    E["CENTER — Gen 8:1: 'God remembered Noah... sent a RUACH over the waters' (re-creation begins)"]
    D2["150 days (waters recede)"]
    C2["40 days"]
    B2["7 days"]
    A2["7 days"]

    A1 --> B1 --> C1 --> D1 --> E --> D2 --> C2 --> B2 --> A2
    A1 -.mirrors.- A2
    B1 -.mirrors.- B2
    C1 -.mirrors.- C2
    D1 -.mirrors.- D2
```

**Prose:** The counted days mirror outward from a single hinge — Genesis 8:1, "God remembered Noah... and he sent a *ruach* over the earth." That center deliberately echoes the *ruach* hovering over the waters in Genesis 1, marking the flood as a re-creation that should resolve, like creation, in Sabbath — here a Sabbath *from destroying*. The "every inclination... only evil" phrase (6:5) and its "even though" echo at the end act as outer bookends around the whole. Cited: *Elle* — "I believe that smells like a chiasm"; *Brent* identifies the center as "8, verse 1."

### 2. The keyword mini-chiasm (Genesis 9:8-17)

Four keywords recur in odd counts, each creating a center; the **middle mentions**, read together, form a hidden central phrase:

- *berit* (covenant) ×7 → middle = 4th
- *ha'aretz* (earth) ×7 → middle = 4th
- *anan* (cloud) ×5 → middle = 3rd
- *keshet* (bow) ×3 → middle = 2nd

```mermaid
flowchart TB
    L["7 berit / 7 ha'aretz / 5 anan / 3 keshet — all ODD, each has a center"]
    MID["MIDDLE MENTIONS COMBINE: '...over the earth and the bow appears in the clouds, I will remember my covenant.'"]
    L --> MID
```

**Prose:** The repetition that made Brent feel he'd "read that three times" is the mechanism: the central occurrence of each counted word lines up to spell the covenant's heart. And the "bow" (*keshet*) is a war-bow, hung pointing *upward* toward God — the warrior-God disarming himself. Cited: *Marty* — "what if the fourth berit, what if the fourth ha'aretz, what if the third anan, and the second rainbow... made up a phrase in the middle of this section? In fact, it does."

## Terminology

- **ruach (רוּחַ)** — wind / breath / spirit; onomatopoeic for an exhale. The *ruach* sent over the floodwaters (8:1) echoes the *ruach* over the waters in creation (Gen 1), and is the word for the Spirit of God. Cited: *Elle* — "it's the word that is used for the [Spirit] of God... it can be translated 'spirit' or 'breath'... that's the word behind 'spirit' and 'breath' and 'wind.'"
- **berit (בְּרִית)** — covenant; appears 7× in Genesis 9:8-17. Cited: *Marty* — "the word for covenant, Hebrew word is berit. So covenant shows up seven times."
- **ha'aretz (הָאָרֶץ)** — the land / the earth; appears 7× in the passage. Cited: *Marty* — "the word ha'aretz—the land, the earth. It's going to show up seven times."
- **anan (עָנָן)** — cloud(s); appears 5× in Hebrew (one is a doubled "clouds clouding" form that reads as 4× in English). Cited: *Marty* — "The word anan shows up five times in the Hebrew."
- **keshet (קֶשֶׁת)** — bow — the implement of war, not "rainbow"; appears 3×, hung in the clouds pointed upward toward God. Cited: *Elle* — "The implement of war—same word." / *Marty* — "That word is keshet."
- **suzerain-vassal covenant** — the ancient Near East treaty form (powerful suzerain / subject vassal); the vassal keeps the sign/receipt and must remember. Genesis inverts it — God keeps the sign himself. Cited: *Marty* — "there's a powerful party, the suzerain, and then there's the vassal, the subject."

## Discussion Questions

1. God hangs his war-bow in the clouds pointed back at himself — he gives away all his leverage. Where in your relationships are you tempted to keep "leverage" or the threat of consequence, and what would it look like to disarm the way God does?
2. In Gilgamesh the gods destroy humanity for being *annoying*; in Genesis God grieves *violence*. How does it change your picture of God to see that his concern is harm done to people and the earth, not his own inconvenience?
3. "Even though every inclination of the human heart..." God commits to creation despite us, not because we've earned it. Where do you still assume God's commitment to you depends on your performance (your "tribute"), like a vassal paying taxes?
4. The ark story is a re-creation that resolves in a Sabbath *from destroying*. What does it mean for you to "rest from destroying" — to stop a pattern of tearing down (yourself, others, something you've built)?
5. The hosts refuse the "did it literally happen?" question in favor of "who is this God?" When you read a hard or strange passage, which question do you default to — and what might you gain by sitting with the second one?
