# The Plot to Kill Jesus

BEMA 130 · Session 3
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/3-130-the-plot-to-kill-jesus.md`
Book: Matthew; John — a thematic study of the characters behind Jesus's execution (Matthew 27:19; John 19:12-16)
Host: Marty Solomon · Co-host: Brent Billings

> **Session 3 overarching arc: the king/Messiah embodying the story.** This episode steps out of the running narrative to ask one "really electric" question — *who brought about Jesus's execution?* — and answers it with hard first-century precision: not "the Jews" (who "loved, adored, and even revered Jesus"), not the Pharisees as a party (Jesus "spends three years confronting the Pharisees... and survives to tell the tale"), but the **chief priests** — "that corrupt religious mafia" of the house of Ananus — who "work Roman political power in their favor." Rome *authorizes and carries out* the crucifixion but, Marty argues, barely bats an eye at "a peasant Rabbi." The episode advances the one story by naming what the king is actually dying *against*: not Rome as a political army but **Empire itself** — "the lie of Empire and the promise of new life and a better kingdom" — which has now set up shop "in their own Jewish house, with their own leadership." Where Session 1 established the **partnership** (a people called to *be* the message) and Session 2 chronicled **the partner's unfaithfulness and God's refusal to quit**, Session 3's king walks into Jerusalem knowing that confronting corrupt religious power is the one fight "nobody survives to tell that tale" — and goes anyway. "My kingdom is not of this world... if it were, my servants would fight."

## Key Lessons

- **"The Jews did not kill Jesus" — this is a question you "do not dive into hastily."** Marty opens by naming the stakes: *"There's so much anti-Semitism behind the whole who killed Jesus thing. Handling this question can be a ticking time bomb."* And flatly: *"'The Jews did not kill Jesus.' This has been one of the most destructive misinterpretations of history that the world has ever seen. Millions of Jews have been slaughtered in the name of this false idea."* The Jewish public "loved, adored, and even revered Jesus" — which is precisely why the plot "hinges completely upon executing Jesus outside the eye of the Jewish public."
- **Not the same crowd.** Marty dismantles a popular preaching cliché: *"I can't tell you how many times I've heard the phrase Jesus entered Jerusalem to cheers from the crowd only to be crucified by them a week later... it was not the same crowd."* Jesus was "arrested, tried and beaten by a corrupt body of ruling priests, and their mafia-style justice" — not the hosanna-waving pilgrims of the triumphal entry.
- **It is the chief priests, specifically — not even "the Sadducees and Pharisees."** Marty narrows it relentlessly: *"the Pharisees and Sadducees did not kill Jesus, the Sadducees killed Jesus more specifically"* — and more specifically still, "the chief priests," "those seven ruling families that formed a mafia of sorts," "the chief families in charge of the rule of Ananus."
- **Some Pharisees helped, but Pharisees as a party tried to *save* Jesus.** Marty: *"the Pharisees tried to save Jesus's life more than once earlier in his ministry"* — the ones who came to warn, "Herod wants to kill you, you need to leave" (Luke), were Pharisees. The few Pharisees who joined the plot were, Marty suspects, "as corrupt as the Sadducees and the chief priests."
- **The crucifixion was chosen as a *message*, not just a death.** The plotters "don't just want him dead, they want him dead in a particular way" — crucifixion, Rome's torture reserved for "insurgents, terrorists, Zealots." The two "robbers" beside Jesus are zealots: "the only people hanging on a cross are Zealots." Their mockery is a last clash of agendas — one of them finally "realizes the error of his zealous ways."
- **Jesus knows he will die in Jerusalem — not by crystal-ball foresight but by reading the room.** Marty: *"This is why Jesus knows... he's going to die in Jerusalem. It's not just because he has his God Goggles on looking into the future — it is because he knows he is headed into Jerusalem to confront corrupt religious power... and nobody survives to tell that tale."* Three years against the Pharisees → survives; less than one week against the chief priests → killed.
- **The trial was the *informal*, corrupt Sanhedrin — a backroom mafia, not a lawful court.** Per Josephus there were two Sanhedrins: the formal body (70-72, half Pharisee, half Sadducee) and the informal one — the chief priests meeting "at the house of the high priest," run by the high priest, making decisions "then brought to the formal Sanhedrin and they simply ratified them." Jesus's night trial at Caiaphas's house "breaks over 15 commandments" of proper Jewish trial procedure "because they don't care."
- **The priests' corruption and leverage trace back to Herod.** The chief priesthood was "sold to the highest bidder under Herod" — the king the priests themselves recruited because his wealth "could match the power of Rome." That origin is the root of both their corruption and the political sway they would later hold over Pilate.
- **Rome's role is real but secondary — and about *political stability*, not threat.** Pilate, Rome's "bulldog" governor of its hardest region, "tried again and again to release Jesus stating that he's found no fault in him." The trial "isn't about Jesus. It's the intersection between Pilate and the corrupt religious leaders" — leveraged by the threat, *"If you let this man go, you are no friend of Caesar."* Marty: "I don't think Rome could care less about a peasant Rabbi. He's a mustard seed."
- **Jesus wages war on Empire itself, not on Rome as an army.** *"Jesus wasn't fighting a worldly war over a worldly kingdom in a worldly way... They are not here to wage war on Rome as a political empire. They are here to wage war on Empire itself"* — and Empire "is showing up in their own house." Hence Jesus "marches on the temple to do some house cleaning of his own."

## East/West Lens

- **Diagnosing corruption by *social structure*, not individual villainy (Hebraic communal reading).** The culprit is a *system* — "a backroom group of people doing backroom deals and then everybody else is just giving the nod" — the two-Sanhedrin arrangement, the seven ruling families, the mafia loyalty. Marty: *"This is the corruption playing itself out."* Guilt is located in a web of power, not a single wicked heart.
- **Word-reclamation against inherited English meanings (Hebraic vs. Western).** "Robbers/thieves" is corrected to **zealots** — *"the word is used throughout extra-biblical literature to talk about Zealots"* — so the two crosses beside Jesus become a statement about *agendas*, not petty crime.
- **Lived geography and material culture as interpretation (Hebraic / contextual).** Meaning is carried by *place*: the house of Caiaphas (with Peter in the courtyard), the Antonia Fortress "with the watchful eye of Rome... That's always seeing you," Pilate entering from the west while Jesus enters from the east. Marty: the fortress "is actually, like a glaring statement about the watchful eye of Rome."
- **Resisting presentism while letting the text indict the present (lens discipline).** Marty refuses to leave corruption safely in the first century: *"I'm so glad that we are beyond this in our systems today"* (with Brent's pointed throat-clear) — the irony turning the ancient mafia into a mirror.
- **The intertextual "mustard seed" read (Hebraic).** Rome's indifference is voiced through Jesus's own parable grammar — "He's a mustard seed planted in a field... the smallest of all garden plants" — letting a kingdom parable interpret the political scene.
- **A deliberately named *Eastern* tradition held as hope, not history (East/West self-awareness).** Marty flags his "deep love for the Eastern Church tradition" and the Coptic legend of Pilate's repentance, explicitly distinguishing *tradition* from *history*: "Is that history? No, that's tradition. But boy, do I love that?"

## Recurring Through-lines

- **The lie of Empire vs. the better kingdom (Session 2 → Session 3).** The episode's thesis is that Jesus's ministry is "about the lie of Empire and the promise of new life and a better kingdom," and that Empire has infiltrated "their own Jewish house." This carries Session 2's unfaithfulness straight into the Messiah's final week — the enemy is no longer only Babylon or Rome but a counterfeit kingdom wearing the temple's clothes.
- **"My kingdom is not of this world" — the upside-down, non-violent kingdom.** Against zealot expectation, *"if it were, my servants would fight."* The king refuses the sword even at his own execution — the servant-shaped kingship the series has been building toward.
- **Religious power and corruption as the deadliest counterfeit.** Marty's stated reason for the whole study: "I want to understand the dangers of religious power and corruption." The chief priests who "claim" God's house put a counterfeit kingdom on display — a dark mirror of Session 1's call to *be* a true witness.
- **Zealot agenda vs. Jesus's agenda (Session 3 motif).** The two crosses stage the clash: zealots mocking Jesus for not seizing power, one bandit finally conceding "which story wins here, because the story I bought into my whole life doesn't win." The repeated BEMA contrast between the way of the sword and the way of the Lamb.
- **The mustard seed / smallest-of-seeds (kingdom-parable callback).** Rome sees "a mustard seed... the smallest of all garden plants" — the series' recurring image of a kingdom that looks negligible and is not.
- **A God/author who is "a stickler for hope."** Marty's closing confession — "I am a sucker for happy endings and a stickler for hope" over the Pilate legend — echoes the series' through-line of a God who "refuses to quit" and keeps bending even enemies toward redemption.

## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

A sequential walkthrough of every teaching beat, in order. Used to verify full coverage.

1. **Cold open & the question.** Brent frames the episode: "who brought about Jesus's execution, acknowledging the different characters at play and the role they each had."
2. **The hint hidden in the reading.** Brent recalls the previous ("Brent Billings") episode's long reading and the clue "right smack dab in the middle" of Matthew 27 — "all the chief priests and the elders of the people made their plans how to have Jesus executed." A joke about ending the episode early.
3. **Naming the stakes: anti-Semitism and the "ticking time bomb."** Marty insists this is "a really electric topic" you "don't dive into hastily," and promises "I'm not going to be letting Rome off the hook."
4. **Why crucifixion specifically.** Rome alone could execute; no stoning in Jerusalem (Stephen notwithstanding). Crucifixion was reserved for zealots/"terrorists" — a torture "designed to just torture you literally to death" and "to send a message to people that are watching."
5. **The two "robbers" are zealots.** The word translated "robbers/thieves" means zealots; "the only people hanging on a cross are Zealots." The mockery, and the one bandit who "realizes the error of his zealous ways," is a clash of agendas.
6. **First clear statement: the Jews did not kill Jesus.** The most destructive misinterpretation in history; the Jewish public loved Jesus; the plot "hinges completely upon executing Jesus outside the eye of the Jewish public." Brent's "3,000 in Acts 2" question is parked as "Session 4 material."
7. **Not the same crowd.** The triumphal-entry pilgrims are not the ones who cried "crucify"; Jesus was tried by "a corrupt body of ruling priests."
8. **Second statement: the Pharisees as a party did not lead this.** Some corrupt Pharisees helped, but Pharisees elsewhere *warned* Jesus (Luke — "Herod wants to kill you"); Jesus "spent three years with these guys" and lived.
9. **Third statement: the chief priests conspired and arranged it.** Three years confronting Pharisees → survives; less than a week confronting the chief priests → killed. This is why Jesus knows he will die in Jerusalem — not "God Goggles" but the logic of confronting a religious mafia.
10. **Marty's confession of how recent this understanding is.** "Within the last five, six years... this wasn't a part of my teaching."
11. **What is the Sanhedrin? The two-Sanhedrin distinction (Josephus).** Formal (70-72, half Pharisee/half Sadducee, like a Supreme-Court-ish ruling body) vs. informal (the chief priests, run by the high priest, meeting at his house). The informal body decides; the formal body ratifies — or members "get knocked off the membership list" ("off with your head").
12. **Where was Jesus tried? At the high priest's house.** Brent reads Matthew 26: Caiaphas's house, teachers of the law and elders, "the whole Sanhedrin," with Peter in the courtyard outside. The model of the house (from the Episode 76 presentation) shows the very courtyard.
13. **A corrupt, rule-breaking trial.** The informal, nighttime trial "breaks over 15 commandments" of proper Jewish procedure because the chief priests "don't care" — though they keep "just enough rules" to stay nominally aligned with Torah.
14. **Origin of the chief priests' corruption under Herod.** The priests recruited Herod (whose wealth "could match the power of Rome"); Herod "holds the chief priesthood up for the highest bidder." Herod's incompetent sons (Philip, Antipas, Archelaus) — especially Archelaus in Jerusalem — set up the Roman takeover.
15. **Enter Rome: who is Pilate?** Archelaus fails within ~2 years; Judea is "the most tumultuous region in the Roman empire" because the Jews "refuse to bend the knee to Caesar." Rome sends its "bulldog," Pilate — "imperial wisdom, an iron fist and Roman rule."
16. **Pilate's politics.** Brent describes Pilate's bafflement ("What is the big deal? What have you even done?"), the festival prisoner-release, the law-and-order-plus-appeasement balance, and the hand-washing. "It's so political."
17. **The west gate / east gate callback.** Pilate enters from the west (Roman power), Jesus from the east on a donkey — an episode already done. The triumphal entry and temple-clearing "got the Romans' attention" from the Antonia Fortress.
18. **The Antonia Fortress as the watchful eye of Rome.** The fortress over the temple courts; its level now ~20 feet below today's Temple Mount; the Via Dolorosa aside and trip plug.
19. **Jesus wasn't picking a fight with Rome — and yet was.** His whole ministry is anti-Empire ("the lie of Empire and the promise of new life and a better kingdom"); *The Last Week* (Crossan & Borg) on "fighting words." But Jesus wages war "on Empire itself," not on Rome's army — "My kingdom is not of this world... my servants would fight."
20. **Rome shrugs; the priests press.** Pilate "found no fault"; the real engine is the priests' threat. Reading John 19: "no friend of Caesar," "we have no king but Caesar" (answered by *the chief priests*), "Pilate handed him over to them to be crucified."
21. **Pilate washes his hands — but is not off the hook.** The coup is "led by the chief priest... the chief families in charge of the rule of Ananus"; Pilate "caves the political pressure."
22. **The Coptic tradition of Pilate.** Prompted by his wife's dream (Matthew 27:19 / "Matthew 26" as Brent reads it), the Eastern/Coptic legend says Pilate repented, fled to Egypt, and helped found the Coptic Church. "Is that history? No, that's tradition. But boy, do I love that."
23. **Closing summary & the mustard seed.** "Who killed Jesus? Not the Jews. Chief priests" — the mafia that knew their "system of power is going to crumble." Rome couldn't care less about "a mustard seed." Marty's metaphor "gets out of hand"; Brent sets up the next episode on "the betrayer" (Judas and Peter).

## Terminology

- **chief priests** — not the whole priesthood or "the Sadducees" broadly, but "those seven ruling families that formed a mafia of sorts," "the chief families in charge of the rule of Ananus." The actual conspirators and arrangers of the execution. Marty: *"it is the chief priests, Chief Priest, if I can bold my statement here, who conspire against Jesus and arrange his execution."*
- **the house / rule of Ananus (Annas)** — the dynastic priestly family behind the plot. Marty: *"this coup is being led by the chief priest... the chief families in charge of the rule of Ananus."*
- **zealots** ("robbers/thieves") — the real sense of the word used for the two men crucified beside Jesus; insurgents/"terrorists" in modern terms. Marty: *"We translate it as robbers and thieves, but the word is used throughout extra-biblical literature to talk about Zealots... the only people hanging on a cross are Zealots."*
- **crucifixion** — Rome's torture-execution "designed to just torture you literally to death," reserved for zealots to "send a message." Not available to Jewish authorities. Marty: *"It wasn't just capital punishment. It was a form of capital punishment designed to just torture you literally to death."*
- **the formal Sanhedrin** — per Josephus, the official ruling body of ~70-72 leaders, "half of them Pharisees, half of them Sadducees," meeting with a set order (Marty's loose analogy: "a weird combination of the Supreme Court and... Congress"). It ratified the informal body's decisions.
- **the informal Sanhedrin** — the chief priests, "run by the high priest for sure," meeting "at the house of the high priest," "much more mafia like." Jesus's night trial happened here. Marty: *"They made decisions in that Sanhedrin and those decisions were then brought to the formal Sanhedrin and they simply ratified them."*
- **Caiaphas** — the high priest at whose house the informal trial was held; "one of the chief priests." Brent (reading): *"took him to Caiaphas the high priest, where the teachers of the law and the elders had assembled."*
- **Pilate** — Rome's governor, "one of their best bulldogs," sent to rule "the most complicated place to be a Governor." He "found no fault" in Jesus and "caves the political pressure." Marty: *"his name is Pilate."*
- **Antonia Fortress** — the Roman guard post "overlooking the Temple Mount," "a glaring statement about the watchful eye of Rome. That's always seeing you." Its level is now "about 20 feet under the one you walk on today."
- **Empire** — the true object of Jesus's war; not Rome-as-army but the whole system of domination, now operating inside Israel's own house. Marty: *"They are here to wage war on Empire itself. Empire is showing up in their own house, their own Jewish house, with their own leadership."*
- **mustard seed** — Jesus's own parable-image, used to describe how negligible he looks to Rome. Marty: *"He's a mustard seed... It's the smallest of all garden plants."*
- **Coptic tradition** — the Eastern Church legend that Pilate repented, fled to Egypt, and helped plant the Coptic Church; held as tradition, not history.

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty calls "who killed Jesus?" a "ticking time bomb" because of how the answer has been weaponized against Jewish people for centuries. Why does *precision* here — chief priests, not "the Jews," not "the Pharisees" — matter morally, not just historically? Where else do sloppy, group-blaming answers to "who did this?" still do real-world harm?
2. The episode argues Jesus knew he would die in Jerusalem "not because he has his God Goggles" but because he was walking knowingly into a confrontation with corrupt religious power that "nobody survives." How does framing Jesus's foreknowledge as *moral courage* rather than *supernatural prediction* change the way you read his final week?
3. Jesus "spends three years confronting the Pharisees... and survives," but "less than one week confronting the corrupt rule of the chief priests and gets himself killed." What does it say that the deadliest opposition came not from theological debate but from *entrenched religious power and money*? Where do you see that same dynamic today?
4. Marty distinguishes waging war on "Rome as a political empire" from waging war on "Empire itself" — a lie that can set up shop "in their own Jewish house." What would it look like to resist "Empire" in your own community or church *without* simply picking a political fight? What does "my servants would fight / but my kingdom is not of this world" rule out?
5. The chief priests kept "just enough rules... that they are aligned with Torah" while breaking "over 15 commandments" about a fair trial — corruption that stays technically legal. How do you tell the difference between faithful obedience and a system that has "figured out how to live within all of these corrupt loopholes"?
6. Marty openly loves the Coptic tradition of Pilate's repentance while admitting "that's not history, that's tradition" — "I am a sucker for happy endings and a stickler for hope." Is it healthy to cherish a hopeful legend you know may not be true? When does hope honor the story, and when does it rewrite it?
