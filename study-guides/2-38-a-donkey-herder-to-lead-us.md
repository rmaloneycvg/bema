# A Donkey Herder to Lead Us

BEMA 38 · Session 2
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/2-38-a-donkey-herder-to-lead-us.md`
Book: Deuteronomy 17:14-15; 1 Samuel 8 (and the move into 1 Samuel 9–16) — Israel's demand for a king and the giving of Saul, then David
Hosts: Marty Solomon & Brent Billings

> This is a "where-have-we-been / where-are-we-going" episode. Marty re-tells the whole meta-narrative — Preface, Introduction, the Tale of Two Kingdoms — to land Israel at the hinge where the Prophets' story opens: God's covenant partner, who was supposed to be *distinct* in order to *bless all nations*, asks instead for "a king like all the other nations." Samuel hears it as rejection; God hears it as the same rejection He has absorbed "from the day I brought them up out of Egypt." And yet God does not quit. He gives them the king they want (Saul, a tall, impressive donkey herder from Benjamin), and then goes and anoints the king He wants (David, the eighth son, a shepherd from Judah). The Session 2 arc stage is already in view: **the partner's unfaithfulness, and God's refusal to walk away from the partnership.**

## Key Lessons

- **A king was never the sin — wanting a king "like all the other nations" was.** Marty cuts off the reflexive Sunday-school reading: *"almost every Sunday school class that I've ever heard starts going crazy because, 'God's people aren't supposed to have a king,' which I don't think is necessarily the case."* Deuteronomy 17 already anticipated a king *"the Lord your God chooses."* The problem is the motive: *"The king is not the problem. The problem is why they want a king and what they want. They don't want what God wants to give them... They want to be like everyone else."*
- **The demand is a rejection of distinctness — and distinctness is the whole mission.** Israel is a kingdom of priests, set apart so they can bless the nations. Marty: *"They're supposed to be different... You can't be just like them, or you'll never be able to bless them."* Brent names the priestly logic plainly: *"You make yourself different... you're supposed to be distinct."* This is the Session 1 partnership (bless all nations, be a kingdom of priests) now being actively abandoned.
- **Rejecting the king means rejecting the God who fought their battles.** The people want a king *"to go out before us and fight our battles"* — but, as Brent notes, *"God did"* that, and His record is *"undefeated."* God's own verdict: *"it is not you they have rejected, but it is me that they have rejected as their king."* The unfaithfulness is not a political preference; it is covenant infidelity.
- **God answers unfaithfulness not with abandonment but with patience — "I'm used to it."** Brent reads the fuller line (*"as they have done from the day I brought them up out of Egypt until this day"*), and Marty's gloss is the heart of the Session 2 arc: *"God's had a rough day... He's like: This isn't even about you, Samuel. It's about me. Trust me, I'm used to it."* God grants the request and keeps working inside it.
- **The donkey herder is a tongue-in-cheek diagnosis of the people.** Israel is meant to be *sheep* led by a *shepherd*; instead God gives Saul, a (bad) *donkey herder* — *"he's chasing a bunch of donkeys because he's lost them... he's not even a very good donkey herder."* Marty: the rabbinic read is *"because you all are being like donkeys... the stubborn, stiff-necked, rebellious."* Saul even hails from Benjamin — the tribe of the atrocity that just ended Judges.
- **God's metrics are not our metrics.** Saul looks right by the world's scale — *"a head and shoulders taller... bigger, he's more impressive. By the world's standards, he's the kind of guy you'd want."* But God rejects him and sends Samuel to anoint David, Jesse's overlooked eighth son, out with the sheep: *"man looks at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart."*
- **God loves to choose the last — a thread that runs straight to Jesus.** Marty: *"He loves to choose the last... the last will be first, the first will be last. This is a very Jesus-y lesson."* He traces it across Genesis (not the firstborn: Noah's son, Avram, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Ephraim over Manasseh). The reasons he offers: *"We are never confused as to whose might and glory it is,"* and the lowly *"are more likely to remember their story."*
- **The right kind of king cares about God's reputation, not his own ego — *kiddush Hashem*.** David's defining commitment (developed next episode): *"to hallow the name of God... Kiddush Hashem... It's not his ego, it's God's reputation. It's not his ego, it's God's place in the cosmos that needs to be thrust out in front of everybody."*

## East/West Lens

- **Community vs individual** — The whole crisis is corporate: *the nation* asks to be *"like all the other nations,"* abandoning its collective priestly vocation. The sin and the mission are both national, not private: *"you're supposed to be different because God wants to bless all those other nations."*
- **Faith as relational experience vs intellectual creeds** — The offense is framed as rejected relationship, not failed doctrine: *"it is not you they have rejected, but it is me that they have rejected as their king."* Kingship here is loyalty to a Person who *"fought their battles"* — not assent to a proposition.
- **God's description (concrete relationship / story)** — God is portrayed through felt, relational language — He's "had a rough day," He's "used to it," He has an "undefeated" record going out in front of His people. The meta-narrative is retold as a marriage (the "Sinai wedding," "a kingdom of priests") rather than a system of propositions.
- **Numbers / symbol over literal** — The shepherd/sheep vs donkey-herder/donkeys contrast is read symbolically, as the rabbis do: Saul's lost donkeys and his Benjamite origin *mean* something about Israel's condition, beyond the plain report. Likewise the recurring "eighth/last son" pattern is a symbol of how God works.
- **Truth over time (unfolding, dynamic)** — The episode is explicitly a review that "fits into the story," and Marty keeps deferring: *"we'll talk more before we're done about the mess that is David's story,"* and *"we're going to see that in the next episode."* Meaning accrues across the arc rather than resolving in one sitting.

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story / the one meta-narrative (the arc itself).** The episode opens by rehearsing the whole story so the listener can *"know where we've been and how this fits into the story... be familiar with the large meta-narrative of Scripture."* The king crisis is positioned as the next beat of that single story.
- **Empire vs. Shalom / right-handed vs. left-handed power (callback to "A Tale of Two Kingdoms," ep. 18).** Marty names *"The Tale of Two Kingdoms, Empire versus Shalom... right-handed power... and then you have the kingdom of Shalom... left-handed power, invitation, grace."* Asking for a battle-fighting king "like the nations" is a lurch toward empire's power.
- **Bless all nations / a kingdom of priests (callback to the Abrahamic call and Sinai).** Repeated as the mission being betrayed: *"He's going to bless all nations... He can't do that if they're not in the middle of all nations,"* and *"a kingdom of priests."* The demand for sameness directly contradicts it.
- **Sheep and shepherd (callback to Session 1).** *"They're supposed to be sheep... their king should be a... shepherd."* The through-line is turned on its head by the donkey-herder gag — and quietly restored when the shepherd David appears.
- **God chooses the last / not the firstborn (callback across Genesis).** Explicitly gathered: Noah's son, Avram, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Ephraim over Manasseh with Jacob's crossed hands — *"this is how God works."*
- **Remember where you came from (callback to Deuteronomy, ep. 31).** God chooses the lowly because *"those people are more likely to remember their story... I bet David knows what it's like to be the last of eight sons."* The alien/orphan/widow logic of Deuteronomy is echoed as the reason God works from the bottom.
- **The Redemption Cycle / God's patience (callback to the Judges episodes and Ruth).** The recap insists Judges is better read as *"the Redemption Cycle"* and notes even Benjamin is preserved — setting up God's continued patience as the people now reject Him outright.

## Terminology

- **kiddush Hashem** (קִדּוּשׁ הַשֵּׁם, "sanctifying/hallowing the Name") — David's defining orientation, named for next episode: *"to hallow the name of God... It's not his ego, it's God's reputation."* The right king magnifies God's place in the cosmos rather than his own.
- **"a kingdom of priests"** — Israel's Sinai vocation, recapped as the thing being betrayed: a *whole* nation set apart to put God on display. Brent: *"you're supposed to be distinct."*
- **right-handed / left-handed power** — the two kingdoms' modes: *"right-handed power (fear/coercion)... and then... left-handed power, invitation, grace, forgiveness, goodness, shalom."* A battle-king "like the nations" leans right-handed.
- **the "priestwich"** — Marty's mnemonic from the recap for Leviticus's priesthood-sandwich structure (two priesthood sections around the living-as-priests middle); invoked here as part of the meta-narrative review.
- **sheep / shepherd vs. donkeys / donkey herder** — the rabbinic symbol at the center of the episode: Israel-as-sheep should get a shepherd-king; getting a donkey herder (Saul) reads as a tongue-in-cheek verdict that they've been acting like *"stubborn, stiff-necked, rebellious"* donkeys.

## Illustrations & Images

- **The whole-Bible recap as "where we've been"** (Marty) — the opening meta-narrative sweep (Preface → Introduction → Two Kingdoms → Exodus/Sinai wedding → tabernacle → Leviticus → Numbers → Deuteronomy → Joshua → Judges/Ruth) functions as a map so the king crisis lands in context: *"We gotta know where we've been and how this fits into the story."*
- **Eli's sons and the un-rendered fat** (Marty) — a concrete picture of priestly corruption opening 1 Samuel: they *"put the forks into the meat pot and pull out meat before the fat's been rendered,"* seizing God's portion. Eli is only *"semi-righteous."*
- **The bad donkey herder** (Marty) — Saul *"chasing a bunch of donkeys because he's lost them... Donkeys are not known for running off and getting lost... he's not even a very good donkey herder."* The failure is the point.
- **"Head and shoulders taller" / Brock Lesnar** (Marty) — the world's ideal leader as an imposing physical specimen: *"he's the Brock Lesnar... I'm just gonna have Brock Lesnar in my head whenever I picture Saul."*
- **The Cinderella / glass-slipper walk-down** (Marty) — Samuel passing the seven brothers while God says *"No, no, no"* until the overlooked eighth son is finally summoned: *"There's a real Cinderella moment for me, like Samuel with the glass slipper."*
- **Jacob's crossed hands** (Marty & Brent) — Joseph arranges Ephraim and Manasseh "as they ought to be" and Jacob crosses his hands anyway; Brent: *"no, no, no, you got it backwards. He's like, no, trust me, this is how God works"* — the physical image of God preferring the last.

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty argues the sin wasn't the king but wanting one "like all the other nations." Where do you most feel the pull to be "like everyone else" rather than distinct — and what mission might that sameness be costing?
2. God says, "it is not you they have rejected, but me." When you've wanted a reliable human "king" (a leader, a system, a security) to "fight your battles," what were you actually asking to replace?
3. God's record was "undefeated," yet that wasn't enough for Israel. Why is a proven, trustworthy God so often *not* enough for us — what does the demand for a visible king reveal about trust?
4. God gives them Saul (the king they wanted) *and* anoints David (the king He wanted) without abandoning the partnership. Where have you seen God keep working inside a bad choice rather than quitting on you?
5. "God's metrics are not our metrics... the Lord looks at the heart." Whom in your world have you written off by the "head and shoulders taller" standard — and what might God be seeing instead?
6. Marty says God chooses the last because they "remember their story." How does remembering where you came from guard you against the ego the wrong kind of king represents (vs. David's *kiddush Hashem*)?
