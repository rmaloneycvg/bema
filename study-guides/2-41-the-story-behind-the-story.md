# The Story Behind the Story

BEMA 41 · Session 2
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/2-41-the-story-behind-the-story.md`
Book: 2 Chronicles 1:14-17; Deuteronomy 17:14-17; 2 Samuel 24:1-4; 1 Chronicles 21:1-4 (with 2 Samuel 7 // 1 Chronicles 17; 1 Kings 10 in view)
Hosts: Marty Solomon · Brent Billings
Core move of the episode: holding two inspired sources — **Samuel/Kings (Source A)** and **Chronicles (Source B)** — side by side to ask *what went wrong* with the united kingdom, and discovering that beneath the familiar morality story there is "a story behind the story."

> **Session 2 overarching arc: the prophets — the partner's (Israel's) unfaithfulness and God's refusal to quit.** Session 1 built the *partnership*: God and a people bound in covenant at the crossroads of the earth, called to bless all nations. This episode sits squarely in the collapse of that partnership — the monarchy from Saul and David through Solomon and the split under Rehoboam — and names the mechanism of the failure. Where Session 1 asked whether the partner would say *yes*, this episode diagnoses the long *no*: the people "lose the plot" and become "the anti-story," no longer giving themselves away but "working on self-preservation," "building empires rather than [being to the] nations [the] conduits of God's work." Marty's warning — "if you don't remember, you may just become Pharaoh yourself" — is the hinge of the whole session: when the covenant partner becomes the oppressor, "God hears the cry of the oppressed and begins to work on rescuing his kids." That refusal to quit is exactly what sets up the prophets. This episode advances the one story by giving us the *reading lens* — two agendas, immorality/idolatry vs. Empire/injustice — that the hosts promise to "circle back to" when they reach the prophets.

## Key Lessons

- **The Bible gives us two inspired sources on the same period, and both are true.** Source A (Samuel/Kings) is "agenda-driven headlines" from Israel's perspective, written nearer real-time; Source B (Chronicles) is "the documentary perspective" written "four or five hundred years later" in exile, from Judah's perspective. Marty: *"they're two different sources doing something unique with the same period of history."* It is "not an either/or" — "it's not, well, Chronicles is more right than Kings."
- **Source A's agenda is immorality / idolatry.** For Samuel/Kings, "the kingdom falls because of immorality": David's definitive turning point is *Bathsheba*, Solomon's is his *700 wives* leading him to idolatry. Marty: *"these men have a problem holding true to God's path... it's their moral failures that lead to the downfall of the story."*
- **Source B's agenda is Empire / injustice — the story behind the story.** Chronicles *omits Bathsheba entirely* and *omits Solomon's wives/idolatry*, and *adds* a huge amount about the temple. Marty: *"there may be a story behind the story... Don't get lost in the details of the immorality and obedience... there's a larger story that gets you to that place in the first place."*
- **Buildings are amoral; the heart that forgets is the danger.** David's cedar palace and the gold temple are morally neutral — "doesn't matter to God." Marty: *"There's nothing wrong with church buildings... But there is this horrible temptation to start to build empires and forget what it means to kiddush HaShem."*
- **Solomon broke the king's checklist on purpose.** Deuteronomy 17 forbids a king to multiply horses (return to Egypt), take many wives, or amass silver and gold; 2 Chronicles 1:14-17 shows Solomon doing all three. Brent: *"It's almost like Solomon made the checklist and then forgot the word 'not.'"* He becomes "an arms dealer" building Empire "at the expense of God's kingdom project" — hence Rehoboam being begged to "loosen the yoke."
- **Becoming the anti-story is the real fall.** Instead of being "at the crossroads of the earth to bless all nations," the people "become the anti-story... no longer blessing. They are oppressing, quite literally." Marty: *"if you don't remember, you may just become Pharaoh yourself, gathering slaves from every corner of the earth."*
- **The census reframe shows the whole method in miniature.** The same story reads "the anger of the Lord... incited David" (Samuel/Kings) and "Satan rose up against Israel and incited David" (Chronicles), with Chronicles adding "why should he bring guilt on **Israel**?" Marty: the chronicler says "Satan was at work and it brought guilt on all of us" — the sin is national (lust for Empire), not merely one leader's bad moment.
- **This lens is the training wheels for reading the prophets and the rest of the Old Testament.** Marty: *"we're going to circle back to this idea of these two different agendas... we want to get used to reading the prophets... in a way that I can discern what it is."* Brent testifies it makes the prophets "so much richer... Jesus is going to look back on these prophets a lot."

## East/West Lens

*(Only categories the episode actually engages, each with a citation.)*

- **Story read as a unified whole with long echoes (Eastern intertext vs. Western proof-text).** The hosts hold Samuel/Kings and Chronicles together as one layered testimony and read the census story *against* Deuteronomy 17 and the Eden serpent. Marty: *"it was the lust after Empire, the lie of the Serpent, the temptation to not trust the story that actually led Israel as a nation... into sin."*
- **Numbers and names as deliberate literary signals (Eastern symbolic reading vs. Western literalism).** The 666 talents of Sheba's gift is read as an authorial flag, not an accounting note. Marty: *"That's got to be a bad number... It's a deliberate attempt by the author to tell us that we've reached a turning point in the story."*
- **Two inspired tellings held without flattening (Eastern both/and vs. Western either/or).** Brent explicitly warns this is *not* like the four Gospels (near-contemporaneous, mutually aware); it is "hundreds of years apart... totally different set of people." Marty: *"it's not an either-or here... No, they're two different sources doing something unique with the same period of history."*
- **Reading from the vantage of the exiled community (Eastern communal/historical frame).** Chronicles is understood as written from inside captivity, which is *why* it reframes a personal sin as national guilt. Marty: *"this is being written from that captivity setting... it brought guilt on all of us."*
- **Covenant memory as formation (Eastern remembering vs. Western information).** The antidote to Empire is embodied remembering — the field, the rotem bush, slavery in Egypt. Marty: *"Remember, David, your days in a field, shepherding sheep. Remember the shade of the rotem bush in the desert, and never forget that you were once slaves in Egypt."*

## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

A sequential walkthrough of every teaching beat, in order. Used to verify full coverage.

1. **No review — jump straight in.** Brent frames the episode: revisit "the two sources comprising this portion of the biblical narrative, analyzing the agendas," to "figure out what went wrong." Marty wants to "keep the conversation going" for bingers coming straight from the last episode; "they're kind of... of a piece."
2. **The setup slide: two sources, one period.** The "united kingdom" of Israel/Judah — Saul, David ("Da-weed"), Shlomo (Solomon), and the *Melechim* (kings) — then the split. **Source A = Samuel/Kings** (Israel's perspective, nearer real-time, "agenda-driven headlines"); **Source B = Chronicles** (Judah's perspective, "centuries later with historical perspective," "the documentary perspective"). Brent's UK-of-Great-Britain gag.
3. **Source A walkthrough (Marty reads prepared notes).** Saul's seven-chapter biography; David's anointing, service, and Goliath; Saul's jealousy; 10+ chapters of pursuit displaying David's humility, morality, and *kiddush HaShem*; Saul's suicide; David mourning his enemy. Two points: (1) early David is "completely void of self-indulgence" (three chapters on bringing the Ark to Jerusalem); (2) his "counterintuitive" way throws his men off — he mourns enemies and executes his own men who kill the king's enemies, "a completely wacky tragedy" for an upside-down kingdom.
4. **The Bathsheba turn.** After the Ammonites and Bathsheba ("not his shining moment"), David's family becomes "a heaping pile of dysfunction"; Absalom chases him (echoes of Saul); David counts his fighting men "a move that will get him in trouble"; 2 Samuel ends.
5. **1 Kings and the 666 signal.** Solomon's throne and building projects; Sheba's visit (ch. 10) and "incredible splendor"; the first clear indication something is wrong is **Sheba's gift weighing 666 talents** — "That's got to be a bad number." Marty's aside about his daughter's "6660" ID number. The treaties, each sealed by marriage; the **700 wives** who lead him to idolatry; "it's at this point where Solomon's kingdom falls apart."
6. **Source A's agenda stated.** "The Samuel/Kings agenda is to say that the kingdom falls because of immorality." David's hinge = Bathsheba; Solomon's = the wives/idolatry. These are "the stories we're familiar with."
7. **Source B walkthrough.** Chronicles opens with genealogies, puts "David's pedigree" (not his morality) on display, and **omits Bathsheba entirely** — Brent: "I never got that far." It likewise omits Solomon's wives and idolatry. "For the author of Chronicles, David's kingdom consisted of something other than a high moral ethic."
8. **What the chronicler *adds*: the temple.** The story is "on some level, about the temple of the Lord." Chronicles rearranges events so that **David's request to build a temple** (1 Chronicles 17 // 2 Samuel 7) becomes the hinge that *replaces* Bathsheba. "Why should I live in a palace of cedar while my Lord lives in a tent?" — God's paraphrased reply: "I like my tent... When did I ever ask you... for a house?"
9. **David's good-intentioned drift toward Empire.** The shepherd boy is now "a big, mighty king... living in a palace of cedar," uncomfortable, and "becomes obsessed with the best of intentions" with building God a glorious house — "a little bit more like Empire and a little bit less like Shalom." Passed to Solomon, who exponentially increases the grandeur.
10. **2 Chronicles 1:14-17 read (Brent).** Solomon's 1,400 chariots and 12,000 horses; silver/gold "as common... as stones"; horses imported from Egypt and Kue; chariots exported to the Hittites and Arameans. Marty: a horse is "a weapon of war," a chariot "like a Humvee or a tank" — "he essentially becomes an arms dealer."
11. **Deuteronomy 17 read (Brent) — the king's checklist.** No foreign king (Solomon passes); but **no many horses / return to Egypt**, **no many wives**, **no amassed silver and gold** — Solomon fails all three. Brent: "Solomon made the checklist and then forgot the word 'not.'" "Solomon is breaking every single rule."
12. **The cost of Empire.** The grandeur "comes at the expense of God's kingdom project"; the first paragraph after Solomon's death has Israel begging Rehoboam to "loosen the yoke." "Building an empire always comes at a price."
13. **The second slide: naming the two agendas.** Source A = **immorality and idolatry**; Source B = **imperial movements, Empire, and injustice.** The chronicler asks what lies *behind* Bathsheba and the 700 wives — "what is it behind all of that that's justifying their decisions?"
14. **Losing the plot / the anti-story.** Saul the donkey-herder from Benjamin; David the shepherd from Judah; Solomon inheriting "a lust for Empire"; the split; "three, maybe four, no more than five" good kings; the rest follow "the sins of Jeroboam" or forsake the Lord. The people become "the anti-story," self-preserving oppressors "building empires." Rob Bell & Don Golden, *Jesus Wants to Save Christians*, recommended.
15. **Brent's Gospels caution.** Some stories appear in some Gospels and not others — but that is *not* the Samuel/Kings vs. Chronicles situation. The Gospels are "basically at the same time," mutually aware; Chronicles is "hundreds of years apart... totally different perspective." Marty: start with the Gospels analogy if it helps, but it's "distinct," and "it's not an either/or."
16. **The fine-point example: the census, read in parallel.** Brent reads **2 Samuel 24:1-4** then **1 Chronicles 21:1-4** — nearly verbatim, "and yet it's not going to be."
17. **Difference 1 (for fun): direction.** Samuel = "from Dan to Beersheba" (Israel's northern vantage); Chronicles = "Beersheba to Dan" (Judah's southern vantage). "I find that more, just, fun than anything else."
18. **Difference 2 (major): the Lord vs. Satan.** "The anger of the Lord... incited David" vs. "Satan rose up against Israel and incited David." Brent: "how much more different could it be?"
19. **Difference 3 (major): "guilt on Israel."** Chronicles adds to Joab's retort, "Why should he bring guilt on Israel?" — reframing a personal sin as **national** guilt, fitting the exile vantage.
20. **The synthesis.** Source A: David's personal immoral/bad-leadership failure, God angry at *David*. Source B: "Satan was at work and it brought guilt on all of us" — "the lust after Empire, the lie of the Serpent... led Israel as a nation... into sin that would lead them into captivity." A census implies a **tax** (Brent) — resources for a building project.
21. **Buildings are amoral; the heart isn't.** Cedar palace or gold temple, tent or temple — morally neutral. The danger is forgetting the field, the rotem bush, and Egypt-slavery, and "becoming Pharaoh." Solomon *did* use forced labor (1 Kings 5:13; 9:15, 21; 2 Chronicles 8:8). "If we become the anti-story, God hears the cry of the oppressed."
22. **Both/and, not either/or.** David really did sin immorally in 2 Samuel ("Joab makes that very clear"); *and* the chronicler warns us not to miss "the larger story that gets you to that dangerous spot in the first place." "That's the story behind the story."
23. **Forward map.** Not covering all the kings; catching **Elijah** and **Naaman** ("two of my favorite stories"), then the **wisdom literature** (Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes), then the **prophets**, where "we're going to circle back to this idea of these two different agendas." Brent testifies the context makes the prophets "so much richer," and "Jesus is going to look back on these prophets a lot."

## Terminology

- **Source A / Source B** — the episode's organizing frame. **Source A = Samuel/Kings**, "agenda-driven headlines" from Israel's perspective, written nearer real-time; **Source B = Chronicles**, "the documentary perspective" written ~400–500 years later in exile, from Judah's perspective. Marty: *"two different sources doing something unique with the same period of history."*
- **the two agendas** — Source A's agenda is **immorality / idolatry** (Bathsheba; the 700 wives); Source B's agenda is **Empire / injustice** ("imperial movements... and injustice"). Marty: *"the chronicler was like: Yeah, yeah, but I want you to see something else that lies behind the Bathsheba moment."*
- **kiddush HaShem** ("hallow / sanctify the Name") — the measure of early David, who acted "completely void of self-indulgence"; the thing Empire tempts a king to "forget." Marty: *"the temptation to start to build empires and forget what it means to kiddush HaShem."*
- **Melechim** (מְלָכִים, "Kings") — the Hebrew name of the book of Kings. Marty: *"Melechim would be how you say that in Hebrew."*
- **Shlomo / Da-weed** — the Hebrew pronunciations of Solomon and David used throughout to keep the Eastern, in-context reading. Marty: *"David ('Da-weed'), and you have Shlomo, Solomon."*
- **666 as a turning-point signal** — Sheba's gift/treaty-tax weighs 666 talents (1 Kings 10), read as an authorial flag that "we've reached a turning point in the story... toward the beastly." Marty: *"That's got to be a bad number."*
- **the king's checklist (Deuteronomy 17:16-17)** — a king must NOT (1) accumulate horses / return to Egypt for them, (2) take many wives, (3) amass silver and gold. 2 Chronicles 1:14-17 shows Solomon doing all three. Brent: *"forgot the word 'not.'"*
- **"the anti-story"** — God's people become the inverse of their calling: instead of blessing the nations at the crossroads of the earth, they are "self-preservation," "oppressing," "building empires." Marty: *"you have a people that have essentially become the anti-story."*
- **"the lie of the Serpent" / "become Pharaoh"** — the Edenic grasping-for-more that drives Empire; the end-state is becoming the oppressor one was rescued from. Marty: *"you may just become Pharaoh yourself, gathering slaves from every corner of the earth."*
- **"the story behind the story"** — the episode's title phrase: the deeper, communal, Empire-and-injustice narrative that lies *beneath* the surface morality tales. Marty: *"Don't miss the opportunity to realize that there's a larger story that gets you to that place in the first place."*

## Illustrations & Stories

- **"6660" ID number (Marty).** His daughter was assigned an ID beginning 6660 — "oh goodness, it's horrible" — a modern-day gut reaction to the number that illustrates how an ancient reader would have felt the 666 talents of Sheba's gift as an ominous signal, not a stray statistic.
- **Chariot = "a Humvee or a tank"; Solomon the "arms dealer" (Marty).** To make 2 Chronicles 1:14-17 land, Marty translates the horses and chariots into modern weapons of war: Solomon is "accumulating war weapons," importing and exporting them — "he essentially becomes an arms dealer."
- **"Solomon made the checklist and then forgot the word 'not'" (Brent).** A wry one-liner (with the "Check, check, check — Got it, I did everything" riff) capturing how Solomon fulfills Deuteronomy 17's king-list in exact reverse.
- **"I'm the executive producer, so that's what I'm going to do" (Marty).** Explaining why they'll skip most of the kings and cover only Elijah and Naaman — "two of my favorite stories" — a candid aside about how the series is curated.
- **Brent "never got that far" in Chronicles (Brent).** His honest admission that he'd never noticed Bathsheba's absence because he hadn't read that far — modeling the discovery the episode is inviting.

## Discussion Questions

1. Marty insists it's "not an either/or": Samuel/Kings (immorality) and Chronicles (Empire) are *both* true. Where in your own reading of Scripture have you been handed only one agenda — and what might the other source be trying to show you "behind the story"?
2. "Buildings are amoral... but there is this horrible temptation to start to build empires and forget what it means to kiddush HaShem." Where in your church, work, or life has a good-intentioned "building project" quietly started to "look a little more like Empire and a little less like Shalom"?
3. Solomon fulfills Deuteronomy 17's king-list in exact reverse — "forgot the word 'not.'" What are the "nots" you most easily edit out when success and splendor are on the line?
4. The census shifts from "the Lord incited David" (a leader's personal sin) to "Satan... brought guilt on Israel" (a whole people's sin). When is it too easy to blame one bad leader rather than reckon with a communal drift you're part of?
5. Marty warns that if God's people forget their story they can "become Pharaoh yourself." Where do you see former victims of oppression becoming oppressors — and what remembering ("the field... the rotem bush... slaves in Egypt") would guard against it?
6. Brent says understanding where the prophets "fit in the story" made them "so much richer." How might reading the prophets through the Empire/injustice lens (not just personal morality) change which verses you reach for — and why?
