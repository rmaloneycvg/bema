# A Tale of Two Kingdoms

BEMA 18 · Session 1
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/1-a-tale-of-two-kingdoms-18.md`
Book: Exodus / Genesis (context episode — references Genesis 47; Exodus 19; walks through Ray Vander Laan video lessons)
Guest: Chris Marshall (talmid)

> A context/synthesis episode that sets up the master frame for the rest of the Bible. Working through three Ray Vander Laan video lessons, the hosts cast **Egypt as a metaphor for empire** — a kingdom of fear where Pharaoh maintains order and people are expendable — against the biblical kingdom of **shalom** built on trust. From Exodus to Revelation, every day we choose which narrative to buy into.

## Key Lessons

- **The whole narrative (Exodus→Revelation) is a tale of two kingdoms: empire vs. shalom.** Marty: *"There's a kingdom of shalom and there's a kingdom of empire and every day you and I get to choose which narrative we buy into."* Empire (Egypt) is built on fear; shalom (the Bible) is built on trust. God's unchanging aim — order, inclusion, everything in its proper place — is the shalom story.
- **Empire's order comes at a cost: some people become expendable.** Via Ray Vander Laan, Brent: *"if you see a culture where life is cheap, and sacrifice of others is acceptable to sustain your own obsession with pleasure... you have the wrong story."* The telltale sign of empire is the poor, refugee, alien, orphan, widow, and elderly pushed to the edges to "keep the vault in order."
- **The deeper problem isn't getting Israel out of Egypt — it's getting Egypt out of Israel.** The Israelites started in Goshen's lush farmland and "bought into the Egyptian narrative" (the prophets say they worshiped Egypt's gods). Marty: *"how do I get Egypt out of my people?"* God's constant work is reorienting hearts from fear back to trust.
- **God hears the cry and looks for partners who hear it too — to *be* the message, not just bring it.** Chris: Israel "never cried out specifically to Adonai... God still heard them." God seeks a leader (Moses) with his heart, then a whole "kingdom of priests" (Exodus 19) to show the world what God is like. Marty: *"not just to bring the message to the world, but to be the message."*

## East/West Lens

- **God's existence / description (concrete vs abstract; where is God?)** — The living metaphor of standing with "one foot in waist-deep barley and one foot in barren desert": would we say God is in the lush side or the desert? Marty: *"I'm not sure we'd look at the desert and say that's where God is."* A challenge to Western assumptions about where/how God shows up.
- **Truth (how/what vs who/why)** — The Bible withholds *which* Pharaoh — because the point isn't the historical individual but what Pharaoh *represents*. Chris: *"you don't need to know which one it is... Each pharaoh is represented by the character of Pharaoh."*
- **Words (word-picture over abstraction)** — Egypt as *metaphor*, the two sticks (Pharaoh's crook of coercive power vs. God's desert shepherd's staff), the cartouche/vault — meaning carried in images, not definitions.
- **Community vs individual** — Shalom is communal (a whole "kingdom of priests" representing God), vs. empire's single mediator (Pharaoh as sole priest-king). God wants "all of them to have become a kingdom of priests."
- **Truth over time (static vs dynamic/discovery)** — The hosts read the Exodus as perennial ("true no matter which era"), applying it to present politics on purpose — understanding of the same truth keeps unfolding into new contexts.

## Recurring Through-lines

- **Trust the story (the arc itself).** The empire/shalom frame *is* the arc made structural: shalom is the kingdom built on trusting God's good story; empire is the refusal. Marty threads it from Genesis's tohu-va'vohu order-from-chaos straight into Exodus. The phrase will "run through my entire teaching from Exodus all the way through Revelation." Recurs across the corpus; condensed at the Session 1 capstone (episode 32).
- **Sin as failing to master the animal instinct ("master the beast").** Present at the collective/systemic scale: empire is the un-mastered instinct — fear, self-preservation, and domination — organized into a civilization (Pharaoh's stick held *over* people). Choosing shalom is mastering it. The macro-scale cousin of the failure anchored in `1-master-the-beast-3.md`. Flag for cross-reference; do not re-explain there.
- **"Egypt gets inside you" (callback to eps. 9–10, 16).** The episode makes explicit what earlier Abraham/Joseph episodes seeded: Egypt isn't just a place but a narrative that lodges in the heart. Links the "harder to get Egypt out of the people than the people out of Egypt" through-line.
- **Numbers/empire-building & settling (Babel callback, ep. 6).** Egypt's cities, metalworking, and vault-keeping echo Babel's "settle and make a name" impulse — empire as the civilized form of mistrust.

## Terminology

- **talmid / talmidim** (תלמיד / תלמידים) — "disciple(s)"; Chris is a *talmid*. Marty: *"Talmidim means disciples, plural."*
- **tohu va'vohu** (תהו ובהו) — "chaos / formless void"; the biblical God speaks *order* out of it (contrast with Egypt's creation myth).
- **yada** (ידע) — "to know" experientially and intimately, not merely cerebrally. Chris: *"To know experientially... intimately."* God acts so all parties *yada* that he is the LORD.
- **mammon** — "wealth" (a later figure/god); "you cannot serve both God and mammon" = cannot serve both shalom and empire.
- **cartouche / the vault** — the Egyptian image of a space of order (with the god/Pharaoh inside) surrounded by watery chaos; Pharaoh does the work to "keep the vault in order."
- **empire vs. shalom** — the episode's master frame: empire (Egypt) built on fear and expendable people; shalom (the Bible) built on trust, inclusion, and everything in its proper place.

## Discussion Questions

1. "Every day you and I get to choose which narrative we buy into." Where in an ordinary week do you feel the pull of the empire story (fear, scarcity, self-preservation) versus the shalom story (trust, enough, inclusion)?
2. The sign of empire is that some people become expendable — "the poor, the refugee, the alien, the orphan, the widow, the elderly." Who gets pushed to the edges in *your* world to keep the "vault" in order?
3. God's harder task was getting Egypt *out of* Israel, not Israel out of Egypt. What "Egypt" (a narrative, comfort, security) is lodged in you that God might be working to draw out?
4. Picture standing with one foot in waist-deep barley and one in barren desert, asked "which God brings you order?" Be honest — which side would you instinctively point to as where God is? What does that reveal?
5. God wanted not just a messenger but a people who *are* the message. What would it look like for your community to *be* God's message rather than merely deliver it?
6. Marty says you can't build a "godly empire" — the two kingdoms rest on opposite foundations. Where are you tempted to try to have both, and what does that cost?
