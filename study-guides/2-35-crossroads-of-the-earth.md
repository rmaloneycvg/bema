# Crossroads of the Earth

BEMA 35 · Session 2
Source transcript: `~/workspace/bema/transcripts/2-35-crossroads-of-the-earth.md`
Book: Joshua → bridging into Judges (read passage: Matthew 5:14-16)
Co-host: Brent Billings · Host: Marty Solomon

> **Session 2 overarching arc: the partner's (Israel's) unfaithfulness and God's refusal to quit.** Session 1 established the *partnership* — God binds himself to a people and gives them a mission ("bless all nations," "a kingdom of priests"). This episode is the hinge where the mission gets a *map*. Having walked through the ethics and conflict of the conquest in the previous episode "from a distance," Marty now asks the missional question: "what is God up to in what he's doing?" The answer is geography — God deliberately plants his people at the **crossroads of the earth**, the one highway (the *Via Maris*) through which every nation must pass, so they can be a blessing and a "city on a hill." But the episode's narrative payoff is the opening note of the unfaithfulness that will define the whole arc: the tribe of **Dan abandons the post God assigned it** — the hard coastal-plain mission next to the Philistines — and migrates to comfortable, well-watered Laish, where it is obliterated and ultimately drops out of the tribal rolls. Marty: *"Do not forsake the thing that God gives you to do… Do not abandon the thing that God has given for you, that only you are designed to do."* This is how the episode advances the one story: it shows the *shape* of the partnership-in-space (the mission is a place, not a holy huddle), and in Dan it shows the partner already drifting off-mission — while God's intent to "bless all nations" does not change. (Note: the episode itself bridges Joshua toward Judges; it does not reach the writing prophets. Frame against drift and faithfulness, not a later stage.)

## Key Lessons

- **God's mission is a *place*: he plants his people at the crossroads of the earth so they cannot help but bless all nations.** The land is chosen for its location, not its luxury. Marty: *"this little chunk of dirt that God gives His people is right in the middle. It is right in the middle of it all. It is the crossroads of the earth."* Because God wants to bless *all* nations ("zero" left out), *"He's going to put them in the middle of all nations. So He literally takes His people and He places them at the crossroads of the ancient world."* A holy huddle is the one thing the geography forbids: *"He does not want His people to take their ball and go hide in the corner."*
- **"A kingdom of priests" is a spatial calling — the priests belong in the middle, like the tabernacle in the center of the camp.** The geography repeats the architecture of Session 1. Marty: *"where did that tabernacle sit?… Right in the middle of the camp… if you're going to be a kingdom of priests, well, the tabernacle, that place where the priests are, needs to be right in the middle of the camp of the world… everybody needed to have access to atonement and priesthood and worship."*
- **The land of milk and honey is a call to dependence, not abundance.** It is read backwards through Western consumer eyes. Marty: *"the land of milk and honey is actually not the phrase we think it is… milk — where do you get milk from?… their flocks… honey is the word for sweets."* The promised land is *less* lush than Goshen: *"You left Goshen. Not to a better land in terms of the world's economy, to a land where you have to depend on God. The blessing of the promised land is not my blessing. It's the Abrahamic blessing. It's external blessing… I want to bless you so that you can bless others."*
- **God calls his people into the shephelah — the uncomfortable in-between — not the safety of the Judah Mountains.** The regional map teaches the crossroads lesson "in an even tighter package." Marty: *"God doesn't want you sitting in the comfort and safety of the Judah Mountains. God wants you blessing all nations. I can't bless them from the Judah Mountains. I need to take up residence in the shephelah… I need to occupy the space of where these two worlds interact. That's how I'm going to bless all nations."*
- **God's people are to *be* the city gate — refuge, provision, and welfare for the needy outsider.** The whole archaeology of the gate (defense + municipal/welfare center) resolves into Matthew 5. Marty: *"the city is your symbol of hope… that's where they take the extra and they give it to those who have need… A city on a hill, that's where I might find a little bit of extra… You would want to be a place where the outsiders would say, 'Hey, this city really takes care of you.'"* Jesus' "you are the light of the world, a city on a hill" is the New Testament payoff of the geography lesson.
- **Do not forsake the mission God entrusts to you — the lesson of Dan.** The episode's advance of the arc. Dan is given the Philistine frontier and abandons it. Marty: *"they don't want that land… they end up moving… all the way up to Dan"* — to lush Laish, which is the *first* stop for every invading empire, so Dan "literally… gets obliterated," and in Revelation "Dan is missing, which is a lesson… Do not forsake the thing that God gives you to do… that only you are designed to do."

## East/West Lens

- **Mission framed spatially / relationally (the crossroads, the gate) vs. abstractly (a set of beliefs to protect).** The engaged category: the Hebraic imagination locates vocation in dirt and highways rather than in insulated ideas. Marty: *"when we build these things in an effort to insulate ourselves, we're missing the thing that God's called us to… God wanted us to be in the middle of those people."*
- **Blessing as external/communal (blessed to bless) vs. blessing as personal possession (comfort I receive).** Marty corrects the Western hearing directly: *"The blessing of the promised land is not my blessing. It's the Abrahamic blessing. It's external blessing… I want to bless you so that you can bless others."*
- **Land/provision as dependence vs. land/provision as prosperity.** The "milk and honey" and "watered with your feet" images read lush-Goshen as the lesser economy and the dependent desert-land as the gift. Marty: *"to a land where you have to depend on God."*
- **Concrete word-picture (the physical city gate) decoding an abstract teaching ("light of the world").** The method of the whole episode: Jesus' metaphor becomes legible only once you *see* the gate. Marty: *"now I have the picture, the biblical picture of what a city on a hill means. The city represents protection."*

## Episode Flow (coverage backbone)

A sequential walkthrough of every teaching beat, in order. Used to verify full coverage.

1. **Framing — a two-parter; from the ethics of conquest to the mission.** Brent sets it up: "wrap up our discussion about Joshua and bridge the gap toward the book of Judges… the mission of God and His choice of real estate." Marty contrasts last episode (the "nitty-gritty… ethics… conflict of the conquest") with today's "missional language": *"what is God up to in what he's doing?"* The scholarship question "swirls around geography and location — 'Location, location, location.'" Both hosts stress the episode leans heavily on the visual presentation.
2. **Regional map — the Via Maris and the crossroads.** The zoomed-out map: Egypt (SW), Greece/Rome (NW, "later"), Assyria/Persia/Babylon (NE). The yellow line is the *Via Maris*, "the one main highway," connecting Egypt to Assyria/Persia/Babylon and on to the Hittites/Silk Road, running straight through Israel — "the turnstile of the ancient world." God places his people "right in the middle of it all."
3. **"Bless all nations" / kingdom of priests — why the middle.** Marty recalls the Abrahamic promise (Brent: "Bless all nations"); "all" means *all*, "zero" left out. Therefore God refuses the holy huddle and plants the people at the crossroads, echoing the tabernacle "right in the middle of the camp" (Brent).
4. **Land of milk and honey — dependence, not abundance.** Marty dismantles the consumer reading: milk from desert-grazing flocks, honey = "sweets" (even "honey from the rock," Deut). "Watered with your feet" (Goshen's lush irrigation) is the *lesser* economy; the promised land makes you "depend on God." Brent confirms from travel that Israel is dry, rocky — "not Glacier National Park." The blessing is the *Abrahamic/external* blessing.
5. **Tribal allotment map.** The land is divvied among the tribes. Levi has no inheritance (priestly, living among the people); Joseph is absent because his two sons **Ephraim and Manasseh** (Brent) carry his portion — restoring the count to twelve. The inheritance "comes with a missional responsibility… you also have to take and steward."
6. **The regional "BLT" zones (Brent's map).** Top bun = **Galilee** (north); bottom bun = the southern deserts (Zin, Paran, Negev). East–west strips: the **Rift Valley**/Jordan (headwaters → Sea of Galilee/Genneseret → Dead Sea), the **Judah wilderness**, the **Judah Mountains** (secure, comfortable; Jerusalem sits here — Brent), and the **coastal plain** where the pagans live (**Philistines** south — Ashdod, Ekron, Gaza; **Phoenicians** north). Brent's running BLT-sandwich confession.
7. **The shephelah — the called-to in-between.** Root "to bow/kneel": the lowland bows from the Judah Mountains down to the coastal plain. The crossroads lesson "in an even tighter package": God calls his people *into* the shephelah to "occupy the space of where these two worlds interact."
8. **The tel — layered cities and the makor (water source).** A tel is a mound of stacked ruined cities (17–21 layers at **Tel Gezer**). *The Source* (Michener) models the pie-slice excavation of a fictional tel named **Makor** ("source"). Cities are rebuilt on the same spot because of water, defensibility, commerce, and farmland (Brent guesses "water"). Photos of **Tel Gezer** and **Tel Lystra** (Acts; newly excavated).
9. **City gates (sha'ar / sha'arim) — defense technology.** Walls require a gate; the gate is a *defensive bottleneck* ("40,000 soldiers… only six at a time"). Chambered-gate progression: one room per side (Abraham) → four-chambered (David) → six-chambered **Solomonic** (the illustrated gate, Gezer/Megiddo; "Solomon's goat") → eight-chambered (Hezekiah), after which armies switched to siege ramps. Brent's drone photos of Tel Gezer.
10. **Gates as municipal/welfare center.** For ~79 of every 80 years (attacks ~once every 80 years), the gate is the city center: courthouse, elders/judges (**Lot**, **Eli** "at the gate"), and the welfare system. Economic strata: richest nearest the center; **casemate housing** (middle class *in* the wall — explains **Rahab** and **Paul** lowered out a window; fillable with stones in war); the poor outside; the destitute at the burning city dump ("weeping and gnashing of teeth"). Brent recants his original "resentment" reading.
11. **Matthew 5:14-16 — the payoff (Brent reads).** With the gate now pictured, "you are the light of the world, a city on a hill" means refuge, provision, and welfare for the needy outsider — God's people called to *be* the city gate. Marty imagines Solomon showing off the welfare system.
12. **The tribe of Dan — advancing the arc.** Dan is allotted a slice of the coastal plain to engage the **Philistines**, with big-brother **Judah** next door (Judah's 3,000 defend **Samson**). Dan dislikes the mission and migrates after Samson's story to **Laish/Dan** far north — lush, spring-fed, but the *first* stop for every invading empire (Assyria, Babylon, Persia, Hittites), with weaker **Naphtali** as its only neighbor. Dan is "obliterated" and is "missing" from the tribes in Revelation. Rabbinic lesson: do not forsake your God-given mission.
13. **Conclusions & the "holding both" exchange.** Marty's two conclusions: (1) God wants his people at the crossroads — not insulating themselves (Christian schools/homeschooling/music have a place, but not as a wall against "those people"); (2) do not forsake the mission. Brent: after the "dark" previous episode, this one is "very much in tension" yet "so much more hopeful." Marty: "it's holding both… both sides of that tension are really important."

## Terminology

- **Via Maris** (Latin, "way of the sea") — the single main highway of the ancient world, funneling all traffic between Egypt and Assyria/Persia/Babylon/the Hittites through the land of Israel; the basis of the "crossroads"/"turnstile" placement. Marty: *"It is the main highway, the one main highway. Runs right through… the land of Israel."*
- **crossroads / "turnstile of the ancient world"** — the strategic middle position God chose for his people so they could bless all nations. Marty: *"If you're going to go anywhere from one major region to another major region… you're going to go through this really this little chunk of dirt."*
- **a kingdom of priests** — the priestly vocation located *in the middle* of the nations, mirroring the tabernacle in the middle of the camp. Marty: *"if you're going to be a kingdom of priests… the tabernacle… needs to be right in the middle of the camp of the world."*
- **land of milk and honey** — not Western abundance but dependence: *milk* from desert-grazing flocks, *honey* = "sweets." Marty: *"this is not an image of prosperity and American abundance."*
- **shephelah** (Hebrew, root "to bow/kneel") — the lowland that "bows down" from the Judah Mountains to the coastal plain; the called-to in-between. Marty: *"God is calling us into the shephelah. It's like the crossroads lesson in an even tighter package."*
- **tel** (Hebrew) — a mound of stacked, rebuilt ruined cities (17–21 layers at Gezer). Marty: *"inside this mound of dirt sits sometimes centuries worth of cities."*
- **makor** (Hebrew, "source") — the water source that dictates where a city (and thus a tel) is built; the name of the fictional tel in Michener's *The Source*. Marty: *"Makor is the word for 'source'… You gotta have water."*
- **sha'ar / sha'arim** (Hebrew, "gate(s)") — the chambered city gate: a defensive bottleneck and, in peacetime, the municipal/welfare center. Marty: *"The city gate is primarily a defense mechanism"*; also where "your elders or your judges are gonna sit."
- **casemate housing** — middle-class apartments built *into* the city wall (fillable with stones in war to thicken it); explains Rahab and Paul lowered out a window. Marty: *"the city walls essentially have apartments built into them."* (Marty's Elle anecdote: a teacher asked "Who was your teacher?" when she named it.)
- **the tribe of Dan** — allotted the Philistine coastal frontier, it abandons the post and migrates to lush Laish/Dan in the north, is destroyed, and is "missing" from the tribes in Revelation. Marty: *"Do not forsake the thing that God gives you to do."*

## Discussion Questions

1. God deliberately placed his people at the crossroads rather than in a safe corner. Where in your own life have you built a "holy huddle" — a Christian space meant to keep certain people *out* — and what would it look like to move into the shephelah instead?
2. Marty insists the "blessing" of the promised land is external — "blessed to bless others," and tied to *dependence* rather than comfort. How does hearing "milk and honey" as dependence-on-God rather than abundance change what you think God's blessing on your life is *for*?
3. The city gate was both a wall of defense *and* the place where surplus was given to the needy outsider — the reason Jesus calls his people "a city on a hill." In practice, how can a community be both secure and radically open to the people outside it?
4. Dan abandoned the hard mission next to the Philistines for beautiful, comfortable land — and it cost them everything. What is the specific thing "only you are designed to do" that you've been tempted to trade for something more comfortable?
5. Brent admits he first read the city-gate system as oppression and resentment before seeing it as hope. Where might a cultural assumption be making you read a part of Scripture (or your own situation) more cynically than it deserves?
6. The hosts refuse to collapse the tension between the "dark" conquest episode and this hopeful missional one — they insist on "holding both." Where in your faith are you tempted to resolve a hard tension too quickly, and what might it mean to sit in it instead?
