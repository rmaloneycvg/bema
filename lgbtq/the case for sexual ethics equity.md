# The Case for Sexual Ethics Equity

**A BEMA-informed appeal to the leadership of Discovery Church, Broomfield, Colorado**

---

## 0. What this document is — and is not

**This is not an argument that Discovery must become affirming.**

It is an argument that Discovery's *formal published position* — that homosexuality is sexual sin — is doing work the biblical text cannot bear on its own, that it functions in practice as a boundary marker rather than a pastoral conviction, and that the people it has pushed out are owed acknowledgment and repair.

The ask is **equity**, not capitulation:

1. Retire the *formal position statement* on homosexuality as a published boundary marker. Hold convictions; stop publishing them as a gate.
2. Name and apologize for the harm — specifically to those who left.
3. Create real space for members who do not hold the traditional view to serve, lead, and belong without being treated as a discipleship problem.

**A note of intellectual honesty that should be stated up front, in the room:** Marty Solomon, the teacher of the BEMA Discipleship podcast, *explicitly declines* to resolve the affirming/non-affirming question. His words, in the middle of the most-cited passage:

> "I'm not going to sit on this podcast and try to unpack the conversation about homosexuality. Our listeners come from a wide swath of church fellowships and experiences, and your church fellowship has a stance I'm sure on these different things and there are different ways to understand. I am not going to sit here on a podcast and try to fix that... Do it on your own. Do it as a fellowship."
> — **BEMA 149, *Romans – Blended Family***

This is not a weakness in the argument. **It is the argument.** BEMA's method demonstrates that (a) the proof texts do not say what the position statement assumes, and (b) the community — not a published statement — is the biblical venue for deciding contested applications. "Do it as a fellowship" is precisely what a formal position statement forecloses.

**Terminology note:** Where a claim comes from the BEMA transcripts, it is cited by episode. Where I have supplied outside material, it is marked `[outside]` so nothing is smuggled in.

## 1. The Interpretive Toolkit: how the text was built to be read

Discovery's position statement presupposes a **static rulebook** hermeneutic: locate verse, extract proposition, apply. The authors of Scripture did not write that way, and the first readers did not read that way.

### 1.1 PaRDeS — four levels of reading

From **BEMA 110, *Jewish Hermeneutics*** and **BEMA 106, *Jury Duty in Sodom***:

| Level | Hebrew | Meaning | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P** | *p'shat* | Surface / plain sense | Read what it says |
| **R** | *remez* | "Hint" — a buried pointer to a Hebrew Scripture passage | Find the quotation or unusual phrase; go read its source |
| **D** | *drash* | Truth hidden in story; what the *remez* unlocks | Wrestle in community |
| **S** | *sod* | Mystery; cannot be taught | Gift of God / Spirit |

Two guardrails Marty insists on, which matter enormously for how this conversation is conducted:

- **"There's nothing wrong with p'shat."** "The deeper levels are not more true, they're not more inspired than p'shat... God does not work any less at a deeper level, anymore at a deeper level than he does on a shallow level." (BEMA 110)
- **The levels do not contradict each other.** Depth "widens... it gives it color and depth and life. It doesn't change the meaning." (BEMA 106)

**Why this matters for Discovery:** a *p'shat*-only reading of Romans 1 or Leviticus 18 is not illegitimate. It is *incomplete*. The problem is not that traditionalists read the surface; it is that a *published institutional boundary* has been built on a surface reading of texts whose *remez* and context point somewhere else. Depth does not overturn p'shat — but it does forbid freezing p'shat into law.

### 1.2 Discovery *is* the method

"The Easterner wants you to **discover** truth... An Easterner believes that you understand a thesis... so much better than you would have if I just explained it to you." A rabbi uses a parable "to make the teaching **harder**, more difficult, not easier." (BEMA 110)

And critically:

> "Jewish hermeneutics demands the interaction of more than one student, the *remez* and the *drash* **have to be** discussed, examined, critiqued as a group in the process of group discovery in order for true discovery to take place. This is why learning is done in the context of a *havurah*." (BEMA 110)

A published position statement is the structural opposite of a *havurah*. It replaces group discovery with a settled answer and makes dissent a deviation rather than a contribution.

### 1.3 "Stringing pearls" and *remez* — Paul's actual method

In **Romans 3:10–18**, Paul strings together six-plus quotations (Psalm 14/53, Ecclesiastes 7, Psalm 5, Psalm 140, Psalm 10 LXX, Isaiah 59, Psalm 36).

> "Paul is doing something known as **'stringing pearls'** in the rabbinic world... underneath it all, there is a Jewish sermon going on... he's going *remez*, *remez*, *remez*, *remez*." (BEMA 149)

And the payload: every one of those quotations is **about Jews**, not Gentiles.

> Marty: "Who are all these about?" Brent: "The Jews." Marty: "Paul is doubling down... **Don't point at the Gentiles and say they're sinners. We know what sin is. It's in our own story.**" (BEMA 149)

Romans 3 — the chapter most quoted to prove universal sinfulness — is, on the *remez* level, a rebuke of religious insiders for pointing at outsiders.

### 1.4 "Fulfill" and "abolish" mean something specific

From **BEMA 95, *Abolish or Fulfill*** (Matthew 5:17–20):

> "When a rabbi says he's **fulfilling** Torah, it means that he is **interpreting it correctly**... you have to interpret it intellectually correctly, and then you have to back it up with your behavior and your obedience in your walk, and then you are fulfilling it. If you don't do that process, it's called **abolishing**... You can abolish Torah by interpreting it intellectually incorrectly... You can also abolish it by interpreting it the right way, but not walking it out correctly." (BEMA 95)

**This is the sharpest available challenge to a position statement.** By this definition, a *correct* interpretation walked out in a way that strips dignity, withholds hospitality, and drives people out is **not fulfillment. It is abolishment.** Orthodoxy of statement plus failure of walk equals abolishing Torah. Discovery cannot claim to be "holding the line on Scripture" while producing exiles; on Jesus' own rabbinic definition, that combination is the failure mode.

### 1.5 "But I say unto you" — commands aim at formation, not compliance

> "Contrary to most popular thought, Jesus is **not upping the ante** for God's expectation. Jesus is claiming that this was **always** God's expectation... God gave you the commandment 'do not murder' so that through Torah, you would learn how to go and be reconciled to your brother." (BEMA 96, *But I Say Unto You*)

Every command is diagnostic of what it *forms in you toward other people*. Applied honestly: what has the position statement formed at Discovery? If the fruit is exclusion and departure, the interpretation is under suspicion by Jesus' own test — "All these commandments were given so that you would learn how to treat people." (BEMA 96)

### 1.6 Binding and loosing — the community's actual authority

This is the single most important section for the "is there a middle ground?". From **BEMA 140, *Not to Burden You*** (Acts 15, Matthew 16:19):

> "There are things in the Text that God has said. What God says is final... **However, there are a lot of things that God does not say.** In other words, some things are black and white. Many other things are very gray. God very rarely engaged in the art of interpretation. **God left that up to us.** God gave us the truth, but how we interpret and apply that truth has always been up to the community of God's people." (BEMA 140)

- **Binding** = the community decides a command applies a given way.
- **Loosing** = the community decides it does not.
- "Whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven. Whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven." (Matthew 16:19)

> This was done at Discovery but can it be taken back or changed?  Was it premature?  Was the full context known and was this representative of the community or only a few?  
>
> I don't think this was done well at Discovery.  It lacked visibility. Staff and elders who started this work were not all involved in finishing it.  Did they change like that in the first churches?  Staff bias is greater because of tenure as well.

And the posture that goes with it:

> "To be sure, the community very rarely unanimously agreed on these issues. That would be almost impossible. We're humans. **There would always be groups of people who disagreed**, but the process of binding and loosing was understood. They respected and submitted to the larger opinion of the community. **They didn't leave and go find another church**" (BEMA 140) 


> "I wish we knew how to respect the dialogue and opinions of others more... in an ironic twist, **we'd actually preserve the space to disagree in a healthy way.** There are things about the church that we belong to, that I don't agree with... For things that I just have a difference of opinion on, and the whole community has gathered together and they decided that they don't agree with me, I'm going to respect that... **I'm going to still talk about it and learn and push and challenge and wrestle because that's a part of the process together.**" (BEMA 140)

**This is the middle ground Discovery says does not exist — and it is the oldest position in the room.** Acts 15 is a recorded instance of a church with two irreconcilable factions, both convinced they were defending Scripture, who argued it out and produced a ruling that *deliberately under-burdened* the newcomers. ==Note what Acts 15 does **not** produce: a doctrinal statement about who is disqualified.==

## 2. The Audience: who was actually in the room when these texts were written

Discovery's position statement addresses a single undifferentiated audience: "people today, about sexual behavior." The biblical letters never do this. **Audience is the variable that changes the answer.**

### 2.1 The three people groups (Acts 13; Galatians)

From **BEMA 139, *Theosabes***:

| Group | Term | Who | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brothers** | Born Jews | Insiders, Sinai covenant |
| 2 | **Children of Abraham** (*b'nei Avraham*) | Proselytes — circumcised, full Torah | Adopted insiders |
| 3 | **God-fearers** (*theosabes*) | Gentiles worshiping Israel's God, uncircumcised | **The contested category** |

> "Everybody agreed about the brothers and the children of Abraham, but **what do we do with the *theosabes*?**" (BEMA 139)

Marty notes that modern translations have *erased* this structure: Acts 13:26 in the NIV84 reads "Brothers, children of Abraham, and you God-fearing Gentiles," and the updated NIV dropped "brothers" as redundant. It was not redundant. It was the whole architecture. (BEMA 139)

**The entire New Testament epistolary corpus is a sustained argument about group three.** Every text Discovery cites about sexual behavior was written into that argument — an argument about *who belongs without first being made to conform*.

### 2.2 The three groups in Rome (Romans 1–3)

Romans has a *different* triad. From **BEMA 149**:

| Group | Who | Paul's move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brand-new Gentile converts out of paganism | Romans 1:18–32 — "your measuring stick produced this" |
| 2 | Mature Gentiles who look down on group 1 | **Romans 2:1** — "you therefore have no excuse" |
| 3 | Returning Jews who look down on both | Romans 2:17–3:20 — "same problem" |

Historical context (BEMA 149): Claudius expelled Jews from Rome c. AD 51 (cf. Acts 18:2). The church went from Jewish-majority with included Gentiles to entirely Gentile. Years later the Jews returned — to a congregation with new leadership, new rhythms, and no seat saved for them.

> "Paul is writing to a church that finds itself splintered and fractured between two dominant groups... **It's not actually a theological treatise to explain to us how one gets saved.** It is a letter addressing a diverse body of people that's having some real diversity fits." (BEMA 149)

> "Paul's point is **not** one of these groups has any more claim to the Gospel, has any more claim to God's love... **You think you're better than the Gentiles. You're not.** You Gentiles think you're better than those Gentiles? No, you're not. We all have the same problem." (BEMA 149)

**Romans is a blended-family reconciliation letter written to a congregation fighting about who belongs.** That is Discovery's exact situation. The letter's own argument turns on its readers at 2:1 the moment they use chapter 1 as a weapon.

## 3. The Proof Texts, Read at Time of Writing

### 3.1 Romans 1:18–32 — the central text

This is the passage Discovery's position most depends on. BEMA's treatment is direct:

> "Here's this passage we're not going to deal with — Paul has just gotten done literally **defining Hellenism**. What he is speaking to is a pagan Hellenism. The two paragraphs that follow are exact descriptions of how that pagan reality lived itself out. Now, **it is foolish if we take those two paragraphs, jump 2000 years in the future, and then apply it to our understanding of some of those same words.**" (BEMA 149)

> "The one thing that Paul is **not** talking about is same-sex marriage, what we understand today as homosexual orientation. He's not talking about any of those conversations. He is talking about an ancient Hellenism which bore a particular kind of fruit." (BEMA 149)

**What Paul's readers would have pictured — pederasty and the dominance ethic:**

> "Pederasty was a very common practice. It was considered the status quo among middle-class, typical Roman citizens. Part of Roman law said it was illegal for a woman to be on top during sex. ==**Sexual intercourse for the Roman culture between men or women or anybody or anything was all about dominance. It was all about penetration. It was about who's penetrating who**==, and so part of becoming a man in the Roman culture was about learning how to penetrate... and you learned that by being penetrated as a boy... almost every middle-class male child was at some point some older male gentleman's boy." (BEMA 149)

> "That's what Paul is addressing here between men and between women, and the horrible dysfunction of Roman inappropriate sexuality. ==**He's not addressing the kind of arguments we have today about monogamous, same-sex marriage and an orientation** and mental and emotional — it's just not even the same conversation."== (BEMA 149)

> "**The Bible doesn't have the same understanding of orientation that we have today** in 2019, 2020. It has a completely different understanding of sexuality. We can't project those two things onto each other." (BEMA 149)

**The structural argument in Romans 1 is about *measuring rods*, not sexual acts.** The literary hinge is Romans 1:23 — "exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images": "They exchanged God as the measuring rod, and they replaced it with man and created things as the measuring rod. **That's an exact description of the culture of Hellenism.**" (BEMA 149)

Paul's own conclusion is a trap that springs on the reader at **Romans 2:1**: "You, therefore, have no excuse, you who pass judgment on someone else." Anyone who uses Romans 1 as a boundary marker has stepped directly into the verse that follows it.

**What this does and does not establish.** It does not establish that Scripture affirms same-sex marriage. It establishes that **Romans 1 cannot be the load-bearing wall of a formal position statement**, because it is not addressing the thing the statement addresses. ==A position built on it is built on a category error.==

### 3.2 Sodom — Ezekiel's own reading

From **BEMA 106, *Jury Duty in Sodom***:

> "Somewhere between 7 to 11 direct references of Sodom in the Old Testament or in the Bible as a whole... **only one of them mentions sexuality. It's the book of Jude at the very end.** Every other reference associates Sodom with the same group of sins. Sexuality does not make the list, just as a passing reference." (BEMA 106)

**State this carefully.** `[outside]` The claim is not that sexuality is *absent* from the Sodom tradition — Jude 7 and **2 Peter 2:6–10** both touch it, and Genesis 19 is itself a text, not merely a later reading of one. The defensible claim, which is the one that matters, is narrower and stronger: **when God gives the summary indictment himself, it is economic and hospitality-based, and it comes first.**

**Ezekiel 16:49–50**, God's own stated indictment: "Now this was the sin of your sister Sodom: **She and her daughters were arrogant, overfed and unconcerned. They did not help the poor and needy.** They were haughty and did detestable things before me."

The generalizing phrase — "detestable things," *toevah* — **follows** the specifics rather than replacing them. A reader is free to fill it out from Leviticus 18:22; what a reader is not free to do is skip the two verses of named, concrete sin that God puts first.

And the audience of that verse — the *remez* payload — is **Jerusalem**, God's own people: "Son of man, confront Jerusalem." (Ezekiel 16:2; BEMA 106)

> "Sodom and Gomorrah was about who? If you go back to Genesis, they took advantage of who?" — "Of foreigners." — "**Foreigners; it's about the mamzer.**" (BEMA 106)

The Genesis 18–19 frame is **hospitality**. Genesis 18 opens with Abraham, days after circumcision, hurrying from the shade to serve three strangers 60 pounds of flour's worth of bread (BEMA on Genesis 18, Session 1): "In the Eastern world, there is this unbelievable premium on hospitality." Sodom is the photographic negative of Abraham's tent. The sin is **the abuse of the vulnerable guest**.

`[outside]` *Toevah*, the Hebrew word rendered "abomination" in Leviticus 18:22, is the same word used of Egyptian dietary boundaries (Genesis 43:32) and dishonest weights (Deuteronomy 25:16). It is boundary-marker vocabulary — "not-done-here" — not a tier of moral gravity.

### 3.3 The "woes" text — and the verse Posture Shift quotes

**Matthew 11:20–24** (woes on Chorazin, Bethsaida, Capernaum) runs immediately into **Matthew 11:28–30** ("Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened"). They are one unit. From BEMA 106:

> "He begins to denounce them because they did not **repent**... The problem is not their belief. The problem is their repentance. **This group of people, they believe more than any of the five groups.** They totally believe. They know their Text. They've got it all memorized. They're committed to walking it out. **They're not hypocrites**, in the sense that we think of hypocrites as two-faced." (BEMA 106)

> ### Note:
> The documented rigorus education of all children and what they memorized and practiced debating is shown in much more detail in other BEMA transcripts, intense

> "It's that in their devotion and in their walk of obedience, **they don't take care of the poor, the needy among them, those on the outside.**" (BEMA 106)

> "I think Jesus says, 'Woe to you, you know your doctrine, you know your theology, you worship me with long songs at your church services, **and yet you don't take care of people that are looking for the face of God.**'" (BEMA 106)

> "Jesus follows up this woe... because they're not taking care of the heavy-laden, the burdened, the tired, the weary. Jesus turns around and says, '**If you're burdened, if you're weary, if you're heavy-laden, come to me and I will give you rest.**'... The Pharisees have a yoke. **This is the pivotal exhibit.** Posture Shift's own about-page invokes exactly this verse — "Come, all who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest for your soul" — as its warrant for a hospitable posture. In its literary context that invitation is addressed to **people made weary by the yoke of doctrinally impeccable, sincerely devout religious insiders**. ==Matthew 11:28 is not a warrant for a kind position statement. It is Jesus offering relief *from* one.==

### 3.4 Acts 15's "sexual immorality"

Acts 15:20, 29 requires Gentiles to abstain from food polluted by idols, from sexual immorality, from strangled meat, and from blood. This phrase is sometimes offered as a New Testament sexual ethic. Its actual basis, from **BEMA 140**:

> "This is the **Noahic covenant**... the Mishnah had basically taken the Noahic covenant, and for its current cultural context had redefined... three parts... idolatry... sexual immorality... and sanctity of life." (BEMA 140)

> "James is saying, 'I can't hold them to the Sinai covenant, because they're not Sinai covenant people, and they don't have to become Sinai covenant people, but I can hold them to the Noahic covenant.' I see James going, '**I'm going to find what few rules I can**'..." (BEMA 140)

Two observations. First, James is *minimizing* — finding the floor, not the ceiling. The letter's own framing is "**not to burden you** with anything beyond the following requirements" (Acts 15:28). Second, the Noahic frame anchors "sexual immorality" in Genesis 6–9 categories — exploitation, violation, the violation of the vulnerable (cf. Genesis 9:20–25) — not in a modern taxonomy of orientation.

**Do not overreach here — and expect this to be the sharpest objection in the room.** Acts 15 loosed circumcision and kosher and **explicitly retained** *porneia*. A prepared traditionalist will say: *the Council loosed the identity markers and kept the ethics — and that is exactly the distinction your own source draws.* They are right that it draws it. BEMA 140 divides Torah in three:

| Category | Contents | Status for Gentiles |
|---|---|---|
| **Cultic** | temple, priesthood, sacrifice — "if you need a temple to do it" | fulfilled in Jesus |
| **Ethical** | "universally true for all people... not true just because God said so arbitrarily... **it's the way God made the universe to function**" — Ten Commandments, Sabbath | **binding** |
| ***Miqsat ma'asay haTorah*** | "the part of the law that makes you Jewish" — circumcision, kosher, tassels, blended fabrics, cleanliness | **loosed at Acts 15** |

Marty even says of James's three prohibitions: "**You can call it ethical law if you want to.**" (BEMA 140)

**So concede the category and contest the content.** This document does *not* argue that Acts 15 loosed sexual ethics. It argues about **what the ethical content is** — and BEMA's own definition of ethical law supplies the test. Ethical law binds not because God arbitrarily said so but because it describes "the way God made the universe to function." That is a **functional** criterion, not a taxonomy of bodies. Scripture states the function in three places already cited:

- **Ezekiel 16:49** — God's own summary of Sodom: arrogance, excess, neglect of the needy.
- **Isaiah 56:4** — God's own criteria for restoring an excluded sexual minority: keep my Sabbaths, choose what pleases me, hold fast to my covenant.
- **Matthew 5:21–30** — every command is measured by what it forms in you toward other people (BEMA 96).

**The ask, precisely stated:** not that ethical law be loosed, but that it be applied as the functional test BEMA's own definition says it is. And note that this is not the lax option. Applied consistently to exploitation, hoarding, and covenant-breaking, it indicts a great deal of heterosexual practice about which Discovery has published nothing.

`[outside]` The two most-cited vice-list terms, **μαλακοί** (*malakoi*) and **ἀρσενοκοῖται** (*arsenokoitai*) in 1 Corinthians 6:9, are not discussed in the BEMA transcripts and should not be attributed to them. *Malakos* means "soft" and is used elsewhere of fine clothing (Matthew 11:8). *Arsenokoitēs* is rare enough that Paul may have coined it, and its precise referent is disputed among scholars who read Greek professionally. **The claim here is not that the term is novel in English** — the KJV's "abusers of themselves with mankind" (1611) shows the referent was read into it long before the word "homosexual" existed. The claim is narrower and holds: this is a **contested lexical judgment**, and a contested lexical judgment is an unstable foundation for an institutional boundary.

Note also what the surrounding list contains — "thieves, the greedy, drunkards, slanderers, swindlers" (1 Cor 6:10). Discovery has published no position on greed or slander. **The list is being mined, not read**, and that selectivity is itself part of what this document is asking about.

## 4. The Trajectory: Scripture revising its own boundary marker

The strongest biblical case is not a verse. It is a **direction of travel** the canon takes with a specific excluded group — and it is the closest structural analogue Scripture offers to the present question.

### 4.1 Step one — the exclusion (Deuteronomy 23:1)

> "As a eunuch, he will not be allowed to worship Adonai and will be excluded from the temple assembly. He would've been forced to worship God as an outsider. Same as the Gentiles... **The book of Deuteronomy explicitly excludes those with damage or altered genitalia from entering the assembly of worship.** Reference for that is Deuteronomy 23:1." (BEMA 137, *A Dry Tree*)

A clear, written, divinely-given boundary marker keyed to sexual anatomy. If any text ever licensed a permanent formal position statement, this is it.

### 4.2 Step two — the reversal (Isaiah 56:3–5)

> "Let no foreigner who is bound to the Lord say, 'The Lord will surely exclude me from his people,' and **let no eunuch complain, 'I am only a dry tree.'** For this is what the Lord says: 'To the eunuchs who keep my Sabbaths, who choose what pleases me and hold fast to my covenant, **to them I will give within my temple and its walls a memorial and a name better than sons and daughters.** I will give them an everlasting name that will endure forever.'"

Marty's live reaction: "Where is he going to put that memorial and name?" — "In the temple." — "**Oh, come on now.**" (BEMA 137)

Isaiah does not reinterpret Deuteronomy 23:1. Isaiah **reverses its application** — and locates the reversal *inside the temple walls*, the precise space the earlier text barred. The criteria shift from **anatomy** to **covenant faithfulness and Sabbath-keeping**: fidelity, not body.

### 4.3 Step three — the enactment (Acts 8:26–40)

Philip meets an Ethiopian eunuch reading Isaiah 53 — a holiday reading, en route home from Jerusalem, where he had stood in the Court of the Gentiles and "caught glimpses — just glimpses — of the house of God." (BEMA 137)

The eunuch's question, "Tell me, please, who is the prophet talking about? Himself or someone else?" (Acts 8:34) is, in BEMA's reading, **not christological**:

> "**My point is that the eunuch's question has nothing to do with the Messiah. His question is about himself.** His question is this: 'Does this exhortation that you are teaching me about, Philip, does it belong only to the prophet Isaiah and the people of God, **or does it even apply to outsiders like me?**'" (BEMA 137)

> "Philip explains to him using Isaiah, possibly Chapter 56, that in Jesus, this good news and call from God is for all people. While he may be excluded from the assembly of worshipers at the temple, **he is not excluded from the community of God and the invitation to partner with him in putting the world back together.**" (BEMA 137)

> "He's asking if there's a place in the Kingdom for him... Philip says, 'In Jesus, yes, you are.' This is what Jesus has shown us about the text... '**I learned from my Jesus,' Philip says, 'that you have a place in God's family.**'" (BEMA 137)

And the eunuch's own words — **Acts 8:36**: "Look, here is water. **What can stand in the way of my being baptized?**" There is no examination, no condition, no statement to sign. Luke records no obstacle.

> "The implications of this new King and a new Kingdom is that **outsiders get to come inside.**" (BEMA 137)

`[outside]` Matthew 19:12 has Jesus speaking approvingly of eunuchs — including "those who were born that way" — as having a legitimate place in the kingdom. And Isaiah 56:7, the verse immediately following the eunuch oracle ("my house will be called a house of prayer for all nations"), is the verse Jesus quotes when he clears the temple (Mark 11:17). Jesus' temple action is grounded in the chapter that abolishes the eunuch's exclusion.

**The disanalogy, stated honestly — because it is the strongest counter-argument available.** A traditional-view reader will point out that eunuchs were excluded for a **bodily condition**, while Isaiah 56 restores them on **behavioral** conditions: "who keep my Sabbaths, who choose what pleases me and hold fast to my covenant." They will then say: *that is precisely our position — welcome the person unconditionally, ask for covenant faithfulness in behavior.* And Matthew 19:12 has a long traditionalist pedigree as a warrant for **celibacy**: some are constituted for abstinence.

That objection deserves to be taken seriously rather than dodged. Here is where it breaks:

**Isaiah's conditions are ones the eunuch can actually meet while remaining a eunuch.** Sabbath-keeping and covenant fidelity are available to him. God does not say "be restored by ceasing to be a eunuch," because that was never on offer. The conditions are real, and they are *reachable*.

**The test this yields:** if the conditions a community sets for full belonging are ones a person cannot meet while remaining who they are, that community has reproduced Deuteronomy 23:1 with additional steps.

This will not be resolved in one conversation — a thoughtful traditionalist will answer that celibacy *is* reachable, and that is a serious answer. What the exchange does establish is that **this is a genuine exegetical disagreement between careful readers of the same texts.** Which is itself an argument against publishing one side of it as an institutional boundary.

### 4.4 Step four — the precedent generalized (Acts 10–11, 15; Ephesians 2)

Peter with Cornelius (Acts 10) receives a vision overturning a written dietary boundary and concludes that God =="did not discriminate between us and them" (Acts 15:9). At the Jerusalem Council he asks: "Why do you try to test God by putting on the necks of Gentiles a yoke that neither we nor our ancestors have been able to bear?" (Acts 15:10)==

**Ephesians 2:14–16**, from **BEMA 158, *Ephesians – Family Membership***:

> "For He himself is our peace, who has made the two groups one, and has destroyed the barrier, **the dividing wall of hostility**... His purpose was to create in himself **one new humanity** out of the two."

> "Paul says that these Gentiles used to find themselves separated from the people of God. They were excluded from citizenship... but Jesus has changed all that and brought them near. In Jesus, **God tore down any sense of division that resided between the groups**... this beautiful scandalous inclusion of the outsiders." (BEMA 158)

The literal dividing wall in the Herodian temple was the one that kept the eunuch in the outer court.

### 4.5 The trajectory, stated plainly

| Stage | Text | Criterion for belonging |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusion | Deuteronomy 23:1 | Anatomy |
| Reversal | Isaiah 56:3–5 | Covenant faithfulness, Sabbath, "what pleases me" |
| Enactment | Acts 8:36 | "What can stand in the way?" — nothing |
| Generalization | Acts 15:28 | "Not to burden you" |
| Theology | Ephesians 2:14 | Wall destroyed; one new humanity |

**The honest claim:** Scripture does not hand us a ruling on same-sex marriage. ## 6. Four cultures, one question

The heaviest weight belongs to the culture at time of writing. The later columns exist to show that Discovery's "traditional" position is itself a cultural artifact with a datable history.

### 6.1 Culture at time of writing (c. 1400 BC – AD 100) — **primary weight**

| Dimension | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Governing sexual logic** | **Dominance and penetration.** "It was about who's penetrating who" (BEMA 149). Roman law reportedly barred a woman from being on top. Status, not gender, was the organizing axis. |
| **Normative practice** | **Pederasty as status quo** among middle-class Romans: "almost every middle-class male child was at some point some older male gentleman's boy" (BEMA 149). |
| **Orientation** | **The concept does not exist.** "The Bible doesn't have the same understanding of orientation that we have today" (BEMA 149). No word, no category, no notion of a durable same-sex disposition. |
| **Marriage** | Property, lineage, household, alliance. `[outside]` Consent and romantic mutuality are not the organizing categories. |
| **The live theological question** | **Who belongs without conforming?** *Theosabes* (BEMA 139); Shammai vs. Hillel on justification (BEMA 140); Acts 15. |
| **Sexual minority actually in view** | **Eunuchs** — excluded by Deuteronomy 23:1, readmitted by Isaiah 56, baptized in Acts 8. |
| **What a biblical author condemning male-male sex is condemning** | Idolatrous Hellenistic cultus, the abuse of boys, and the dominance ethic — "the horrible dysfunction of Roman inappropriate sexuality" (BEMA 149). |

**The decisive point:** the biblical authors are addressing **exploitation, idolatry, and domination**. Discovery's position statement addresses **covenanted, mutual, consensual partnership between adults**. These are different subjects. The statement is not applying the text; it is transplanting vocabulary across a categorical gap the text itself does not bridge.

### 6.2 c. 1600 — Reformation / early modern

`[outside]` Same-sex acts were criminal ("sodomy" statutes, England 1533) and capital. The category was **act**, not identity. Crucially, "sodomy" in this period was a broad and unstable legal category covering various non-procreative acts — and Genesis 19 was read as its charter, *against* Ezekiel 16:49's own stated indictment. The Reformation's interpretive gains — original languages, grammatical-historical reading — were not applied here; Genesis 19 was read allegorically-moralistically, exactly the method the Reformers rejected elsewhere. **Discovery's position inherits a pre-critical reading of Sodom that Ezekiel contradicts.**

### 6.3 c. 1900 — high modernity

`[outside]` The pivot. "Homosexual" (coined 1869) enters English usage; the question shifts from *act* to *type of person*, framed medically as pathology. Church teaching absorbs the new category and retrofits it onto ancient texts. The phrase "homosexuality is sin" — the grammar of Discovery's statement, predicating sin of a **condition or orientation** rather than an act — is **not available before this period**. It is a roughly 120-year-old formulation, not an ancient one. In 1946 the RSV renders *arsenokoitai* "homosexuals," and the retrofit becomes visible in the English Bible itself.

### 6.4 Now

`[outside]` Orientation is understood as a durable, largely non-chosen disposition; civil marriage is available; the church-facing question has become the exit of a generation. **Published boundary positions now function primarily as departure triggers** — which is why the pastoral cost and the interpretive question have converged.

### 6.5 What the comparison establishes

| | At writing | 1600 | 1900 | Now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is "orientation" a category? | No | No | Yes (as pathology) | Yes (as disposition) |
| Governing axis | Status / dominance | Act / criminality | Identity / pathology | Identity / dignity |
| Can you predicate sin of a *condition*? | Category unavailable | No | **Yes — new** | Contested |
| Is the live question "who belongs?" | **Yes** | No | No | **Yes** |

The first and last columns share the live question. **Discovery's position statement is framed in the grammar of the 1900 column** — the one column with no biblical warrant for its central move.

## 8. The Marginalized: the non-negotiable, and the one place Scripture *does* speak flatly

If Discovery wants a clear, repeated, unambiguous biblical mandate about a category of people, it exists — and it is not about sexual orientation.

### 8.1 Uncut corners (Leviticus 19:9–10; 23:22)

From **BEMA 135, *Uncut Corners***:

> "You were commanded **not to cut the corners of your field** during this harvest, leaving it for **the alien, the orphan and the widow**... At Shavuot, you're not just bringing a thank you gift to God at the temple, you're also leaving even more behind you at the corners of your field." (BEMA 135)

> My teacher, Ray, "jumping up and down and saying, '**If there are no uncut corners in your celebration of Jesus, you are not Pentecostal. I don't care how many of you are speaking in tongues.**' In the Jewish mind, it's not Shavuot unless those in need are blessed." (BEMA 135)

Acts 2 ends with the church selling property to meet need (Acts 2:44–45) — Luke's uncut corners. Pentecost is validated by provision for the vulnerable, not by fervor.

### 8.2 The *mamzer*

From **BEMA 84, *Matthew – Mumzer***: a *mamzer* is literally a child of an illegitimate, non-Torah-sanctioned birth — excluded by law from the assembly. Marty uses it "more poetically, as in just **outsider, outcast.**"

> "Matthew's agenda for his Gospel, starting from the opening verses of his genealogy, is the outsider... **that Kingdom means that there are no outsiders.** This Kingdom... is open and available to anybody who wants to participate in the thing that God is doing. Every time you turn the page in Matthew... **you're going to hear stories of all these people who aren't supposed to belong, finding belonging.**" (BEMA 84)

Matthew's genealogy deliberately names Tamar, Rahab, Ruth the Moabite, and "Uriah's wife" — the women "that we would typically like to push out or avoid, that have these dark stories. Matthew is going out of his way to say **those dark stories are a part of God's story.**" (BEMA 84)

### 8.3 What this establishes

The mandate to the alien, orphan, widow, and poor is **stated flatly, repeatedly, and without interpretive ambiguity**. The sexual-ethics texts are **narrow, contested, and addressed to specific cultural practices**. Discovery has published a formal position on the ambiguous category and no formal position on the unambiguous one.

Ezekiel 16:49, again, is the fusion point: the sin of Sodom is arrogance, excess, and neglect of the needy — and the addressee is Jerusalem.

## 9. Discovery, Posture Shift, and where the seam actually is

Discovery follows the **Posture Shift** framework (postureshift.com). Engaging it on its own terms is essential; it is a serious, pastorally-motivated model, and much of it is genuinely good.

### 9.1 Posture Shift in its own words

> "Throughout scripture, **position** is what we believe and **posture** is how we treat others. Posture Shift Ministries, Inc. holds to a **traditional biblical ethic regarding marriage and sexuality (position)**. With equal importance, we believe God calls us to offer His presence, love, care and protection to LGBT+ people (posture)."

> "To those who have been ostracized and condemned, Jesus has two messages. He invites us, 'Come, all who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest for your soul.' And He commands us, 'Now, go and sin no more.'"

> "We believe in the **Creation script** that God's intention for humanity is to be born male or female."

> On gender minorities: "having this experience is **'not' a sex act — it is not sexual immorality. It is an extreme suffering.**"

### 9.2 Genuine common ground — lead with this

| Posture Shift says | BEMA / this document says |
|---|---|
| God goes "around religious legalism to reach people rejected by men" | Acts 8, Acts 10, Acts 15, Matthew's *mamzer* agenda — yes, emphatically |
| Weaponizing faith against people is forbidden | Matthew 11:20–24; the woe falls on devout insiders (BEMA 106) |

**Start the conversation here.** Discovery is not being asked to abandon compassion it already professes. It is being asked to notice where its own framework is already straining.

### 9.3 Where the framework does not hold

**(a) The position/posture split does not survive contact with Matthew 5:17–20.** Posture Shift separates what we believe from how we treat people and calls them of "equal importance." Jesus' rabbinic definition does not permit the split: interpretation *plus* walk equals fulfillment; either one failing equals **abolishing** (BEMA 95). Belief and behavior are one act of obedience. A published position that reliably produces departures is not "correct position, imperfect posture" — ==on this definition it is a failed interpretation, because the walk is the test of the interpretation.==

**(b) Matthew 11:28 is quoted against its own context.** Posture Shift's warrant verse sits four verses after woes pronounced on the most doctrinally rigorous, most sincerely devout communities in Galilee — for failing outsiders (BEMA 106). The weary in Matthew 11 are weary *from religious burden*. Using that invitation to soften a boundary marker inverts the passage.

**(c) "Go and sin no more" presupposes the conclusion.** Pairing Matthew 11:28 with John 8:11 assumes the disputed premise — that what is in view *is* sin in the sense the statement means. And in John 8, Jesus' first act is to dismantle the accusers' standing, and the woman is being used as bait to trap him. `[outside]` The pericope (John 7:53–8:11) is also absent from the earliest manuscripts. It is a thin foundation for a categorical demand.

**(d) The "not a sex act — it is extreme suffering" distinction is already the trajectory.** Posture Shift makes precisely the move Isaiah 56 makes: it separates an embodied, unchosen condition from moral culpability, and responds with belonging and protection rather than exclusion. It applies this to gender minorities. **The argument for stopping there is not exegetical; it is where the framework's authors chose to stop.** Isaiah 56 shows what happens when the same reasoning is carried through: the excluded body is given a name in the temple.

**(e) "Creation script" is a *p'shat* reading treated as the only reading.** Genesis 1's male/female pairing is p'shat, and p'shat is not wrong (BEMA 110). But Genesis 1 also pairs day/night and land/sea, and the canon is full of dawn, dusk, marshes, and estuaries. `[outside]` Matthew 19:12's "eunuchs... born that way" is a third category acknowledged by Jesus inside a discourse on marriage. Depth does not overturn p'shat; it forbids freezing it into a boundary.

### 9.3b The strongest objection to this document, stated in full

Intellectual honesty requires putting the best counter-argument on the table rather than waiting to be handed it. **This is the one that lands hardest, and it comes from the same BEMA episode this document leans on most.**

> *"Your own source divides Torah in three: cultic law, fulfilled in Jesus; **ethical law**, which is 'universally true for all people... the way God made the universe to function'; and* miqsat ma'asay haTorah*, the identity markers. Acts 15 loosed the identity markers — circumcision, kosher, tassels. It did not loose ethics. And the textual proof is that James dropped circumcision and kosher while **explicitly keeping** porneia. Your source even says of James's three prohibitions: 'You can call it ethical law if you want to.' Sexual ethics is ethical law. Your own framework says so."* (BEMA 140)

**The taxonomy is real and should be granted, not denied.** Denying it costs credibility that is needed elsewhere. See §3.4 for the full response; in summary:

| Their move | The response |
|---|---|
| Sexual ethics is ethical law, not an identity marker | **Granted.** This document does not argue Acts 15 loosed sexual ethics. |
| Therefore the traditional application is binding | **Non sequitur.** The category being settled does not settle the *content*. |
| Ethical law means the received list of prohibited acts | Ethical law binds, by BEMA's own definition, because it describes "**the way God made the universe to function**" — a *functional* test, not a taxonomy of bodies. |
| What would a functional test look like? | Exactly what Scripture supplies: Ezekiel 16:49 (arrogance, excess, neglect), Isaiah 56:4 (Sabbath, covenant fidelity), Matthew 5:21–30 (what it forms in you toward others). |
| This is the lax option | It is not. Applied consistently to exploitation, hoarding, and covenant-breaking, it indicts a great deal of heterosexual practice about which nothing has been published. |

**The precise ask that follows:** not that ethical law be loosed, but that it be applied as the functional test BEMA's own definition says it is — and that a contested application of it not be published as an institutional boundary.

### 9.4 "Affirming means universalism" — the conflation to name directly

This is the load-bearing fear, and it is a **category error**. Three distinct questions:

1. **Soteriology** — how is anyone reconciled to God? (Romans 3:21ff — "righteousness from God... by faith")
2. **Ecclesiology** — who belongs in the community, and on what terms? (Acts 15; Ephesians 2)
3. **Ethics** — how is this specific relationship evaluated? (Ezekiel 16:49; Isaiah 56:4)

Universalism is an answer to (1). The affirming/non-affirming question is (2) and (3). **They are not the same question, and Acts 15 is the proof.** The Jerusalem Council loosed Gentiles from the *miqsat ma'asay haTorah* — circumcision, kosher, the identity-marking law — without altering the gospel by one degree. Nobody at that council thought "Gentiles are in without circumcision" meant "everything goes." James still bound three things (Acts 15:20).

> "They decided to side with grace, and they **loosed** the Gentiles from the *miqsat ma'asay haTorah*. **May we find the courage to follow in their steps.**" (BEMA 140)

==Loosing a boundary marker is not dissolving the gospel. It is what the first church council did.==

### 9.5 "There is no middle ground"

Acts 15 is the middle ground, and BEMA 140 names its mechanics: the community binds or looses; dissenters "still talk about it and learn and push and challenge and wrestle because that's a part of the process together"; nobody leaves.

Then Acts 15:36–41: **Paul and Barnabas split over Mark anyway.**

> "Even after the meeting, even after the discussion, they still have a hard time working together. Why? **Because they're just like you and me.**" And: "spoiler alert, by the time Paul is done, he's going to bring Mark back into the fold... He's going to have gone about the work of **reconciliation and restoration.**" (BEMA 140, on 2 Timothy 4:11)

Scripture records both the healthy process **and** its failure **and** the later repair. The canon's own model for "we disagreed and it went badly" is not a permanent statement. It is reconciliation.

## 10. Where Scripture generalizes, and how that applies

Caution: the Bible generalizes, and those generalizations must be handled carefully — and we should not answer them with equally sloppy generalizations about "today's culture."

**Where Scripture generalizes legitimately:**

- **Vice lists** (Romans 1:29–31; 1 Corinthians 6:9–10) are **rhetorical sweeps**, not statutes. Romans 1's list runs "envy, murder, strife, deceit, malice, gossips, slanderers, insolent, arrogant, boastful... they disobey their parents." Anyone treating one item as a disqualifying boundary while ignoring gossip and arrogance is not reading the list; they are mining it. And the list's function is to set up the trap at 2:1.
- **"All have sinned"** (Romans 3:23) is a generalization about the **futility of every measuring rod** in a specific fractured congregation, not an abstract anthropology: "He's not making some theological case for the sinfulness of all human beings... That's not Paul's point." (BEMA 149)
- **Group nouns** — "the Jews," "the Gentiles," "Judeans" — are contextual shorthand that translations flatten and that flattening distorts (BEMA 139 on Acts 13:26).
- **"The alien, the orphan, the widow, the poor"** is a generalization *intended* to be applied broadly — a category deliberately left open so each generation must ask who occupies it now (BEMA 135).

**How to apply them:**

1. Ask who is in the room (BEMA 139, 149).
2. Ask what practice the author's readers would have pictured (BEMA 149 on pederasty).
3. Read the *remez* — where does the quotation come from, and who was its original audience? (BEMA 106, 149)
4. Ask what the command was formed to produce in you toward other people (BEMA 96).
5. Ask whether your walk matches your interpretation, because that is the test (BEMA 95).

**What I am not claiming.** I am not claiming "culture has advanced past the Bible," and I am not offering generalizations about contemporary culture as evidence. Every load-bearing claim here is a claim about **the text in its own setting**. The modern material in §6.2–6.4 exists only to date Discovery's formulation — to show that "homosexuality is sin," predicated of a condition, is a roughly 120-year-old sentence, not an ancient one.

## 11. The Ask

### 11.1 Retire the formal position statement

Not: change your convictions. Not: adopt an affirming theology.

**Specifically:** stop publishing a position on this question as an institutional boundary marker.  It would be just as harmful to say we are affirming.  We should be neither intentionally. Remove the Febuary 22 sermon from streaming or maybe follow up with something else.  Discuss the problems with position and why it doesn't work to set that boundary in posture shift classes.  Follow the example of https://www.churchclarity.org/about.  More clarity is needed for honest representation of values.  

1. **Category error.** Its principal text (Romans 1) addresses idolatrous Hellenism, pederasty, and a dominance ethic — "it's just not even the same conversation" (BEMA 149).
2. **Wrong venue.** Contested application belongs to community binding and loosing (Matthew 16:19; Acts 15; BEMA 140), and a published statement pre-empts the *havurah*.
3. **Failed walk.** Interpretation plus walk equals fulfillment; either failing equals abolishing (BEMA 95). The fruit — exiles — is evidence about the interpretation.
4. **Misallocated clarity.** Formal weight sits on Scripture's contested category while Scripture's unambiguous mandate to the vulnerable carries none (BEMA 135, 106).

### 11.2 Apologize, specifically

Not "we could have been kinder." Name the mechanism: **a published boundary marker that made belonging conditional on a contested interpretation, and that stripped dignity and withheld hospitality from people who came looking for the face of God** (BEMA 106). Zach stated this was a non-essential.  The position boundaries makes it essential and non-equitable.

Scripture's word for this is *t'shuvah* — return. Peter's Pentecost call was not conversion but repentance: "This is not a conversion, this is a repentance... **He's telling them, 'Come back!'**" (BEMA 135) Repentance is what the Triangle towns *believed* without *doing* (BEMA 106).

### 11.3 Reach out to those who left

Acts 15's failure was repaired: Paul brought Mark back (BEMA 140). The uncut corner is not a passive policy — it is deliberately *leaving something behind for someone you may never meet* (BEMA 135). Applied: seek them out. Let them set the terms.

### 11.4 Build equity

- Members who do not hold the traditional view serve, lead, teach, and belong — without being handled as a discipleship problem.
- Both convictions are stated as convictions, not one as orthodoxy and one as compromise.
- Disagreement is structured, not settled: "I'm going to still talk about it and learn and push and challenge and wrestle **because that's a part of the process together**" (BEMA 140).
- Protect this so it is not re-litigated on individuals: **the cost of disagreement must not be borne by the minority alone.**

### 11.5 The closing appeal, in Discovery's own grammar

Posture Shift says the church should be a "safe refuge." Discovery agrees. The disagreement is only about whether a **published position** can coexist with a **safe refuge**.

Acts 8 answers it. A man from a sexual minority, excluded by explicit written Scripture from the assembly, asks: "**What can stand in the way of my being baptized?**"

Philip — who knew his text, who could have quoted Deuteronomy 23:1 — quotes Isaiah instead. Luke records no obstacle.

> "The implications of the Kingdom is that all the people who didn't belong, all the people who were on the outside, all the people who needed to be blessed are finding the blessing. **They're finding the belonging, they're finding the inclusion and they're finding a place to sit at the table** in this new Jesus community." (BEMA 137)

The question on the table is not whether Discovery will affirm. It is whether the community is stronger together — and whether anything Discovery has published should stand in the way.

**Two things to concede early and voluntarily, because conceding them buys credibility for everything above:**

- **Sexual ethics is ethical law, not an identity marker.** Acts 15 loosed circumcision and kosher and kept *porneia*. This document does not dispute that. It disputes what the ethical **content** is — and BEMA's own definition of ethical law ("the way God made the universe to function") is a *functional* test, not a list. See §3.4 and §9.3b.
- **The eunuch trajectory can be read to land on celibacy.** Isaiah 56 restores on behavioral conditions. The reply is that those conditions were *reachable* for a eunuch — and that conditions a person cannot meet while remaining who they are reproduce Deuteronomy 23:1 with extra steps. See §4.3.
